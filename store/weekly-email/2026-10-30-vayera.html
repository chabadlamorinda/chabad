A WORD FROM THE RABBI

Abraham's open tent

This week's Torah portion, Vayera, opens with Abraham sitting at the door of his tent on the hottest day of the year. He is ninety-nine years old and still in pain from his circumcision. Then he sees three strangers on the road, and he doesn't wait for them to knock. He runs to greet them, and he serves them himself.

Our Sages learn that welcoming guests is so great that Abraham even paused his conversation with G-d to do it. The Rebbe often encouraged us to open our homes, especially for a Shabbat meal, to anyone who needs a place. A warm table and a kind word can change someone's whole week.

So here is my invitation: our door is open this Friday night and every Friday night. And if there is someone in your life who could use a Shabbat table, bring them along. That's how we walk in Abraham's footsteps.

Have a wonderful Shabbat and a great week!

Rabbi Israel & Tzipora Labkowski

PARSHAH IN A NUTSHELL · GENESIS 18:1–22:24

Vayera וַיֵּרָא · "And He appeared"

Abraham welcomes three travelers, who turn out to be angels. They announce that Sarah will have a son within a year, and Sarah laughs. Abraham pleads with G-d to spare Sodom for the sake of fifty righteous people, then down to ten. Not even ten are found. Lot and his daughters are rescued, and Lot's wife looks back and becomes a pillar of salt.

Isaac is born to Abraham and Sarah in their old age. Finally G-d tests Abraham with the Binding of Isaac on Mount Moriah. At the last moment an angel stops him, and Abraham offers a ram instead.
