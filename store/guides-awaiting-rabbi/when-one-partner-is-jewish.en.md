If one partner is Jewish and the other is not, you are welcome at Chabad of Lamorinda in Lafayette, and the Rabbi is glad to talk with you privately, warmly and without judgment. In Jewish law a child is Jewish if his or her mother is Jewish (or if he or she converted properly), and the Jewish partner and children are encouraged to live Jewishly through Shabbat, holidays and learning.

This guide is honest and kind at the same time. Please call or write if any part of it raises a question about your own family.

## Every family is welcome

Chabad exists to welcome Jews, whatever their background. The Rebbe once said that people at a beginners' service are not "people without a Jewish background" but descendants of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. A Jew who arrives is a Jew at home.

You and your family are welcome at classes, Shabbat meals and holiday celebrations. We do not ask anyone about membership. Look at [what's on](/events) or read [not religious? you are welcome](/learn/not-religious-welcome).

## Who is Jewish: the mother's line

In Jewish law, a person is Jewish if born to a Jewish mother, or if he or she converted properly. This has been the rule through the generations. So:

- If the mother is Jewish, the children are Jewish.
- If the father is Jewish and the mother is not, the children are not Jewish unless they convert.
- A Jew remains a Jew: the Jewish partner's soul and heritage are always his or her own.

This can be hard to hear, and we say it plainly because you deserve an honest answer. For your own situation, "ask the Rabbi." See also [who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew) and [converting to Judaism](/learn/converting-to-judaism). Conversion is a serious, thoughtful process with a rabbinic court, and the Rabbi can tell you how it works.

## What Chabad.org says about marriage

Chabad.org's position, based on the Torah, is that a Jew marries a Jew, and so the Rabbi does not officiate at a wedding in which one partner is not Jewish. Chabad.org also says that the children of such marriages benefit greatly from a strong Jewish education and a Jewish home, and that parents who live Jewishly give their children the best chance to feel secure in their identity.

None of this makes a family unwelcome, and it does not change the Rabbi's readiness to listen. If you are considering marriage, the Rabbi is happy to talk privately about the questions you have.

## Ways to live Jewishly at home

Small steps are real steps. Some families start with:

1. [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) on Friday night, lit by the woman of the house.
2. A [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) on the front door.
3. A Friday night meal, even a simple one: see [your first Shabbat dinner](/learn/hosting-your-first-shabbat-dinner).
4. A [Jewish preschool](/programs/jewish-preschool) or [Hebrew school](/programs/hebrew-school) for the children.
5. A Hebrew name, and a plan for [Bar and Bat Mitzvah](/programs/bar-bat-mitzvah) when the time comes.

Which of these fits your family, and how, are good questions to take to the Rabbi. Parents of every kind are welcome to ask.

## What the non-Jewish partner can do

A partner who is not Jewish is warmly welcome at every class, Shabbat and holiday. Many are a great support to a Jewish family's Jewish life. Judaism does not ask non-Jews to convert, and a good person of any background has a place in G-d's world: see the guide on the [Noahide laws](/learn/noahide-laws). If a partner is interested in learning more or in conversion, the Rabbi will gladly talk, with no pressure.

## Common questions

### Can an interfaith couple come to Chabad?
Yes. Every Jew and every family is welcome at classes, Shabbat meals and holidays at Chabad of Lamorinda in Lafayette. Call 925-384-7242.

### Are my children Jewish if only my husband is Jewish?
In Jewish law, children follow the mother, so if the mother is not Jewish the children are not Jewish unless they convert. We know this is sensitive; the Rabbi will gladly talk with you privately.

### Are my children Jewish if only I, the mother, am Jewish?
Yes. A child born to a Jewish mother is Jewish, whoever the father is.

### Will the Rabbi officiate at our wedding?
No. Based on the Torah, the Rabbi does not officiate at a wedding in which one partner is not Jewish. He is nonetheless glad to talk with you privately.

### Does my partner have to convert?
No. Judaism does not ask non-Jews to convert. If your partner wants to explore it, the Rabbi can explain the process.

### Can I just talk to the Rabbi privately?
Yes. Call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 or use [talk to the Rabbi](/help/talk-to-the-rabbi). Everything you say stays private.
