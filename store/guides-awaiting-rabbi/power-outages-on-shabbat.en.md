If the power goes out on Shabbat or a holiday, stay calm, use what you prepared before Shabbat, and do not flip switches to try to bring the power back. The one exception is danger to life or health: then you do whatever is needed, and you ask the Rabbi afterwards.

Winter storms and PG&E's Public Safety Power Shutoffs (PSPS), when electricity is turned off on purpose during dangerous fire weather, can reach Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda. A little planning on Friday makes a blackout a quiet Shabbat instead of a stressful one.

## Before Shabbat: a short checklist

PG&E says it aims to send advance notices, beginning about 48 hours before a planned shutoff, to customers who may be affected (Contra Costa Health, and see the City of Lafayette's page on PSPS). So:

1. **Sign up for alerts** from PG&E and for Contra Costa County's emergency alerts (cwsalerts.com), and keep your contact details current.
2. **Check the weather on Thursday and Friday** if it is storm or fire season.
3. **Put out flashlights or lanterns before Shabbat** and turn them on before Shabbat begins if you may want light (see below). Don't rely on candles alone around children.
4. **Prepare food that is fine at room temperature:** challah, salads, cooked dishes already hot in an insulated container, fruit, nuts. Keep the refrigerator and freezer closed.
5. **Set aside what must stay cold,** such as medicines, in a cooler with ice packs.
6. **Charge your phone** before candle lighting, for an emergency.
7. **Write down emergency numbers** on paper, since you cannot use a screen on Shabbat. Find this week's time at [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times); this week Kabbalat Shabbat is {kabbalat-shabbat}.

## If the power goes out on Shabbat

Chabad.org explains that using electrical appliances is forbidden on Shabbat, and that turning a light on or off is a Shabbat violation (Electricity on Shabbat). Read more in [electricity on Shabbat](/learn/electricity-on-shabbat). So when the lights go out:

- **Do not flip switches.** Do not try the circuit breaker. Do not unplug or move things to "fix" it.
- **Leave switches as they are.** If the power returns while you are not home or are asleep, lights or appliances that were left "on" may turn on by themselves. You may not have turned them on, but it is best to be careful. Many people ask the Rabbi before Shabbat about how to leave things that may restart.
- **Use the Shabbat candles and the light you set up.** Ask the Rabbi in advance about using flashlights and lamps that were left on before Shabbat.
- **Don't open the refrigerator more than needed.** Food stays cold longer. If your refrigerator has a light, ask the Rabbi how to handle it before Shabbat.
- **Cooking.** If the stove is out, keep to foods that need no cooking. Read [keeping food warm on Shabbat](/learn/keeping-food-warm-on-shabbat).
- **Dark house, no candle burning,** and children around? Choose safety over everything.

## When life or health is at risk

Pikuach nefesh, saving a life, overrides Shabbat (Chabad.org). If someone depends on a medical device, if a person is ill, elderly or frail, if it is dangerously cold or there is a fire or gas danger, do what is necessary: use lights, a heater, a phone or a car, and call emergency services. Don't delay to ask. Medical needs during an outage, such as battery power for a device, are also a matter to plan for ahead with PG&E, which offers programs for customers who rely on medical devices. Call the Rabbi afterwards.

Chabad.org also explains that you may call the fire department or use a fire extinguisher if there is danger (Mechabeh). Candles left burning in a blackout are a fire danger. Never leave them alone.

## When the power comes back on

If the electricity returns on Shabbat, do not turn anything on or off in response. Lights or appliances that start by themselves are a question for the Rabbi, because the details depend on what was left on and how. That is why many families ask the Rabbi before Shabbat how to leave their switches. After Shabbat, reset clocks and check the freezer.

## Common questions

### Can I turn on a light on Shabbat if the power is out?

No. Do not flip switches; if the lights come on by themselves, you generally need to ask the Rabbi what to do. If anyone's health or safety is at risk, do whatever is needed.

### Is it okay to use a flashlight on Shabbat during a blackout?

A light left on before Shabbat is a question to ask the Rabbi in advance. Turning one on during Shabbat is not allowed unless there is danger. Ask the Rabbi about your situation.

### What if the food in my fridge spoils on Shabbat?

Keep the door closed as much as possible and don't worry about it until after Shabbat. If food safety is a concern for health, ask the Rabbi. Dispose of any unsafe food.

### What is a PG&E Public Safety Power Shutoff?

It is a planned power outage that PG&E may use in dangerous fire weather to reduce wildfire risk. PG&E aims to notify customers in advance.

### Can I light candles in a blackout on Friday evening?

Yes, you can light Shabbat candles at the proper time, with extra safety: stable tray, no curtains near, never unattended.

### Who should I call if I have a question during an outage on Shabbat?

If it is an emergency, call 911. For questions about Shabbat and holidays, call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242, or leave a message and he will respond after Shabbat.
