Orthodox, Conservative and Reform are names that describe different approaches to Jewish life, which took shape in recent centuries; none of them appears in the Torah. Chabad teaches that there is one Torah and one Jewish people, and at Chabad of Lamorinda in Lafayette every Jew is welcome as he or she is, with no label and no membership required.

## Where the labels came from

Many Jews today describe themselves with one of these three words. They are used to describe how much of traditional Jewish observance a person or a community keeps and how they relate to it. These names are modern: they come from the last few centuries, not from the Torah or from classical Jewish writings.

Our guide does not describe the teachings of any movement, and we do not rank anyone. The question we answer here is the one visitors often ask us: what does Chabad think, and do I need to fit a label to come?

## What Chabad.org says

Chabad.org has published a piece that puts the Chabad view plainly: labels divide a people that is, at its core, one. Its reasoning runs along these lines:

- The labels are modern and have no basis in the Torah or classical Jewish literature.
- Once a person is labeled, it can become easy to stop exploring beyond what the label is thought to allow.
- A label can also become a way to feel Jewish without growing in Jewish life.
- All Jews share one identity: Jewish. The Kol Nidrei prayer on Yom Kippur, which asks that vows be annulled, was described there as a symbolic setting aside of the labels that cloud that shared identity.

In short, Chabad's focus is on the person, not the label.

## One mitzvah at a time

Chabad also teaches, following the Lubavitcher Rebbe, that observance is not all or nothing. Every mitzvah is precious. G-d values effort and growth more than perfection. Some Jews keep more mitzvahs and some keep fewer, and a Jew who does one more mitzvah this week than last week has done something real.

That is why Chabad centers welcome people at every level of experience. You might light [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) for the first time, put up a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah), or come to a class. Each is a step, and nobody is behind.

## Is Chabad Orthodox, and do I have to be Orthodox to come?

Chabad is a Chassidic movement within Orthodox Judaism, and it follows traditional Jewish law. But you do not need to be Orthodox, observant or "religious" to come. Chabad exists to welcome every Jew. Read [not religious? you are welcome](/learn/not-religious-welcome) and [what Chabad is](/learn/chabad).

Is it comfortable if you are not used to Jewish practice? Visit for a class or a Shabbat meal and see for yourself.

## Ahavat Yisrael: love for every Jew

The Chabad word for this is ahavat Yisrael (אַהֲבַת יִשְׂרָאֵל), the love of every Jew, because every Jew has a soul that is a part of G-d. That love is not conditional on what a person keeps or believes. See our guide on [ahavat Yisrael](/learn/ahavat-yisrael).

## Common questions

### What is the difference between Orthodox, Conservative and Reform Judaism?
They are modern names for different approaches to Jewish life and observance, which arose over the past few centuries. For how any particular approach understands Jewish law, ask a rabbi of that community; Chabad's own approach is to welcome every Jew warmly.

### Is Chabad Orthodox?
Yes, Chabad is a Chassidic movement within Orthodox Judaism, and its Rabbis follow traditional Jewish law. It also welcomes every Jew, with no requirement to be Orthodox or observant.

### Do I have to be Orthodox to go to Chabad?
No. Chabad of Lamorinda welcomes every Jew, whatever the background, however much or little he or she keeps, and no membership is needed.

### What kind of Jew am I?
You are a Jew, and that is enough. A Jew is someone born to a Jewish mother or who converted properly (see [who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew)). Judaism is lived one step at a time.

### Why does Chabad avoid labels?
Chabad.org's view is that labels are modern, divide a people that is one, and can limit a person's growth. Chabad prefers to ask what mitzvah you can do next.
