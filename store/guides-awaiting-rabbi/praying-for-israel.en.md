You can pray for Israel from anywhere by saying Psalms, speaking to G-d in your own words, and taking on a mitzvah in honor of the people of Israel. Chabad.org recommends five chapters of Psalms for difficult times, 20, 22, 69, 124 and 150, and says that the Jewish people's strength comes from prayer and from mitzvahs.

This guide is about prayer and mitzvahs only. It does not take up news, policy or politics.

## Why Psalms

Psalms (Tehillim, תְּהִלִּים) were written by King David, and for thousands of years Jews have turned to them in times of fear and hope. They give words when we have none. Anyone can say them: you do not need a minyan, and you can say them in Hebrew or in English. See our guide on [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim).

## Five Psalms for difficult times

Chabad.org suggests these five chapters, for private and communal prayer. Here is what each one is about:

1. **Psalm 20.** A prayer for help on a day of distress, trusting in G-d and not in military strength.
2. **Psalm 22.** A cry of anguish at feeling far from help, which turns to trust that G-d will rescue.
3. **Psalm 69.** A plea for rescue from overwhelming danger, humiliation and enemies.
4. **Psalm 124.** A song of thanks for having been spared from being overwhelmed.
5. **Psalm 150.** Pure praise, which closes the set.

For comfort and guard in general, many people also say [Psalm 121](/learn/psalm-121) and [Psalm 23](/learn/psalm-23).

## A short prayer in your own words

Chabad.org also encourages setting aside a daily prayer for Israel, for the injured, the families of those who lost loved ones, and those in captivity, and speaking to G-d in your own words. Something like:

*"Father in Heaven, please protect Your people and the Land of Israel. Heal the injured, comfort those who mourn, bring home those who are far from home, and bring peace. Amen."*

You do not need fancy words. Just speak from the heart.

## Mitzvahs you can take on

Chabad.org lists practical, spiritual things you can do:

- **Tefillin.** Men and boys over bar mitzvah age can put on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) every weekday.
- **Shabbat candles.** Women and girls can light [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) before Shabbat and before holidays.
- **Mezuzahs.** Have your mezuzahs checked: [checking mezuzahs](/learn/checking-mezuzahs-and-tefillin).
- **Tzedakah.** Give [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah). Chabad.org notes the protective power of charity is considered especially strong.
- **Good deeds and Torah.** Do a mitzvah in honor of the people of Israel, and learn some Torah: see [daily Torah study](/learn/daily-torah-study).
- **Faith.** Share your trust in G-d with family and friends.

Read more in [Israel and the Jewish people](/learn/israel-and-the-jewish-people).

## Common questions

### How do I pray for Israel?
Say Psalms, such as 20, 22, 69, 124 and 150, and add a few words of your own. Then take on a mitzvah in honor of the people of Israel.

### Which Psalms should I say for Israel?
Chabad.org recommends Psalms 20, 22, 69, 124 and 150 for difficult times. Many people also add Psalm 121.

### How can I help Israel from here?
Chabad.org suggests daily prayer, Psalms, tefillin, Shabbat candles, checking your mezuzahs, giving tzedakah, doing mitzvahs and sharing faith in G-d.

### Do I need a minyan to say Psalms?
No. Psalms can be said by anyone, alone or in a group, at any time.

### Can I say Psalms in English?
Yes. Hebrew is traditional, but you may say them in a language you understand.
