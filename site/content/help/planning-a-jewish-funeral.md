Planning a Jewish funeral in advance is one of the kindest things you can do for your family. It spares them hard decisions at a painful time, and it makes sure your wishes and Jewish tradition are honored. Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski is glad to sit with you, privately and with no pressure, to explain what is involved and help you plan. Call **925-384-7242** to set up a time.

If a loved one has just passed away, please see [When a Loved One Passes Away](/help/someone-passed-away) and call the Rabbi now.

## Why plan ahead

- **Peace of mind.** You know that you, or your parents, will have a proper Jewish burial.
- **A gift to your family.** Your children will not have to guess what you wanted or make rushed choices.
- **Avoiding mistakes.** Some arrangements, like embalming or cremation, go against Jewish tradition. Planning ahead makes sure everyone knows what to do.

## Step by step

1. **Talk with the Rabbi.** He will explain the Jewish way of burial and answer your questions.
2. **Choose a Jewish cemetery plot.** A Jewish burial takes place in a Jewish cemetery or a Jewish section of a cemetery. The Rabbi can help you understand your options in the area.
3. **Write down your wishes.** Include your Hebrew name and your father's and mother's Hebrew names, the people to call, and a request that the Rabbi and the Chevra Kadisha be contacted first.
4. **Tell your family.** Share your plans and keep a copy with your important papers.
5. **Keep our number handy.** Make sure your family knows to call 925-384-7242 first.

## What a Jewish funeral includes

- **Tahara.** The Chevra Kadisha (Jewish burial society) gently washes the body and dresses it in simple white linen shrouds called tachrichim.
- **A simple casket.** Traditionally a plain wooden casket, so that all are equal in death and the body returns naturally to the earth.
- **No embalming, no viewing.** The body is treated with dignity and privacy.
- **Burial soon after death.** Ideally within a day.
- **A short service.** Psalms, a eulogy, the memorial prayer El Malei Rachamim, and Kaddish at the graveside. Family and friends help fill the grave, a final act of kindness.

## Common questions

### Is it morbid to plan my own funeral?
Not at all. Jewish tradition sees it as a responsible and loving act. Many people feel relieved once it is done.

### Can someone who was not observant be buried in a Jewish cemetery?
Yes. Every Jew is entitled to a Jewish burial. The Rabbi will help you with the details.

### Can a non-Jewish spouse be buried next to a Jewish spouse?
This depends on the cemetery and on Jewish law. It is a sensitive question, and the Rabbi will guide you personally.

### What should I write down for my family?
Your full Hebrew name, your parents' Hebrew names, your cemetery plot details, and a clear request to call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242 before anything else.

### Does Judaism allow cremation?
Jewish tradition requires burial in the earth and does not permit cremation. The Rabbi is happy to explain why, gently and without judgment.
