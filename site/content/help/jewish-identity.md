Questions about Jewish identity are more common than you might think. Maybe a DNA test surprised you, a grandparent shared a family secret, or you grew up with little connection and want to know more. Whatever your question, Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski of Chabad of Lamorinda is happy to talk with you, privately and without judgment. Call **925-384-7242** or reach out through [/contact](/contact).

## Who is Jewish?

According to Jewish law, a person is Jewish if their **mother is Jewish**, or if they converted according to Jewish law. Being Jewish does not depend on how you were raised, what you believe, or how much you practice. A Jew is always a Jew.

This means some people are Jewish without knowing it, and some who feel Jewish need to look into their background. The Rabbi will help you understand your own situation. Learn more in our guide [Who Is a Jew?](/learn/who-is-a-jew)

## Exploring Jewish ancestry

If you think you may have Jewish roots, here is how to start:

1. **Talk to family.** Ask older relatives about your mother's mother, and her mother. Names, towns and stories can all help.
2. **Look for documents.** Old ketubahs (marriage contracts), synagogue records, burial records, letters and photos can all be useful.
3. **Talk to the Rabbi.** He can help you understand what you have found and what else might help. Each case is different, and the Rabbi will guide you.

DNA tests can be a clue, but they do not on their own establish Jewish status under Jewish law.

## Returning to Judaism

Many Jews grew up with little or no Jewish life. If you want to reconnect, you are warmly welcome. There is no test to pass and no starting line. Every mitzvah counts, and you can begin with one small step.

- Join us for [Shabbat](/shabbat) or an [event](/events).
- Learn something new from our guides, such as [prayer](/learn/prayer) or [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah).
- Put on tefillin with the Rabbi, light Shabbat candles, or put up a mezuzah.
- Simply come and talk.

The Lubavitcher Rebbe taught that every Jew has a spark of G-d within that can never be extinguished. It is always there, waiting.

## Interfaith families

If you are in an interfaith relationship or family, you are welcome to talk with the Rabbi. He will listen with respect, answer your questions honestly, and explain the Jewish view with warmth. Many interfaith families have meaningful conversations with us about their children, holidays, and what Judaism means to them.

## Conversion

People sometimes ask about converting to Judaism. Judaism does not seek converts, and a proper conversion is a serious, lifelong commitment overseen by a qualified Beth Din. If you are considering it, the Rabbi is happy to have an honest conversation with you and point you in the right direction.

## Common questions

### My father is Jewish but my mother is not. Am I Jewish?
According to Jewish law, Jewish status passes through the mother. The Rabbi would be glad to talk with you personally about your situation and your connection to Judaism.

### My grandmother was Jewish but nobody talked about it. What should I do?
If it was your mother's mother, you may well be Jewish. Gather what information you can and call the Rabbi. He will guide you.

### Does a DNA test prove I am Jewish?
A DNA test can be a helpful clue, but it is not proof under Jewish law. Family history and documents matter more.

### I have not been involved in Judaism for years. Am I still welcome?
Absolutely. Every Jew is welcome at Chabad of Lamorinda, always.

### Can I talk to the Rabbi if I am not Jewish?
Yes. If you have questions about Judaism or a Jewish family member, the Rabbi is happy to talk.
