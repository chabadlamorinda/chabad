Every marriage and every family goes through hard times. If you are struggling, you do not have to carry it alone. Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski and Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski are here to listen, privately and without judgment. Call **925-384-7242** or reach out through [/contact](/contact) to set up a confidential conversation. You can talk with the Rabbi, the Rebbetzin, or both, alone or as a couple.

## Come early, not last

We help with every kind of dispute: between husband and wife, parents and children, siblings, in-laws, neighbors, business partners, clients and employees, and with great success. Don't wait until things spiral out of control. The best time to talk is at the first sign of trouble, when a misunderstanding can still be cleared up in one conversation. And if things have already gone far, it is never too late: with the Torah's way and a positive outlook, there is a way forward from any situation.

## What we can offer

- **A safe place to talk.** Sometimes it helps simply to say what is happening to someone who cares and keeps it private.
- **Jewish wisdom.** Torah has deep teachings about marriage, respect, patience, and repairing relationships. We can share them in a practical way.
- **A fresh perspective.** A calm outside voice can help you see a way forward.
- **Referral to professionals.** When a marriage counselor, family therapist or other professional is the right help, the Rabbi can refer you to trusted people.

The Rabbi is not a licensed therapist, and our conversations are not a substitute for professional counseling. We often work best alongside it.

## If you are not safe

If you or your children are in danger, call **911** right away. Your safety comes first. Jewish law is very clear that no one should stay in a home where they are being hurt. After you are safe, the Rabbi and Rebbetzin are here to support you and help you find the right resources.

## What is shalom bayit?

**Shalom bayit** means "peace in the home." Judaism treats it as one of the highest values. The Talmud teaches that G-d even allowed His own name to be erased, in the ritual of the Sotah, to bring peace between husband and wife. That is how important a peaceful home is.

Shalom bayit does not mean pretending there are no problems. It means working, with honesty and respect, to build a home where everyone feels safe, valued and loved.

## Some Jewish ideas that help

- **Respect.** The Talmud teaches that a husband should honor his wife more than himself, and Jewish tradition asks spouses to treat each other with dignity, especially in hard moments.
- **Words matter.** Judaism takes speech very seriously. Hurtful words leave marks. Kind words can heal.
- **Judge favorably.** Try to see the good intention behind your spouse's actions.
- **Give before you get.** Small acts of kindness can change the atmosphere of a home.
- **Bring holiness in.** Many couples find that Shabbat, family meals and Jewish practices bring them closer.

## Family difficulties

Tension with parents, adult children, siblings or in-laws can be painful. The Rabbi can listen, help you think through what to do, and, if everyone agrees, help bring family members together to talk. See also [Family and Business Disputes](/help/disputes).

## If the marriage is ending

Sometimes, despite every effort, a marriage ends. If you are Jewish and divorcing, a **get** (Jewish divorce) is important for both spouses, especially before either remarries. The Rabbi can explain the process and refer you to a proper Beth Din. We will treat you with care and respect at every stage.

## Common questions

### Is what I share kept private?
Yes. Conversations with the Rabbi and Rebbetzin are confidential. The only exception is when someone's safety is at risk.

### Can I talk to the Rebbetzin instead of the Rabbi?
Of course. Many women prefer to speak with the Rebbetzin. Call 925-384-7241 and ask.

### Do we have to be observant to come?
Not at all. We help Jews of every background, and interfaith couples are welcome to talk.

### What is a get and why is it needed?
A get is a Jewish divorce document. Without it, a couple remains married under Jewish law. The Rabbi will explain and refer you to a proper Beth Din.

### Is the Rabbi a marriage counselor?
The Rabbi offers spiritual guidance and a caring ear. For counseling, he can refer you to trusted professionals.
