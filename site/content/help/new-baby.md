Mazal Tov! A new baby is a tremendous blessing, and we would love to celebrate with you. Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski and Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski of Chabad of Lamorinda can help you arrange a bris for a baby boy, a naming at the Torah for a baby girl, a pidyon haben when it applies, and help choosing a Hebrew name. Call **925-384-7242**, ideally soon after the birth, and we will take it from there.

## If you had a boy: the bris

The **bris milah** (circumcision) is the covenant between G-d and the Jewish people, going back to Abraham. It takes place on the **eighth day** of the baby's life, even on Shabbat or a Jewish holiday, as long as the baby is healthy.

1. **Call the Rabbi soon after the birth.** He will help you work out the correct date. Remember that the Jewish day starts at sunset, so a baby born in the evening may have a different bris date.
2. **Arrange a mohel.** The Rabbi can help you arrange a qualified mohel, a person trained in both the medical and religious parts of the bris.
3. **Plan the ceremony.** We can help with honors for family members, the blessings, and a festive meal afterward.

If the baby has jaundice or any health concern, the bris is postponed until the baby is well. The mohel and your doctor will advise, and the Rabbi will guide you.

Learn more in our [brit milah guide](/learn/brit-milah).

## If you had a girl: the baby naming

A baby girl receives her Hebrew name when her father, or another family member, is called up to the Torah in synagogue. This is usually done at the first Torah reading after the birth, on a Monday, Thursday or Shabbat. A special blessing is said for the mother and baby.

Call us to arrange the naming. You are welcome to sponsor a Kiddush to celebrate at [/sponsor](/sponsor). See our [baby naming guide](/learn/baby-naming).

## Choosing a Hebrew name

A Hebrew name is the name of the soul. Many families name after a relative who has passed away, or after a figure from the Torah. Jewish tradition teaches that parents have a touch of prophecy when choosing a name. The Rabbi is happy to help you find a meaningful name. See our guide to [Hebrew names](/learn/hebrew-names).

## Pidyon haben

If your baby is a firstborn son, born naturally to his mother, and neither parent is a Kohen or Levi, there is a special ceremony called **pidyon haben**, the redemption of the firstborn, on the 31st day. The Rabbi will guide you on whether it applies and help you arrange it with a Kohen.

## The first weeks

- Call the Rabbi as soon as you can after the birth.
- It is a custom to place a card with Psalm 121 (Shir Hamaalot) in the baby's room for protection.
- Many families give charity in honor of the new baby.
- Rest. Let the community help with meals. Just ask.

## Common questions

### When is the bris?
On the eighth day after birth, counting the day of birth as day one. A baby born on Monday has his bris the next Monday. Births after sunset count as the next day.

### Can you help us find a mohel?
Yes. The Rabbi can help you arrange a qualified mohel.

### We are an interfaith family. Can we still have a bris or naming?
Please call the Rabbi. He welcomes every family and will talk with you about your situation warmly and personally.

### Does a baby girl have a ceremony?
Yes. She is named at the Torah, usually at the first Torah reading after birth, and families often celebrate with a Kiddush.

### What is an upsherin?
A Jewish boy's first haircut at age three. Learn more in our [upsherin guide](/learn/upsherin).
