When someone you love is sick, it can feel like there is little you can do. Judaism teaches that there is a great deal you can do. Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski and Chabad of Lamorinda are here to help. Call **925-384-7242** to ask for a visit, add a name to our prayer list, or simply talk. We will pray for your loved one, visit if they wish, and stand by your family.

## What you can do today

1. **Add their name to our Tehillim prayer list.** We need their Hebrew name and their mother's Hebrew name, for example "Moshe ben Sarah" or "Rivka bat Leah." If you do not know the Hebrew names, English names are fine. Visit [/tehillim](/tehillim) or call us.
2. **Ask for a Mi Shebeirach.** This is a special prayer for healing said when the Torah is read in synagogue. We will say it for your loved one.
3. **Say Tehillim.** Psalms are the traditional words of prayer in hard times. Even one chapter a day helps. Psalm 20 and Psalm 121 are often said for the sick.
4. **Give charity in their merit.** Tzedakah is a powerful merit. You can give any amount, or sponsor a Kiddush or class in their honor at [/sponsor](/sponsor).

## Bikur cholim: visiting the sick

Visiting the sick, called **bikur cholim**, is one of the most important mitzvot in Judaism. The Talmud teaches that a visit removes a part of the person's suffering.

The Rabbi and Rebbetzin are glad to visit at home, in the hospital or in a care facility. A visit can be a few quiet minutes, a prayer, a blessing, or just a friendly conversation. If you would like to visit someone yourself, keep it short, be cheerful, listen more than you talk, and offer a prayer if it feels right.

## Checking mezuzahs and tefillin

There is a long-standing Jewish custom, encouraged by the Lubavitcher Rebbe, to have the mezuzahs in the home checked by a qualified scribe when someone is unwell. A mezuzah is a sign of G-d's protection over the home, and the parchment can fade or crack over time. If the person puts on tefillin, those are checked too.

The Rabbi can arrange for your mezuzahs and tefillin to be checked, and help you replace any that are not kosher.

## Taking care of yourself

Caring for someone who is sick is hard. You may feel tired, worried or alone. Please reach out. We can listen, help you find support in the community, and refer you to trusted professionals when needed. In a medical emergency, always call 911 first.

## The Jewish meaning

Judaism sees healing as coming from G-d, working through doctors and medicine. The Torah commands us to seek medical care, and it also invites us to pray. Prayer, charity and good deeds are not instead of medicine. They are alongside it, opening channels of blessing.

## Common questions

### How do I add someone to the prayer list?
Call 925-384-7242 or visit [/tehillim](/tehillim). Share the person's Hebrew name and mother's Hebrew name. English names are fine if you do not know them.

### What is a Mi Shebeirach?
It is a prayer for healing said during the Torah reading. We say it for everyone on our list, and you are welcome to come and hear it said for your loved one.

### Can the Rabbi visit someone who is not Jewish, or a Jewish person who is not observant?
The Rabbi visits Jews of every background, with no judgment. For interfaith families, he is glad to talk with you about what would be most comforting.

### Why check the mezuzahs when someone is ill?
It is a custom of care for the home's spiritual protection. A kosher mezuzah is a mitzvah, and checking it is a way of doing everything we can.

### What if the illness becomes serious?
Please call the Rabbi. See [When Someone Is Very Ill or Near the End of Life](/help/end-of-life).
