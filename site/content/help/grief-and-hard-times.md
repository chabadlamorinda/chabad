Life can be very hard. Whether you are grieving, lonely, struggling with illness, addiction, a crisis, or just a heavy heart, you are not alone. Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski and Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski of Chabad of Lamorinda are here to listen and to help, with warmth and complete confidentiality. Call **925-384-7242** or reach out through [/contact](/contact). There is no need to explain why. Just say you would like to talk.

## If you are in crisis

**If you are thinking about harming yourself, or someone is in danger, call 911 now.** In the US, you can also call or text **988** to reach the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline at any time.

The Rabbi cares deeply, but he is not a substitute for emergency services or professional mental health care. Please reach out to them first. Then reach out to us too.

## How we can help

- **Talk to the Rabbi or Rebbetzin.** A private, unhurried conversation. You can share as much or as little as you like.
- **Community.** Loneliness is one of the hardest things. Come to a Shabbat meal, a class or an event. You will be welcomed warmly. See [/events](/events) and [/shabbat](/shabbat).
- **Prayer.** We can add your name, or a loved one's, to our [Tehillim list](/tehillim), and pray with you.
- **Practical help.** Sometimes what is needed is a meal, a visit, or a connection. Let us know.
- **Referral to professionals.** When a therapist, counselor, doctor or support group is the right help, the Rabbi can refer you to trusted professionals.

## Grief

Grief does not follow a schedule. Judaism understands this. Its mourning stages, shiva, shloshim, the year of Kaddish, and the yahrzeit each year, give structure and community to a process that can feel overwhelming. They let us mourn fully, and slowly return to life.

If you have lost someone, even long ago, you are welcome to talk. You can say [Kaddish](/kaddish) with our minyan, light a yahrzeit candle (see our [yahrzeit calculator](/tools/yahrzeit)), or do a mitzvah in their memory.

## Loneliness

Many people in our community live alone, are far from family, or feel disconnected. Judaism teaches, "It is not good for man to be alone." We would love to have you at our table. Call us. We mean it.

## Addiction and other struggles

There is no shame in struggling. Many good people face addiction, depression, anxiety or other challenges. The Rabbi will listen without judgment, offer spiritual support, and help you find professional help.

## A Jewish perspective on suffering and hope

Judaism does not pretend to explain every pain. Even Moses asked G-d why the righteous suffer. But Jewish tradition holds a deep belief that G-d is with us in our pain, that every soul has a purpose, and that darkness can be turned to light. The Lubavitcher Rebbe taught that we should "think good, and it will be good." Hope is a Jewish value.

Small steps help: a prayer, a chapter of [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim), a kind act for someone else, a Shabbat candle. Each brings a little light.

## Common questions

### Do I need to be religious to talk to the Rabbi?
No. The Rabbi helps Jews of every background, and no question or feeling is out of place.

### Is it confidential?
Yes. What you share stays private, except where someone's safety is at risk.

### Can the Rabbi replace therapy?
No. The Rabbi offers spiritual support and a caring ear. For mental health care, he can refer you to trusted professionals.

### What does Judaism say about grief?
Judaism honors grief with clear stages of mourning, community support, and ways to keep a loved one's memory alive, such as Kaddish and the yahrzeit.

Call 925-384-7242, or come to a Shabbat meal or event. We would love to meet you.
