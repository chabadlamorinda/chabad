Disputes with family members, business partners or others in the community can be among the most painful things we face. If you are in a conflict, Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski of Chabad of Lamorinda is here to help. He can listen in confidence, help you think it through, mediate in the spirit of Torah, or refer you to a Beth Din for a din Torah. Call **925-384-7242** or reach out through [/contact](/contact).

## How the Rabbi can help

1. **Listening.** Often the first step is a private conversation. You can share the whole story with someone who cares and will keep it confidential.
2. **Guidance.** The Rabbi can share what Torah teaches about fairness, honesty, forgiveness and your obligations, and help you see the situation more clearly.
3. **Mediation.** If both sides are willing, the Rabbi can bring you together for a calm conversation, in the spirit of Torah, to look for a solution that both can accept.
4. **Din Torah.** When a formal decision is needed, the Rabbi can refer you to a proper Beth Din (rabbinical court) for a din Torah.
5. **Referral.** Where you need a lawyer, accountant or other professional, the Rabbi can refer you to trusted people.

The Rabbi does not give legal advice, and his help is not a substitute for a lawyer.

## What is a din Torah?

A **din Torah** is a case heard by a Beth Din, a court of rabbis expert in Jewish civil law. Jewish tradition encourages Jews to bring disputes with one another to a Beth Din rather than to secular court where possible. A Beth Din can act as mediator, as arbitrator, or both, and its decision can often be made binding by a signed arbitration agreement.

Whether a din Torah is right for your situation depends on many details. The Rabbi will guide you.

## Wills and inheritance disputes

Arguments over a will or an estate can tear families apart, often at a time of grief. Jewish law has its own laws of inheritance, and many families today want to make sure their wills respect both civil law and Torah.

- **If you are planning a will,** the Rabbi can talk with you about Jewish perspectives and suggest you work with a lawyer to prepare documents that reflect your wishes.
- **If there is a dispute,** the Rabbi can listen, try to help the family come together, and refer you to a Beth Din if needed.

## Business disputes

Torah has detailed laws about business: honest weights and measures, paying workers on time, keeping your word, and not taking advantage of others. When partners fall out, or a deal goes wrong, the Rabbi can help both sides find a fair and respectful resolution, or refer you to a Beth Din.

## The Jewish meaning

The Torah says, "Seek peace and pursue it." Aaron the High Priest was known for going from person to person to make peace between them. Judaism teaches that resolving a dispute fairly, and forgiving when we can, brings blessing to everyone involved. Even when we cannot agree, we can treat each other with dignity.

## Common questions

### Will the Rabbi take sides?
No. When mediating, the Rabbi aims to be fair to everyone and to help both sides reach a peaceful outcome.

### Is a Beth Din decision legally binding?
In many cases, when both parties sign an arbitration agreement, a Beth Din's ruling can be legally binding. The Rabbi will explain how it works for your case.

### Is it wrong to go to secular court?
Jewish law encourages bringing disputes between Jews to a Beth Din. There are situations where other routes are allowed. The Rabbi will guide you.

### Can the Rabbi help with my will?
The Rabbi can share Jewish guidance on inheritance and encourage you to work with a lawyer so your wishes are clear.

### Is our conversation confidential?
Yes. What you share with the Rabbi stays private.
