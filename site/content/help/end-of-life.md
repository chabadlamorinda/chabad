When someone you love is very ill or near the end of life, you do not have to face it alone. Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski and Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski are here for you and your family. Please call **925-384-7242**. The Rabbi can visit at home, in the hospital or in hospice, pray with your loved one, sit with your family, and gently help you prepare for what lies ahead, in a way that honors Jewish tradition and your loved one's wishes.

## What to do now

1. **Call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242.** Let him know where your loved one is and how they are doing.
2. **Ask for a visit.** A visit from the Rabbi can bring great comfort, both to the person who is ill and to the family.
3. **Add their name to our prayer list.** Share their Hebrew name and their mother's Hebrew name (or English names if you do not know them). See [/tehillim](/tehillim).
4. **Talk about wishes, if the time is right.** The Rabbi can help you have gentle conversations about Jewish burial and other wishes.

## How we help

- **Visits and prayer.** The Rabbi will visit, recite Tehillim (Psalms), and offer a blessing.
- **Being with the family.** We sit with you, listen, and help you through hard decisions and long days.
- **Vidui.** When the time is near, there is a short, beautiful prayer called Vidui, a final confession and declaration of faith, ending with the Shema. The Rabbi can say it with your loved one, or guide a family member to do so. It does not hasten death, and many people who say it recover.
- **Medical decisions.** Jewish law has much to say about end-of-life care. For case-specific questions, the Rabbi will guide you, alongside your loved one's medical team.
- **Planning ahead.** We can help you prepare for a Jewish burial so that, when the moment comes, everything is in place.

## Being present in the final hours

Jewish tradition teaches that it is a great kindness to stay close to a person in their final hours. Speak to them softly, hold their hand, and say the Shema together if you can. Hearing is thought to remain until the end. Many families keep the room calm and peaceful, with words of love and prayer.

If you are unsure what to say, the Rabbi can help. Sometimes simply being there is the most important thing.

## Jewish burial and cremation

Some families ask about cremation. We understand this is a sensitive subject, and we will always speak with you gently and without judgment.

Judaism teaches that the body is sacred. It housed a holy soul for a lifetime, and it is returned to the earth whole, in a simple, natural burial. This has been the Jewish way since the time of Abraham. Jewish tradition does not permit cremation, and we believe a Jewish burial is a final gift of love to the soul.

If your loved one has asked for cremation, or you are unsure, please talk with the Rabbi. He will listen and help you understand the Jewish view.

## Common questions

### Can the Rabbi visit someone in hospice or the hospital?
Yes. The Rabbi visits people at home, in hospitals, care facilities and hospice. Call 925-384-7242 to arrange a visit.

### What is Vidui and when is it said?
Vidui is a short prayer said near the end of life, asking forgiveness and affirming faith in G-d. It ends with the Shema. The Rabbi can guide you on when and how to say it.

### Does Judaism allow cremation?
Jewish tradition requires burial and does not permit cremation, because the body is treated as sacred. The Rabbi is glad to talk this through with you gently.

### What should we do when our loved one passes away?
Call the Rabbi right away, before calling a funeral home if possible. See [When a Loved One Passes Away](/help/someone-passed-away).

### Can the Rabbi help with medical decisions?
The Rabbi can explain what Jewish law says and guide you on your specific situation. He works alongside, not instead of, your loved one's doctors.
