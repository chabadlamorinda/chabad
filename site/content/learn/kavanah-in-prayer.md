Kavanah (כוונה) means "intention," the focus and awareness you bring to prayer, and the best way to build it is to start small: give five minutes of real attention to one prayer, and gently return to the words each time your mind wanders. Almost everyone's mind drifts when they pray, so this is a skill you grow into, not a test you pass or fail.

## What kavanah means

Kavanah is knowing that you are standing before G-d, and understanding, at least in general, what you are saying. The Shulchan Aruch, as presented on Chabad.org, has a whole chapter on concentrating during prayer. It teaches that prayer is meant to be said with attention to the meaning and to the One we speak to.

That is a high goal, and the Chabad.org articles on this topic are honest about it: it takes time. Chabad.org answers a reader who cannot focus on the words of the prayers by encouraging patience and steady progress, not guilt.

## Five gentle ways to focus

1. **Start small.** Aim for about five minutes of genuine concentration at first, and lengthen it over time. Like physical exercise, spiritual exercise works best when it is built up slowly. Overdoing it can backfire.
2. **Choose one prayer.** If you cannot focus on the whole service, give full attention to one section, such as the first paragraph of the [Amidah](/learn/amidah), or the [Shema](/learn/shema), or [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani).
3. **Point along.** Follow the words in the prayer book with your finger. It keeps your eyes and mind on the page.
4. **Understand the words.** Prayer becomes much more meaningful when you know what it says. Read the English, learn the meaning of a few lines, or ask the Rabbi. Pronouncing each word carefully also helps you stay with it.
5. **Smile and begin again.** When you notice your mind has drifted, do not use a related thought to bring yourself back. Chabad.org suggests simply stopping and redirecting yourself back to the words. Smile instead of getting frustrated. A smile lifts your mood, and mood makes focus easier.

## When your mind wanders

It will. The moment you notice is the moment you have already returned. Treat it like a friend who got distracted, not a failure. Don't scold yourself. Take a breath, find your place, and continue.

Some people find that a quiet moment before praying helps: think about who you are about to speak to. Our sages teach that prayer is like standing before a king. Some people cover their eyes while saying the Shema's first verse, which is a Jewish custom for concentration. For more on quiet and presence, read [Jewish meditation](/learn/jewish-meditation).

## For busy parents and professionals

You do not need an hour. A few minutes can change the day:

- Say Modeh Ani when you wake up, before you reach for your phone.
- Say one blessing a day slowly, with its meaning in mind. See [blessings](/learn/blessings).
- Put a small prayer book where you will see it, in the car, at your desk, on the nightstand.
- Join a minyan once a week, where the shared rhythm carries you when your own focus is thin.
- Ask the Rabbi to teach you one prayer well.

Families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda often tell us that what helped was not a big resolution but one small, steady habit.

## Common questions

### What is kavanah in Judaism?
Kavanah means intention or focus in prayer. It is knowing before Whom you stand and paying attention to what you are saying.

### How do I concentrate when I pray?
Start with five minutes of real focus on one prayer, follow the words with your finger, and learn what they mean. Chabad.org also suggests smiling and refocusing instead of getting frustrated.

### Is it normal for my mind to wander during prayer?
Yes. It happens to nearly everyone. When you notice, simply return to the words, without scolding yourself.

### Do I need to understand Hebrew to pray with kavanah?
It helps to understand what you are saying, so read the English translation or learn the meaning of key prayers. You can also pray in a language you understand. Ask the Rabbi what is best for you.

### Why do some people cover their eyes for the Shema?
It is a custom that helps concentration on the first verse. Ask the Rabbi if you would like to try it.

### What if I cannot focus on the whole service?
Choose one section, such as the first paragraph of the Amidah, and give it your full attention. Chabad.org recommends this.
