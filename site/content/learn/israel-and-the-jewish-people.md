The Land of Israel is the land G-d promised to the Jewish people. For more than three thousand years, Jews everywhere have prayed toward Jerusalem, longed for Zion and felt a deep bond with the land, a connection that is spiritual, historical and personal.

## A promise from G-d

The story begins with Abraham. G-d told him, "Go from your land... to the land that I will show you" (Genesis 12:1), and promised, "To your descendants I will give this land." The promise was repeated to Isaac and Jacob, and Moses led the Jewish people out of Egypt toward it.

The Torah describes Israel as "a land that the L-rd your G-d looks after; the eyes of the L-rd your G-d are always upon it, from the beginning of the year to the end of the year" (Deuteronomy 11:12).

## A holy land

Jewish tradition teaches that the Land of Israel has a special holiness. Jerusalem is its heart, and the site of the Holy Temple is the holiest place on earth. Many mitzvot, such as tithing crops and letting the land rest every seventh year, apply only in the Land of Israel.

## Never forgotten

After the destruction of the Second Temple, most Jews were exiled. Yet for nearly two thousand years, they never stopped remembering:

- **We pray facing Jerusalem.** Synagogues around the world face toward the Temple Mount.
- **The Amidah,** said three times a day, asks G-d to rebuild Jerusalem and gather the exiles.
- **Every Passover Seder and Yom Kippur** ends with "Next year in Jerusalem."
- **At every Jewish wedding,** a glass is broken to remember Jerusalem, and the Sheva Brachot pray for joy to return to its streets.
- **Grace after Meals** includes a blessing for the land and for Jerusalem.
- **[The Three Weeks](/learn/three-weeks) and Tisha B'Av** are days of mourning for the Temple's destruction.

The Psalmist wrote, "If I forget you, O Jerusalem, let my right hand forget its skill" (Psalm 137:5). That promise has been kept for generations.

## One people, one family

The bond with Israel is also a bond between Jews. Jews in Lamorinda and Jews in Jerusalem are one family. When Jews in Israel face danger or sorrow, Jews everywhere feel it, and when they celebrate, we celebrate.

## Praying for Israel

Prayer is one of the most powerful ways to help. Many communities say chapters of [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) (Psalms) for the safety of the people of Israel and its soldiers. A well-known verse says, "Behold, the Guardian of Israel neither slumbers nor sleeps" (Psalm 121:4).

Ways to strengthen the bond:

1. **Say Tehillim** for the safety of the people in Israel. You can find every chapter on our [Tehillim page](/tehillim).
2. **Give tzedakah** to help people in need in Israel.
3. **Do an extra mitzvah** in their merit. The Rebbe often encouraged this as a source of protection.
4. **Visit** if you can, and pray at the Western Wall.
5. **Learn** about the land in the Torah and Jewish history.

## Common questions

### Why is Jerusalem so important in Judaism?
Jerusalem is where the Holy Temple stood, the place G-d chose for His Presence to rest. It is mentioned hundreds of times in the Bible and in every daily prayer.

### What is the Western Wall?
It is a remaining outer wall of the Temple Mount from the time of the Second Temple. It is the closest place to the site of the Temple where Jews pray today, and people from all over the world come there to pray.

### Is there a special prayer for Israel?
Many communities say a prayer for the people and soldiers of Israel on Shabbat, and Tehillim is said in every generation. You can always pray in your own words too.

### Do I need to visit Israel to feel connected?
No. Prayer, learning and mitzvot connect every Jew to the land, wherever they live. A visit, when possible, can deepen that bond.
