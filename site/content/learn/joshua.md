Joshua (יהושע, Yehoshua) was the devoted student and attendant of Moses who became the leader of the Jewish people after Moses died. He led them across the Jordan River into the Land of Israel, and he is the second link in the chain that passes the Torah from one generation to the next.

## Who was Joshua?

Joshua, from the tribe of Ephraim, was born in Egypt. He was first called Hoshea, and Moses added a letter to his name, so it became Yehoshua, which Chabad.org explains as "may G-d save you." For years he served Moses and, as the Torah describes, "did not leave the tent." He learned Torah directly from his teacher. See [Moses](/learn/moses).

## One of the two faithful spies

Moses sent twelve leaders to scout the Land of Israel. Ten returned frightened and said it could not be conquered. Only Joshua and Caleb said, trust in G-d. Because the people believed the ten, that generation did not enter the land, except Joshua and Caleb. Joshua teaches that faith means standing up for what is right even when everyone else is afraid.

## Leading the people

Before his passing, Moses appointed Joshua as his successor. After the mourning for Moses, G-d told Joshua to prepare to cross the Jordan, and the river split, as the sea had for Moses. G-d told him to be strong and courageous and to keep the Torah.

- **Jericho.** Joshua sent two spies, who were hidden by Rahab, a woman who recognized G-d. For six days the people circled the walls, and on the seventh, with the sound of the shofar, the walls fell. Rahab and her family were saved. Chabad.org teaches that she later joined the Jewish people.
- **Battles and dividing the land.** The Book of Joshua describes the years of battle and the division of the land among the tribes. See [the twelve tribes](/learn/twelve-tribes).
- **Renewing the covenant.** Near the end of his life, Joshua gathered the people at Shechem and urged them to stay devoted to G-d and the Torah, and they promised. He passed away at 110.

## The chain of Torah

[Pirkei Avot](/learn/pirkei-avot) begins: "Moses received the Torah from Sinai and gave it over to Joshua." Joshua passed it to the elders, and from them to the prophets. See [what is a prophet](/learn/what-is-a-prophet). The Talmud compares Moses to the sun and Joshua to the moon: Moses brought light from above; Joshua reflected it into the physical world. Chabad teaching sees in this the work of every Jew, to bring Torah's light into daily life.

## Lessons for parents and teens

- Greatness comes through loyal learning and service. Joshua became a leader by first being a student.
- Stand up when others are afraid.
- Pass it on. Every parent and teacher is a link in the chain.

## Common questions

### Who was Joshua in the Bible?
Joshua was Moses' student and successor, who led the Jewish people into the Land of Israel after Moses passed away.

### What does Joshua bin Nun mean?
It means Joshua son of Nun. Nun was his father's name. See the Rabbi for the traditional explanations of the name.

### What happened at Jericho?
The people circled the city for seven days, the shofar sounded, and the walls fell, as G-d had told Joshua.

### Was Joshua a prophet?
Yes. His book is among the books of the Prophets in the [Tanach](/learn/tanach).

### Why was Joshua chosen to lead?
Because he was Moses' devoted student who never left his side.
