To bring Shabbat home on time when you commute, look up this week's candle-lighting time, work backwards from it, and leave the office early enough that traffic or a BART delay cannot catch you. Candles are lit about 18 minutes before sunset, and lighting after sunset is too late, so a Friday plan made on Thursday is what keeps Shabbat calm.

Many families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda work in San Francisco, Oakland or the Peninsula. BART's Yellow Line runs between Antioch and SFO and stops at both Lafayette and Orinda, and Highway 24 is the other main road home. Both are lovely on a Tuesday and unpredictable on a winter Friday. Here is a simple way to handle it.

## Start with the time that matters

Check [this week's Shabbat times](/shabbat-times) on Monday, not Friday. Candle lighting changes every week, and in the winter Shabbat starts very early in the afternoon. The site works the time out for every town, so you never have to guess; see [how to read Shabbat times](/learn/shabbat-times-in-lamorinda-explained).

Chabad.org teaches that candles are lit 18 minutes before sunset as a margin for error, and that sunset is the absolute deadline: after it, Shabbat has begun and lighting is forbidden. Your goal is not to arrive at the last minute. Your goal is to be home with time to breathe.

## Work backwards, with a cushion

Try this order:

1. **Pick your candle-lighting time** for your town.
2. **Subtract the cooking, showering and table-setting you still need to do.** Be honest; most people need more time than they think.
3. **Subtract your travel time on a bad day**, not a good one. Friday afternoon traffic and train delays are real.
4. **Add a cushion** of at least half an hour.
5. **That is the time you walk out of the office.**

In the long days of summer this barely matters. In winter it can mean leaving in the early afternoon, which is why talking to your manager ahead of time makes such a difference. See [Shabbat in winter](/learn/shabbat-in-winter) and [Shabbat for working parents](/learn/shabbat-for-working-parents) for ideas.

## Talk to your boss early

Most workplaces are more flexible than people fear when the request is calm, clear and made in advance. Many people agree to start earlier on other days, make up the hours on Sunday, or work from home on winter Fridays. You do not have to explain Jewish law at length. "I leave early on Fridays in the winter for a religious observance, and I will make the time up" is usually enough. If you are unsure how to raise it, call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242.

## Do the cooking before you leave

Shabbat food is much easier when most of it is done by Thursday night.

- Set up a slow cooker or put a pot on the stove Thursday evening, so Friday is only reheating.
- Set the table the night before.
- Make sure your timers are set and your hot plate is ready. See [preparing for Shabbat](/learn/preparing-for-shabbat).
- Put your phone's charger where it belongs, and tell your family when you will be home.

## If you are stuck and the clock is running out

Even good plans fail sometimes: a stalled train, an accident on Highway 24. Chabad.org gives clear guidance:

- You may light until shortly before sunset, even if the 18 minutes have passed, but not after sunset.
- Another adult in the household can light for the family. If someone already lit on your behalf, there is nothing to make up.
- If you really cannot make it, do not rush in a way that puts anyone in danger. Safety comes first.
- If you miss the lighting because you were careless, Chabad.org suggests adding one candle in the weeks that follow as a reminder. If it was truly beyond your control, nothing is added.

Your situation may be different, so call the Rabbi and ask. Any Friday afternoon is a good time to have that number saved in your phone.

## Common questions

### How early do I need to leave work on Friday to be home for Shabbat?

You need to be home with time to light candles and prepare, which depends on this week's candle-lighting time in your town. Work backwards from it and add a cushion for traffic and BART delays; see [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times).

### Can I light Shabbat candles at the office if I will not make it home?

This is a case to ask the Rabbi about, because it depends on where you will spend Shabbat. Call 925-384-7242 before Friday and he will walk you through it.

### What if I get stuck on BART or Highway 24 and it is almost sunset?

You may light until shortly before sunset, but never after, and someone else at home can light for the family. If you are stuck, stay safe and call the Rabbi.

### How late may I light Shabbat candles?

Candles are lit about 18 minutes before sunset, and Chabad.org teaches that you may still light up until just before sunset if you are running late. After sunset, it is too late.

### How do I ask my employer for Friday afternoons off in the winter?

Ask early, calmly and with a plan to make up the time, and say that it is for religious observance. If you want help with the wording, the Rabbi can talk it through with you.
