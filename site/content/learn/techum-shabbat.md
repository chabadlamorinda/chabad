On Shabbat you may walk about 2,000 amot, roughly just under 3/5 of a mile (about 1 km), from the edge of your city, and not farther. This boundary is called *techum Shabbat* (תְּחוּם שַׁבָּת), the "Shabbat limit," and it is a different law from the eruv that allows carrying.

Chabad.org gives the basic rules, and they matter for anyone in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda who takes a long Shabbat walk or visits friends in another town. The details are technical, so for a specific route, ask the Rabbi.

## What is the Shabbat limit?

The Torah and the Sages limit how far a person may travel on foot on Shabbat. According to Chabad.org, the distance is 2,000 amot, an *amah* (אַמָּה, "cubit") being about 18 to 19 inches. In everyday terms, that is just under 3/5 of a mile. Chabad.org notes that the 2,000-amah limit is a Rabbinic law according to one authority, while Maimonides holds that the Torah itself restricts travel to a longer distance. In either case, we keep the limit of 2,000 amot.

This is separate from the rule on carrying. The [eruv](/learn/eruv) allows you to carry within a marked area. The Shabbat limit says how far you may go. A walk could be fine in one way, and not in the other.

## Where do you start measuring?

The measurement depends on where you are when Shabbat begins.

- **In a city:** the 2,000 amot are measured from the edge of the city, squared off using its farthest houses, even if there is no wall around it.
- **In a house:** it starts from the outer edges of the house.
- **Out in the open, outside a city:** you start from a small square around where you stand, and measure from its edges in every direction.

Chabad.org notes that the limit follows the path you actually walk and the shape of the land, not a straight line on a map.

## How is the edge of a city decided?

The edge of a city is decided by continuous housing, not by the municipal boundary on a map. Chabad.org explains that any house within roughly 70 amot (about 110 feet) of the last one extends the city, so towns that run into each other count as one. A gap bigger than that likely marks the edge.

This is important in Lamorinda, where homes run along hills and open spaces. Whether a certain trail is inside your boundary depends on where the last houses are, so ask the Rabbi before relying on a measurement. This is not something to work out on a phone.

## What if you cross the line?

If someone leaves the boundary by accident, Chabad.org says that they should stop as soon as they realize, and in some cases they may return. If someone crosses on purpose, they must stay within a small space where they are. Saving a life overrides the limit. These situations are rare but serious; call the Rabbi.

## The eruv techumin: extending the limit

*Eruv techumin* (עֵרוּב תְּחוּמִין) lets you extend your walking limit in one direction. Before Shabbat, you place food for two meals at the 2,000-amot mark. Then, Chabad.org explains, you may walk another 2,000 amot from that spot, up to 4,000 in one direction, but you lose the ability to walk 2,000 amot in the opposite direction.

This is not a casual thing to do, and it needs the right wording and a specific place. If you think you might need one, for example for a long hike or a distant family simcha, speak to the Rabbi well before Shabbat.

## Common questions

### How far can you walk on Shabbat?

About 2,000 amot, just under 3/5 of a mile, from the edge of your city, according to Chabad.org.

### What is techum Shabbat?

*Techum Shabbat* is the Shabbat boundary, the limit on how far you may walk outside your city on Shabbat.

### How do you measure the edge of a city for techum Shabbat?

By continuous housing, not by the official city limits. Houses within about 70 amot of each other make one city. Because it is complicated, ask the Rabbi.

### What is an eruv techumin?

It is a way of extending the walking limit. Food for two meals is placed at the 2,000-amot mark before Shabbat, which lets you walk up to 4,000 amot in one direction.

### Is techum Shabbat the same as the eruv?

No. The eruv allows carrying in a marked area. Techum Shabbat limits how far you may walk. See [what is an eruv](/learn/eruv).

### Can I walk to another town on Shabbat?

It depends on how far the edge of the next town is from yours. If the houses are continuous, the towns count as one. For a specific trip, ask the Rabbi at 925-384-7242.
