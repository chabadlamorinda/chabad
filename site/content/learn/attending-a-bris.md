At a bris, the guests gather in the morning for a short ceremony in which a baby boy enters the covenant of Abraham and receives his Hebrew name, and then everyone shares a festive meal. You do not need to know Hebrew or what to do: dress modestly, arrive on time, say "Mazal tov!" and enjoy.

If you live in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda, you may be invited to a bris at a home, at Chabad or in a hall. Here is what to expect, step by step. The ceremony itself is explained in our [brit milah guide](/learn/brit-milah); this guide is for the guest.

## Before you go

- **Timing.** A bris is on the baby's eighth day and is usually held in the morning. If the baby was unwell, the date can change, so check with the family. See [when a bris is postponed](/learn/postponing-a-bris).
- **The invitation.** There is often no formal invitation. A call, email or message to friends counts as the invitation.
- **What to wear.** Dress as you would for a religious service, a little less formal than for a wedding. Women generally wear a modest top and skirt or a modest dress. Men wear a kippah (head covering); extra ones are usually available.
- **A gift is optional.** A small outfit, a toy, or something for the baby's older siblings is appropriate.

## What happens at the ceremony

1. **The baby is brought in.** By custom, a couple called the kvater and kvaterin carry him from his mother to the room. Guests greet him with "Baruch haba!" (בָּרוּךְ הַבָּא, "Blessed is the one who comes").
2. **The baby is placed on the chair of Elijah** and then held by the sandek, an honored person, often a grandfather. See [sandek and kvater](/learn/sandek-and-kvater) and [Elijah the Prophet](/learn/elijah-the-prophet).
3. **The mohel performs the circumcision.** The whole thing takes only a few minutes. If the baby cries, he is soothed with a few drops of wine and goes back to his mother.
4. **The blessing.** The father or another honoree recites a blessing, and the guests answer with a Hebrew wish that, as the baby entered the covenant, so may he enter Torah, marriage and good deeds.
5. **The naming.** The baby is given his Hebrew name, and a cup of wine is drunk. See [baby naming](/learn/baby-naming).
6. **Mazal tov!** Guests congratulate the family, and often pass the baby's name along to those who could not come.

## At the festive meal

After the ceremony comes the seudat mitzvah (סְעוּדַת מִצְוָה), a meal that is itself part of the mitzvah. It is usually a brunch, with bagels, salads and fish. Guests wash hands and say the blessing over bread, and Grace After Meals has a few special additions for the occasion. Some families seat men and women separately; simply follow the people around you. If you can, offer to help clear up.

## What to say

You do not need a speech. "Mazal tov!" (מַזָּל טוֹב) or "Congratulations" is perfect. Compliments on the baby or the name are always welcome. If you would like to give a traditional blessing, wish the parents to raise him to Torah, chuppah and good deeds. For more ideas, see [what to say to new Jewish parents](/learn/congratulating-new-parents).

## Common questions

### What should I wear to a bris?
Dress modestly, as for a religious service. Men wear a kippah, and women usually wear a modest dress or skirt. It is less formal than a wedding.

### Do I have to bring a gift to a bris?
No. Gifts are optional. A small baby outfit, a toy or something for the older siblings is a thoughtful choice.

### Do I need to know Hebrew to attend a bris?
No. Most of the ceremony is in Hebrew, but simply being present is the mitzvah. You may answer "Amen" to the blessings along with everyone else.

### How long does a bris last?
The ceremony itself takes only a few minutes, and the festive meal follows. Plan on a morning out. If you need to leave early, eat something before you go, since joining the meal is part of the mitzvah.

### Is the baby okay during a bris?
The mohel is trained in both the Jewish laws and the medical procedure, and parents are given clear care instructions. For questions about your own baby's care, ask the mohel and your pediatrician.

### What is said to the baby's parents at a bris?
"Mazal tov!" is the usual greeting. Many people add a wish that the baby grow to Torah, marriage and good deeds.
