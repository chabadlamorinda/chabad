The chazzan (חזן), also called the shliach tzibbur (שליח ציבור, "the community's messenger"), leads the congregation in prayer, saying key parts aloud on its behalf. Many people lead for the first time during the year of mourning for a parent or on a yahrzeit, and the Rabbi and community are glad to help.

## What the leader does

According to Chabad.org, the chazzan represents the congregation. When there is a minyan of ten, he says the parts of the service that are reserved for the leader:

- **Barchu** (ברכו), the call to prayer before the Shema.
- **Kaddish** (קדיש), said between sections of the service.
- **The repetition of the Amidah** (עמידה), the central prayer, in the morning and afternoon (not at night), including Kedushah. He pauses between blessings so the congregation can answer "Amen."

In the Ashkenazi custom, the leader reads the opening and closing lines of each paragraph aloud while the congregation prays the rest quietly. Learn about [the Amidah](/learn/amidah) and [minyan](/learn/minyan).

## Who may lead

Chabad.org explains that the leader should be a Jewish man past bar mitzvah age, obligated to pray. The ideal is a person of good character, humble, with a pleasant voice and fluent in the prayers. If no one meets every ideal, the community chooses someone wise and caring. Today, mourners and those observing a yahrzeit often lead the weekday prayers, while communities seek their most qualified leader on the High Holidays. Questions about who may lead in a specific place are for the Rabbi.

## The easiest parts to start with

Many first-timers start with **Mincha**, the short afternoon service, and with **Ashrei** (אשרי), which opens it. Read [Mincha and Maariv](/learn/mincha-and-maariv) and [Ashrei](/learn/ashrei). Ask the Rabbi which parts to begin with in your first time. It is wise to start with a short weekday service, and a mourner who also says Kaddish can learn it in advance. See [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish) and the [yahrzeit guide](/learn/yahrzeit).

## How to practice

1. **Ask the Rabbi to go through it with you** before the day, and note exactly where you stand and when you pause.
2. **Practice the order.** Read the text aloud in the siddur, with a transliteration if needed. See [the siddur](/learn/siddur).
3. **Use familiar melodies.** Chabad.org advises using tunes the congregation knows and not stretching the prayers, which burdens the congregation.
4. **Pace yourself.** Speak clearly and not too fast.
5. **Dress with respect,** as if standing before a king, and ask the Rabbi about tallit customs, which vary.
6. **Pause at the right moments.** Ask the Rabbi to mark them in your siddur.

## Nerves, kavanah and community

Feeling nervous is natural. Chabad teaches that prayer comes from the heart, and what matters is your sincerity, not a perfect voice. Chabad.org notes that someone who is asked by the rabbi should simply accept. The community supports you, and if you stumble, someone beside you can help. Many people say that leading for a parent gives a feeling of closeness that they did not expect. See [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish) and, for other roles in the service, [Who Does What in Shul](/learn/synagogue-roles).

## Common questions

### How do I lead davening for the first time?
Ask the Rabbi to go through the service with you, start with a short weekday Mincha, and practice reading it aloud. The community will help.

### Who can lead the prayers in a synagogue?
In traditional Jewish law, a man past bar mitzvah age who is obligated to pray. The best leader is humble, respected and fluent. Ask the Rabbi about any case.

### Can a mourner lead the prayers?
Yes. Mourners and those observing a yahrzeit often lead weekday services. Call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242 for guidance.

### What is a shliach tzibbur?
The shliach tzibbur, "the community's messenger," is the prayer leader, also called the chazzan.

### What parts of the service does the leader say aloud?
Barchu, Kaddish and the repetition of the Amidah, when there is a minyan. See [Minyan](/learn/minyan).

### Do I need a good voice to lead?
No. A pleasant voice is an ideal, but the most important thing is sincerity and familiar, clear singing.
