Many people feel a pull to connect more: after a loss, a birth, a trip to Israel, or simply a feeling that something is missing. Judaism is not all or nothing. Every mitzvah counts on its own, and every step forward is precious.

## Start with one thing

The Lubavitcher Rebbe encouraged people to start with one mitzvah and add from there. Choose what speaks to you:

- **Light [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles)** on Friday before sunset.
- **Put up a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah)** on your front door.
- **Put on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin)** in the morning, even for a few minutes.
- **Say the [Shema](/learn/shema)** when you go to sleep and wake up.
- **Give [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah)** every weekday; keep a [pushka](/learn/pushka) in the kitchen.
- **Learn a little Torah** every day; see [daily Torah study](/learn/daily-torah-study).
- **Have one Shabbat dinner** with family or friends.

See also the Rebbe's [ten mitzvah campaigns](/learn/ten-mitzvah-campaigns).

## Grow at your own pace

- **Add slowly and keep it.** A step you keep is better than a big leap you can't sustain.
- **Learn the "why".** Understanding makes a mitzvah meaningful; see [why mitzvot](/learn/why-mitzvot).
- **Find a teacher and a community.** Questions are welcome; nobody is expected to know everything.
- **Don't compare.** Everyone is on their own journey.

## Bringing your family along

- **Lead with joy, not pressure.** Make Shabbat the best meal of the week.
- **Talk with your spouse** and grow together; agree on the next step.
- **Kids love traditions:** candle lighting, challah, a bedtime Shema.

## Common questions

### Do I have to keep everything to call myself Jewish?
No. Every Jew is fully Jewish. Each mitzvah is a connection to G-d in its own right.

### Isn't it hypocritical to keep some mitzvahs and not others?
No. Doing one mitzvah is good, full stop. You are not a hypocrite; you are growing.

### I don't read Hebrew. Can I still pray?
Yes. You can pray in English or any language you understand. You can also learn to read Hebrew at any age; see [Hebrew reading for adults](/learn/hebrew-reading-for-adults).
