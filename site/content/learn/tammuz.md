Tammuz (תַּמּוּז) is the fourth month of the Jewish year counting from Nissan, falling in the summer, around June and July, and it has 29 days. It is a month of both light and sadness: days of Chassidic celebration and remembrance in the first half, and the fast of the 17th of Tammuz, which begins the Three Weeks of mourning.

## Where Tammuz fits in the year

The Jewish calendar follows the moon, so each month begins with the new moon and the months move through the seasons with the year. Tammuz follows Sivan, the month of Shavuot, and comes before Av. See the [Jewish calendar](/learn/jewish-calendar) for how the months work. The site's [this week](/this-week) page always shows the current Hebrew date, so you never have to count.

Tammuz has no Torah holiday of its own. Its special days are Chassidic days of gratitude and one fast day that recalls the destruction of Jerusalem.

## Gimmel Tammuz: the third of Tammuz

The 3rd of Tammuz is the yahrzeit of the Lubavitcher Rebbe, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson, who passed away in 5754 (1994). It is a day to learn his teachings, give tzedakah and add a mitzvah. Read the full story in [Gimmel Tammuz](/learn/gimmel-tammuz) and about [the Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe).

## 12-13 Tammuz: the Previous Rebbe's liberation

The 12th and 13th of Tammuz mark the birthday of Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn, the sixth Chabad Rebbe, born in 1880, and the day of his release in 1927 from a death sentence and imprisonment in Soviet Russia, where he had been arrested for strengthening Jewish life. Chabad counts it as one of three "Festivals of Liberation," together with 10 Kislev and 19 Kislev. On these days Chabad communities omit the Tachanun prayer, and gather for joyful farbrengens (Chassidic gatherings of stories, songs and Torah).

## The 17th of Tammuz: the Three Weeks begin

The 17th of Tammuz is a public fast day. On this day the Romans broke through the walls of Jerusalem, three weeks before the Temple was destroyed on the ninth of Av. It begins the Three Weeks. Special prayers (Selichos) are said, and customs of mourning start: for example, the Shehecheyanu blessing is not said on a new fruit during this time. See the [Three Weeks](/learn/three-weeks) and [minor fast days](/learn/minor-fast-days), including how to fast safely: if you have a health condition, ask the Rabbi.

## Making hard days into growth

Jewish teaching sees even sad days as seeds of growth. The Rebbe encouraged turning the Three Weeks into a time of extra Torah study, tzedakah and kindness, which strengthen the very things that were weakened by the destruction. A person who feels something is missing in life can start here: choose one small, steady act, a Shabbat candle, a Torah thought, a call to a friend, and add to it each day.

## Common questions

### What is the Jewish month of Tammuz?
Tammuz is the fourth month counting from Nissan, usually falling in June and July, with 29 days. It follows Sivan and comes before Av.

### What happens in the month of Tammuz?
Tammuz includes 3 Tammuz (the Lubavitcher Rebbe's yahrzeit), 12-13 Tammuz (the Previous Rebbe's liberation) and the fast of the 17th of Tammuz, which begins the Three Weeks.

### What is the 17th of Tammuz?
It is a public fast day that begins the Three Weeks. It recalls the day the Romans broke through Jerusalem's walls.

### What is Gimmel Tammuz?
Gimmel Tammuz is the 3rd of Tammuz, the yahrzeit of the Lubavitcher Rebbe. People mark it by learning, tzedakah and good deeds.

### What is 12-13 Tammuz?
It marks the birthday of the Previous Lubavitcher Rebbe and his release from Soviet imprisonment in 1927. Chabad celebrates it as a Festival of Liberation.

### Do I have to fast on the 17th of Tammuz?
Healthy adults traditionally fast, from dawn to nightfall. If you are ill, pregnant or elderly, ask the Rabbi.
