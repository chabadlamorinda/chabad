Vegan food is not automatically kosher. A vegan or vegetarian label does not tell you whether the equipment was kosher, the wine or grape juice was certified, or the ingredients were checked, so a kosher-certified restaurant or product is the reliable standard.

## Why "vegan" does not mean "kosher"

Many Bay Area diners assume plant-based food is automatically fine for Jewish dietary laws. Chabad.org explains why it is not:

- **Ingredients.** Vegan standards apply "as far as is possible and practical," and animal-derived traces can appear in unexpected places. Modern foods also contain additives and oils that need kosher supervision.
- **Equipment.** Pots, ovens and countertops must be kosher. If equipment was ever used for non-kosher food, it has to be koshered first.
- **Wine and grape juice.** These must be certified kosher, and many dishes, even in places with no wine list, contain them. See [kosher wine](/learn/kosher-wine).
- **Some cooked foods.** Certain foods must be cooked or baked with a Jew's involvement.
- **Insects in produce.** Greens and some fruits must be checked. See [checking for insects](/learn/checking-for-insects).

## Is "dairy-free" the same as pareve?

No. Pareve (neutral) means a food has no meat or dairy and is certified kosher. A "dairy-free" label may still show a dairy-equipment warning. Learn more in [pareve](/learn/pareve).

## A vegetarian kitchen is closer than you think

A reader on Chabad.org asked whether a home that never cooked meat is already kosher. The rabbi's answer: it still needs koshering, but the adjustments are minimal. The hardest parts of kashrut, meat sources and separate meat and dairy dishes, would not apply. What remains is having a rabbi kosher the kitchen, buying certified products, checking greens, using kosher wine, and immersing new serving utensils. The rabbi estimated about 90 percent of foods are already supervised.

If you are interested, ask the Rabbi, since every kitchen is a little different. See also [the kosher kitchen](/learn/kosher-kitchen).

## Eating out as a vegetarian

A vegan restaurant would find kosher certification relatively easy to obtain, but until it has it, a person who keeps kosher should avoid it. For options and etiquette see [eating out kosher](/learn/eating-out-kosher).

## Common questions

### Is vegan food kosher?
Not automatically. It needs kosher-certified ingredients, kosher equipment and kosher wine or grape juice where relevant.

### Are vegan restaurants kosher?
Only if they have kosher certification. The vegan label does not address kosher requirements.

### Is a vegetarian kitchen kosher?
It still needs koshering, but the changes are small because there is no meat and dairy to separate.

### What does pareve mean?
Pareve means neutral, with no meat or dairy, and certified kosher.

### Do vegetables need to be checked?
Yes. Leafy greens and some fruits need checking for insects.
