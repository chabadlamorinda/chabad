A bar mitzvah is the moment a Jewish boy turns 13 and becomes responsible for keeping the mitzvot (commandments) of the Torah. It happens automatically on his 13th Hebrew birthday; the ceremony and party celebrate this milestone, but he becomes a bar mitzvah whether or not there is an event.

## What does bar mitzvah mean?

Bar mitzvah means "son of the commandment." Until 13, a child is educated in Jewish practice by his parents. From 13, he is considered a Jewish adult in religious terms: he counts toward a minyan (prayer quorum of ten), can be called to the Torah, and is personally responsible for his choices.

Chassidic teaching explains that at 13 a person's "G-dly soul" becomes fully expressed, giving him the inner strength to choose good.

## When is the bar mitzvah?

The date is the boy's 13th birthday on the Hebrew calendar, which usually differs from his English birthday. Use our [bar and bat mitzvah date calculator](/tools/bar-bat-mitzvah) to find it. Since a Jewish day starts at sunset, a boy born in the evening counts as being born on the next Hebrew date.

## The key mitzvah: tefillin

The central practice a bar mitzvah boy takes on is putting on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) each weekday morning. The custom is to begin practicing about a month or two before the bar mitzvah, so he is comfortable by his 13th birthday. Getting his own pair of kosher tefillin is a highlight of the preparation.

## How to prepare

Preparation is about more than learning a Torah portion. A good program includes:

1. **Learning to put on tefillin** and say the Shema.
2. **Understanding mitzvot** that are now his responsibility.
3. **Reading from the Torah or haftarah,** if he chooses, or learning the blessings for an aliyah.
4. **Preparing a short talk** (a d'var Torah) on a Torah idea.
5. **A mitzvah project** to give back, such as tzedakah or helping others.

## The celebration

Common elements include:

- **Aliyah:** the boy is called to the [Torah](/learn/torah) for the first time, often on a Monday, Thursday or Shabbat near his birthday.
- **Blessing by the father:** the father traditionally says a short blessing thanking G-d for this milestone.
- **Speech:** the boy shares his d'var Torah.
- **Seudat mitzvah:** a festive meal with family and friends.

Celebrations can be big or simple. What matters most is the meaning: a young man connecting to his heritage.

## Common questions

### Can you have a bar mitzvah as an adult?

Yes. Many men who never had a bar mitzvah celebrate one later, at 30, 50 or 80. Technically every Jewish man became bar mitzvah at 13, but marking it with an aliyah and tefillin is deeply meaningful at any age.

### Does my son need to read from the Torah?

No. Reading from the Torah is a beautiful option, but the essential parts are taking on mitzvot, especially tefillin, and being called up for an aliyah. Each boy can prepare at his own pace.

### When should we start preparing?

Many families begin about a year in advance. This gives time to learn without pressure and to buy tefillin, which can take time to order. Reach out early and we will help plan.

### Can the bar mitzvah be held on a different day than the birthday?

The boy becomes bar mitzvah on his Hebrew birthday regardless. The celebration and aliyah can be held on a nearby day that works for the family. Ask the Rabbi about scheduling.
