The shofar is a ram's horn blown on Rosh Hashanah, and hearing its sound is the main mitzvah of the day. Its piercing, simple cry is a call to wake up, return to G-d and crown Him as King of the world for the new year.

## What is a shofar?

A shofar is a natural animal horn, usually from a ram, hollowed out and shaped so it can be blown like a trumpet. A cow's horn may not be used. Many shofars are short and curved, while others, often from a kudu or Yemenite ram, are long and spiraled. All are kosher if made properly from a permitted animal.

The Torah calls Rosh Hashanah "a day of sounding" (Numbers 29:1). The mitzvah is to hear the shofar, not to blow it yourself, so everyone who listens fulfills the commandment.

## Why we blow the shofar

The Sages and later teachers gave many reasons. Among them:

- **Crowning the King.** Trumpets are sounded at a coronation. On Rosh Hashanah we accept G-d as our King anew.
- **A wake-up call.** Maimonides writes that the shofar says: "Sleepers, wake from your slumber," look at your deeds and return to G-d.
- **The binding of Isaac.** The ram's horn recalls the ram Abraham offered in place of Isaac, and his complete devotion.
- **A wordless cry.** Chassidic teaching describes the shofar as the cry of the soul, deeper than words can express.

## The sounds of the shofar

There are three basic sounds:

1. **Tekiah:** one long, straight blast.
2. **Shevarim:** three medium, broken sounds, like sighing.
3. **Teruah:** a series of short, rapid blasts, like sobbing.

These are combined in set patterns. The full order includes 100 sounds on each day of Rosh Hashanah, spread through the morning prayers.

## When the shofar is blown

The shofar is blown on both days of Rosh Hashanah during the daytime. When the first day falls on Shabbat, the shofar is not blown that day, and it is heard only on the second day.

Many communities also blow the shofar every weekday morning during the month of [Elul](/learn/elul) to prepare for the High Holidays, and a single long blast is sounded at the end of Yom Kippur.

## The blessings

Before the blowing, the person blowing the shofar says two blessings on behalf of everyone listening, who answer "Amen."

**Hebrew:** בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו, וְצִוָּנוּ לִשְׁמוֹעַ קוֹל שׁוֹפָר

**Transliteration:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu lishmo'a kol shofar.

**Translation:** Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us to hear the sound of the shofar.

The second blessing is Shehecheyanu, thanking G-d for bringing us to this time. On the second day, the custom varies, so follow your synagogue.

## How to hear the shofar properly

- Listen to every sound from beginning to end, and do not talk until the blowing is finished.
- Have in mind that you are fulfilling the mitzvah, and the blower has you in mind too.
- Hear the actual shofar, not a recording, livestream or phone call.

## Common questions

### Can I hear the shofar if I cannot get to synagogue?
Yes. Many communities arrange short shofar blowings at homes, parks and hospitals so that everyone can hear it. If you are homebound, contact us and we will try to help.

### Do women and children need to hear the shofar?
Men are obligated. Women are not strictly obligated, since it is tied to a set time, but women have accepted it as a near-universal practice. Children are brought to hear it as part of their education.

### Can I blow the shofar for practice on Rosh Hashanah?
Outside of the mitzvah itself, blowing for practice on the holiday is not permitted. Practice in Elul instead.

### Why is the shofar not blown on Shabbat?
The Sages were concerned that someone might carry a shofar in a public area to learn how to blow it, which is forbidden on Shabbat. So when Rosh Hashanah falls on Shabbat, the shofar waits until the second day.
