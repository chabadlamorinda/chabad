Tu B'Av, the 15th day of the Hebrew month of Av, is a minor Jewish holiday of joy, love and new beginnings. The Mishnah says there were no days as festive for the Jewish people as the 15th of Av and Yom Kippur.

## What happened on Tu B'Av

The Mishnah describes how, in the time of the Temple, the young women of Jerusalem would go out to the vineyards on this day, all dressed in borrowed white clothing so that no one would be embarrassed by having less. There they would dance, and young men would come to find a match. That is why Tu B'Av is often called the Jewish day of love.

The Talmud lists several happy events that took place on this date:

- **Tribes could marry one another.** Restrictions on marrying between tribes, which applied in the desert generation, were lifted.
- **The tribe of Benjamin was welcomed back.** After a tragic civil war, the other tribes allowed marriage with Benjamin again.
- **The dying in the desert ended.** The generation condemned to die in the wilderness after the sin of the spies stopped dying on this date.
- **Pilgrims could go to Jerusalem.** Guards that had blocked the northern tribes from going up to the Temple were removed.
- **The dead of Betar were buried.** After the fall of Betar in the Bar Kochba revolt, the Romans finally allowed the dead to be buried, and their bodies had miraculously not decayed.
- **Wood for the altar was complete.** It was the last day each year for cutting wood for the Temple altar, and it was celebrated as a festival.

## Why it matters after Tisha B'Av

Tu B'Av comes six days after Tisha B'Av, the saddest day of the year. The Jewish calendar moves from the deepest mourning to great joy. The message: after every descent comes a greater ascent, and the redemption follows the destruction.

## How to mark Tu B'Av

There are no special prayers or meals, but there are meaningful customs:

1. **No Tachanun.** The penitential prayer is skipped, as on other joyful days.
2. **Add Torah study.** From the 15th of Av the nights grow longer, and the Talmud teaches that we should use the extra night hours for more Torah learning. The Rebbe encouraged everyone to add to their learning from this day on. See our guide to [daily Torah study](/learn/daily-torah-study).
3. **Pray for a good match.** Many single people see Tu B'Av as a special time to pray for their soulmate, and many friends help with introductions. See our guide to [Jewish dating](/learn/jewish-dating).
4. **Celebrate love in marriage.** Couples can strengthen their bond with a kind word or time together. Read about [shalom bayit](/learn/shalom-bayit).
5. **Bless others.** From Tu B'Av, many begin wishing each other a good and sweet new year, since Rosh Hashanah is only weeks away.

## Common questions

### When is Tu B'Av?
It is on the 15th of Av, usually in late July or August. Use our [Hebrew date tool](/tools/hebrew-date) to find it.

### Is Tu B'Av a Jewish Valentine's Day?
In Israel it is often called that, and many weddings take place around it. But its roots are deeper: it celebrates unity, healing and the joy of the Jewish people.

### Are weddings held on Tu B'Av?
Yes. It is a popular and auspicious date for weddings, especially since weddings are not held during the Three Weeks before it.

### Why did everyone wear borrowed white clothes?
So that rich and poor would look the same and no one would feel ashamed. It is a lesson in sensitivity that still applies today.
