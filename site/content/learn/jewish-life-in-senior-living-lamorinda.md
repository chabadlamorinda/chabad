You can keep Jewish life alive for a parent in senior living by bringing small, familiar pieces of Shabbat and the holidays into their room: a blessing over candles, a Kiddush cup, challah, a mezuzah on the door, and a visit from someone who sings the old melodies. For families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, the Rabbi and Rebbetzin are happy to hear from you, and you do not need to belong to anything first.

## Why this matters so much

Jewish tradition places great weight on honoring parents and respecting the elderly (see [honoring the elderly](/learn/honoring-the-elderly)). Chabad.org suggests that adult children honor their parents not only through physical care but through their spiritual lives, including teaching caregivers about the holidays and customs a parent keeps, so that aides can help. That is what this guide is about.

The move to senior living can feel like losing a home, a routine, and sometimes a community. Familiar Jewish sights, tastes and songs can bring comfort, belonging and dignity, even when memory is fading. You are not trying to make your parent more observant than they were; you are bringing them back to what they know.

## Bringing Shabbat in

Shabbat is the easiest place to start, because it comes every week.

1. **Candles.** Many care communities do not allow open flames. Ask the staff what they allow, and ask the Rabbi what is best in your parent's situation. This is a question of Jewish law, and the answer depends on the case. Learn more in [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).
2. **Kiddush and challah.** A small cup of wine or grape juice and a piece of challah are enough to make Friday night feel like Shabbat. See [Kiddush](/learn/kiddush) and [challah](/learn/challah). Check with the staff about any dietary or medical limits first.
3. **Songs.** Familiar melodies reach people when words do not. Sing a few verses together.
4. **A visit.** Even twenty minutes in person is a mitzvah, and one that fills the room.

## Holidays and kosher food

Holidays are wonderful reasons to visit: apples and honey, a shofar, a menorah, matzah at Passover, a taste of the sukkah. Ask the facility in advance about their policies, and call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 to hear what is planned and whether a visit for your parent is possible. See the [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays) calendar, which the site computes for you.

If your parent keeps kosher, talk to the facility about what is possible, since each place is different. Even a simple step, such as sealed kosher meals or separate disposable dishes, may help. For specific questions on what is kosher in a facility, ask the Rabbi.

## A mezuzah on the door

A mezuzah on the doorpost of your parent's room tells everyone, staff and visitors, that a Jewish person lives here. Chabad.org lists helping an elderly Jewish neighbor obtain mezuzahs among the most meaningful acts. Check with the facility first, and see [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) and [mezuzah questions](/learn/mezuzah-questions).

## When memory fades

If your parent has dementia, remember that old prayers, blessings and songs often last longest. A familiar Shema, a blessing over bread or a lullaby can be a gift. Speak slowly, bring photographs, and do not correct. See [when a parent has dementia](/learn/when-a-parent-has-dementia). Teens and grandchildren can help: a short visit with a drawing, a song or a picture of the family brightens a day. See [caring for aging parents](/learn/caring-for-aging-parents).

## Caring for yourself

Caregivers often forget themselves. Rest, ask for help, and let others bring a meal. The Torah's commandment to guard your own health and the mitzvah of honoring parents go together. See [caregiver support](/learn/caregiver-support). If you want to talk privately with the Rabbi or Rebbetzin, use [talk to the Rabbi](/help/talk-to-the-rabbi). This guide is general information and not medical or legal advice.

## Common questions

### How can I bring Shabbat to my parent in assisted living?
Bring a small Kiddush cup, challah and a candle option the facility allows, and sing familiar songs. Ask the Rabbi about the best way to light in your parent's situation.

### Can my parent have a mezuzah in a care home?
Usually yes, if the facility agrees. Ask the staff first, and call 925-384-7242 for guidance on choosing and placing a mezuzah.

### Can the Rabbi visit my parent near Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda?
Call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 to ask. Please tell us your parent's name and the facility's visiting rules.

### What if my parent can no longer remember Jewish customs?
Songs, blessings and tastes often remain when other memories fade. Keep them simple and gentle, and do not expect too much.

### How do I bring Passover or the holidays to a nursing home?
Ask the facility what it allows, bring small ceremonial items, and call 925-384-7242 to hear what is planned. Do not assume the food is kosher; ask the Rabbi.

### How do I help my parent keep kosher in a facility?
Speak with the staff about the options they offer. The Rabbi can advise on what is best in your parent's case.
