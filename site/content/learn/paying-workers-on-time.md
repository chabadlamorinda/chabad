The Torah commands employers to pay a worker's wages on time, generally on the day the work is finished: a day worker should be paid before sunrise the next morning, and a night worker by the following nightfall. This mitzvah applies to anyone who hires help, including a babysitter, gardener, house cleaner or contractor.

Many families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda hire help every week, so you may be keeping, or breaking, this mitzvah without knowing it.

## What the Torah says

The sources are Leviticus 19:13 ("The wages of a hired worker shall not remain with you until morning") and Deuteronomy 24:15 ("On the same day you shall give him his wages"). The Hebrew name is bal talin (בל תלין), "do not leave overnight."

According to Chabad.org, the timing depends on the worker's schedule:

- A **day worker** who finishes at the end of the day must be paid before the next sunrise.
- A **night worker** who finishes at daybreak has until the following nightfall.
- If work ends before the day is over, payment is due by nightfall. If it ends in the middle of the night, by morning.

## Why it matters

Workers often depend on their wages for food and basic needs, and delay can cause real hardship. The mitzvah also trains employers in compassion. Chassidic teaching adds that wages represent the energy the worker invested, and so paying promptly respects that. The Torah ties timely payment to a long and good life.

## Who is covered

All workers are included, whatever their gender, age, wealth, religion or background. A babysitter, Jewish or not, deserves to be paid on time. The rule also applies to renting objects or animals.

- **Salaried workers** must be paid on their regular payday.
- **Contract workers**, such as a painter or a gardener hired for a job, must be paid when the job is finished.
- If a worker knowingly agrees to later payment, the employer does not have to pay that day. A clear agreement in advance avoids problems.

## Babysitters: a common case

Chabad.org's guide on hiring babysitters notes several points for families:

1. **Pay when the job ends.** Sitters are often teenagers who may be too embarrassed to ask. If you cannot pay on time, tell them in advance.
2. **Do not cancel casually.** If you cancel at the last minute and the sitter turned down other work, you may owe compensation. Local custom matters, so ask the Rabbi.
3. **Shabbat.** Payment for work done only on Shabbat is not permitted. Ask the Rabbi how to arrange Shabbat care.

Other details, such as the kosher kitchen with a sitter present, are covered in the guide: ask the Rabbi about your own household.

## If you cannot pay right now

The Chabad.org article explains that if an employer genuinely lacks the money, he is exempt from the deadline until he can pay, and borrowing to pay is praiseworthy. If funds are limited, poorer workers come first. Even after the deadline passes, withholding wages you have is still forbidden. For specific employer-employee questions, such as contracts, benefits or business situations, ask the Rabbi and your own professional advisers.

## Small steps to keep the mitzvah

- Keep cash or a payment app ready for babysitters.
- Set a clear payday with employees and stick to it.
- Pay contractors at the agreed time and say thank you.
- If you are a business owner, build payroll into your calendar. See [business ethics](/learn/business-ethics).

## Common questions

### What is the Torah rule on paying workers?
The Torah requires paying a worker on time, no later than the end of the day (or night) the work was done. The sources are Leviticus 19:13 and Deuteronomy 24:15.

### What is bal talin?
Bal talin (בל תלין) means "do not leave overnight" and refers to the prohibition against delaying a worker's pay. It is the Torah's rule about wages.

### Does this apply to a babysitter or house cleaner?
Yes. All hired help is included, whether Jewish or not. A babysitter should be paid when the job ends, or at a payday agreed in advance.

### What if I forgot to pay on time?
Pay as soon as you can. Once the deadline has passed, you are still not allowed to hold back wages you owe. Ask the Rabbi if you are unsure how to put it right.

### Does the rule apply to Shabbat work?
Payment for work performed only on Shabbat is not permitted, so Shabbat arrangements need care. Ask the Rabbi.
