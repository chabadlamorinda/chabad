Kabbalah is the mystical tradition of Judaism, the inner dimension of the Torah. It explores the nature of G-d, how He creates the world, and the deep purpose of the soul and the mitzvot.

## What does Kabbalah mean?

Kabbalah means "receiving." It refers to wisdom received and passed down from teacher to student, going back to Mount Sinai. Kabbalah is not a separate religion or a set of magic practices. It is part of the Torah itself, like the soul is part of the body.

## Key ideas in Kabbalah

**Ein Sof: the Infinite.** G-d in His essence is beyond all description or limit. Kabbalah calls Him Ein Sof, "without end."

**The Sefirot.** G-d expresses Himself through ten divine attributes called sefirot, such as kindness (chesed), strength (gevurah) and harmony (tiferet). The human soul has these same qualities, reflecting the idea that we are created "in G-d's image."

**Tzimtzum.** To make room for a finite world, the Infinite "contracts" His light. This idea, taught by the Arizal, explains how a limited world can exist within G-d's unlimited presence.

**Sparks of holiness.** Everything in the world contains sparks of G-dliness. When we use the physical world for a good purpose, by eating kosher food with a blessing or giving charity, we elevate those sparks.

**Tikkun.** Every mitzvah helps repair and complete the world, preparing it for the era of [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach).

## Key texts and teachers

- **The Zohar:** the central text of Kabbalah, taught by Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai in the second century and revealed more widely in 13th-century Spain.
- **Sefer Yetzirah:** an ancient text about creation through the Hebrew letters.
- **The Arizal:** Rabbi Isaac Luria (1534 to 1572) of Safed, whose teachings shaped Kabbalah as it is studied today.

## Kabbalah and Chassidus

For centuries, Kabbalah was studied by a small number of scholars. In the 18th century, the Baal Shem Tov began the Chassidic movement, which brought these deep ideas to everyone. [Chassidus](/learn/chassidus), and especially Chabad Chassidus in the [Tanya](/learn/tanya), translates Kabbalah into language the mind can grasp and the heart can feel, so it becomes a guide for daily life.

## Who may study Kabbalah?

Traditionally, Kabbalah was reserved for mature, learned individuals who were firmly grounded in Torah and mitzvot. Chassidus opened the door wider: its teachings about the soul and G-d's closeness are meant for everyone. Authentic study always goes together with living Jewishly.

## Common questions

### Is the red string bracelet part of Kabbalah?

Some people wear a red string as a folk custom, but it is not a core teaching of Kabbalah. Authentic Kabbalah is about understanding G-d and refining ourselves through Torah and mitzvot.

### Do you have to be 40 to study Kabbalah?

That is a well-known traditional guideline for the deepest parts of Kabbalah. Chassidic teachings, which present Kabbalah in an accessible way, are studied by people of all ages.

### Can non-Jews study Kabbalah?

Some general ideas about G-d and the soul are relevant to everyone. Kabbalah's detailed teachings are closely tied to Torah and mitzvot, which are the foundation of Jewish life.

### Where should a beginner start?

Start with Chassidus, such as the Tanya or accessible books about the soul. A teacher can help you build a solid foundation.
