A first day at a new job, the keys to a new office, a business you built from an idea: these are moments of hope and a little nervousness. Judaism has a way of beginning things well. Here is a simple plan for starting your new work with blessing.

## Begin with a prayer

Before the first day, take a moment to speak to G-d in your own words: thank Him for the opportunity, and ask for success, good colleagues and the wisdom to do the work well. Many people give a coin to tzedakah and say a chapter of Tehillim (Psalm 23 or Psalm 121 are favorites) on the morning they begin. See [talking to G-d in your own words](/learn/talking-to-g-d-in-your-own-words) and [Tehillim](/tehillim).

## Effort and trust: the Jewish balance

Judaism asks us to work hard and, at the same time, to know that the results are in G-d's hands. The Torah says G-d will bless you "in all that you do": we have to do, and He sends the blessing through our doing. Chassidic teaching calls work the "vessel" and the blessing the "light." Make the vessel wide: show up, learn, do honest work. Then leave the worry to G-d. That trust, *bitachon*, is what lets a person sleep at night when a deal is uncertain. See [trust in G-d](/learn/bitachon) and [parnassah: the Jewish view of work](/learn/parnassah-work-and-livelihood).

## Give from the first earnings

A beautiful custom is to give tzedakah from your very first paycheck or first sale, before you spend any of it. It says: this came from G-d, and I am a partner, not only an owner. From then on, many set aside a tenth of their income (*maaser*) for tzedakah. Giving does not shrink a livelihood; our sages promise that it opens the channels for more. See [maaser](/learn/maaser) and [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).

## Honesty is the first question

The Talmud teaches that the first question a person is asked in the World to Come is "Did you deal honestly in business?" Honest weights, honest hours, honest descriptions of what you sell, paying workers and suppliers on time, keeping your word: these are mitzvot as real as kosher and Shabbat. A business run this way is a daily *kiddush Hashem*, a sanctification of G-d's name. See [business ethics](/learn/business-ethics) and [keeping your word](/learn/keeping-your-word).

## Shabbat and holidays at work

Decide now, before you are busy, how you will keep Shabbat and the holidays. A short, friendly conversation early on, with the dates written down, almost always works; offer to cover for colleagues on their days. If you run your own business, closing on Shabbat is a weekly statement of what matters most. Practical help: [Jewish holidays at school and work](/learn/jewish-holidays-at-school-and-work) and [Shabbat for working parents](/learn/shabbat-for-working-parents). Hard cases: ask the Rabbi.

## A mezuzah for your business

A Jewish-owned store, office or studio should have a mezuzah on its doors, just like a home. It is a sign that G-d's presence and protection are welcome in the place where you work, and customers and colleagues often ask about it with warmth. Rabbi Labkowski is happy to come and affix it with you, say the blessing, and wish the business success. See [the mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) and [choosing a mezuzah](/learn/choosing-a-mezuzah).

## A few more customs for a good start

- Keep a *pushka* (charity box) on your desk or by the register, and drop in a coin each day. See [the pushka](/learn/pushka).
- Thank G-d for the first sale, the first good review, the first paycheck. Gratitude keeps success from going to our heads. See [hakarat hatov](/learn/hakarat-hatov).
- Set aside a few minutes a day for Torah, even five. See [daily Torah study](/learn/daily-torah-study).

## Common questions

### Is there a special blessing for a new job?
There is no fixed blessing for starting a job. People say a personal prayer, give tzedakah, and say Tehillim. When you buy a significant new item for the business, or on the first day of a venture you waited for, some say *Shehecheyanu*; ask the Rabbi. See [Shehecheyanu](/learn/shehecheyanu).

### How do I ask for Jewish holidays off at a new job?
Early and kindly. Give your manager the list of dates for the year (the website's [holiday calendar](/learn/jewish-holidays-at-school-and-work) helps), explain which days you cannot work at all, and offer to make up time. Most employers appreciate the notice.
