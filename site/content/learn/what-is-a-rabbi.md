A rabbi is a teacher of Torah and a guide in Jewish life. The word comes from the Hebrew *rav*, meaning "great" or "master," and *rabbi* literally means "my teacher."

## What does a rabbi do?

A rabbi is not a go-between who stands between a person and G-d. In Judaism, every Jew can pray to G-d directly. Instead, a rabbi is a teacher, a guide and a friend who helps people live Jewish lives. A rabbi typically:

- **Teaches Torah** to adults and children, in classes, sermons and one-on-one learning.
- **Answers questions of Jewish law** (halacha), from kosher food and Shabbat to business ethics.
- **Leads prayer services** and Torah readings, though any knowledgeable Jew may lead services.
- **Guides families through life events**: a brit milah, a [bar mitzvah](/learn/bar-mitzvah), a [wedding](/learn/jewish-wedding), a funeral and mourning.
- **Offers counsel and comfort** in hard times, in sickness and in moments of doubt.
- **Builds community**, bringing people together for Shabbat, holidays and celebrations.

## How does someone become a rabbi?

A rabbi receives *semicha* (ordination) after years of study. The core of that study is Jewish law: the [Talmud](/learn/talmud), its commentaries and the codes of law, especially the laws people meet every day, such as kosher food, Shabbat and mourning. At the end, a senior rabbi tests the student and certifies that he is able to answer questions of Jewish law.

Many rabbis study in a yeshiva for a decade or more before ordination. Semicha is not a priesthood and not a holy status passed down in families. It is a certification of knowledge and a trust to teach.

## Rabbi, Rebbe and priest: what is the difference?

- **Rabbi:** a Torah teacher and authority in Jewish law, often serving a community.
- **Rebbe:** a Chassidic leader and spiritual guide, a rabbi of rabbis. When Chabad speaks of "the Rebbe," it means the Lubavitcher Rebbe, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson. Read more about [the Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe).
- **Kohen (priest):** a descendant of Aaron, the brother of Moses. The Kohanim served in the Holy Temple. Today a Kohen still gives the Priestly Blessing and is called first to the Torah, but this is by birth, not training. A rabbi may or may not be a Kohen.

## What is a Chabad rabbi?

A Chabad rabbi is usually a *shliach*, an emissary of the Rebbe, who moves with his family to a community to serve every Jew there. Together with his wife, the Rebbetzin, he runs a Chabad center offering Shabbat meals, holidays, classes, children's programs and personal help. A Chabad rabbi serves everyone, whatever their background, with no membership required. Learn more about [Chabad](/learn/chabad).

## Common questions

### How do I address a rabbi?
Simply say "Rabbi" or "Rabbi" with his last name, for example "Rabbi Labkowski." In writing, "Rabbi" or the abbreviation "R." is used. His wife is often called "Rebbetzin."

### Can I talk to a rabbi if I am not religious?
Absolutely. A rabbi is there for every Jew, wherever you are on your journey. You do not need to keep any mitzvot, belong to a synagogue or know any Hebrew to ask a question or share what is on your mind.

### Do rabbis get married?
Yes. Judaism values marriage and family, and rabbis marry and raise families like everyone else. In Chabad, the rabbi and the Rebbetzin usually lead the community together as a couple.

### Is what I tell a rabbi kept private?
A rabbi treats personal conversations with care and discretion. If privacy is important to you, say so at the start, and ask the Rabbi about anything specific you are concerned about.
