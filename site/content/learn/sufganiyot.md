Sufganiyot (סופגניות) are Chanukah jelly doughnuts: yeast dough, fried in oil and filled with jam. We eat them because they are fried in oil, which recalls the miracle of the Temple oil that burned for eight days.

## What are sufganiyot, and why do we eat them?

A *sufganiyah* is a round, fried doughnut, usually filled with jelly and dusted with powdered sugar. Chabad.org explains that since Chanukah recalls the miracle of the oil, eating oily foods is customary, and jelly doughnuts and potato pancakes ([latkes](/learn/latkes)) are the popular favorites. Read more in [Chanukah foods](/learn/chanukah-foods).

## A sufganiyot recipe

This is retold from a Chabad.org recipe for about 30 small doughnuts. As written it uses milk and butter, so it is dairy; see the pareve note below.

**Ingredients:**

- 4 1/2 teaspoons dry yeast and 1/4 cup warm water
- 1 1/2 cups milk
- 3/4 cup sugar (plus 1 teaspoon for the yeast)
- 1 teaspoon kosher salt
- 2 eggs
- 6 tablespoons butter, melted
- 6 or more cups flour
- Oil for frying
- 1/2 cup jam, and 1/2 cup confectioners' sugar for dusting

**Steps:**

1. **Wake the yeast.** Mix the yeast, warm water and 1 teaspoon sugar in a small bowl and let it sit 10 to 15 minutes until bubbly.
2. **Make the dough.** Mix the yeast with the rest of the sugar, butter, eggs, salt, milk and 2 cups of the flour. Add the remaining flour gradually until the dough is no longer sticky, then knead about 5 minutes.
3. **Let it rise.** Cover with a damp cloth and leave about an hour, until doubled.
4. **Shape.** Divide the dough into 3 or 4 pieces, roll each about 1/2 inch thick and cut circles with a cookie cutter or the rim of a glass. Let them rise about 30 minutes.
5. **Fry.** Heat oil over low-medium heat. Oil that is too hot browns the outside before the middle cooks. Fry a few at a time, turning so both sides brown, and drain on paper towels.
6. **Fill.** Let them cool completely. Poke a small hole in the side, put the jam in a plastic bag with a corner snipped, and squeeze a little inside.
7. **Dust** with confectioners' sugar through a fine strainer.

Before eating, say the blessing over the food.

## Making them pareve

Chabad.org offers soy or almond milk in place of milk. The recipe also calls for butter or margarine; if you want them pareve, use a certified pareve margarine and check labels. See our [pareve](/learn/pareve) guide, and ask the Rabbi if you are unsure.

## Frying and filling tips

- **Keep the oil on low to medium heat,** so the doughnuts cook through.
- **Let them cool fully before filling,** so the jam stays put.
- **An adult should always do the frying,** since hot oil is dangerous. Children can cut circles, fill and dust.
- **Gather everything first,** wash your hands and keep the workspace clear.

## Kosher notes

Use kosher-certified ingredients, including the jam and confectioners' sugar (see [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols)), and keep meat and dairy separate. Chabad.org's recipe is made with milk, so serve it as a dairy treat unless you switch to pareve ingredients.

## Common questions

### What are sufganiyot?
Sufganiyot are fried yeast doughnuts, usually filled with jelly and dusted with sugar, eaten on Chanukah.

### Why do we eat doughnuts on Chanukah?
They are fried in oil, recalling the miracle of the Temple oil that burned for eight days. Chabad.org lists jelly doughnuts among the customary oily foods.

### Are sufganiyot dairy or pareve?
The Chabad.org recipe uses milk and butter, so it is dairy. You can use soy or almond milk and a certified pareve margarine to make them pareve; check labels.

### How do I keep doughnuts from burning outside and raw inside?
Fry over low to medium heat. If the oil is too hot, the outside burns before the center cooks.
