The four parshiyot (ארבע פרשיות) are four special Torah readings added on four Shabbats in late winter and early spring, before Passover: Shekalim, Zachor, Parah and HaChodesh. Each one adds a short extra passage to the weekly reading, and together they take us step by step from the month of Adar to the Passover Seder.

## Why are there extra readings?

After the winter, the Jewish calendar moves toward spring and the holiday of Passover. The sages set four special readings to prepare us. The first two lead into Purim, the last two into Passover. Because the Jewish calendar adds a second month of Adar in a leap year, the exact Shabbats shift from year to year, so check [this week's page](/this-week) and [the Jewish holiday calendar](/jewish-holidays). Learn about the month in [Adar](/learn/adar) and about the weekly reading in [the Torah reading](/learn/torah-reading).

## 1. Shabbat Shekalim: the half-shekel

On the Shabbat on or before the start of Adar, a second scroll is read with the passage from Exodus 30:11-16, about the first half-shekel donation. The haftarah (II Kings 11:17-12:17) tells how King Jehoash set aside communal funds for repairs to the First Temple.

In Temple times every Jew gave a half-shekel (*machatzit hashekel*) each year toward the Temple, due on the first of Nissan. The courts began reminding everyone on the first of Adar, a month ahead. Most of the money bought the communal offerings. Chabad.org notes that the Talmud teaches that Haman's decree was averted in the merit of this mitzvah, and that today people give a commemorative half-shekel to charity on the Fast of Esther. See [the Fast of Esther](/learn/fast-of-esther).

The lesson: every Jew counts equally and every Jew is needed. Nobody gives more or less than half; we are only whole together.

## 2. Shabbat Zachor: remember Amalek

On the Shabbat before Purim we read Deuteronomy 25:17-19, the command to remember what Amalek did and to blot out its memory. The haftarah (I Samuel 15:2-34) is about G-d's command to King Saul regarding Amalek. Chabad.org notes that many authorities hold that every man has a Biblical obligation to hear this reading, so many come to the synagogue for it. The full story is in [Parshat Zachor](/learn/parshat-zachor), and it leads into [Purim](/learn/purim-for-beginners).

The lesson: coldness toward what is holy is the enemy within. We remember so that we can choose warmth.

## 3. Shabbat Parah: the red heifer

On the next Shabbat we read Numbers 19, the laws of the red heifer, which purified a person who had contact with the dead. The haftarah (Ezekiel 36:16-36) is G-d's promise to purify the Jewish people when He brings us back to the Land of Israel in the Messianic Era.

We cannot perform those rituals today. Chabad.org explains that studying their laws is a spiritual preparation for Passover: in Temple times everyone had to be pure to bring the Passover offering, so we prepare our hearts too.

## 4. Shabbat HaChodesh: the first mitzvah

On the Shabbat on or before the first of Nissan we read Exodus 12:1-20. It describes what G-d told Moses in Egypt about two weeks before the Exodus: this month is the first of the months, and the Jewish people must bring the Passover offering, eat matzah and bitter herbs and tell the story to their children. It is the first mitzvah the Jewish people were given as a nation: to sanctify time itself. The haftarah (Ezekiel 45:18-46:15) is a prophecy about the Passover offering in the Third Holy Temple.

The lesson: time is a gift. Each month, and each day, is a chance to begin again.

## Common questions

### What are the four parshiyot?
They are four special Torah readings before Passover: Shekalim, Zachor, Parah and HaChodesh. Each adds a short passage to the weekly reading.

### What is Parshat Shekalim about?
It is about the half-shekel every Jew gave each year to the Temple (Exodus 30:11-16). It is read on the Shabbat on or before the start of Adar.

### What is Parshat Zachor?
It is the reading before Purim (Deuteronomy 25:17-19) that commands us to remember Amalek. Many authorities say every man should hear it.

### What is Parshat Parah?
It is Numbers 19, the laws of the red heifer, read as a spiritual preparation for Passover. Its haftarah is from Ezekiel 36.

### What is Parshat HaChodesh?
It is Exodus 12:1-20, the first mitzvah to the Jewish people, about the new month of Nissan and the Passover offering.
