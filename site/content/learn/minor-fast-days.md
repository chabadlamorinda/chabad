The minor fast days are four days in the Jewish year when we refrain from eating and drinking from dawn until nightfall: the Fast of Gedaliah, the Tenth of Tevet, the Fast of Esther and the Seventeenth of Tammuz. They recall tragic events, or in the case of Esther, a day of prayer, and they call us to reflect and return to G-d.

## The four minor fasts

1. **Tzom Gedaliah (3 Tishrei).** The day after Rosh Hashanah. It recalls the murder of Gedaliah ben Achikam, the Jewish governor after the destruction of the First Temple. His death ended the last Jewish self-rule of that time and led to exile.
2. **Asarah B'Tevet (10 Tevet).** Recalls the start of the Babylonian siege of Jerusalem, which led to the destruction of the First Temple. It is also observed in many communities as a day of Kaddish for victims of the Holocaust whose date of death is unknown.
3. **Ta'anit Esther (13 Adar).** The day before Purim. It recalls the fast of Queen Esther and the Jewish people before she approached the king, and the fact that Jews fasted and prayed before going to battle.
4. **Shivah Asar B'Tammuz (17 Tammuz).** The walls of Jerusalem were breached before the destruction of the Second Temple. It begins the [Three Weeks](/learn/three-weeks) of mourning that lead to Tisha B'Av.

For the major fasts, Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av, see our [fast days guide](/learn/fast-days).

## How the minor fasts work

- **Time:** from dawn (alot hashachar) until nightfall (tzeit hakochavim). Use our [zmanim tool](/tools/zmanim) for exact times.
- **What is restricted:** only eating and drinking. Washing, leather shoes and work are permitted.
- **Eating before dawn:** you may eat before dawn if you had this in mind before going to sleep. Many say so explicitly the night before.
- **Shabbat:** fasts are not held on Shabbat. If the date falls on Shabbat, the fast is moved to Sunday, except Ta'anit Esther, which moves to the Thursday before. The Tenth of Tevet can fall on a Friday and is kept then.

## Who does not fast

Children under bar or bat mitzvah age do not fast. Pregnant and nursing women, and people who are ill, are generally exempt from the minor fasts. A person who feels weak or unwell should not push themselves. Since every situation is different, ask the Rabbi.

Those who do not fast should still eat simply and not indulge.

## Special prayers

1. **Selichot,** prayers for forgiveness, are added in the morning service. See our [Selichot guide](/learn/selichot).
2. **Avinu Malkeinu** is said in the morning and afternoon.
3. **Aneinu** ("Answer us") is added to the Amidah by those fasting.
4. **The Torah is read** at Shacharit and Mincha, from the passage of Moses praying for forgiveness after the golden calf. A haftarah is read at Mincha.

These public readings need a minyan with enough people fasting.

## The purpose of fasting

The Rambam teaches that the fasts exist to awaken our hearts to [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah). Fasting alone is not the goal. The day is meant for reflection, prayer, kindness and tzedakah. Many give the money they would have spent on food to charity.

## Common questions

### Can I brush my teeth on a minor fast?
Many authorities allow it with care not to swallow, and some are stricter. Ask the Rabbi.

### Can I take medicine on a fast day?
People who need medication should take it. Ask the Rabbi about how, and about whether you should fast at all.

### What is the Fast of the Firstborn?
On the day before Passover, firstborn males fast in gratitude for being spared in Egypt. Most fulfill it by attending a siyum, a celebration of finishing a section of Torah, which ends the fast.

### Do the minor fasts start the night before?
No. Unlike Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av, they begin at dawn. You may eat dinner as usual the night before.
