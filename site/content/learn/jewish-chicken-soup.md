Jewish chicken soup is a golden broth made by simmering chicken with onions, carrots, celery and other vegetables for several hours, and it is the classic first course of a Shabbat meal. Families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda make it on Thursday or Friday, so it is ready when Shabbat begins.

## Why chicken soup belongs on Shabbat

Shabbat meals are meant to be special, and soup is the dish that makes a kitchen smell like Shabbat. Many Jewish families have served it on Friday night for generations, and it is also the food people bring to someone who is ill, which is why it has the nickname "Jewish penicillin." Bringing a pot of soup to a sick friend is a lovely way to do the mitzvah of [visiting the sick](/learn/bikur-cholim). For more about the foods of the Shabbat table, see [Shabbat meals](/learn/shabbat-meals) and [Jewish foods](/learn/jewish-foods).

## A simple chicken soup, adapted from Chabad.org

This method is retold in our own words from a recipe on Chabad.org. It serves 8 to 10 people.

**Ingredients**
- 2 chicken bottoms (drumstick and thigh together)
- 2 carrots
- 2 onions
- 3 stalks of celery
- 1 sweet potato
- 1 zucchini
- 6 cloves of garlic
- About 1 tablespoon of kosher salt, to taste
- 12 cups of water

Use kosher ingredients, and ask the Rabbi about specifics. Kosher chicken is covered in our guide to [kosher meat](/learn/kosher-meat).

**Steps**
1. Peel the carrots, onions and sweet potato. Leave the zucchini peel on.
2. Cut the vegetables into good-sized chunks, not tiny ones.
3. Put everything in a large pot (the chicken can go in straight from the freezer) and bring it to a rolling boil.
4. Skim the foam that rises to the top and throw it away.
5. Turn the heat down very low and let the soup barely bubble for 4 to 6 hours. The longer it cooks, the richer it tastes.
6. Cool the pot and refrigerate it overnight. The fat will set in a layer on top.
7. Lift off the fat, bring the soup back to a boil, then keep it warm until you serve.
8. Serve it plain, with matzah balls, or with the chicken and vegetables from the pot.

**Tip:** for a very clear broth, pour the soup through cheesecloth.

## Matzah balls and kreplach

Two traditional additions turn soup into a full first course. Matzah balls are light dumplings of matzah meal that cook in simmering water or broth, and kreplach are small dumplings filled with chicken or beef. Chabad.org has recipes for both, including a traditional chicken kreplach in golden chicken soup. Ask a friend or call us if you would like to cook them together.

## Cooking ahead, freezing and keeping it warm

Soup is the perfect make-ahead dish, since it tastes better after a night in the refrigerator. Cool it fully, pour it into containers and freeze it for up to a few months. Since cooking is not done on Shabbat, soup is usually fully cooked before candle lighting and then kept hot. See [keeping food warm on Shabbat](/learn/keeping-food-warm-on-shabbat) for how it is done, and ask the Rabbi about your own stove or hot plate.

## Common questions

### What is Jewish chicken soup?
Jewish chicken soup is a clear golden broth made from chicken and vegetables simmered slowly for hours. It is traditionally served on Friday night and on holidays.

### Why do Jews eat chicken soup on Shabbat?
It is a beloved family tradition that makes the Shabbat meal feel warm and festive. There are many explanations, and there is no single rule, so ask the Rabbi if you want to hear more.

### Why is it called Jewish penicillin?
Families have long served the soup to anyone feeling unwell, and the nickname grew out of that comfort. Bringing soup to a sick person is a kindness that fits the mitzvah of [bikur cholim](/learn/bikur-cholim).

### Can I make chicken soup ahead and freeze it?
Yes. Cool it completely, remove the hardened fat, and freeze it in containers. Thaw it in the refrigerator and reheat it before Shabbat.

### Do I need kosher chicken?
Yes. Use kosher chicken and kosher ingredients, and ask the Rabbi about any specifics at 925-384-7242.

### Is chicken soup meat or dairy?
It is a meat dish, so it is not served with dairy in the same meal. See [pareve](/learn/pareve) for foods that go with either.
