Yes, you can pray in English, or any language you understand. Chabad.org says the Amidah, the heart of Jewish prayer, may be recited in any language, and that Hebrew is preferred when possible. What matters most is that you understand what you are saying and mean it.

## What Jewish law says about the language of prayer

Chabad.org teaches that one should pray in Hebrew when possible, and otherwise in a language one understands. On the Amidah, all opinions agree that it can be said in any language. On the Shema, the Talmud records a dispute: one view says it must be said in the original Hebrew, the other allows any language. Because of that, many people say the Shema in Hebrew, even if they also read the English alongside it. If this matters in your own situation, please ask the Rabbi.

## Why Hebrew still matters

Hebrew is the language of the Torah, and the prayers were composed in Hebrew. Many Jews feel that saying the same words that Jews have said for generations connects them to the whole Jewish people. Chabad.org notes that because not everyone was fluent in Hebrew, the prayer leader repeats the Amidah aloud so the congregation can answer "Amen."

## Why understanding matters more

Chabad.org says that you can say every word in Hebrew, but if your heart is not in it, you have not truly prayed. It stresses concentration, or *kavanah* (כַּוָּנָה), as the most important part of prayer, and describes prayer as standing before the King: "Know before Whom you stand." A little said with attention is better than a lot said without understanding. Chabad.org also quotes the Alter Rebbe's caution: someone who does not understand the meaning of certain prayers, such as the Shema, the Amidah, Hallel and Grace after Meals, should not simply recite them in the Holy Tongue without understanding them. That is not a ban on English. It is a push to understand.

## A simple path: English, then Hebrew

1. **Start with a siddur that has English.** Read the translation slowly. See [The Siddur](/learn/siddur).
2. **Pick a few lines to learn well.** Chabad.org suggests beginning with a few lines you have learned to understand, then slowly adding more.
3. **Say the Shema.** Learn its first line in Hebrew with its meaning. See [Shema](/learn/shema).
4. **Learn the letters.** You do not have to be fluent to start. Try [Hebrew Reading for Adults](/learn/hebrew-reading-for-adults) and [Hebrew Lessons in Lamorinda](/learn/hebrew-lessons-in-lamorinda).
5. **Keep speaking from the heart.** English works for your own words and requests. See [Talking to G-d in Your Own Words](/learn/talking-to-g-d-in-your-own-words).

## At a service, you can follow in English

In synagogue, you can read the English next to the Hebrew, say "Amen," and let your own words fill the gaps. Learn how in [Finding Your Place in the Siddur](/learn/finding-your-place-in-the-siddur). See also [Kavanah in Prayer](/learn/kavanah-in-prayer).

## Common questions

### Can you pray in English in Judaism?
Yes. Chabad.org says a person should pray in Hebrew when possible, and otherwise in a language they understand. All agree the Amidah may be said in any language.

### Do I have to pray in Hebrew?
No, but it is preferred. Understanding is more important than the language. Ask the Rabbi about your own situation.

### Can I say the Shema in English?
There is a Talmudic dispute: one opinion says Hebrew only, another says any language. Many say it in Hebrew and read the English meaning. Ask the Rabbi what to do.

### Is prayer in English as meaningful as Hebrew?
Prayer is meaningful when it is understood and sincere. Chabad.org says that words without heart are not true prayer, in any language.
