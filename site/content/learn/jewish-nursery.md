A Jewish nursery usually has a kosher mezuzah on the door, a Shir Hamaalot (Psalm 121) card near the crib, a tzedakah box, and a few Jewish books and pictures. These simple touches surround a baby with holiness from the first days, following the Lubavitcher Rebbe's guidance that a baby's first impressions should be holy.

For new parents in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, this can be a gentle weekend project before the baby arrives. For older children, see [a Jewish child's bedroom](/learn/a-jewish-childs-bedroom); this guide is for the baby's first year.

## A kosher mezuzah on the door

Every room used by people needs a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah), and a nursery is no different. The scroll must be a kosher, handwritten parchment, and it is placed on the right side of the doorway as you enter, at the bottom of the top third of the doorway, tilted inward in Chabad custom. A beloved custom is for a child, when older, to kiss the mezuzah. If you are not sure the room qualifies, or you have a new home, ask the Rabbi. Our [mezuzah form](/forms/mezuzah-tefillin) can help.

## Shir Hamaalot near the crib

Chabad.org describes the custom of hanging a card with Psalm 121 near the newborn's crib, so that the baby's first sight is something holy and for protection. Because it carries Divine Names, it is covered in any room where the baby is changed, and there are different opinions about how, so ask the Rabbi. Read more in [Shir Hamaalot cards for a newborn](/learn/shir-hamaalot-for-newborns).

## A tzedakah box

A tzedakah box on the wall or shelf teaches giving as a part of daily life. Even a baby can see a parent put in a coin before Shabbat, and later hold the coin and drop it in. See [the tzedakah box](/learn/pushka) and [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah). Choose where the money goes together as the child grows.

## Holy words and pictures

- **The aleph-bet.** The Lubavitcher Rebbe taught that a baby's first sight should be something holy, such as the letters of the aleph-bet, so many families hang a colorful aleph-bet chart.
- **Pictures.** Pictures of Jerusalem, Shabbat or Jewish holidays are easy and lovely choices.
- **Books.** A children's book of Psalms, a children's siddur and picture books of Jewish stories can sit on a low shelf. See [Jewish parenting](/learn/jewish-parenting).
- **A letter in a Torah.** Many families buy a letter in a children's Torah scroll in honor of the baby. See [a letter in a Torah](/learn/letter-in-a-torah).

## Songs and early habits

Chabad.org teaches that children begin learning very early. As soon as a child starts to speak, he is taught key Torah verses, including the Shema, and the mother is often the most effective teacher. Even before words, a baby hears the songs we sing. Read about the [Shema before bed](/learn/bedtime-shema) and [Jewish lullabies](/learn/jewish-lullabies). The first blessings and prayers traditionally begin around age three.

## Common questions

### Does a nursery need a mezuzah?
Yes. A room used by people for living, including a baby's room, needs a kosher mezuzah. Ask the Rabbi about doors that are unusual.

### What should be in a Jewish baby's room?
A mezuzah on the door, a Shir Hamaalot card, a tzedakah box and a few holy books and pictures. All are customs, so do what you can.

### Where do I hang the Shir Hamaalot card in the nursery?
On or near the baby's crib. Cover it in any room where the baby is changed, and ask the Rabbi how, since opinions differ.

### Can I decorate the nursery in any style?
Yes. Any color and design is fine. Chabad.org suggests avoiding pictures of non-kosher animals or negative images, and adding a few meaningful, holy touches.

### When should we set up the nursery's Jewish items?
Ideally before the due date, so they are in place when the baby comes home. See [new parents in Lamorinda](/learn/new-parents-in-lamorinda).
