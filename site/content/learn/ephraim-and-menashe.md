Ephraim and Menashe (אפרים ומנשה) were the sons of Joseph, born in Egypt, and the grandsons of Jacob. Jewish parents bless their sons "May G-d make you like Ephraim and Menashe" because these were the first Jewish children raised in exile who stayed faithful to their heritage, and who got along as brothers.

## Who were Ephraim and Menashe?

According to Chabad.org, they were the sons of Joseph and his wife Asenath. Though they were born in Egypt, a spiritually difficult place, they were raised in the ways of their forefathers. Near the end of his life, Jacob made them each a full tribe, so there would be two tribes from Joseph. That is why the number of tribes stays twelve. See [the twelve tribes](/learn/twelve-tribes).

## Jacob's blessing

The story is in the portion Vayechi, at the end of [Genesis](/learn/book-of-genesis). Jacob was old and his eyes were weak. He placed his right hand on Ephraim, the younger, and his left on Menashe, the firstborn. Joseph tried to correct him, but Jacob said, "I know, my son, I know." Menashe would be great, but Ephraim's descendants would be greater. Joshua came from the tribe of Ephraim. Jacob asked that the angel who had redeemed him bless the boys, and that they carry the names of Abraham and Isaac. And he said: "By you shall Israel bless, saying: May G-d make you like Ephraim and Menashe."

## Why these two?

- **They stayed Jewish in exile.** Chabad.org notes they were the first Jewish children to grow up away from home, in a foreign culture, and they stayed steadfast. Parents in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda who raise children among many cultures can feel this blessing.
- **Their names carry two messages.** Menashe, "G-d made me forget," recalls Joseph's pain and distance from his father's house. Ephraim, "G-d made me fruitful in the land of my affliction," means finding holiness and growth even in a challenging place. Chabad teaching says Ephraim shows the deeper purpose, which is why Jacob placed his right hand on him.
- **No jealousy.** Unlike most brothers in Genesis, they did not compete. Many parents see this as a blessing for siblings to love each other.

## How the blessing is said

The words are: **Yesimcha Elokim ke'Efrayim vechi'Menasheh** (יְשִׂמְךָ אֱלֹקִים כְּאֶפְרַיִם וְכִמְנַשֶּׁה), "May G-d make you like Ephraim and Menashe." Parents place a hand on the child's head and then say the Priestly Blessing. Daughters are blessed to be like our Matriarchs. See the full words and steps in [Blessing your children](/learn/blessing-your-children).

Chabad.org notes that many families give the blessing every Friday night, and that this is not the Chabad custom for everyone. Ask the Rabbi what is best for your family.

## Common questions

### Who were Ephraim and Menashe?
They were the sons of Joseph and Asenath, born in Egypt, who became two of the twelve tribes of Israel.

### Why do parents say "May G-d make you like Ephraim and Menashe"?
Because Jacob said that Israel would bless its sons this way. They stayed faithful to their heritage in exile and were never jealous of each other.

### Why did Jacob put his right hand on the younger brother?
Jacob saw that Ephraim's descendants would be greater, and Chabad teaching adds that Ephraim's name stands for growing and succeeding even in hard places.

### Do we say it every Friday night?
Many families do. Chabad.org says it is not the Chabad custom in every home, so ask the Rabbi.

### Is there a blessing for daughters?
Yes: "May G-d make you like Sarah, Rebecca, Rachel and Leah." See [the Matriarchs](/learn/the-matriarchs).
