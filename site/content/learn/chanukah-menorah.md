On each of the eight nights of Chanukah, we light a menorah, adding one more light every night until all eight burn on the last night. The lights recall the miracle of the oil in the Holy Temple and publicize that light overcomes darkness.

## If you have never done this before

Welcome. Here is everything in one place. You need only six things, and about five minutes.

1. **A menorah** (or eight small cups or a row of candles in a straight line, all at the same height) and **one extra light**, the shamash (helper), set apart.
2. **Lights:** a box of Chanukah candles has 44, enough for all eight nights plus the shamash. Olive oil with wicks works too.
3. **Matches or a lighter**, and a safe tray or foil underneath.
4. **Timing:** after sunset or at nightfall (see tonight's time on [the menorah page](/menorah)), and on Friday before the Shabbat candles.
5. **A place:** a window facing the street or the doorway, away from curtains and anything that burns.
6. **The words:** below, in Hebrew, in English letters and in English. You can read them from this page. Nobody expects you to know them by heart.

How many lights each night: night 1 is one light plus the shamash, night 2 is two plus the shamash, and so on up to eight.

## What the menorah recalls

Over 2,100 years ago, the Greek-Syrian rulers of the Land of Israel tried to force Jews to give up the Torah. A small group, the Maccabees, fought back and won. When they came to rededicate the Holy Temple, they found only one jar of pure oil, enough for one day. It lasted eight days.

The Chanukah menorah, also called a chanukiah, has eight branches in a straight row, plus a ninth light called the shamash (helper), set apart, used to light the others.

## What you need

- A menorah with eight lights in a straight line, all at the same height.
- Olive oil and wicks, or candles. Olive oil is preferred because it recalls the miracle, and beeswax candles are a good choice too.
- Each light must be able to burn at least 30 minutes after nightfall.

## Where to place the menorah

- In a doorway, on the left side as you walk in, opposite the [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah). This is Chabad custom.
- Many place it in a window facing the street, so passersby can see it.
- Ideally the lights should be between about 10 and 40 inches above the floor, and never higher than about 30 feet.

## When to light

Light after sunset or at nightfall, according to your custom; Chabad lights at sunset. On Friday afternoon, light before the Shabbat candles, using enough oil or long enough candles to burn until 30 minutes after nightfall. On Saturday night, light after Shabbat ends and after Havdalah.

## How to light, step by step

1. **Set up the lights.** On the first night, place one light on the far right. Each night add a light to the left of the previous one.
2. **Light the shamash.**
3. **Say the blessings** (below), holding the shamash.
4. **Light the newest light first**, the one on the far left, then move to the right.
5. **Sing Haneirot Halalu and Maoz Tzur**, and stay near the lights for a while, at least the first half hour.

## The blessings

Say the blessings **before** you light, holding the shamash. Read the Hebrew, or the English letters if you cannot read Hebrew. Press play on the video above or the audio button to hear them said out loud first.

**First blessing (every night):**

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו, וְצִוָּנוּ לְהַדְלִיק נֵר חֲנֻכָּה

Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu l'hadlik ner Chanukah.

Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us to kindle the Chanukah light.

**Second blessing (every night):**

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, שֶׁעָשָׂה נִסִּים לַאֲבוֹתֵינוּ, בַּיָּמִים הָהֵם בִּזְּמַן הַזֶּה

Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, she'asah nissim la'avoteinu, bayamim haheim bizman hazeh.

Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who performed miracles for our ancestors in those days, at this time.

**Third blessing (first night only, or the first time you light this Chanukah):**

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, שֶׁהֶחֱיָנוּ וְקִיְּמָנוּ וְהִגִּיעָנוּ לַזְּמַן הַזֶּה

Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, shehecheyanu v'kiy'manu v'higi'anu lazman hazeh.

Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has kept us alive, sustained us and brought us to this season.

On the first night say all three blessings, then light. On the other seven nights say only the first two. On the first night, light the newest candle (the far-right one) and then, on later nights, begin from the newest candle at the far left.

### After the lighting: Haneirot Halalu

Right after lighting, sing or read this short passage:

הַנֵּרוֹת הַלָּלוּ אֲנַחְנוּ מַדְלִיקִין עַל הַנִּסִּים וְעַל הַנִּפְלָאוֹת, עַל הַתְּשׁוּעוֹת וְעַל הַמִּלְחָמוֹת, שֶׁעָשִׂיתָ לַאֲבוֹתֵינוּ בַּיָּמִים הָהֵם בַּזְּמַן הַזֶּה. וְכָל שְׁמוֹנַת יְמֵי חֲנֻכָּה הַנֵּרוֹת הַלָּלוּ קֹדֶשׁ הֵם, וְאֵין לָנוּ רְשׁוּת לְהִשְׁתַּמֵּשׁ בָּהֶם אֶלָּא לִרְאוֹתָם בִּלְבָד, כְּדֵי לְהוֹדוֹת וּלְהַלֵּל לְשִׁמְךָ הַגָּדוֹל עַל נִסֶּיךָ וְעַל נִפְלְאוֹתֶיךָ וְעַל יְשׁוּעָתֶךָ

Haneirot halalu anachnu madlikin al hanissim v'al hanifla'ot, al hatshu'ot v'al hamilchamot, she'asita la'avoteinu bayamim haheim bazman hazeh. V'chol shmonat y'mei Chanukah haneirot halalu kodesh heim, v'ein lanu r'shut l'hishtamesh bahem ela lir'otam bilvad, k'dei l'hodot ul'halel l'shimcha hagadol al nisecha v'al nifle'otecha v'al y'shu'atecha.

These lights we kindle for the miracles, the wonders, the salvations and the wars that You performed for our ancestors in those days, at this time. All eight days of Chanukah these lights are holy, and we have no permission to use them, only to look at them, in order to thank and praise Your great Name for Your miracles, Your wonders and Your salvations.

### Then Maoz Tzur

The best-known Chanukah song, first verse:

מָעוֹז צוּר יְשׁוּעָתִי, לְךָ נָאֶה לְשַׁבֵּחַ. תִּכּוֹן בֵּית תְּפִלָּתִי, וְשָׁם תּוֹדָה נְזַבֵּחַ. לְעֵת תָּכִין מַטְבֵּחַ מִצָּר הַמְנַבֵּחַ, אָז אֶגְמוֹר, בְּשִׁיר מִזְמוֹר, חֲנֻכַּת הַמִּזְבֵּחַ

Ma'oz tzur y'shu'ati, l'cha na'eh l'shabei'ach. Tikon beit t'filati, v'sham todah n'zabei'ach. L'et tachin matbei'ach mitzar ham'nabei'ach, az egmor b'shir mizmor chanukat hamizbei'ach.

Rock of my salvation, to You it is fitting to give praise. Restore my house of prayer, and there we will offer thanks. When You prepare the slaughter of the barking enemy, then I will complete, with a song of psalm, the dedication of the altar.

## Common questions

### Can I use the menorah lights for reading or other purposes?
No. The Chanukah lights are only for looking at, not for using. That is one reason for the shamash, so any benefit comes from it.

### Who should light?
Every Jew should light or be included in someone's lighting. Both men and women are obligated to light, or to take part in the household lighting, and children are encouraged to light their own menorah. Customs differ on whether each person lights their own, so ask the Rabbi. A woman living on her own lights her own menorah.

### What if I am traveling or staying with family?
Light where you are sleeping that night. A guest can contribute toward the host's oil or candles and be included, or light their own. Ask the Rabbi for your situation.

### Can I use an electric menorah?
An electric menorah does not fulfill the mitzvah, though it can be a nice decoration. For a hospital or other place where flames are not allowed, ask the Rabbi.
