On each of the eight nights of Chanukah, we light a menorah, adding one more light every night until all eight burn on the last night. The lights recall the miracle of the oil in the Holy Temple and publicize that light overcomes darkness.

## What the menorah recalls

Over 2,100 years ago, the Greek-Syrian rulers of the Land of Israel tried to force Jews to give up the Torah. A small group, the Maccabees, fought back and won. When they came to rededicate the Holy Temple, they found only one jar of pure oil, enough for one day. It lasted eight days.

The Chanukah menorah, also called a chanukiah, has eight branches in a straight row, plus a ninth light called the shamash (helper), set apart, used to light the others.

## What you need

- A menorah with eight lights in a straight line, all at the same height.
- Olive oil and wicks, or candles. Olive oil is preferred because it recalls the miracle, and beeswax candles are a good choice too.
- Each light must be able to burn at least 30 minutes after nightfall.

## Where to place the menorah

- In a doorway, on the left side as you walk in, opposite the [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah). This is Chabad custom.
- Many place it in a window facing the street, so passersby can see it.
- Ideally the lights should be between about 10 and 40 inches above the floor, and never higher than about 30 feet.

## When to light

Light after sunset or at nightfall, according to your custom; Chabad lights at sunset. On Friday afternoon, light before the Shabbat candles, using enough oil or long enough candles to burn until 30 minutes after nightfall. On Saturday night, light after Shabbat ends and after Havdalah.

## How to light, step by step

1. **Set up the lights.** On the first night, place one light on the far right. Each night add a light to the left of the previous one.
2. **Light the shamash.**
3. **Say the blessings** (below), holding the shamash.
4. **Light the newest light first**, the one on the far left, then move to the right.
5. **Sing Haneirot Halalu and Maoz Tzur**, and stay near the lights for a while, at least the first half hour.

## The blessings

**First blessing:**
בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו, וְצִוָּנוּ לְהַדְלִיק נֵר חֲנֻכָּה

Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu l'hadlik ner Chanukah.

Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us to kindle the Chanukah light.

**Second blessing:**
בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, שֶׁעָשָׂה נִסִּים לַאֲבוֹתֵינוּ, בַּיָּמִים הָהֵם בִּזְּמַן הַזֶּה

Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, she'asah nissim la'avoteinu, bayamim haheim bizman hazeh.

Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who performed miracles for our ancestors in those days, at this time.

**Third blessing, first night only:** Shehecheyanu.

## Common questions

### Can I use the menorah lights for reading or other purposes?
No. The Chanukah lights are only for looking at, not for using. That is one reason for the shamash, so any benefit comes from it.

### Who should light?
Every Jew should light or be included in someone's lighting. In Chabad custom, each boy and man lights his own menorah, and women and girls are included in the household lighting. A woman living on her own lights her own menorah. Customs vary, so ask the Rabbi.

### What if I am traveling or staying with family?
Light where you are sleeping that night. A guest can contribute toward the host's oil or candles and be included, or light their own. Ask the Rabbi for your situation.

### Can I use an electric menorah?
An electric menorah does not fulfill the mitzvah, though it can be a nice decoration. For a hospital or other place where flames are not allowed, ask the Rabbi.
