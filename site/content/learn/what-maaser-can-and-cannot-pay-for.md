Maaser can be given to the poor and, as many authorities allow, to other voluntary Jewish causes such as a synagogue, Jewish education and community institutions. It should not be used to pay for something you already owe, because maaser is meant to be a gift beyond your obligations. When in doubt, ask the Rabbi.

## Where maaser can go

Chabad.org explains that some authorities say maaser is only for the poor, as the tithe for the poor was in the Torah. Many others permit other mitzvah purposes, provided the mitzvah is voluntary and you would likely not do it without using maaser. Most opinions, including the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, allow this.

Chabad.org lists examples of communal causes: a synagogue, a yeshivah, a mikvah and a Torah library. It also says that supporting someone else's Torah learning is fine, and so is publishing Torah books. Poor relatives come before others, and a charity dinner is fine, as long as you subtract the value of the meal you receive.

See [Tzedakah Priorities](/learn/tzedakah-priorities) for more on where giving does the most good.

## What maaser should not pay for

The principle from Chabad.org is simple: maaser may not be used to fulfill something you were already obligated to do. Its examples:

- **A mitzvah you must do anyway,** such as buying a mezuzah.
- **A pledge or debt that already existed.** If you made a pledge without intending to pay it from maaser, it must come from your regular funds. Debts that are due now, like unpaid wages or loans, come before maaser.
- **Synagogue honors** (such as an aliyah) are paid from maaser only if you meant to do so from the start.
- **Your own Torah learning** is problematic to pay for from maaser.
- **Required Purim gifts to the poor** (matanot la'evyonim) cannot come from maaser, though extra charity can.

People often ask about three common cases:

**Children's tuition.** Chabad.org says this is complicated. Some say maaser may be used for Torah education tuition, while others limit it, depending on the child's age, the family's finances and what the money covers. Do not decide alone: ask the Rabbi.

**Synagogue dues and program fees.** The Chabad.org pages we reviewed do not mention them by name, so please ask the Rabbi.

**Paying for something you receive.** At a charity dinner, Chabad.org says, only the part above the value of the meal counts as maaser.

## Mark your maaser from the start

A simple habit from Chabad.org's guidance is to decide, when you set the money aside, that it is maaser, and keep it in its own account. That way you know what it is for. We describe it in [Starting Maaser Step by Step](/learn/starting-maaser-step-by-step).

## Common questions

### Can I pay my children's tuition with maaser?
Chabad.org says opinions differ and the answer depends on factors like the child's age and your finances. Ask the Rabbi before counting it.

### Can I give maaser to my synagogue?
Yes, supporting a synagogue is a recognized use. Dues you owe are a different question, so ask the Rabbi.

### Can I give maaser to Chabad of Lamorinda?
Yes. To direct your maaser to Jewish life in Lamorinda, call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 or Rebbetzin Labkowski at 925-384-7241.

### Does a donor-advised fund grant count as maaser?
Yes, Rabbi Labkowski has confirmed that a grant from a donor-advised fund to Chabad of Lamorinda counts as maaser. Call to arrange it.

### Can maaser go to family members?
Chabad.org says poor relatives come first. But if you can support your parents from your regular income, do so from there instead of from maaser.
