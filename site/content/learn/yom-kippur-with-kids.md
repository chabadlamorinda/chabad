Children under nine do not fast on Yom Kippur, and healthy children are gradually trained to fast starting around age nine, as they approach bar or bat mitzvah. Health always comes first: a child who cannot fast, or for whom fasting is a risk, should not fast. For your own child, ask the Rabbi.

## Do children fast on Yom Kippur?

Children are not obligated in mitzvot until bar or bat mitzvah, but we teach them in advance. Chabad.org gives this guidance for Yom Kippur:

- **Under 9:** No fasting is required, and children who want to fast are discouraged. Some hold that a child may fast for a few hours by custom, if able.
- **From age 9 (a healthy child) or 10 (a weaker child):** Training begins. A child gradually extends the fast: for example, delaying breakfast an hour, and over the years building toward a full day.
- **Ages 11 and 12:** The stricter view is to train them to complete the full fast, while the more lenient view requires only a few hours. Weaker children may follow the lenient view, and children before bar or bat mitzvah are generally not pressed to finish unless they are known to be strong and healthy.

For a fast of this kind, there is no shame in eating. Children who eat should have plain, regular food, not special treats. Anyone with a medical condition, such as diabetes, should consult both the doctor and the Rabbi. See also [the Yom Kippur fast](/learn/yom-kippur-fast).

## What children still do

Even young children can take part in the spirit of the day:

- Wear non-leather shoes, as adults do. Chabad.org notes children too should be taught this.
- Skip the sweets and treats; with older children who understand the day's seriousness, keep these to a minimum.
- If a child is training to fast, skip food after sundown on the eve of Yom Kippur and delay breakfast a little.
- Come to services, even for a short time.

## Bringing kids to services

The services are long, so plan around your children. Come for a short part, such as [Kol Nidre](/learn/kol-nidre), the Yizkor memorial service or the closing hour of [Neilah](/learn/neilah). The sound of the long shofar blast at the end is one of the most memorable moments for a child. Call the Rabbi to hear what is planned for children and families.

## Blessing the children

Before Yom Kippur it is a custom for parents to bless their children, as on Shabbat. See [blessing your children](/learn/blessing-your-children). A few warm words, a hand on the head, and a wish for a good year can make this a day your child remembers. Ask the Rabbi if you would like the words.

## What to tell kids, by age

- **Little ones:** "Today is the day we say sorry and G-d forgives us. We stay close to G-d, and then we feel clean and happy."
- **School age:** Tell the story of Jonah, a prophet who ran away and was forgiven (see [the Book of Jonah](/learn/book-of-jonah)). Ask: "What is one thing you want to do better this year?"
- **Teens:** Talk about [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah) as a fresh start, not guilt, and about the power of a clean slate.

## Common questions

### At what age should a child start fasting on Yom Kippur?
Children under nine do not fast, and a healthy child begins training at about nine, extending the fast gradually. A weaker child may begin at ten.

### Should my young child fast on Yom Kippur?
No. Children who are not yet nine should not be encouraged to fast, and any child who cannot fast safely should not. Ask the Rabbi about your child.

### What can my child do on Yom Kippur if they are not fasting?
They can wear non-leather shoes, go easy on treats, come to part of the service and take part in the spirit of the day.

### How do I explain Yom Kippur to children?
Say it is the day we say sorry and ask G-d to forgive us, and that we start the year fresh. The story of Jonah helps.
