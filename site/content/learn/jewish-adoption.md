"Whoever raises an orphan in their home is considered as if they gave birth to the child," the Talmud says. Adoption is one of the most beautiful kindnesses a family can do, and Jewish tradition honors it.

## Jewish status

A child's Jewish status comes from the birth mother. So:

- **A child born to a Jewish mother** is Jewish, whoever adopts the child.
- **A child born to a mother who is not Jewish** becomes Jewish through conversion: for a boy, a brit milah and immersion in a mikvah; for a girl, immersion in a mikvah, before a rabbinic court (beit din). When the child comes of age, they may affirm it.

Speak with a rabbi early in the process, ideally before the adoption is final, so the conversion is done properly and in time. See our [conversion](/learn/converting-to-judaism) guide.

## Naming and ceremonies

- A boy is given his Hebrew name at his brit or conversion; a girl at her conversion or in the synagogue at a Torah reading.
- An adopted child born to a non-Jewish mother is usually called "ben Avraham" or "bat Avraham" (son or daughter of Abraham), the father of the Jewish people. For a child born Jewish, the Rabbi will advise how the name is given.
- See our guide to [Jewish baby names](/learn/jewish-baby-names).

## Growing up

An adopted child is fully part of the family. They celebrate their [Bar Mitzvah](/learn/bar-mitzvah) or Bat Mitzvah, go to Hebrew School and grow up in the family's Jewish life like any other child. There are some details in Jewish law (for example about family relationships and privacy at home as children grow) that a rabbi can explain simply.

## Talking with your child

Experts recommend speaking openly with children about their adoption, in words that suit their age. A Jewish message to share: "G-d brought you to us, and you are our child forever."

## Common questions

### Does an adopted child need to convert?
Only if the birth mother is not Jewish. If she was Jewish, the child is already Jewish.

### At what age should the conversion be done?
Usually as soon as possible after the adoption. Ask the Rabbi to guide you through the timing.

### Can an adopted son say Kaddish for his adoptive parents?
Yes. An adopted child may say Kaddish for the parents who raised him, with great merit for their souls.
