You can bring Chanukah to a homebound neighbor, parent or friend with a short visit, a phone call, a simple gift and, if possible, lighting the menorah together. Light is the heart of the holiday, and a person who cannot get out can still receive it, because the greatest gift you bring is your company.

## Why this matters on Chanukah

Chanukah (חֲנֻכָּה) is the holiday of light in dark places. Each night we add one more candle, because we keep growing in light. In a talk on the second night of Chanukah, the Rebbe urged each of us to add: more Torah, more mitzvot, more tzedakah, and more unity among Jews. Visiting someone who is alone is a way of adding.

There is also a story of a man at a rehabilitation hospital in New York, where oil and candles were not allowed. He lit a small electric menorah every evening for the Jewish residents, many of them with memory loss, so the lights would remind them that the holiday had come. On the last night, one bulb failed, and he searched until he found a replacement, because he believed they deserved all the lights. His effort tells us how much a small light can mean to someone who rarely gets visitors.

## How to visit well

1. **Call first.** Ask what time suits, and whether the person feels up to company. A short visit is better than a long one.
2. **Come ready to listen.** Many homebound people want to talk about the past: past Chanukahs, a childhood home, the family. Ask a question and listen.
3. **Bring light.** If the person lives at home and can safely light, bring a menorah, candles or oil, and light together with the blessings. See [How to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah). Make safety the first priority: a sturdy tray, nothing flammable nearby, and never leave a flame alone. If the person cannot safely have a flame, see [Chanukah in senior living](/learn/chanukah-in-senior-living) for other options, and ask the Rabbi.
4. **Bring a song.** Haneirot Halalu and Maoz Tzur may bring memories back. See [Chanukah songs](/learn/chanukah-songs). Bring a printed copy in large type.
5. **Bring something small.** A card from children, a drawing, a few coins of gelt, or a treat the person's diet allows. See [Chanukah foods](/learn/chanukah-foods). Check about food allergies and diets first.
6. **Say goodbye warmly,** and promise only what you can keep. If you say you will call, call.

## When you cannot visit

- **Phone call.** A call on any evening of Chanukah is a gift. Sing a verse together, tell them about your own lighting, or say the story. Weekday evenings are best. On Shabbat, do not use the phone.
- **Send a card or a photo.** A picture of your menorah, a drawing from a grandchild or a handwritten note can brighten a whole week.
- **Mail a small package.** A menorah and candles, with a note on how to use them, if the person can light safely.
- **Ask a neighbor to look in.** Or let the Rabbi know about someone who would appreciate a visit.

## Common questions

### How can I bring Chanukah to someone who cannot leave home?
Call first, visit briefly, and bring a menorah, a song and something small. If the person can light safely, light together. Otherwise bring the holiday by song, story and warmth.

### Can I light the menorah for a homebound person?
You may help them light, if it is safe. A person who is able should light themselves, with the blessings, and a visitor can help. If they cannot light, ask the Rabbi what to do in their case.

### What do I say to a homebound person on Chanukah?
Say "Happy Chanukah," ask about their memories of the holiday and listen. A greeting and your time matter more than any speech.

### What if the person has memory loss?
Keep it simple and familiar: lights, familiar songs and a calm voice. A visual cue like a menorah can show that the holiday has come. See [honoring the elderly](/learn/honoring-the-elderly).

### Can I visit on Shabbat?
Yes, a visit on Shabbat is a lovely mitzvah. Shabbat rules apply to the visit, including no phone calls, so plan it around them and ask the Rabbi if you have questions. See [Chanukah on Shabbat](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat).
