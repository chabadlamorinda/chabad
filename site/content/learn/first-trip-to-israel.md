For many Jews, the first trip to Israel changes everything: standing at the Western Wall, a Shabbat in Jerusalem, walking where our ancestors walked. Here is how to make it more than a vacation.

## Before you go

- **Ask for a blessing,** and take tzedakah money from friends to give in Israel. Someone on a mitzvah journey is protected, the sages teach, and carrying tzedakah makes the trip a mission.
- **Learn a little:** the stories of [Jerusalem](/learn/jerusalem) and the [Western Wall](/learn/western-wall).
- **Plan around Shabbat:** almost everything closes from Friday afternoon to Saturday night in much of the country, and public transport stops.
- **Pack:** a [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) set if you put them on, Shabbat candles for women, modest clothing for holy sites.

## Places of the heart

- **The Western Wall (Kotel)** in Jerusalem: pray, put a note in the stones, put on tefillin with the Chabad stand there.
- **The tunnels and the Old City,** the City of David.
- **Hebron:** the Cave of the Patriarchs, resting place of Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Sarah, Rebecca and Leah.
- **Safed (Tzfat):** the city of Kabbalah.
- **Rachel's Tomb,** near Bethlehem.

## Shabbat in Israel

A Shabbat in Jerusalem is unforgettable: the whole city slows down. Chabad Houses across Israel welcome travelers for meals; ask Rabbi Labkowski for a contact.

## Kosher food

Most restaurants in Israel are kosher, but not all. Look for a kosher certificate (teudat kashrut) on the wall.

## Common questions

### Is it safe to travel to Israel?
Check your government's current travel advice before you go and follow local instructions. Millions visit every year.

### Do I need to be religious to pray at the Wall?
No. The Western Wall belongs to every Jew.

### Can I bring back a mezuzah or tefillin?
Yes, from a reliable scribe. Ask the Rabbi where to buy, or order through us; see [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah).

### What should I say at the Wall?
Speak to G-d in your own words, say a chapter of [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim), and pray for your family and the Jewish people.
