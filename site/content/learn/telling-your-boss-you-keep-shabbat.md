To tell your boss you keep Shabbat, make a calm, specific request ahead of time: say that you observe Shabbat from Friday before sunset until Saturday night, explain which hours you need, and offer a plan to make up the time. In the United States, employers are generally required to reasonably accommodate religious practice unless it causes undue hardship, according to the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC). This guide is not legal advice.

For people in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda who commute to San Francisco, Oakland or elsewhere in the Bay Area, the practical question is usually the Friday afternoon. Here is how to approach it.

## Why Shabbat affects work

Shabbat lasts about 25 hours, from before sunset on Friday until Saturday night. Observant Jews do not do creative work, use phones and electronics, or drive. See [thirty-nine melachot](/learn/thirty-nine-melachot) and [Shabbat for beginners](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners). In the winter, candle lighting can come as early as the afternoon, so an early Friday is part of keeping Shabbat; see [Shabbat in winter](/learn/shabbat-in-winter) and [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times).

## Before you talk: get ready

1. **Know your times.** Look up the candle-lighting time for each Friday of the year, so you can say clearly what you need. See [Shabbat times in Lamorinda explained](/learn/shabbat-times-in-lamorinda-explained).
2. **Decide what you can offer.** For example, starting earlier on other days, working from home, working Sunday or covering a different shift.
3. **Think about the whole year,** including the Jewish holidays, which also have days when work is not done. See [Jewish holidays at school and work](/learn/jewish-holidays-at-school-and-work). Holiday dates are on [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays).
4. **Ask the Rabbi** if your job includes on-call duty or emergencies. Some situations are individual.

## What to say: sample scripts

Keep it brief, friendly and clear. You do not have to teach Jewish law.

- *For a new job:* "I observe Shabbat, so I am not available from Friday before sunset through Saturday night. I am glad to work other hours to make up for it."
- *For an existing job:* "I would like to talk about my Friday schedule. In the winter I need to leave early for a religious observance, and I can make up the hours on other days."
- *For a holiday:* "I observe several Jewish holidays when I cannot work. I will send you the dates as early as I can, and arrange coverage."

Say it in person or in writing, and keep a short written note afterwards. A calm, positive tone usually works best.

## What the law says, in general terms

Please check current sources; this is general information, not legal advice. According to the EEOC, employers must reasonably accommodate employees' religious beliefs and practices unless doing so would cause an undue hardship, and accommodations can include flexible scheduling and shift swaps. The Supreme Court decision *Groff v. DeJoy* (2023) says undue hardship means a burden that is substantial in the overall context of the employer's business. The EEOC suggests telling your employer you need an accommodation for religious reasons and taking part in a discussion about it. If you have a problem, the EEOC says a charge generally must be filed within 180 days, which state laws may extend. Laws differ for different jobs and states, so for your situation consult an employment attorney or the EEOC.

## If the answer is no

Stay calm. Ask whether another option works, such as a shift swap or working from home. Keep records. Talk to the Rabbi at 925-384-7242, and if needed, seek legal advice.

## Common questions

### How do I ask my boss for Shabbat off?
Ask early, in a calm and specific way. Say which hours you need and offer to make up the time. See the sample scripts above.

### Can my employer refuse to let me observe Shabbat?
In general, US employers must reasonably accommodate religious practice unless it causes undue hardship, according to the EEOC. Whether it applies to your job is a legal question; this guide is not legal advice.

### What if I have to leave early on winter Fridays?
Tell your manager in advance and offer to make up the time. Candle lighting can be in the afternoon in winter. See [Shabbat in winter](/learn/shabbat-in-winter).

### Do I have to explain my religion in detail?
No. A short, clear sentence is usually enough. The EEOC says you should tell your employer that you need the accommodation for religious reasons.

### What about Jewish holidays?
Tell your employer the dates early. See [Jewish holidays at school and work](/learn/jewish-holidays-at-school-and-work).
