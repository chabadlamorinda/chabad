For Passover, any packaged or processed food needs a reliable "Kosher for Passover" label, while fresh fruit and vegetables, and eggs, need none. Buy your matzah, wine and Seder items first, then check the rest of your list item by item, and ask the Rabbi about anything you are unsure of.

## The simple rule

Passover (פֶּסַח) is the week when Jews avoid chametz (חָמֵץ): anything made from wheat, barley, spelt, rye or oats that has been allowed to rise or ferment. A processed food might hide chametz in its ingredients, its flavors or the machines that made it, so the year-round kosher symbol is not enough. The product needs a special "Kosher for Passover" mark. To understand the rules, see [kosher for Passover](/learn/kosher-for-passover) and [chametz](/learn/chametz).

## What needs a Kosher for Passover label

Look for a reliable Passover symbol on:

- Packaged and canned foods, sauces, condiments and dressings
- Drinks, juice, soda and liquor (see [beer and spirits](/learn/kosher-beer-and-spirits))
- Spices, seasoning mixes, sugar substitutes and baking ingredients
- Dairy products such as cheese, yogurt and butter
- Frozen foods, snacks, candy and cereal alternatives
- Processed meat and fish, and cooked or marinated products
- Coffee, tea and cocoa beyond the plain whole product: ask about a specific one
- Medicines and cosmetics you take or put in your mouth: ask the Rabbi (see [medicines and vitamins](/learn/kosher-medicine-and-vitamins))

## What is usually fine without a label

- Fresh fruit and vegetables, washed and checked (see [checking for insects](/learn/checking-for-insects))
- Fresh eggs, after checking (see [kosher eggs](/learn/kosher-eggs))
- Plain fresh kosher meat and poultry bought from a kosher source, as long as it has not been processed
- Plain water

Even here, some items vary: for example, coffee, tea, oil and salt can have additives or processing, so check or ask. For each item, the Rabbi can tell you.

## Chabad custom and kitniyot

Like most Ashkenazi Jews, Chabad custom is not to eat kitniyot (קִטְנִיּוֹת), legumes and grains such as rice, corn, beans and lentils, on Passover. So a "Kosher for Passover" label does not always make a product right for you: products with kitniyot ingredients are for those whose custom allows them. Ask the Rabbi what applies to your family.

## Seder-night shopping list

1. **Matzah:** handmade shmurah matzah for the Seder if possible, and regular kosher-for-Passover matzah for the rest of the week. Use the [matzah form](/forms/matzah).
2. **Wine or grape juice:** four cups per person, kosher for Passover.
3. **Seder plate items:** a roasted bone, eggs, bitter herbs (horseradish or romaine lettuce), karpas (a vegetable such as parsley or potato), charoset (apples, nuts and wine), and salt water. See the [Seder plate](/learn/seder-plate).
4. **Candles** for the table.
5. **Meal ingredients:** all with Passover labels.

For those who cannot eat wheat, see [gluten-free matzah](/learn/gluten-free-passover).

## Common questions

### What needs a Kosher for Passover label?
Any packaged, canned, bottled or processed food, plus spices, drinks and dairy products. Fresh produce and eggs do not.

### Is coffee kosher for Passover?
Plain whole coffee beans are generally not an issue, but flavored, ground or instant coffee may need a Passover label. Ask the Rabbi about your brand.

### Do I need Kosher for Passover labels on fresh fruit and vegetables?
No. Fresh fruit and vegetables need no label, but they should be washed and checked.

### Can I use my regular kosher products on Passover?
Not if they are processed. A regular kosher symbol is not enough, because chametz may be present. Look for a Passover symbol.

### Is kosher for Passover the same as gluten-free?
No. Passover foods may contain matzah meal, and gluten-free foods may contain kitniyot or lack a Passover symbol.
