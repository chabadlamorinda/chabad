Jewish life in the Bay Area is almost as old as the modern Bay Area itself. When the Gold Rush brought thousands of newcomers to San Francisco in 1849, Jews were among them, and within a few years they had held services, founded congregations and begun building a community. Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda are part of the same story, which is still being written.

## The Gold Rush: the first services

Many of the early Jewish arrivals were young merchants and shopkeepers, a large number of them from German-speaking lands. They followed the crowds to a boom town that had almost nothing: no synagogue, no kosher butcher, no rabbi.

They did what Jews have always done. They gathered a minyan. Congregation Emanu-El tells the story that the first High Holy Day services in San Francisco were held in 1849, in a tent, and describes them as the first Jewish service on the West Coast. Emanu-El was then founded in 1850 and chartered in 1851. Sherith Israel was also founded around 1849 to 1851. The two congregations arose partly because the early Jews came with different prayer customs, a good reminder that Jews have always prayed in more than one way, and that we are one people.

## Building a community

The pioneers did more than pray. In the early years Jews also set up cemeteries and charities, because looking after one another is part of what it means to be Jewish. This is the Torah value of [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) and [chesed](/learn/chesed) in action: a stranger in a new city is not left alone.

By 1880 San Francisco's Jewish community was reportedly the second largest in the United States. Then, in 1906, the earthquake and fires devastated most of the city, where close to 30,000 Jews lived. The community rebuilt, as it had before.

## Levi Strauss: a Jewish pioneer

The best-known name is Levi Strauss. He was born in 1829 in Buttenheim, Bavaria, and arrived in San Francisco in 1854 to join his sister's family. In 1873 he and the tailor Jacob Davis received a patent for riveted work pants, the beginning of blue jeans. Levi Strauss never married. He gave generously to charity, including funds for orphans and scholarships at the University of California, and he helped found Congregation Emanu-El. Another Jewish pioneer was Adolph Sutro, who was elected San Francisco's mayor in 1895.

## Across the bay and into Contra Costa

Over the last century and a half the Jewish population spread out from the city. A large community study of the Bay Area in 2018, by the Jewish Community Federation, counted about 350,000 Jews across ten counties, one of the largest Jewish populations in the United States. It found that the East Bay's Jewish adult population had grown by about a third since 2011, while San Francisco's share was about 17 percent of the total. Many families now live in suburban towns like ours. See [Jewish life in the East Bay](/learn/jewish-east-bay), [Jewish Lafayette](/jewish-lafayette), [Jewish Moraga](/jewish-moraga) and [Jewish Orinda](/jewish-orinda).

## A lesson from the pioneers

The first Jews of California started with nothing and still built Jewish life, with a tent, a minyan and a few friends. Chassidic teaching says that every Jew is a link in a chain, and every place can become a home for Torah. That is as true in a Lafayette living room as it was in a Gold Rush tent.

## Common questions

### When did Jews first come to San Francisco?
Jews arrived with the Gold Rush, from 1849. The first High Holy Day services are described as taking place that year, in a tent, and the first congregations were founded in the years right after.

### Who was Levi Strauss?
He was a Jewish immigrant from Bavaria who came to San Francisco in 1854. With the tailor Jacob Davis he received the 1873 patent for riveted work pants, and he supported charities and Congregation Emanu-El.

### What is the oldest synagogue in San Francisco?
Congregation Emanu-El and Congregation Sherith Israel were both founded in the first years after the Gold Rush began, around 1849 to 1851. Historians describe them as the city's first congregations.

### How many Jews live in the Bay Area?
A 2018 study by the Jewish Community Federation counted about 350,000 Jews in the ten-county Bay Area. Numbers change, so treat it as the best recent count, not today's exact figure.

### Is there Jewish history in the East Bay?
Yes. The East Bay's Jewish community has grown a great deal, and Berkeley is home to the Magnes Collection of Jewish Art and Life. Read more in our guide to [Jewish San Francisco](/learn/jewish-san-francisco).
