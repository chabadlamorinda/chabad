Rachel is one of the four Matriarchs of the Jewish people, the beloved wife of Jacob and the mother of Joseph and Benjamin. She is remembered above all as the mother who weeps for her children, and as the one who, according to tradition, pleads with G-d to bring His children home.

## Jacob meets Rachel at the well

Rachel's story begins in Genesis, when Jacob, fleeing his brother Esau, arrives in Padan Aram and meets Rachel at a well as she brings her father Laban's sheep to drink. Her name means "sheep" (רָחֵל), and Chabad.org links it to her gentle nature. Jacob loved her at once and agreed to work seven years for her hand. The Torah says those years seemed to him "like a few days" because of his love.

This is the Torah reading of Vayetze, which we read in late fall. Read more about [Jacob our father](/learn/jacob-our-father).

## Rachel's kindness to her sister

When the wedding day came, Laban secretly substituted his older daughter Leah under the wedding canopy. According to Chabad.org, Rachel, who knew about the plan and the secret signs Jacob had given her, passed the signs on to her sister so that Leah would not be shamed. Jacob later worked seven more years to marry Rachel as well.

This act of kindness is one of the most moving in the Torah. Rachel gave up her own moment of joy to protect her sister's dignity. Our sages hold it up as a model: a person who would rather be hurt than hurt another.

## Years of waiting, and two sons

Leah had six sons while Rachel had none, and Rachel's longing was deep. At last she gave birth to Joseph, whose name expresses her wish for another son. Her prayers for children have become a model for Jewish women who long to be mothers (see [the Matriarchs](/learn/the-matriarchs) and [Joseph and his brothers](/learn/joseph-and-his-brothers)).

## Her death and burial by the road

As the family traveled, Rachel died giving birth to her second son, Benjamin, and Jacob buried her on the road near Bethlehem, on the way to Efrat. Unlike the other Patriarchs and Matriarchs, she was not buried in the Cave of the Patriarchs in Hebron.

Chabad.org explains the traditional reason. Jacob foresaw that the Jewish people would one day pass along that road as exiles, and he buried Rachel there so she could pray for them as they went by.

## Rachel weeps for her children

The prophet Jeremiah says (31:14-16): "A voice is heard in Ramah, lamentation and bitter weeping; Rachel is weeping for her children." Then comes G-d's answer: "Hold back your voice from weeping... for your work shall be rewarded, and they shall return from the land of the enemy." Read more in [Jeremiah](/learn/jeremiah).

According to a Midrash cited by Chabad.org, Rachel argued before G-d for mercy for her children, reminding Him of her own kindness to Leah. G-d was moved and promised to bring the exiles home. This is why Jewish mothers have turned to Rachel in prayer for their children, and why Rachel's tomb is a place of prayer.

## Rachel's Tomb today

Rachel's Tomb is near Bethlehem, just south of Jerusalem. Chabad.org describes it as a place where people pour out their hearts, particularly women praying for children. Tradition marks her passing on the 11th of Cheshvan, and people come to the tomb to pray on that day. One custom connected to her grave is placing a small stone on a grave after visiting, which Chabad.org traces to Jacob's sons, who each placed a stone on Rachel's grave. For more about this custom, see [visiting a grave](/learn/visiting-a-grave) and [yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit).

## What Rachel teaches us

Rachel is the model of selfless love: love for a spouse, for a sister and for children. A mother's love is not logical; it simply pours out. In Chassidic teaching, Rachel's soul is rooted in "speech," the power to influence others.
Whatever you are carrying, you can turn to the Matriarchs in prayer. And whenever you give a child a hug, you are following Rachel.

## Common questions

### Who was Rachel in the Bible?
Rachel was the beloved wife of Jacob, one of the four Matriarchs, and the mother of Joseph and Benjamin.

### Why is Rachel buried on the road and not in Hebron?
Chabad.org explains that Jacob buried her by the road near Bethlehem so that she could pray for her children when they would pass by in exile.

### What does "Rachel weeps for her children" mean?
It comes from the prophet Jeremiah (31:14-16). G-d answers her that her children will return to their land.

### When is Rachel's yahrzeit?
Tradition marks it on 11 Cheshvan. See [yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit) for how Jews remember loved ones.

### Why do women pray at Rachel's Tomb?
Chabad.org notes that people pour out their hearts there, and women who long for children pray there in particular. Ask the Rabbi if you would like help with a personal prayer.

### Why did Rachel give her sister the signs?
According to Chabad.org, she did it so that Leah would not be shamed on her wedding day.
