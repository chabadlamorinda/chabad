Plain beer and plain, unflavored spirits such as vodka, gin and most whiskey are generally considered kosher, because they are made from grain and not from grapes. Wine-based drinks like brandy and cognac, flavored liqueurs, and some specialty or barrel-aged drinks are different and need a reliable [kosher symbol](/learn/kosher-symbols) or a question to the Rabbi.

## Why wine is different from beer

Kosher wine has strict rules (see [kosher wine](/learn/kosher-wine)): it must be made under Jewish supervision, and wine handled by non-Jews is forbidden. Those rules apply to grape products. Beer is made from grain, hops, water and yeast, and plain whiskey and vodka are distilled from grain or potatoes, so the grape rules usually do not come into play.

## Where problems can hide

- **Added ingredients.** Flavored beers, fruit beers, stouts with lactose (milk sugar) and flavored liquors can contain dairy, grape products or other ingredients that need supervision. See [hidden kosher ingredients](/learn/hidden-kosher-ingredients).
- **Casks.** Some whiskeys are aged or finished in barrels that previously held wine or sherry. That is a real question, and the answer depends on the details. Ask the Rabbi about a specific bottle; never rely on a guess.
- **Grape-based spirits.** Brandy, cognac and grappa are made from wine, so they need kosher supervision. Many liqueurs and cocktail mixers use grape or other ingredients too.
- **Craft and specialty products.** Small breweries and distilleries change recipes often. A reliable symbol is the safest guide, and the Rabbi can help when there is none.

## Is there a kosher liquor list?

Several major kosher agencies publish lists of beers and spirits they have reviewed. Use a list from an agency you trust, check its date, and when your bottle is not on it, ask. Chabad of Lamorinda does not endorse a brand: call 925-384-7242 and we will help you find the answer.

## The blessing on beer and spirits

On beer, whiskey, vodka and even brandy or cognac, the blessing is *Shehakol nihyeh bidvaro* (שֶׁהַכֹּל נִהְיֶה בִּדְבָרוֹ), "by whose word everything came to be." Although brandy is made from wine, the wine taste is gone, so the usual custom is that the blessing for drinks applies; ask the Rabbi if you are unsure. Wine itself has its own blessing, *Borei pri hagafen*. See [blessings](/learn/blessings).

## L'chaim, and doing it responsibly

Jewish gatherings, especially a farbrengen (a Chassidic gathering with stories, song and Torah), often include a small L'chaim ("to life") with a drink. It is meant to lift the spirit, not to cause harm. Judaism asks us to guard our health and our judgment: a small cup is enough, no one should feel pressured, and anyone who is driving, pregnant, in recovery or simply not drinking can say L'chaim with juice or water. Never drink and drive. If alcohol is a struggle, see [addiction and recovery](/learn/addiction-and-recovery).

## Beer and whiskey on Passover

Beer, whiskey and most grain spirits are chametz (חָמֵץ), fermented grain, so they may not be eaten, kept or used during Passover. Drink or give away what you will not sell; the rest is sold with your chametz through the [sell-chametz form](/forms/sell-chametz). Kosher-for-Passover vodka, wine and liqueurs exist; look for a clear "Kosher for Passover" symbol, and read [kosher for Passover](/learn/kosher-for-passover).

## Common questions

### Is beer kosher?
Plain beer is generally considered kosher, because it is made from grain, not grapes. Flavored beers, or those with added dairy or other ingredients, need a symbol or a question to the Rabbi.

### Is whiskey kosher?
Plain whiskey is generally considered kosher, but whiskey aged in wine or sherry casks, or flavored whiskey, is a question for the Rabbi.

### Is vodka kosher?
Plain vodka made from grain or potatoes is generally fine; flavored vodka needs a reliable symbol, because of the flavorings.

### Is brandy or cognac kosher?
Only with kosher supervision, because they are distilled from wine. The blessing, if kosher, is Shehakol.

### Is whiskey kosher for Passover?
Usually not. Whiskey is made from grain and is chametz. Do not drink it on Passover, and include it when you sell your chametz.
