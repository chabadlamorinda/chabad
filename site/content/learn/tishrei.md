Tishrei (תשרי) is the seventh month of the Jewish calendar, yet the Jewish year begins with it on Rosh Hashanah. It always has 30 days, usually falls in September and October, and holds more holidays than any other month: Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah.

## Why does the year start in the seventh month?

The Torah calls Rosh Hashanah "the first day of the seventh month." The Jewish calendar counts months from Nissan, the month of the Exodus, but the *year* is counted from Tishrei, which our sages link to the anniversary of the creation of Adam and Eve. Chabad.org explains that this is why there are several "new years" in Judaism. See [the Jewish calendar](/learn/jewish-calendar).

## A map of the month

Here are the holidays in order, with their Hebrew dates. For this year's calendar dates, see [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays); the site calculates them for you.

1. **Rosh Hashanah, 1 and 2 Tishrei.** The Jewish New Year: the shofar, round challah and honey, and crowning G-d as King. See [Rosh Hashanah for beginners](/learn/rosh-hashanah-for-beginners).
2. **The Fast of Gedaliah, 3 Tishrei.** A minor fast day in memory of the assassination of Gedaliah, the governor of Judea after the destruction of the First Temple. See [minor fast days](/learn/minor-fast-days).
3. **The Ten Days of Repentance, 1 to 10 Tishrei.** The days of closeness and return. See [the Ten Days](/learn/ten-days-of-repentance).
4. **Yom Kippur, 10 Tishrei.** The Day of Atonement: fasting, prayer and forgiveness, from [Kol Nidre](/learn/kol-nidre) to [Neilah](/learn/neilah). See [Yom Kippur for beginners](/learn/yom-kippur-for-beginners).
5. **Sukkot, 15 to 21 Tishrei.** Seven days of dwelling in the [sukkah](/learn/sukkah), with the lulav and etrog. See [Sukkot for beginners](/learn/sukkot-for-beginners).
6. **Hoshana Rabbah, 21 Tishrei.** The last day of Sukkot, with its special prayers. See [Hoshana Rabbah](/learn/hoshana-rabbah).
7. **Shemini Atzeret, 22 Tishrei.** The "eighth day of assembly." See [Shemini Atzeret](/learn/shemini-atzeret).
8. **Simchat Torah, 23 Tishrei (outside Israel).** Dancing with the Torah scrolls and starting the reading again. See [Simchat Torah for beginners](/learn/simchat-torah-for-beginners).

## How the holidays flow into each other

Notice the pattern: first awe (Rosh Hashanah), then forgiveness (Yom Kippur), then four days of preparation (Yom Kippur ends and the sukkah is built), and then joy (Sukkot, Simchat Torah). The month moves from the most serious days to the happiest ones.

Our sages say we do not move from the holy to the ordinary all at once: we move from one mitzvah to the next. For example, after Yom Kippur we start building the sukkah that same night; see [the break-fast](/learn/break-fast-after-yom-kippur). Sukkot has its own joy, the water-drawing celebration, described in [Simchat Beit Hashoevah](/learn/simchat-beit-hashoevah).

## A month that sets direction

Tishrei is a good time to set direction for the rest of the year. Many people use the month to decide on a few concrete commitments: a regular class, Shabbat candles, a daily moment of prayer, a regular donation to charity. Chassidic teaching often speaks of Tishrei as the "head" of the year, giving energy for all the months that follow; ask the Rabbi if you would like to explore this idea.

## Common questions

### What is the month of Tishrei?
Tishrei is the seventh month of the Jewish calendar and the month in which the Jewish year begins. It has 30 days and holds Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah.

### What holidays are in Tishrei?
Rosh Hashanah (1-2 Tishrei), the Fast of Gedaliah (3), Yom Kippur (10), Sukkot (15-21), Hoshana Rabbah (21), Shemini Atzeret (22) and Simchat Torah (23, outside Israel).

### Why does the Jewish new year start in the seventh month?
The Torah counts months from Nissan, but the year begins in Tishrei, associated with the anniversary of the creation of Adam and Eve.

### When is Tishrei this year?
The calendar dates change each year because the Jewish calendar follows the moon and the sun. See our [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays) page for this year's dates.

### How long does Tishrei last?
Tishrei always has 30 days and falls roughly between September and October.
