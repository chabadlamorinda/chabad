Every Jewish child deserves a bar or bat mitzvah, and at Chabad of Lamorinda every family is welcome: families who are not religious, who belong nowhere, who come from mixed backgrounds, who know very little about Judaism, or whose child is starting late. There is no membership, no test and no judgment. We meet your family where it is.

## "We're not religious"

Many families who call us say exactly this. That is fine. We do not ask how observant you are before teaching your child. We teach with warmth, explain everything simply, and let each family choose its own next steps. Many parents tell us they learned as much as their child.

## "We don't belong anywhere"

You do not need to belong to a synagogue or pay dues. Families from Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda and the nearby towns come to us just for the bar or bat mitzvah, and many stay because they feel at home. Either way, you are welcome. See [not religious? You belong](/learn/not-religious-welcome).

## "We know very little"

No Hebrew and no Jewish background are needed, for your child or for you. The Rabbi and the Rebbetzin start from the very beginning and explain every word and custom. Read [preparing without years of Hebrew school](/learn/bar-mitzvah-prep-without-hebrew-school).

## "Our family has mixed backgrounds"

Many Lamorinda families have relatives of different backgrounds and faiths. Everyone is welcome to come, to celebrate and to be part of the day, and we are glad to explain what is happening so that grandparents and friends who are new to Jewish customs feel included.

Sometimes a family is not sure about a child's Jewish status, for example when only the father is Jewish, or the family records are unclear. Please do not let that keep you away. Talk privately with the Rabbi: he will listen kindly, explain gently and help you find the right path for your child. Read more in [who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew).

## "Our child is starting late"

Whether the date is a year away or a few months away, or your child is already past 12 or 13, it is not too late. A child becomes bar or bat mitzvah on the Hebrew birthday regardless, and we can mark it meaningfully at any point. See [missed it at 13?](/learn/teen-bar-bat-mitzvah-not-too-late)

## "We're worried about the cost"

A bar or bat mitzvah does not need a big party. What matters is your child's first steps as a Jewish adult. If cost is a concern, call the Rabbi and talk it through privately; money should never keep a child from a bar or bat mitzvah. See [a bar or bat mitzvah on a budget](/learn/bar-bat-mitzvah-on-a-budget).

## "Our child has special needs"

Every child can become bar or bat mitzvah, and we adapt to your child. See [bar and bat mitzvah for a child with special needs](/learn/special-needs-bar-bat-mitzvah).

## Common questions

### Can we have a bar mitzvah if we are not religious?
Yes. Many families who prepare with Chabad of Lamorinda are not observant. Your child learns at their own pace, and your family chooses its own next steps.

### Can we have a bat mitzvah without being members of a synagogue?
Yes. Chabad of Lamorinda has no membership. Call 925-384-7242 or use the [bar and bat mitzvah form](/forms/bar-bat-mitzvah).

### Is a bar or bat mitzvah possible in an interfaith family?
Every family is welcome to talk with us. Questions about Jewish status are personal, so please call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 for a private, friendly conversation; he will help you find the right path for your child.

### Can relatives who are not Jewish come to the celebration?
Yes. Family and friends of every background are warmly welcome to come and celebrate.

### What if my child knows almost nothing about Judaism?
That is a fine place to start. The Rabbi and the Rebbetzin begin from the beginning and explain everything simply.
