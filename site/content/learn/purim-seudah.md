The Purim seudah is the festive meal held on the afternoon of Purim day, and it is one of the four mitzvahs of Purim. Families and friends gather around a table full of good food and wine to celebrate how G-d saved the Jewish people in the days of Mordechai and Esther.

## Why we have a festive meal

The Megillah calls Purim "days of feasting and joy" (Esther 9:22). The miracle saved the Jewish people from physical destruction, so we celebrate in a physical way, with food and drink. The seudah is one of the four Purim mitzvahs, together with hearing the [Megillah](/learn/megillah), sending [mishloach manot](/learn/mishloach-manot) and giving gifts to the poor. See our [Purim page](/holidays/purim).

## When to hold it

- **On Purim day**, not the night before. A nice dinner on Purim night is good, but it does not count as the seudah.
- **In the afternoon.** Most people start after the afternoon prayer (Mincha), and begin the meal while it is still daytime. The meal may continue into the night.
- **When Purim falls on Friday,** the seudah is held in the morning or early afternoon, so everyone is ready for Shabbat.

## What to serve

There are no required dishes, but a proper festive meal includes:

1. **Bread.** Wash hands and say Hamotzi, so the meal is a full seudah.
2. **Meat** is customary for a holiday celebration, along with wine.
3. **Traditional foods**, like kreplach (dough filled with meat, hidden like the miracle of Purim) and hamantaschen.
4. **Seeds, beans and nuts**, which some eat in memory of Esther, who ate vegetarian food in the palace to keep kosher.

## The custom of drinking

The Talmud says one should drink on Purim "until he does not know the difference between cursed is Haman and blessed is Mordechai." Many authorities explain that a person fulfills this by drinking a little more than usual, enough to feel the joy, and then perhaps taking a nap. The goal is joy and closeness to G-d, never losing control.

Important guidelines:

- **Never drink and drive.** Plan rides ahead.
- **Children and teens do not drink alcohol.** Grape juice works beautifully.
- **Anyone with a health concern** or who does not drink should not. Joy comes in many forms.

## Making the seudah meaningful

- **Invite guests,** especially people who may be alone. Hosting is part of the joy of Purim.
- **Sing, tell the Purim story, and share words of Torah.** Let kids act out the story in their costumes; see [Purim costumes](/learn/purim-costumes).
- **Give tzedakah** during the meal.
- **Say Al HaNissim** in the Grace After Meals. Read more about [Birkat Hamazon](/learn/birkat-hamazon).

## Common questions

### Does the Purim seudah have to include bread?
Many authorities say a proper seudah includes bread, and it is the best way to do the mitzvah. If you have a question for your situation, ask the Rabbi.

### Do I have to get drunk on Purim?
No. Many authorities say it is enough to drink a little more than usual and rest. Never drink to the point of danger, and never drive after drinking. Children should not drink alcohol.

### Can I have the Purim seudah at night?
The seudah is held on Purim day. Start in the afternoon, while it is still light, and you may continue after dark.

### What is Al HaNissim?
It is a short paragraph thanking G-d for the miracles of Purim, added to the Amidah and to the Grace After Meals on Purim. If you forget it, you do not repeat the prayer.
