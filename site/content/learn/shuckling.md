Jews sway back and forth during prayer, a custom called shuckling (from the Yiddish word for "shaking"), to pray with the whole body and to focus the mind. It is a custom, not a requirement, and many Jews pray perfectly well standing still.

## The most common explanations

- **The soul is like a flame.** The Book of Proverbs says, "The soul of man is the lamp of G-d" (Proverbs 20:27). A flame flickers and strives upward, and the soul longs to rise toward its Source. When we pray, the body moves with it.
- **"All my bones shall say."** King David wrote, "All my bones shall say: L-rd, who is like You?" (Psalm 35:10). Prayer is not only words. The whole person, bone and body, takes part.
- **Concentration.** Many people find that moving helps them stay focused, much as some people pace while thinking hard.
- **Like learning Torah.** Jews have swayed while studying for centuries, as a way of making the words come alive.

## Do you have to shuckle?

No. There is no commandment to sway. Many Jews, including great scholars, pray standing still. Whether to sway is a personal and community custom. If you are new, you can stand quietly, and no one will mind. Ask the Rabbi if you wonder what feels right for you.

## Other movements in Jewish prayer

Prayer has a choreography of its own, which will make more sense once you see it:

1. **Standing for the [Amidah](/learn/amidah).** The silent prayer is said standing, with feet together, facing Jerusalem.
2. **Three steps.** We take three steps forward as we begin, as if approaching a king, and three steps back at the end, as if leaving his presence.
3. **Bowing.** At certain moments of the Amidah and in the Aleinu prayer, we bow to express humility.
4. **Covering and wrapping.** Men wear a [tallit](/learn/tallit) and [kippah](/learn/kippah), and on weekdays tefillin.
5. **Kissing.** We kiss the Torah or the siddur out of love and respect.

## Common questions

### Why do Jews rock when they pray?
Many Jews rock because it helps them focus and express the soul's longing for G-d. It is a custom, not a law.

### What is shuckling?
Shuckling is the Yiddish word for the swaying or rocking motion during Jewish prayer or study.

### Do all Jews sway during prayer?
No. Some do, and many do not. It depends on custom and personal habit.

### Do I have to sway at services?
No. Stand or sit quietly if you prefer. No one will expect you to move in a particular way.

### Why do Jews bow in prayer?
Bowing expresses humility before G-d. It happens at set places in the Amidah and in Aleinu.
