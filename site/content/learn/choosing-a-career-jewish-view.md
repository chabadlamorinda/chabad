Jewish tradition does not tell you which career to choose, but it gives you a way to think about it: do your honest best to find the work that suits you, trust that G-d guides the outcome, and treat your job as a place to do good in the world. This guide draws on Chabad teaching for students, young adults and parents in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda.

## Effort and trust go together

A text on trust in G-d presented on Chabad.org (the work of Rabbeinu Bachya) teaches that trust does not mean sitting back. A person must still pursue the natural means that seem best, such as study, applications and hard work, and then rely on G-d for the result. It rejects the idea that because G-d provides, we need not work for a living. Chabad teaching calls the two partners: we do the effort, and G-d gives the outcome. Read more in [parnassah, work and livelihood](/learn/parnassah-work-and-livelihood).

## Two callings

In a chapter on making a living, drawn from the Rebbe's teachings, Chabad.org presents the idea that each person has two callings: an inner one, to grow spiritually and improve ourselves, and an outer one, to bring that growth into the material world. The same chapter says that money and livelihood are not separate from G-d, but a place where hidden spiritual value can be discovered. So a career is not only a way to earn; it can be a way to do good.

## Don't let worry push you around

The Rebbe once answered a young man who was worried about how he would earn a living. He said it was too early to worry: career decisions come later, interests and circumstances change, and anxiety now accomplishes nothing. He advised the student to focus on his studies, which he called the best possible preparation for future life. The Rebbe was speaking to a yeshivah student, so it is not a rule for everyone, but the principle applies widely: do what is in front of you wholeheartedly, and the next step will be clearer. If worry is weighing on you, read [anxiety and worry](/learn/anxiety-and-worry).

## Questions to ask yourself

- What am I good at, and what do others say I am good at?
- Will this work let me be honest in all my dealings? (See [business ethics](/learn/business-ethics).)
- Will it leave me time for Shabbat, family, prayer and learning? (See [Shabbat for Lamorinda commuters](/learn/shabbat-for-lamorinda-commuters) and [telling your boss you keep Shabbat](/learn/telling-your-boss-you-keep-shabbat).)
- How can this job help other people?
- Who can I ask for advice?

## Helping the next generation

Parents want their children to be able to support themselves honorably. Help them find that path while respecting who they are. If you are a parent, listen to your teen's strengths. And if you are not sure how a career choice fits with Jewish life, ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### Does Judaism tell me what career to choose?
No. Jewish tradition asks for honest work and trust in G-d, but the choice of career is yours. A rabbi can help you think it through.

### Is it right to pick a career just for money?
Making a living is important, and the Rebbe saw livelihood as a place to do good. Ask yourself whether the work is honest, whether it uses your gifts and whether it leaves room for your family and your Jewish life.

### What if I am worried I will not find a job?
Chabad teaching asks for both effort and trust. Do your part, honestly and steadily, and trust G-d with the result. See [success and failure](/learn/success-and-failure).

### Can I keep Shabbat and still build a career?
Many people do. See [telling your boss you keep Shabbat](/learn/telling-your-boss-you-keep-shabbat), and call the Rabbi for personal guidance.
