Selichot are special prayers asking G-d for forgiveness, said in the days before Rosh Hashanah and through Yom Kippur. Ashkenazi Jews begin them on the Saturday night before Rosh Hashanah, while Sephardic Jews say them for the entire month of Elul.

## What are Selichot?

Selichot means "forgiveness." The prayers are a collection of verses, poems and confessions, written over many centuries, that ask G-d for mercy. They are said with a minyan, and the heart of every Selichot service is the Thirteen Attributes of Mercy.

## The Thirteen Attributes of Mercy

After the sin of the golden calf, Moses pleaded for forgiveness for the Jewish people. G-d taught him a prayer, describing Himself with thirteen attributes of compassion: "G-d, G-d, merciful and gracious, slow to anger and abundant in kindness and truth..." (Exodus 34:6 to 7).

The Talmud says G-d promised Moses that whenever the Jewish people recite these attributes, their prayers will not go unanswered. This is why they are repeated many times in Selichot.

## When are Selichot said?

### Ashkenazi custom

Selichot begin on the Saturday night before Rosh Hashanah, after midnight. If Rosh Hashanah falls early in the week, so that there would be fewer than four days of Selichot, they begin a week earlier. They continue each morning before the morning prayers until the day before Rosh Hashanah, and during the Ten Days of Repentance until Yom Kippur.

### Sephardic custom

Sephardic Jews say Selichot every morning from the beginning of Elul until Yom Kippur.

## Why late at night or early in the morning?

The first Selichot are said after midnight, and the rest early in the morning before dawn. These are times of special Divine favor, when the world is quiet and the heart is open. King David said, "At midnight I rise to give thanks to You" (Psalms 119:62).

Saturday night was chosen for the first Selichot because it follows Shabbat, when people are uplifted from a day of rest, prayer and study.

## What to expect at a Selichot service

1. **Opening prayers**, including Ashrei and half Kaddish.
2. **Poems (piyutim)** asking for mercy, which change from day to day.
3. **The Thirteen Attributes**, said aloud by the whole congregation, many times.
4. **Confession (Vidui)**, said while lightly tapping the chest.
5. **Closing prayers**, including "Avinu Malkeinu" on some days, and Kaddish.

Selichot are said from a special Selichot book, usually with Hebrew and English. The Thirteen Attributes are said only with a minyan. Someone praying alone may say the other parts.

## The meaning of Selichot

Selichot are not about feeling guilty. They are about remembering that G-d is merciful and always ready to accept us back. By saying "G-d, G-d, merciful and gracious," we also commit to imitating His mercy: being patient, forgiving and kind to others. This is the essence of [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah).

## Common questions

### Do I have to go to synagogue for Selichot?
It is best to say Selichot with a minyan, since some parts can only be said with ten. If you cannot, you can say most of the prayers alone from a Selichot book, skipping the Thirteen Attributes or reading them with the special Torah melody as a reading only.

### How long is Selichot?
A Selichot service usually takes 30 to 60 minutes, depending on the day and the pace of the congregation. The first night is often longer.

### Can I come to Selichot if I do not read Hebrew?
Yes. Many Selichot books have English translations. You can follow along, pray in your own words, and join in the Thirteen Attributes.

### Is Selichot a holiday?
No. It is a time of preparation, not a holiday. There are no restrictions on work, and it leads up to Rosh Hashanah.
