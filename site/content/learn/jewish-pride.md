Jewish pride means feeling good about being Jewish and living it openly, with confidence and joy. It is not about feeling better than anyone else. It is about knowing who you are, where you come from, and the light you have to share with the world.

## Why Jewish pride matters

The first Jew, Abraham, was called *Avraham Halvri*, "Abraham the Hebrew." The Sages explain that *ivri* means "from the other side": the whole world stood on one side, and Abraham stood on the other, unafraid to be different.

For thousands of years, Jews have kept their identity in every kind of place and time. Being proud of that heritage is a way of honoring the generations who kept it alive, often at great cost.

Jewish pride also protects. Children and adults who feel good about being Jewish are more resilient, more connected and better able to respond to prejudice.

## Pride, not arrogance

Judaism values humility. Moses, the greatest leader, was "the most humble of all men." True Jewish pride is not boastful. It is a quiet confidence that being Jewish is a gift and a responsibility, and that every Jew has something precious to contribute.

## Responding to hate with light

When Jews face antisemitism, the Lubavitcher Rebbe taught that the answer is not to hide but to add more light: more Jewish learning, more mitzvot, more acts of goodness. A little light pushes away a lot of darkness.

That is the message of the [Chanukah menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah), lit in the window to share the miracle with the world.

## Everyday ways to express Jewish pride

1. **Put up a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah)** on your front door, a clear sign of a Jewish home.
2. **Light Shabbat candles** every Friday before sunset.
3. **Wear a [kippah](/learn/kippah)** or a Jewish star, or keep a Jewish book on your desk.
4. **Use your Hebrew name** and teach your children theirs.
5. **Celebrate Jewish holidays** at home and in community, and invite friends.
6. **Learn Jewish history,** from the Exodus to modern times.
7. **Do a mitzvah in public,** such as sharing a holiday with a friend or giving tzedakah openly.
8. **Connect with other Jews.** Community makes pride natural.

## Raising proud Jewish children

Children learn pride from what they see. When they watch their parents light candles, celebrate holidays and speak positively about being Jewish, they absorb it. Jewish schooling, Hebrew school and Jewish friends give them a sense of belonging. Answer their questions honestly and help them feel that being Jewish is something to treasure.

## Common questions

### Can I be proud to be Jewish if I am not religious?
Yes. Every Jew can be proud of being Jewish. Pride is often the first step toward exploring more, at your own pace.

### What if I feel uncomfortable showing I am Jewish?
That is understandable, especially in hard times. Start in ways that feel right to you, such as a mezuzah or Shabbat candles at home, and let your confidence grow. If you ever feel unsafe, reach out to us.

### My family was not very Jewish. Where do I start?
Start with one thing: a Shabbat dinner, a holiday, or a conversation. See our page on [Jewish identity](/help/jewish-identity) or call the Rabbi.

### How can I help my children deal with antisemitism?
Listen to them, take their experiences seriously, and help them feel proud and supported. Give them positive Jewish experiences, so being Jewish is linked with joy, not only with challenges.
