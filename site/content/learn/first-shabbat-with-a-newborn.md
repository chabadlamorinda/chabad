Your first Shabbat with a newborn can be a real gift of rest: light candles, keep the meal simple, accept help, and ask the Rabbi about anything specific to the new mother's health. A baby's needs and a mother's health always come before any stringency, and Shabbat is meant to bring peace, not stress.

For new parents in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, here is a realistic way to approach it, drawing on Jewish guidance on Shabbat and childbirth. This guide is general, and personal questions are for the Rabbi.

## Start small, and keep what you can

You do not need to do everything. A beautiful first Shabbat might be only candles, a short Kiddush over wine or grape juice, a simple meal, and some quiet time together. [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) are the heart of it. Light them, even if you must sit down to rest right after. The mother lights, and if she is not able, ask the Rabbi what to do. Candle lighting times for Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda are on our [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times) page, and Friday night services at Chabad begin at {kabbalat-shabbat} this week.

## The new mother and Shabbat

The Jewish laws about a woman after childbirth are generous. Jewish law, as set out in the Shulchan Aruch, treats a mother in the first days after childbirth as a patient who may be in danger, so Shabbat may be set aside for her needs. After that the rules depend on how she feels and how many days have passed, and later on Shabbat help can be given in other ways. These laws depend on each person's health, so please call the Rabbi, or the Rebbetzin, and do not worry or hesitate if there is any health need. See [pikuach nefesh](/learn/pikuach-nefesh) and [giving birth](/learn/giving-birth-jewish-customs).

## Meals: let people help

The advice for postpartum recovery is to ask friends to bring food and to have one person coordinate. For Shabbat, this is perfect: food prepared in advance means no cooking on Friday. If you would like a Shabbat meal, see [the Shabbat meal form](/forms/shabbat-meal). Friends can also read [bringing meals to new parents](/learn/meals-for-new-parents). More ideas are in [Shabbat meals](/learn/shabbat-meals).

## Baby gear on Shabbat

The laws of baby care on Shabbat cover the practical details, and our guide [babies and toddlers on Shabbat](/learn/babies-and-toddlers-on-shabbat) summarizes them. Briefly:

1. **Bottles and baby food** are prepared before candle lighting.
2. **A stroller** may be used only where there is an eruv. Read about the [eruv](/learn/eruv) and ask the Rabbi where you can go.
3. **Diapers** have their own rules; for example, tapes are not opened for the first time on Shabbat. Ask the Rabbi.
4. **A sick or hurting baby** always comes first, so call the doctor without hesitation.

## Shalom Zachar and Shabbat after the birth

If your baby is a boy, there is a custom of a gathering on the Friday night after his birth, called a [Shalom Zachar](/learn/shalom-zachar). On the Shabbat morning, the father is called to the Torah, and a blessing is said for the mother and baby. For a girl, see [baby naming](/learn/baby-naming).

## Common questions

### Can I keep Shabbat with a newborn?
Yes. Keep the parts you can, such as candles and a simple meal, and ask the Rabbi about everything else. The baby's and mother's health always come first.

### Can a new mother break Shabbat if she needs to?
When health is involved, yes. For the first days after childbirth, Shabbat may be set aside for the mother's needs. After that, ask the Rabbi about her situation.

### What do I do about bottles and formula on Shabbat?
Prepare them before Shabbat. There are Shabbat rules about warming, so read [babies and toddlers on Shabbat](/learn/babies-and-toddlers-on-shabbat) or ask the Rabbi.

### Can I use a stroller on Shabbat?
Only where there is an eruv. See [the eruv guide](/learn/eruv), and ask the Rabbi.

### Who can bring us a Shabbat meal after a birth?
Friends and family can, and our [meal form](/forms/shabbat-meal) is a place to ask. Call 925-384-7242 to hear what is available.

### How long should the first Shabbat meal be?
As long as is comfortable. A short, simple meal is perfectly fine for new parents.
