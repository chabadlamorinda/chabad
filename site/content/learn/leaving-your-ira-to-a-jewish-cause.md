To leave your IRA or 401(k) to a charity such as a synagogue, you fill out a beneficiary designation form with the company that holds the account and name the organization on it. Chabad of Lamorinda accepts gifts from retirement accounts, and a gift like this helps fund Jewish education for children and families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda.

## Why a form and not a will

A retirement account passes to whoever is named on the account's beneficiary form. Chabad.org, describing gifts from retirement plans (IRAs, 401(k)s, 403(b)s and similar accounts), says the steps are simple: get a beneficiary designation form from your plan administrator or custodian, write in the organization and the percentage you choose, and return it. No new will is needed. The [legacy page](/legacy) says the same.

## Why many people choose a retirement account

Chabad.org describes a retirement account as one of the most tax-efficient ways to fund a gift to a nonprofit, which may let you leave other assets, taxed less heavily, to your family. How this applies to you depends on your situation, your family and the law. Please ask your tax advisor and your attorney. This guide gives no tax or legal advice and no figures.

## Steps to name Chabad of Lamorinda

1. **Decide what you wish to do.** You can name a charity for all of an account or for a percentage, and name family for the rest.
2. **Call the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin.** Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski is at 925-384-7242 and Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski at 925-384-7241. They would be honored to hear your wishes.
3. **Ask your plan administrator or custodian for a beneficiary designation form.** Many companies have the form on their website, or you can call them.
4. **Fill in the organization's details.** Chabad of Lamorinda, 3477 Golden Gate Way, Lafayette, CA 94549, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, tax ID 47-4642750.
5. **Return the form and keep a copy.** Check your other beneficiaries too, especially after a marriage, a death or a divorce. Ask your attorney or advisor if you are not sure how your choices fit together.
6. **Let your family know,** and let the Rabbi know if you wish.  He is glad to speak with you privately.

## A gift of values

Jewish tradition cares about what we hand on, and about how we use what we have. A gift from your retirement savings is a way to continue your tzedakah (צְדָקָה, giving that is also justice) after your lifetime. See [Tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah). ## Common questions

### How do I leave my IRA to a charity?
Ask your IRA custodian for a beneficiary designation form, write in the charity's name and the percentage you choose, and return it. As Chabad.org notes, no new will is needed.

### Can I name Chabad of Lamorinda as an IRA or 401(k) beneficiary?
Yes. Chabad of Lamorinda accepts gifts from retirement accounts. Call Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski at 925-384-7242 or Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski at 925-384-7241, and give your plan company the details on the [legacy page](/legacy).

### Why do people name a charity on a retirement account?
Chabad.org describes a retirement account as one of the most tax-efficient ways to fund a gift to a nonprofit. Ask your tax advisor how this applies to you.

### Can I name both my children and a charity?
Yes. A beneficiary form usually lets you give each person or organization a percentage. Ask your plan company or advisor.

### Can I change the beneficiary later?
Usually you can, by sending a new form. Ask your plan company.

### Does the form replace my will?
No. The form covers that account only, and works alongside your will or trust. See [How to Leave Money to a Synagogue in Your Will](/learn/leaving-chabad-in-your-will).
