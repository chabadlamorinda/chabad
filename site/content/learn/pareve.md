Pareve (also spelled parve, פַּרְוֶה) means a food that is neither meat nor dairy, so it can be eaten with either one. Fruits, vegetables, grains, eggs, fish and plain nuts are pareve, as long as they are prepared without any meat or dairy.

## What is pareve?

The word is Yiddish, and it is also used in Hebrew. A pareve food is the neutral third category in a kosher kitchen, next to [meat and milk](/learn/meat-and-milk). Because Jewish law keeps meat and dairy apart, pareve foods are the bridge: a salad, a potato, rice, or a plain cake can sit on the table at a meat meal or a dairy one.

Common pareve foods:

- Fruits, vegetables, beans, rice, pasta and plain bread
- Eggs (eggs are pareve even though they come from an animal, just as milk is not meat)
- Fish with fins and scales (see [kosher fish](/learn/kosher-fish))
- Nuts, oil, sugar, coffee, tea, water
- Dishes, pots and utensils that are used for neither meat nor dairy are called pareve too

## Are eggs pareve? What about fish?

Yes, eggs are pareve, but check each egg for a blood spot before using it, and set aside any egg that has one. Fish is also pareve, but the Sages forbade eating it together with meat out of a health concern, so fish is served as its own course. Some families also have a custom not to eat fish with milk. Ask the Rabbi about your family's custom.

## How pareve food can become meat or dairy

A pareve food does not stay pareve in every situation. It becomes meat or dairy when it is cooked with them, or in their pots:

- Cooked vegetables in a meat pot become meat.
- Rice in a dairy pot becomes dairy.
- Pareve bread baked with milk or rendered fat is no longer pareve.

Note that how long ago a pot was used, and whether it was clean, affects the details. These rules are detailed, so ask the Rabbi about a particular pot.

## Pareve labels and "DE"

A "pareve" label on a package is only as good as the certification behind it. Two points to know:

1. **"Pareve" only counts with a kosher symbol.** Read about [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols).
2. **"DE" means dairy equipment.** A pareve product made on the same equipment as dairy products may say "DE" or "Pareve DE." It has no dairy ingredients, but the equipment was dairy. Many families eat these foods, others avoid them with meat, so ask the Rabbi what your family should do.

Also remember that a pareve food that looks like meat or dairy, such as almond milk or a soy burger, can confuse guests. Chabad.org suggests marking it, such as leaving the container on the table, so that people know it is pareve.

## A pareve dish for every table

Pareve baking is especially useful after a meat meal, when a dairy dessert is not allowed. A fruit salad, sorbet, a plain sponge cake or a compote are classics. Keeping some pareve dishes on hand also makes it easy to host a mixed meal, or to serve a guest who keeps kosher. See [kosher kitchen](/learn/kosher-kitchen) for how to set one up.

## Common questions

### What does pareve mean?
Pareve means neither meat nor dairy. A pareve food can be eaten with either meat or dairy foods.

### Are eggs pareve?
Yes. Eggs are pareve, though each egg should be checked for a blood spot before use.

### Is fish pareve?
Fish is pareve, but it is not eaten together with meat. It may be eaten with dairy, though some have a custom not to.

### What does "DE" on a label mean?
"DE" means the food was made on dairy equipment. It has no dairy ingredients, but ask the Rabbi whether to eat it with meat.

### Can pareve food become dairy or meat?
Yes. A pareve food cooked in a meat pot becomes meat, and one cooked in a dairy pot becomes dairy.

### Can I eat pareve dessert after a meat meal?
Yes, if the dessert is truly pareve and its ingredients and equipment have kosher certification. That is why so many families keep pareve desserts on hand.
