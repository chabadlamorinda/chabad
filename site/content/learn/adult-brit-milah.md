Yes, a Jewish man or older boy who never had a brit milah (בְּרִית מִילָה, the covenant of circumcision) can still have one, at any age. Chabad teaches that it is never too late, and that it is done by an experienced mohel, usually with a local anesthetic and proper medical care.

This guide is for men and boys in Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda and across the East Bay and the Bay Area, including many who grew up in the former Soviet Union where it was dangerous or impossible. Whatever your story, you are welcome, and a call to the Rabbi is private.

## Why it still matters

Brit milah is the very first mitzvah given to Abraham, and Jews have kept it for nearly four thousand years. Tradition teaches that it is a sign of the covenant between G-d and the Jewish people, built into the body itself. It is a mitzvah done once in a lifetime, and it is never too late to do it.

Many men who have not had one tell us they felt that "something was missing." Others carry old embarrassment from school, a gym or a pool. Some are fathers who want to do it for themselves before their sons or in memory of a parent who could not. All of these are good reasons, and none requires an explanation.

## Who can have an adult brit milah

- **A Jewish-born man or boy who was never circumcised.** This includes those whose parents were unable to do it in the Soviet Union or elsewhere.
- **A man who was circumcised medically** but not in a Jewish way. In this case a short, simple procedure called *hatafat dam brit* may be needed. Ask the Rabbi; the answer depends on the details.
- **A man converting to Judaism.** See [converting to Judaism](/learn/converting-to-judaism).
- **A boy past his eighth day.** An older boy can still have a brit milah, and the Rabbi will guide the family.

If you are unsure whether you are Jewish under Jewish law, read [who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew) or ask the Rabbi first. This is a quiet conversation, not a test.

## How it is done today

An adult brit milah is a medical procedure and a mitzvah together. Chabad.org explains that adult circumcisions are more complex than infant ones, so you want a mohel with real experience with adults. A local anesthetic is typically used, and a licensed physician is often involved.

Recovery takes some time, so plan a few days of rest, and follow the doctor's and mohel's instructions. Your own doctor should be part of the decision, especially if you have a medical condition or take blood thinners.

A rabbi can connect you to an experienced mohel. Call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 and we will help you find the right person, quietly.

## Privacy

This is among the most personal things a person can decide. A call to the Rabbi is a confidential conversation. You do not need to tell your family, your friends or the community. If you wish to include them later, it is your choice. The same is true for a mother calling about her son.

## After the brit

Many men receive a Hebrew name, put on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) for the first time, and share a small meal with a few close people. It can be one of the proudest days of a man's life. ## Common questions

### Can an adult Jewish man still have a brit milah?
Yes. An adult can have a brit milah at any age, and Chabad teaches that it is never too late. It is performed by an experienced mohel, and the Rabbi can help you find one.

### Is a bris for an adult painful?
A local anesthetic is typically used to keep pain low. Your mohel and doctor will explain the procedure and recovery beforehand. Ask them every question you have.

### I was circumcised in a hospital as a baby. Do I need a bris?
Possibly a short procedure called *hatafat dam brit*, depending on the details. Ask the Rabbi privately, since the answer depends on your situation.

### I am from the former Soviet Union and never had a bris. Is it still possible?
Yes. Many Jewish men from the former Soviet Union have a brit milah as adults, and it is a moving step. Call 925-384-7242; you are very welcome.

### Is it confidential?
Yes. A call to the Rabbi is private, and you decide who knows.

### Can a boy older than eight days have a bris?
Yes. An older boy can still have a brit milah. For a baby, see [brit milah](/learn/brit-milah). For an older boy, speak to the Rabbi so the family can plan together.
