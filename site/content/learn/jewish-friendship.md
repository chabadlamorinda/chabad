"Acquire for yourself a friend" (Pirkei Avot 1:6). Judaism takes friendship seriously. A good friend is not a luxury; it is one of the main ways we grow, keep our spirits up and serve G-d. Here is what Jewish tradition teaches about finding, being and keeping a good friend.

## "Acquire" a friend

The Mishnah says "make for yourself a teacher, and acquire for yourself a friend." Why "acquire," a word used for buying? The commentaries explain that a true friend is worth paying for: with time, patience, giving in on small things, and sometimes literally spending money to be together. Friendship does not happen by itself. We invest in it.

## David and Jonathan

The Torah's great example of friendship is David and Jonathan, the son of King Saul. Jonathan stood to inherit the throne, yet he loved David "as his own soul" and protected him, even against his own father. Pirkei Avot calls this "a love that does not depend on anything": it was not about what either could get from the other, and so it lasted forever.

## Love your fellow as yourself

"Love your fellow as yourself" is, in the words of Rabbi Akiva, a great principle of the whole Torah. Hillel put it simply: "What is hateful to you, do not do to your friend." The Baal Shem Tov taught that love of a fellow Jew is the first step to love of G-d. Friendship is where we practice it every day.

## What makes a good friend, the Jewish way

- **Be honest, kindly.** "Love rebuke," say the Sages: a real friend tells you the truth, gently and at the right time.
- **Guard your tongue.** Do not speak badly about your friend, and do not let others do it. See [lashon hara](/learn/lashon-hara).
- **Judge favorably.** "Judge every person favorably": when a friend lets you down, assume the best first.
- **Show up.** Visit when they are sick, comfort them when they mourn, and dance at their simchas.
- **Learn together.** Jewish learning is done in pairs, "chavruta," because two friends sharpen each other. "Either a study partner or death," says the Talmud, about how much we need each other.
- **Forgive and make peace.** Every friendship has hurts. See [forgiveness and making peace](/learn/forgiveness-and-making-peace).

## Choosing friends

"Stay far from a bad neighbor, and do not befriend a wicked person," says Pirkei Avot. The people we spend time with shape who we become. Look for friends who make you better: kinder, more honest, more joyful.

## Common questions

### Does Judaism say anything about online friends?
The same values apply: kindness, honesty and guarding your words. But real presence matters; try to turn online friendships into real conversations and shared moments.

### How do I make Jewish friends as an adult in Lamorinda?
Come to a Shabbat dinner, a class or a holiday event at Chabad of Lamorinda. Everyone is welcome, no membership needed. See [upcoming events](/events).

### What if a friendship has become harmful?
Judaism values friendship, but not at the cost of your well-being. It is right to step back from a relationship that hurts you. Talk privately with the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin.
