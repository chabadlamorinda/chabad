Sheva Brachot means "seven blessings." They are recited under the chuppah at a Jewish wedding and again at festive meals held in honor of the bride and groom during the week after the wedding.

## The seven blessings

The blessings move from creation, to the creation of humanity, to the joy of the couple and the future redemption of Jerusalem. In brief:

1. **Wine:** praising G-d, who creates the fruit of the vine.
2. **Creation:** "who created all things for His glory."
3. **Humanity:** "who formed man."
4. **The image of G-d:** praising G-d, who formed man in His image and made from him an everlasting structure.
5. **Zion:** a prayer that Jerusalem will rejoice when her children are gathered back to her in joy.
6. **The couple:** asking G-d to bring great joy to these "loving companions," like the joy of Adam and Eve in the Garden of Eden.
7. **Joy:** praising G-d, who created joy and gladness, groom and bride, love, harmony, peace and friendship, and praying that the sounds of joy will soon be heard again in the streets of Jerusalem.

Under the chuppah, the blessing on wine comes first. At the meals during the week, it is said last.

## The week of Sheva Brachot

After the wedding, the couple enjoys seven days of celebration, beginning with the wedding itself. Family and friends host festive meals in their honor, and at each meal the seven blessings are said again. It is a week for the new couple to focus on each other and to be surrounded by love and joy.

For the blessings to be said at a meal, two things are needed:

- **A minyan:** ten Jewish men, including the groom, with at least some of them eating bread at the meal.
- **A new face (panim chadashot):** at least one guest who was not at the wedding meal or an earlier Sheva Brachot, whose presence adds new joy. On Shabbat, the holiness of the day itself counts as the "new face."

## How the blessings are said at a meal

1. The meal begins with washing for bread.
2. At the end, [Birkat Hamazon](/learn/birkat-hamazon) (Grace after Meals) is led over a cup of wine, with a special opening that mentions the joy of the couple.
3. A second cup of wine is poured. Guests are honored with saying the seven blessings over it, one by one, with the blessing on wine last.
4. The wine from the two cups is mixed together and poured into a third cup, then shared. The bride and groom drink, and guests often enjoy a sip too.

Being honored with one of the blessings is a sweet way to include close friends and family.

## Customs for the week

The bride and groom are treated like royalty. They are often accompanied when they go out, and many couples spend the week celebrating with different hosts. Words of Torah, songs and heartfelt toasts fill the meals.

## Common questions

### What if there is no minyan at the meal?
The full seven blessings need a minyan. When there is no minyan, or no new face, the rules change, and sometimes only part of the blessings is said. Ask the Rabbi before the meal so you know what to do.

### Do Sheva Brachot apply if either partner was married before?
If both bride and groom were previously married, the blessings are generally said only on the wedding day. Ask the Rabbi about your situation.

### Who can host a Sheva Brachot?
Anyone: parents, relatives or friends. It can be a simple dinner at home or a larger event, as long as it is a meal with bread and meets the requirements above.

### Can women say the blessings?
The custom is that the seven blessings are recited by men, since they require a minyan. Women play a central role in hosting, speaking and celebrating the couple.
