Yes, renters put up a mezuzah too: outside Israel the obligation begins after 30 days in a rented home, but it is a widespread Chabad practice to put one up right away. Dorm rooms where you sleep and live need a mezuzah as well, while offices and businesses have their own rules, so ask the Rabbi about your specific space.

## Do I need a mezuzah if I rent?

A mezuzah (מְזוּזָה) is the handwritten scroll on the doorpost that marks a Jewish home (see [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah)). The mitzvah applies to anyone who lives in a home, whether they own it or rent it. Outside Israel, the Sages say a renter becomes obligated after 30 days, since the stay might still be temporary. Chabad custom, however, is to affix mezuzahs as soon as you move in, and the Rebbe encouraged doing it right away. The usual Chabad practice is to put them up right away without a blessing; after 30 days one mezuzah is taken down and put back with the blessing. Ask the Rabbi about the blessing for your home.

If you rent in Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda or anywhere in the East Bay, your landlord may have rules about nails or screws. Strong double-sided tape is a fine solution, and a landlord's concern should not stop you. See [choosing a mezuzah](/learn/choosing-a-mezuzah) and [moving to Lamorinda](/learn/moving-to-lamorinda).

## Which rooms need a mezuzah?

Every doorway of a room used for living gets a mezuzah: the front door, living and dining rooms, bedrooms, kitchen, a home office and a playroom. Bathrooms do not, and some very small rooms, closets and storage spaces are exempt. Garages, porches and unusual doorways vary: ask the Rabbi. Remember to check that each mezuzah is in the right place (see the steps in [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah)).

## Offices and businesses

The rules for a workplace differ from those of a home, because a mezuzah is primarily for places where people live. Many Jewish business owners put mezuzahs on their office and shop doors anyway, and Chabad encourages it as a source of blessing and protection. Whether it is obligatory depends on the type of space. Ask the Rabbi, and he will help you choose which doors.

## College dorms and apartments

A dorm room or student apartment where you live and sleep is a home, so it should have a mezuzah. A dorm room may be only for a school year, and school rules sometimes limit nails: use tape. If your roommate is not Jewish, you can still do it for your side of the room. See [Jewish college students](/learn/jewish-college-students). A mezuzah is also a lovely gift for a student heading off to school.

## Moving out: what to do with your mezuzahs

1. **Do not just throw it out.** A mezuzah scroll is holy.
2. **If a Jew will live there next,** it is customary to leave the mezuzahs for them. Tell them.
3. **If you do not know, or the next person is not Jewish,** take your mezuzahs with you, carefully, and put them on your new doors.
4. **Do not leave empty cases** or half-removed pieces.

When you move in: check mezuzahs you find on the doors. A previous owner may have been Jewish, but the scrolls may be old or not kosher. Have them checked (see [checking mezuzahs](/learn/checking-mezuzahs-and-tefillin)), and ask the Rabbi about what to do with any that are damaged.

## Common questions

### Do I need a mezuzah if I rent?
Yes. Outside Israel the obligation starts after 30 days, but Chabad custom is to put them up as soon as you move in.

### Do I need a mezuzah on my office door?
It depends on the space. Many Jews put one up anyway for blessing and protection. Ask the Rabbi about your office.

### Do I need a mezuzah in a college dorm?
Yes, a dorm room where you live and sleep is considered a home, so it should have one.

### Which rooms need a mezuzah?
Rooms used for living, such as bedrooms, the kitchen and the living room. Bathrooms and very small closets do not. Ask about unusual doorways.

### What do I do with my mezuzahs when I move?
If a Jew is moving in, leave them. Otherwise take them with you and put them on your new door. Never throw them out.
