The last days of Passover are a holiday of their own: the seventh day (and the eighth, outside Israel) marks the day the Red Sea split for the Jewish people, and ends with Moshiach's Meal, a festive meal of matzah and four cups of wine introduced by the Baal Shem Tov to look forward to the final redemption. Jewish families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda can spend them in the synagogue and at the table.

## The seventh day: the sea splits

The Exodus did not end when the Jews left Egypt. Pharaoh changed his mind and chased them, and the Jewish people stood at the edge of the Red Sea with the Egyptian army behind them. According to tradition, on the seventh day of Passover the sea split and the people crossed on dry land. Then the water returned and the pursuing army was gone. Our sages tell us that Nachshon ben Aminadav was the first to walk into the water, until it reached his nose, and only then did the sea split. See [the Exodus story](/learn/exodus-story).

On this day the Torah reading includes the Song at the Sea, which Moses and the people sang. The seventh day is a full Yom Tov: no work, festive meals, candle lighting before it starts (see [Shabbat and holiday times](/shabbat-times) for Lafayette). It is also Yom Tov outside Israel on the eighth day. Passover is seven days in Israel and eight elsewhere.

## Why does the festival continue?

Chassidic teaching explains that the first days of Passover are about leaving Egypt: freedom from outside slavery. The last days are about the sea: a deeper freedom, when nature itself yields and the people see that nothing can stand in the way of G-d's plan. The splitting of the sea is also a sign that, in the end, all the world will be redeemed. For that reason the last days of Passover look ahead to Moshiach. See [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach).

## The custom of staying up

Some have a custom to stay up on the night of the seventh day learning Torah and singing, in memory of the night the Israelites crossed the sea. This is a custom, not a requirement: ask the Rabbi what suits you and your family.

## Yizkor and the eighth day

On the last day of Passover (the eighth outside Israel) we recite [Yizkor](/learn/yizkor), the memorial prayer for loved ones who have passed away. Customs differ about who stays in the room, and all are welcome to attend; ask the Rabbi. The Haftarah of the day, from Isaiah, speaks of Moshiach and the age of peace, when the wolf will dwell with the lamb.

## Moshiach's Meal

The Baal Shem Tov, the founder of Chassidism, established a festive meal at the end of the last day of Passover, in the afternoon, as the holiday ends. It is called *Moshiach's Seudah*, Moshiach's Meal, and it is eaten with matzah and four cups of wine, like a second small Seder. Chassidic teaching says that on this day the light of Moshiach shines, so we eat as if Moshiach had come. The Rebbe encouraged every Jew to take part. It is a joyful, singing meal with stories and Torah thoughts, and it is open to everyone. Read about [the Baal Shem Tov](/learn/baal-shem-tov).

## Common questions

### What is the seventh day of Passover?
The seventh day of Passover marks the day the Red Sea split for the Israelites, and it is a full holiday with festive meals and a special Torah reading, the Song at the Sea.

### Why is the seventh day of Passover a holiday?
It marks the splitting of the Red Sea, the climax of the Exodus when the Egyptian army was no more. We celebrate it as a Yom Tov.

### What is Moshiach's Meal?
Moshiach's Meal is a festive meal at the end of the last day of Passover, with matzah and four cups of wine, started by the Baal Shem Tov to celebrate the coming of Moshiach.

### Do you say Yizkor on the last day of Passover?
Yes, Yizkor is recited on the last day of Passover. Many families come to remember loved ones.

### Is it a custom to stay up on the seventh night of Passover?
Some people have this custom, in memory of the crossing of the sea. It is optional; ask the Rabbi.
