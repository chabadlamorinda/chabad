To plan a Jewish wedding, you need a rabbi, a date, a ketubah, two witnesses, a chuppah, a ring and a quiet room for yichud. Chabad.org's wedding checklist covers the items needed for the religious parts of the ceremony, and this guide puts them in the order you will need them.

## 1. Start with the rabbi and the date

- **Call the rabbi first.** In Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda, call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242, ideally several months ahead.
- **Choose the date together.** The Jewish calendar matters when choosing a wedding date, so check with the rabbi before you book a hall. See [Choosing a wedding date](/learn/choosing-a-wedding-date).
- **Think about the engagement.** Some families have the *tenaim* ceremony (see [Tenaim](/learn/tenaim)), and others skip it.

## 2. Prepare yourselves

- **Learning.** Many couples take a few classes: [kallah classes](/learn/kallah-classes) for the bride and chosson classes for the groom.
- **Mikvah.** The bride immerses in a [mikvah](/learn/mikvah) before the wedding; the Rebbetzin can help arrange this privately. Chabad.org adds that it is proper for the groom to immerse on the wedding day.
- **Questions of Jewish status.** If either of you is unsure of Jewish status or has questions about paperwork, speak privately with the Rabbi.

## 3. The ceremony checklist

From Chabad.org's wedding checklist:

1. **Chuppah.** See [What is a chuppah?](/learn/what-is-a-chuppah).
2. **Wedding ring.** A plain band the groom owns; see [The Jewish wedding ring](/learn/jewish-wedding-ring).
3. **Ketubah.** The officiating rabbi normally provides a standard one. If you want a designer ketubah, have the rabbi approve the text first. See [The ketubah](/learn/ketubah).
4. **Yichud room and refreshments.** A private room, with food arranged with the caterer; see [Yichud](/learn/yichud).
5. **Kosher wine and a cup.** Preferably white, and best mevushal. By Chabad custom, the glass cup used for the wine is the same one the groom later breaks.
6. **Glass and cloth.** A glass for the groom to break, with a cloth to wrap it.
7. **Veil.** For the bride, lowered at the bedeken.
8. **Kittel.** The white garment Ashkenazi grooms wear under the chuppah.
9. **Candles and matches.** For those who escort the bride and groom to the chuppah.
10. **Mechitzah.** A divider between the men's and women's dancing circles, if you follow that custom.
11. **Printed blessings and Grace after Meals booklets** for guests.
12. **Chabad weddings:** the text of the Rebbe's letter, which is read beneath the chuppah.

Only if you do the tenaim: the document, and a plate with a cloth.

## 4. Choose your honorees

Chabad.org's honoree list suggests roles: an emcee, a cantor, two witnesses for the betrothal, a reader of the ketubah and six men to recite the seven blessings under the chuppah, plus a leader of Grace after Meals. The witnesses must be Torah-observant adult Jewish men who are not relatives of the couple or of each other. Ask the Rabbi how to match people to roles.

## 5. The wedding and the week after

The wedding day includes the fast, prayers, rejoicing with the bride and groom, and Grace after Meals with the seven blessings. For a week afterward, friends host [sheva brachot](/learn/sheva-brachot) meals in the couple's honor. For food, ask the Rabbi about kosher arrangements; see also [Kosher catering for a simcha](/learn/kosher-catering-for-a-simcha-in-lamorinda).

## Common questions

### What do you need for a Jewish wedding?

A rabbi, a chuppah, a plain ring owned by the groom, a ketubah, two kosher witnesses, wine and a glass to break. The Rabbi helps you arrange each one.

### How far ahead should we start planning a Jewish wedding?

As early as you can; call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242, ideally several months ahead.

### Who provides the ketubah?

The officiating rabbi normally provides a standard ketubah. If you want a designer ketubah, have the rabbi approve the text.

### Who can be a witness at a Jewish wedding?

Torah-observant adult Jewish men who are not related to the couple or to each other.

### Do we need classes before the wedding?

Many couples take kallah and chosson classes. Ask the Rabbi what he recommends.

### Do we have to do tenaim?

No, it is a custom some families follow and others skip. Ask the Rabbi.
