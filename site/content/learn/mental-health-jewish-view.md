Many people, of every age and background, struggle with anxiety, depression, loss or stress. Judaism takes this seriously. Your mind and soul are precious, and caring for them is a mitzvah.

**If you or someone you love is thinking about self-harm or suicide, call or text 988 (the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline) now, or call 911.**

## Seeking help is a Jewish value

The Torah commands us to "guard your souls very carefully," and Jewish law treats a danger to life, including mental illness, with the utmost seriousness. Going to a good therapist, psychiatrist or doctor is not a lack of faith; it is doing your part, as Judaism teaches for any illness. Medication prescribed by a doctor is used like any other medicine.

## Faith and therapy together

Professional help and spiritual strength work best together:

- **[Bitachon](/learn/bitachon)**, trust in G-d, helps us let go of what is not in our hands.
- **[Simcha](/learn/simcha)**, joy, is a core Chassidic teaching: we choose to focus on the good, even in small steps.
- **[Tehillim](/learn/tehillim)**, the Psalms, have comforted Jews in pain for 3,000 years.
- **Routine and community:** a weekly Shabbat, a class or a regular visit can give structure and connection.

Chassidus teaches that every person has a G-dly soul that is never damaged, whatever the person is going through. Your worth does not depend on your mood or your productivity.

## Helping someone you love

- Listen without judging, and tell them you care.
- Encourage them to see a professional, and offer to help find one or come along.
- Check in regularly. A call, a meal or a Shabbat invitation can mean a great deal.
- Take care of yourself as well.

## Common questions

### Is it a lack of faith to take medication for depression?
No. Using medicine that a doctor prescribes is what Jewish law asks of us for any illness.

### Can the Rabbi be my therapist?
The Rabbi is glad to listen, support and pray with you, and to help you find a good professional. For treatment, see a licensed therapist or doctor.

### Is what I tell the Rabbi kept private?
Personal conversations with the Rabbi are treated with care and discretion. If someone's life is in danger, getting them help comes first.
