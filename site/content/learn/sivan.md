Sivan (סִיוָן) is the third month of the Jewish calendar, falling in May or June, and it is the month in which the Jewish people received the Torah at Mount Sinai. Shavuot, the festival of the giving of the Torah, is celebrated on the 6th of Sivan (and the 7th outside Israel), at the end of the 49 days of the Omer.

## What does Sivan mean?

Sivan is the third month counting from Nissan. Chabad.org explains that its name, like the other Hebrew month names we use today, was adopted during the Babylonian exile, and that it means "fixed date" or "time." It alludes to Mount Sinai and to the miracles that took place there. You can read how the months work in [the Jewish calendar](/learn/jewish-calendar) and [Nissan](/learn/nissan) and [Iyar](/learn/iyar), the months before it.

## Rosh Chodesh Sivan: arriving at Sinai

On the first day of Sivan, the Jewish people arrived at Mount Sinai and camped there. The Torah uses a singular verb, "vayichan," and the Sages teach that this means "they camped as one person with one heart." That is the lesson of the month: the Torah was given to a people who stood together. Unity came first, and then the Torah. Chabad.org notes this is why Sivan is a good time to work on love and peace between people.

## The days before Shavuot

Between Rosh Chodesh Sivan and Shavuot, the Jews prepared to receive the Torah. The days leading up to Shavuot are a time to prepare for the Torah with extra learning and kindness; ask the Rabbi about the customs of these days. The Omer count, which started on Passover, ends the night before Shavuot. Each of the 49 nights is a step of improving one's character. See [counting the Omer](/learn/counting-the-omer).

## Shavuot: the Torah is given to everyone

Shavuot means "weeks" because it ends seven weeks of counting, and also "oaths," because G-d and the Jewish people pledged themselves to each other. The Sages compare the giving of the Torah to a wedding. On Shavuot morning the [Ten Commandments](/learn/ten-commandments) are read in the synagogue, and the Rebbe asked that every Jew, including every child, come to hear them. Many Jewish families stay up learning on the first night; see [Shavuot night learning](/learn/shavuot-night-learning). We also eat dairy foods, as explained in [Shavuot dairy foods](/learn/shavuot-dairy-foods), and read the story of Ruth, in [the Book of Ruth](/learn/book-of-ruth). For the full holiday, see [Shavuot for beginners](/learn/shavuot-for-beginners) and the [Shavuot holiday page](/holidays/shavuot).

The Torah was given to all Jews, men, women and children, at the same time. That is why every Jew, whatever his or her background, is welcome to stand at Sinai again every year.

## Another day in Sivan

The Baal Shem Tov, the founder of the Chassidic movement, passed away on the 6th of Sivan, the day of Shavuot, in 1760, according to Chabad.org.

## Common questions

### What is the month of Sivan?
Sivan is the third month of the Jewish calendar, falling in May or June. It is the month the Torah was given at Mount Sinai, and Shavuot is celebrated in it.

### What happened on Rosh Chodesh Sivan?
The Jewish people arrived at Mount Sinai and camped there. The Torah uses a singular verb to show they were united, as one person.

### What does Sivan mean?
Sivan means "fixed date" or "time," a name adopted in the Babylonian exile. It is thought to allude to Mount Sinai and the miracles there.

### When is Shavuot?
Shavuot is on the 6th of Sivan, and the 7th as well outside Israel. See the [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays) page for the exact dates this year.

### Why is Sivan the month of Torah?
Because the Torah was given at Sinai in this month. Every year we receive it again, and Chabad teaches that we can renew our commitment.
