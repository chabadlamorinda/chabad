Everyone gets angry sometimes. Judaism does not pretend otherwise. But it takes anger very seriously, because a few angry moments can undo years of love, and because anger changes who we are while it lasts.

## "Like worshipping idols"

The Talmud (Shabbat 105b) says that a person who breaks things in anger should be seen as one who worships idols. Why so strong? The Alter Rebbe explains that when a person is angry, they forget that what happened came from G-d. In that moment, "my" pride and "my" plans become the center of the world, which is a kind of idolatry.

The sages add that when a person gets angry, if he is wise, his wisdom leaves him. Even Moses, after becoming angry, forgot a law he knew (Numbers 31). And they taught that a person's true character shows in three things: "his cup, his pocket and his anger" (Eruvin 65b), meaning how he drinks, how he spends, and how he handles anger.

## Maimonides: the middle path, with one exception

[Maimonides](/learn/maimonides) teaches that for most traits we should walk the middle path: not stingy and not wasteful, not timid and not reckless. But for **anger and pride** he says the middle is not enough. We should go far away from them. A person may sometimes show anger outwardly, for example to correct a child, but should stay calm inside.

## Practical ways to calm down

1. **Pause before you speak.** Count to ten, take a few slow breaths, or leave the room for a few minutes.
2. **Lower your voice.** It is hard to stay furious while speaking softly. Nachmanides wrote to his son to speak gently to everyone, at all times.
3. **Remember who is in charge.** Things go wrong, but G-d runs the world. See [bitachon](/learn/bitachon) and [hashgacha pratit](/learn/hashgacha-pratit).
4. **Decide in advance.** Know your triggers (tired, hungry, rushed) and plan for them.
5. **Sleep on it.** Do not send the email or make the decision while angry.
6. **Learn about humility.** Most anger comes from feeling slighted. See [humility](/learn/humility).

## When you have already lost your temper

Apologize soon and specifically, without "but." Repair any harm. Then do not stay stuck in guilt: [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah) is always possible. Our guide to [forgiveness and making peace](/learn/forgiveness-and-making-peace) explains how.

## Parents and anger

Children learn how to handle anger by watching us. Correct with a calm voice, set clear limits, and if you raised your voice, apologize to your child too. That teaches more than any lecture. See [Jewish parenting](/learn/jewish-parenting).

If anger ever turns into threats, hitting or fear in the home, get help right away. That is never acceptable, and support is available. Call the Rabbi in confidence.

## Common questions

### Is anger always wrong?
Feeling upset is human. The danger is acting from anger. Judaism asks us to keep anger far away and never let it decide what we say or do.

### What about righteous anger at injustice?
Use the energy to act wisely, not to lash out. Maimonides allows outward sternness when needed, while staying calm inside.

### How do I stop yelling at my kids?
Start small: notice the moment before you yell, pause, and speak softly. Apologize when you slip. The Rebbetzin is happy to talk with parents.

### Is there a prayer for anger?
Many people say a chapter of [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) when they feel tension rising. Before bed, the [bedtime Shema](/learn/bedtime-shema) includes forgiving anyone who hurt us.
