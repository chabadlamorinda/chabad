Rabbi Yossy Goldman writes that the one thing Jews have shared in every place and every era is the Torah, with its commandments and its way of life. Great empires rose and vanished, but the Jewish people, small and scattered, have kept their beliefs and practices alive. According to this Chabad teaching, survival comes from living Torah, not from sentiment.

## What the survival of the Jewish people looks like

Goldman asks a question that many people ask: how have the Jews survived when civilizations like Egypt, Babylon, Greece, Rome and Persia have disappeared? He notes that Jews rebuilt Jewish life and communities even after the Holocaust, and calls that one of the great miracles of our age. The basic answer, he says, is that G-d will not allow the Jewish people to disappear. But he asks what the human part of the story is.

## What it is not

Goldman looks at three common guesses and shows why none of them is the whole answer.

- **A homeland.** The Land of Israel is the eternal homeland and it is prayed for every day. But Jews have lived outside the land for longer than in it.
- **A shared language.** Hebrew is the language of the prayerbook, but most Jews do not speak it. Over the centuries Jews spoke Aramaic, Greek, Arabic, Yiddish, Ladino and now English.
- **A shared culture.** Foods, music and customs differ widely between Sephardic and Ashkenazi communities and from place to place. Jews took on local styles wherever they lived.

## What it is: Torah

The one thing Jews have shared everywhere, from ancient Babylon to Minsk, Madrid, Johannesburg and San Francisco, is the Torah and its way of life. That is a defined system of values and practices, not a vague feeling of being Jewish. Goldman observes that where Jewish communities dropped Torah traditions, assimilation followed quickly and those communities did not survive.

## What this means for each family

Goldman's conclusion is practical. G-d is the ultimate source of Jewish survival, but there is no magic. Being born Jewish does not by itself guarantee that the chain continues. It continues through a concrete commitment to three things:

1. **Studying Torah.** See [How to Start Learning Torah](/learn/how-to-start-learning-torah).
2. **Teaching it to our children.** See [choosing Jewish education](/learn/choosing-jewish-education) and our [Hebrew school](/programs/hebrew-school).
3. **Keeping its eternal practices,** like [Shabbat](/learn/shabbat-in-lamorinda), kosher and the mezuzah.

Every Jewish family in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda who lights Shabbat candles or tells a child the story of Passover is a link in this chain.

## Common questions

### How did the Jewish people survive for 3,000 years?
The traditional answer is that G-d preserves the Jewish people, and that the human key is Torah: studying it, teaching it to children and keeping its practices. Where communities dropped Torah, they were absorbed by assimilation.

### Is it a homeland or a language that kept the Jews together?
Not primarily. Jews have spent longer outside the land of Israel than in it, and have spoken many different languages. The Torah is the one thing shared across all places and times.

### Why do Jews study Torah and teach it to children?
Because this is how the Torah's way of life continues from generation to generation. Jewish tradition says survival requires a concrete commitment to Torah study, teaching children and its practices.

### Does being born Jewish guarantee that Jewish life continues?
No. A person is Jewish from birth, but Goldman says birth alone does not guarantee survival. The chain continues only when people live and pass on the Torah.
