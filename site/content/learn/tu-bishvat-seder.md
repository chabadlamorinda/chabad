A Tu BiShvat seder is a festive gathering on the 15th of the Hebrew month of Shevat, the New Year for Trees, where we eat a variety of fruits, especially those for which the Land of Israel is praised, and say blessings over them. It is a simple, joyful way to thank G-d for His gifts and to learn about the trees of Israel.

## What Tu BiShvat is

Tu BiShvat is one of the four "new years" mentioned in the Mishnah. It marks the new year for trees in Jewish law, used to count the age of trees and the tithes on their fruit. Today it is celebrated by eating fruit, especially fruits of the Land of Israel.

The Torah compares a person to a tree. Like a tree, we need deep roots in faith, a strong trunk of good habits, and branches that produce good fruit: our mitzvot and kind deeds.

## Where the seder comes from

The Kabbalists of Safed, about 400 years ago, created a structured order for Tu BiShvat with many kinds of fruit, readings from the Torah and Zohar, and four cups of wine ranging from white to red. This "Tu BiShvat seder" spread across many communities.

In Chabad, the main custom is simpler: to eat fruit, especially from the seven species, and to say the blessings with care. A structured seder is a lovely way to bring family and friends together and learn.

## The seven species

The Torah praises the Land of Israel as a land of wheat, barley, grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives and dates (honey). Five of these are fruits, and they are the stars of the Tu BiShvat table:

- Olives
- Dates
- Grapes
- Figs
- Pomegranates

Wheat and barley can be included as cake, crackers or bread.

## How to hold a Tu BiShvat seder, step by step

1. **Set the table** with a range of fruits: the seven species, fruits with hard shells (like almonds), fruits with inedible pits (like dates), and fully edible fruits (like figs).
2. **Pick a new fruit** that no one has eaten this season, so you can say [Shehecheyanu](/learn/shehecheyanu).
3. **Say the blessing on fruit:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, borei pri ha'etz. ("Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who creates the fruit of the tree.") Say it over one of the seven species if possible, preferring olives, then dates, grapes, figs and pomegranates, following the order of the verse.
4. **Say Shehecheyanu** over the new fruit, if you have one.
5. **Share and learn.** Read the verses about the seven species, talk about the person as a tree, or tell stories.
6. **Add wine or grape juice**, with the blessing borei pri hagafen, if you like the four-cup custom.
7. **Say the after-blessing.** For grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives or dates, and for wine and grain foods, say Al Ha'etz or the combined Al Hamichyah. For other fruits, say Borei Nefashot. See our [blessings guide](/learn/blessings).

## Customs

- Many eat carob (bokser), which grew in Israel and sustained Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai.
- Many pray for a beautiful etrog for the coming Sukkot.
- Some plant trees in Israel or care for a garden.
- Tachanun, the penitential prayer, is not said.

## Common questions

### When is Tu BiShvat?
It falls on the 15th of Shevat, usually in January or February. Check our [Tu BiShvat page](/holidays/tu-bishvat) or the [Hebrew date tool](/tools/hebrew-date).

### Do I need to say a blessing for every fruit?
One borei pri ha'etz covers all tree fruits on the table if you have them in mind. Vegetables and other foods have their own blessings.

### Can I do a Tu BiShvat seder with kids?
Yes, it is perfect for children. Let them sort fruits by type, guess fruits by taste with eyes closed, and say the blessings.

### Is the seder required?
No. It is a beautiful custom, not an obligation. Eating fruit with the blessings is the heart of the day.
