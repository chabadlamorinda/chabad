The Book of Jonah tells how the prophet Jonah tried to run away from G-d's mission, was swallowed by a great fish, and finally warned the city of Nineveh, whose people repented and were spared. It is read as the haftarah (the reading from the Prophets) at the afternoon service of Yom Kippur because it shows that no one can escape accountability, and that sincere teshuvah always brings G-d's mercy.

## The story in brief

1. **The mission.** G-d tells Jonah, a Jewish prophet, to go to Nineveh, a great non-Jewish city, and warn its people to change their ways or face destruction.
2. **The flight.** Jonah instead boards a ship to flee "from before G-d."
3. **The storm.** A violent storm threatens the ship. The sailors realize Jonah is the cause, and at his word they throw him overboard, and the sea calms.
4. **The fish.** G-d prepares a large fish that swallows Jonah. For three days he prays from inside it, and then the fish releases him safely onto dry land.
5. **Nineveh.** G-d tells him again to go to Nineveh. This time he goes. The king orders everyone to fast and repent, and the city is spared.
6. **The plant.** Jonah sits outside the city and grieves. G-d makes a plant grow to shade him, then sends a worm and the plant withers. When Jonah is upset about the plant, G-d asks him: should I not care about Nineveh, with all its people and animals?

Rashi, the classic commentator, explains why Jonah fled: he feared that if the people of Nineveh repented so quickly, it would make Israel, who did not listen to their own prophets, look bad by comparison.

## Why we read it on Yom Kippur

Chabad.org gives two main reasons.

- **You cannot escape accountability.** Jonah tried to flee from G-d and could not. We also cannot escape responsibility for what we have done, and Yom Kippur is the day we face it honestly.
- **Repentance brings mercy.** G-d had decreed Nineveh's destruction, and yet when its people truly changed, He spared them. No matter what a person's past, [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah) is available.

The key lesson: accountability is unavoidable, and so is forgiveness for anyone who sincerely returns. The book is read at Minchah, the afternoon service, as the day moves toward [Neilah](/learn/neilah), the closing prayer.

## Lessons for our own lives

- **You cannot run from your mission.** Every one of us has something only we can do. If we avoid it, life has a way of bringing us back to it.
- **Anyone can do teshuvah.** Even a whole city of strangers changed course in a day. Nobody is too far gone.
- **G-d cares about every person.** The book ends with G-d's compassion for the people of Nineveh, and for its animals. Our own compassion should reach beyond our own circle.
- **It is never too late to say "I'll go."** Jonah got a second chance. So do we.

## A retelling for children

Jonah was told to give an important message but ran the other way. A storm came, and a big fish swallowed him. Inside he said he was sorry, and the fish set him free. This time Jonah did what G-d asked. The people listened, said sorry and became better, and G-d forgave them. Ask: "Is there something you were supposed to do but put off? What helps you try again?" ## Common questions

### Why do we read the Book of Jonah on Yom Kippur?
We read it because it teaches two things central to Yom Kippur: that no one can escape accountability for their actions, and that sincere repentance brings G-d's compassion, as it did for Nineveh.

### When is the Book of Jonah read on Yom Kippur?
It is the haftarah of the afternoon (Minchah) service of Yom Kippur.

### What is the story of Jonah and the whale?
G-d sends Jonah to warn Nineveh; Jonah runs away by ship; a storm comes; he is swallowed by a large fish for three days; he prays, is released, and delivers the message; Nineveh repents and is spared.

### Was Jonah swallowed by a whale?
The Hebrew text says a "large fish" prepared by G-d swallowed him. Many people say "whale" in English, but the Bible's word is a fish.

### What is the lesson of the story of Jonah?
That you cannot run from your responsibilities, that anyone can do teshuvah, and that G-d cares about every person.
