The Megillah is the Book of Esther, handwritten on a parchment scroll and read aloud on Purim, once at night and once during the day. Hearing every word of it is one of the four mitzvot of Purim.

## What is the Megillah?

Megillah means "scroll." While there are five scrolls in the Bible, "the Megillah" almost always means Megillat Esther. It tells how Haman, a minister of the Persian king Achashverosh, plotted to destroy all the Jews, and how Queen Esther and her cousin Mordechai saved them.

The name of G-d does not appear even once in the Book of Esther. This teaches that G-d is often hidden behind events that look natural. The miracle of Purim was a miracle "dressed in nature."

## The four mitzvot of Purim

1. **Hearing the Megillah**, at night and in the day.
2. **[Mishloach manot](/learn/mishloach-manot):** sending a gift of at least two ready-to-eat foods to at least one friend.
3. **Matanot la'evyonim:** giving money or food to at least two poor people.
4. **A festive Purim meal** on the day of Purim.

## When the Megillah is read

- **At night:** on the evening that Purim begins, after nightfall.
- **In the day:** the next morning, any time from sunrise until sunset. The day reading is the main one.

Purim falls on the 14th of Adar. In Jerusalem and other ancient walled cities, it is celebrated on the 15th, called Shushan Purim.

## How to hear the Megillah, step by step

1. **Find a reading** from a kosher handwritten scroll. A printed book or a recording does not fulfill the mitzvah.
2. **Listen to the blessings** said by the reader before the reading, and answer "Amen."
3. **Hear every single word.** Missing even one word means you have not fulfilled the mitzvah. Following along in a printed text helps.
4. **Make noise at Haman's name.** It is customary to stamp feet or spin a gragger when Haman's name is read, to "blot out" his memory. The reader waits for quiet before going on.
5. **Listen to the blessing after the reading**, said when the reading is done with a minyan.

## The blessings before the reading

Three blessings are said before the reading: on the mitzvah of reading the Megillah, for the miracles, and Shehecheyanu. In the day reading, Chabad custom is to say Shehecheyanu again, with the other mitzvot of Purim in mind.

The first blessing:

**Hebrew:** בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו, וְצִוָּנוּ עַל מִקְרָא מְגִלָּה

**Transliteration:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu al mikra megillah.

**Translation:** Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us concerning the reading of the Megillah.

## Common questions

### Do women and children need to hear the Megillah?
Yes. Women are fully obligated, since they were also saved in the Purim miracle, and Queen Esther was at the center of it. Children are brought so they can learn and take part.

### Can I hear the Megillah on Zoom or the radio?
No. The mitzvah requires hearing the reading directly from the person reading it. If you cannot get to a reading, contact us and we will try to arrange one for you.

### Why do we fast before Purim?
The Fast of Esther, on the day before Purim, recalls how Esther and the Jewish people fasted and prayed before she approached the king. When Purim falls on Sunday, the fast is moved to the Thursday before.

### What if I miss a word?
If you missed a word, you can read it yourself from a printed text and catch up to the reader, and you still fulfill the mitzvah. Paying close attention makes this rarely necessary.
