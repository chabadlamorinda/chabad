Chanukah is eight days long because, according to the Talmud, the Maccabees found only one small jar of pure oil in the Holy Temple, enough for one day, and it burned for eight. Jewish teachers have asked for centuries why we celebrate all eight days, since the first day's oil was natural, and they gave several beautiful answers.

## The question: why not seven?

If the oil was enough for one day, the miracle was only the seven days after it. So why is the first night a holiday too? This is one of the oldest Chanukah questions, and it appears in the classic commentaries. There are many answers, all retold below in plain words. For the story itself, see [the Chanukah story](/learn/chanukah-story).

## Answer 1: The oil was divided into eight parts

One classic explanation says that the Maccabees poured the oil into the menorah in eight equal portions, one for each night. Each portion was only enough for a short time, yet every night the lamps burned the whole night. On this view, each of the eight nights holds a miracle of its own.

## Answer 2: The jar or the cups stayed full

Another view says the Maccabees lit the menorah with the oil they had, and the next morning the cups were still full. If so, even the first night was already a miracle. A similar view holds that the jar itself refilled each night. Either way, the first night counts.

## Answer 3: Other things happened on the first day

Some teachers say the first day recalls the victory in the war, or the finding of the oil, and the remaining seven days recall the oil. The word Chanukah itself means "dedication," and the first day celebrates the dedication of the Temple altar and its menorah. Others note that the Torah's commandment of the menorah comes right after the eight days of Sukkot, and say that Chanukah's eight days echo that festival, which the Jews had been unable to celebrate during the war.

## Answer 4: Seven and eight

The Maharal of Prague, a famous Jewish thinker, taught that the number seven stands for nature (the seven days of creation), while eight stands for what is above nature. The Chanukah miracle was one that went beyond nature, so it is eight.

## Why we count up, not down

On Chanukah we add a light each night, because in holy things we go up, not down. In the story of the oil, each night shines brighter than the one before. That is also a lesson for life: whatever good you did yesterday, do a little more today. See [lessons of Chanukah](/learn/lessons-of-chanukah) and [night by night](/learn/chanukah-night-by-night).

A word of honesty: these are explanations offered by great Jewish teachers over the centuries, and Jews have not settled on a single one. That is part of the beauty of Chanukah: there is room for every answer, and for your own question.

## Common questions

### Why is Chanukah eight days and not seven?
The jar of oil was enough for one day and lasted eight, so the miracle seems to be seven days long. Jewish teachers offered many answers: the first night was also a miracle, or the first day recalls the victory or the dedication of the Temple, or eight points to what is above nature.

### How long did the oil last in the Chanukah story?
According to the Talmud, one jar of pure oil, enough for one day, burned for eight days until new pure oil could be prepared. Details of how long it took to prepare new oil are explained in different ways by the commentaries.

### Why do we light one more candle each night?
We add a light every night because in matters of holiness we increase and never decrease. This follows the opinion of the school of Hillel, which is the accepted practice.

### Why does the menorah have nine lights if the holiday is eight days?
Eight lights are the mitzvah. The ninth is the shamash, the helper candle, used to light the others. Read [what the shamash is](/learn/what-is-the-shamash).

### When is the right time to light each night?
Shortly after sunset, according to Chabad custom. See [when to light the menorah](/learn/when-to-light-the-menorah) and tonight's candles on [the menorah page](/menorah).

### Is the Chanukah miracle only about oil?
No. The oil is the visible miracle, but Chanukah also celebrates the victory of a few over many, and the survival of Jewish life and Torah. For families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, both parts are worth sharing with children.
