Mi Sheberach is a short prayer, usually said at the Torah, asking G-d to bless a person. It is best known as the prayer for healing, said for someone who is ill, but there are also Mi Sheberach prayers for someone called to the Torah, for a new baby, for the community and more.

## What it means

Mi Sheberach means "He Who blessed." The prayer begins: "He Who blessed our fathers, Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, and our mothers, Sarah, Rebecca, Rachel and Leah, may He bless..." We ask G-d to bless this person just as He blessed our ancestors.

## Why we pray at the Torah

The Torah is open, and the congregation is gathered. The Sages describe this as a time of special Divine favor, when prayers are more readily accepted. That is why it is customary to say Mi Sheberach during the [Torah reading](/learn/torah-reading), often right after an [aliyah](/learn/aliyah).

## The prayer for healing

The Mi Sheberach for the sick asks G-d to grant a refuah shleimah, "a complete recovery," of body and soul. The name of the person is said in Hebrew, along with their mother's Hebrew name, for example "Rivka bat Sarah" or "Yosef ben Leah."

Why the mother's name? King David said, "I am Your servant, the son of Your maidservant" (Psalms 116:16). When asking for mercy, we invoke the mother, whose compassion is boundless and whose Jewish identity defines the child's.

## How to request a Mi Sheberach

1. **Find the person's Hebrew name and their mother's Hebrew name.** If you do not know them, the English name can be used.
2. **Ask the Rabbi or gabbai** to include the name in the Mi Sheberach. Many synagogues keep a list.
3. **Give tzedakah.** It is customary to pledge or give [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) on behalf of the person, adding merit to the prayer.
4. **Keep praying.** Say [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) for the person, and include them in the healing blessing of the Amidah.

## Other kinds of Mi Sheberach

- **For one called to the Torah,** blessing him and his family
- **For a new baby girl,** when she is named
- **For a bar mitzvah boy or a groom**
- **For a woman after childbirth**
- **For the community,** said on Shabbat for all who support the synagogue
- **For those who protect the Jewish people**

## Common questions

### Can I say a Mi Sheberach for someone who is not Jewish?
Yes. We can pray for anyone's recovery. In that case the person's name is used with their mother's name, or simply their name.

### Do I need to be in synagogue to say it?
The formal Mi Sheberach is said at the Torah, but you can pray for someone anywhere and at any time, in any language, from the heart. Visit [/tehillim](/tehillim) to join in prayer.

### How long should a name stay on the list?
Keep the name on the list for as long as the person needs healing. Let the Rabbi know when they recover so you can give thanks.

### Is there a Mi Sheberach for mental or emotional health?
Yes. The prayer asks for healing of body and soul, refuat hanefesh and refuat haguf, which includes emotional and mental well-being.
