Holiday (Yom Tov) candles are lit before sunset on the first night of Rosh Hashanah, Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret, Passover, Shavuot and on Yom Kippur eve, with the blessing "l'hadlik ner shel yom tov" followed by Shehecheyanu. On the second night, candles are lit after nightfall from a flame that was already burning before the holiday began.

## Which holidays have candle lighting?

Candles are lit on Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret, the first and last days of Passover, and Shavuot. They are not lit on Chanukah (when we light the menorah) or Purim. Women and girls traditionally light the candles; ask the Rabbi about a household without one. Find your local time at [Shabbat and candle-lighting times](/shabbat-times) and the dates on the [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays) page.

## When to light

- **First night:** ideally 18 minutes before sunset. They may be lit any time before the holiday meal, but if lit after sunset use a pre-existing flame. If the first night is Friday, light before sunset.
- **Second night:** light after nightfall, from a pre-existing flame. Do not set up candlesticks and candles before nightfall.
- **Second night on Friday:** light before sunset, from a pre-existing flame.
- **First night on Saturday night:** light after nightfall from a pre-existing flame, after Shabbat ends.

## What is a pre-existing flame?

It is a candle or pilot flame that was lit before the holiday began and has been burning since. On Yom Tov we may use an existing flame but never create a new one. A 25-hour or 48-hour candle lit before the holiday is perfect: take your flame from it. Also, never put out a flame: have a place ready to set down the match or candle.

## The blessings

Say the holiday blessing, then Shehecheyanu, after lighting.

1. **Yom Tov** (Passover, Shavuot, Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret): *Baruch atah Adonai Eloheinu melech ha'olam, asher kidishanu b'mitzvotav v'tzivanu l'hadlik ner shel yom tov.*
2. **Rosh Hashanah:** the same, ending *l'hadlik ner shel yom hazikaron.*
3. **Yom Kippur:** ending *l'hadlik ner shel yom hakippurim.*
4. **Yom Tov on Shabbat:** *l'hadlik ner shel Shabbat v'shel yom tov* (on Rosh Hashanah: *shel Shabbat v'shel yom hazikaron*).
5. **Shehecheyanu:** *Baruch atah Adonai Eloheinu melech ha'olam, shehecheyanu v'kiymanu v'higianu lazman hazeh.* It thanks G-d for giving us life and bringing us to this special day.

Shehecheyanu is not said on the last days of Passover. On the second night of Rosh Hashanah, wear a new garment or put a new seasonal fruit on the table, and have it in mind. If you do not have either, say the blessing anyway.

## Yom Tov that begins after Shabbat

When Yom Tov follows Shabbat, wait until nightfall and light from a pre-existing flame. Before lighting, say the blessing *Baruch hamavdil bein kodesh lekodesh*, "blessed is the One who separates the holiness of Shabbat from the holiness of the holiday." A woman who has already said the evening prayers, including Va'todeinu, does not say it. Read [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) for the weekly mitzvah.

## Sukkot

On Sukkot, light the candles inside the sukkah and keep them there through the meal. If wind threatens to extinguish them, place them in a window or doorway where those in the sukkah can see them.

## Common questions

### What is the blessing for lighting Yom Tov candles?
The blessing ends "l'hadlik ner shel yom tov," "to kindle the light of the holiday." It is followed by Shehecheyanu.

### When do you light candles on the second night of Yom Tov?
After nightfall, from a pre-existing flame. Do not prepare the candles before nightfall.

### Can I light a new match on Yom Tov?
No. Use a flame that was lit before the holiday, such as a long-burning candle or a pilot flame.

### Do you say Shehecheyanu on the last days of Passover?
No. Shehecheyanu is not said on the last days of Passover.

### What if Yom Tov begins on Saturday night?
Wait until nightfall after Shabbat ends and light from a pre-existing flame, after the separation blessing.

### Do you light candles on Chanukah or Purim?
No. Holiday candles are not lit on Chanukah or Purim.
