A yahrzeit candle is a memorial candle that burns for about 24 hours, lit in memory of a loved one. It is lit at sundown on the eve of the yahrzeit, the anniversary of the passing on the Hebrew calendar, and on several other days of remembrance during the year.

## Why a candle?

King Solomon wrote, "The soul of man is the candle of G-d" (Proverbs 20:27). Like a flame, the soul is always reaching upward. A burning candle reminds us that the soul of our loved one lives on, and the light we kindle in their memory brings elevation to their soul.

## When to light a memorial candle

- **The yahrzeit:** the anniversary of the passing on the Hebrew date. Since the Jewish day begins at nightfall, light the candle before sunset on the evening before. Use our [yahrzeit calculator](/tools/yahrzeit) to find the date.
- **Yizkor days:** Yom Kippur, Shemini Atzeret, the last day of Passover and the second day of Shavuot, when [Yizkor](/learn/yizkor) is said. The candle is lit on the evening the day begins.
- **Shiva:** a large candle that lasts seven days burns in the shiva home throughout the week of mourning. See our guide to [shiva](/learn/shiva).

## How to light it, step by step

1. **Choose a safe spot** where the candle can burn undisturbed for a full day, on a plate or a fireproof surface, away from curtains and children.
2. **Light it before sunset** on the evening the yahrzeit begins.
3. **Take a quiet moment.** No blessing is said, but many say a few words of prayer, a chapter of [Tehillim](/tehillim), or simply think of the person and what they taught you.
4. **Let it burn out on its own.** Do not blow it out.

## When the yahrzeit falls on Shabbat or a holiday

Fire may not be lit on Shabbat, and on a holiday it may only be lit from an existing flame. So:

- **Shabbat:** light the yahrzeit candle on Friday before you light the Shabbat candles.
- **A holiday:** light it before the holiday begins. On the second night of a holiday, a candle may be lit only from an existing flame, and many prefer to light a candle before the holiday that lasts through both days. Ask the Rabbi about your dates.

## What kind of candle to use

Most people use the 24-hour candles in a glass sold in many supermarkets. Any candle that burns for the full day is fine, and some use an oil lamp. Where an open flame is not allowed, such as in a hospital or care facility, an electric memorial lamp may be used.

## More ways to honor the day

The candle is one part of the yahrzeit. Many also say [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish) with a minyan, give tzedakah, study Torah, sponsor a Kiddush, and do an extra mitzvah in the person's memory. Read more in our guide to the [yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit).

## Common questions

### Is there a blessing for lighting a yahrzeit candle?
No. Lighting a memorial candle is a custom, not a commandment, so no blessing is said. Many say a personal prayer or a chapter of Tehillim instead.

### Can I light one candle for two people?
Many light a separate candle for each person, so if two yahrzeits fall on the same day, they light two candles. Ask the Rabbi if you have a question.

### Can I light a yahrzeit candle for a friend or someone not related to me?
Yes. You may light a memorial candle for anyone whose memory you wish to honor, including a friend, a teacher or a grandparent.

### What do I do with the empty glass?
Once the candle has burned out, the glass can be cleaned and reused or thrown away. It has no holiness of its own.
