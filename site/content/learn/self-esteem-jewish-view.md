In Judaism, healthy self-esteem means knowing that you have real worth and a mission that only you can fulfill, while humility means recognizing that your gifts come from G-d. The two do not conflict: humility is not thinking you are nothing, and self-worth is not thinking you are better than others.

Whether you are a teenager in Orinda, a parent in Moraga or a professional in Lafayette, this teaching can help on a hard day.

## Humility is not low self-worth

Many people think humble means "I am worthless." Chabad.org explains that this is not Jewish humility at all. Seeing yourself as worthless, it teaches, is a kind of ingratitude: it ignores the gifts G-d gave you.

Chabad.org describes three attitudes. Arrogance overstates one's worth. False humility understates it. Both compare oneself to others and come from insecurity. A truly humble person sees his or her whole self, including real abilities and achievements, and does not take credit for them. Read the guide to [humility](/learn/humility).

## The two-pocket teaching

Rabbi Simcha Bunim of Peshischa, a Chassidic master, kept two slips of paper, one in each pocket. On one was written, "The world was created for me." On the other, "I am but dust and ashes."

Chabad.org presents this as a recipe for balance:

- **On a low day,** reach into the first pocket. You matter. The world needs what you came to do.
- **On a proud day,** reach into the second pocket. Your gifts are a gift, and you are not above anyone.

You need both. The first guards you from despair, and the second from arrogance.

## Your soul is part of something greater

Chassidic teaching, especially in the Tanya, says each person has a soul that is "a part of G-d above." This is why every person matters. Your worth does not depend on grades, a job title, money or what others think. Read more about [the soul](/learn/the-soul).

## You have a mission only you can do

Chabad.org teaches that each person has unique gifts and a mission only he or she can fulfill, so no one is competing with anyone else. Talent is called a "gift" for a reason: it is something you were given.

The right question is not "Why am I great?" or "Why am I nothing?" but "Why did G-d give me these gifts, and what are they for?" A [mitzvah](/learn/what-is-a-mitzvah) is a concrete way to use them.

## Seeing greatness in others

Chabad.org quotes Rabbi Jonathan Sacks, who met the Lubavitcher Rebbe. He described a quiet, unassuming man who nevertheless made him feel like the most important person in the world, asking about him, listening and seeming to believe in him more than he believed in himself. Sacks concluded that humility is not thinking you are small, but knowing that other people have greatness within them.

You can practice this at home. Notice what is good in your child, spouse, coworker and in yourself.

## Practical steps for a hard week

1. **Name one thing you did well today.** Say it out loud.
2. **Do one small mitzvah.** Give a coin to charity or call someone who is alone.
3. **Remember it is not a competition.** Your mission is not another person's.
4. **Forgive yourself when you fall short.** See [forgiving yourself](/learn/forgiving-yourself).
5. **Reach out.** A trusted friend, teacher or Rabbi can help.

## When it is more than a bad day

Low self-worth can sometimes be more than a mood. If you or someone you love feels persistently hopeless, please talk to a doctor or a mental health professional, and see [the Jewish view of mental health](/learn/mental-health-jewish-view). ## Common questions

### What is the Jewish view of self-esteem?
Judaism teaches that every person has real worth because the soul is a part of G-d, and that each of us has a mission only we can do. This worth does not come from achievements or approval.

### Is humility the same as low self-esteem?
No. Chabad.org teaches that false humility is a form of ingratitude. A humble person knows his or her gifts and credits them to G-d.

### What is the two-pocket teaching?
Rabbi Simcha Bunim kept one note that said "The world was created for me" and one that said "I am but dust and ashes." Use the first when you feel low and the second when you feel proud.

### How can I feel better about myself in a Jewish way?
Remember your mission, do a small mitzvah, forgive yourself and notice the good in others. Speak to the Rabbi if you feel stuck.

### Is it arrogant to say the world was created for me?
Not when it is balanced by humility. It reminds you that you have a purpose, and that others do too.
