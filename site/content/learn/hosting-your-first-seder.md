To host a Passover Seder, gather your guests, give everyone the same Haggadah, prepare the Seder plate, matzah and four cups of wine or grape juice, and follow the Haggadah's fifteen steps while pausing to talk and ask questions. You do not need to be an expert: the goal of the night is to tell the story of the Exodus to your children and guests, and a warm welcome matters more than a perfect meal.

## Weeks before: plan and invite

1. **Invite sincerely.** Think of people who may especially need an invitation: a neighbor, a coworker, a student, someone who lives alone. A phone call shows you really want them there. Chabad.org advises that texts and emails are fine, but a call is the most effective.
2. **Get enough Haggadahs.** Use the same edition for everyone so you can follow along together. If guests speak different languages, a multilingual edition helps. See [what a Haggadah is](/learn/haggadah).
3. **Plan the food.** Read [kosher for Passover](/learn/kosher-for-passover), [Passover cleaning](/learn/passover-cleaning) and the [shopping list](/learn/passover-shopping-list). Handmade shmurah matzah is ideal for the Seder. For a guest who cannot eat wheat, ask the Rabbi what can be done.
4. **Think about the cups.** Chabad.org suggests small cups holding at least 3 fluid ounces for the four cups. Offer grape juice for anyone who cannot drink wine. See [the four cups](/learn/four-cups).
5. **Study a little.** Read a few insights ahead of time and ask family members to do the same, then share them at the table.

## The day before and the afternoon

- Prepare the items for the [Seder plate](/learn/seder-plate): the egg, a roasted bone (zeroa), bitter herbs, charoset and a vegetable for karpas, plus salt water, three matzahs and an Elijah's cup.
- Have two matzahs for each participant in addition to the three on the plate.
- Light the holiday candles before the holiday begins, from a flame that was lit before sunset. The women and girls usually light them. Holiday times are on [/this-week](/this-week) and in [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times).
- Set up the Seder plate after evening services, and involve everyone present.

## The night: step by step

1. **Seat the leader in the middle**, where everyone can hear and see. Speak clearly and translate unfamiliar words.
2. **Read in Hebrew if you can; English is fine.** If your Hebrew is limited, read in the language you understand.
3. **Pause between paragraphs.** Ask questions, share a thought, let the children talk. This is the heart of the Seder: the Haggadah is meant to be a conversation.
4. **Follow the cues** for raising cups and covering and uncovering the matzah. Everyone recline to the left while drinking the four cups and eating the matzah.
5. **Let the children shine.** A child traditionally recites the [Four Questions](/learn/four-questions). If no child is present, an adult asks. See [Passover with kids](/learn/passover-with-kids) and the [afikoman](/learn/afikoman).
6. **Eat the matzah and bitter herbs without delay,** and finish the afikoman before halachic midnight on the first night. Chabad.org suggests checking the exact time for your location.
7. **Close with song** and "Next year in Jerusalem."

## Make everyone feel at home

Chabad.org offers practical hospitality tips. Let reading aloud be optional, and share the readings in advance so people can prepare. Seat guests who may need help next to someone who can assist. If guests do not know the tunes, send them a song link beforehand. Hosting is stressful, but guests remember the welcome far more than a burnt dish. Spend time with each guest.

## Common questions

### How do I host a Passover Seder for the first time?
Invite your guests, get the same Haggadah for everyone, prepare the Seder plate, matzah and four cups, and follow the Haggadah's steps, pausing to discuss. You can use English, and the Rabbi is happy to walk you through it.

### What do I need for a Seder?
You need a Seder plate with its items, three matzahs plus two for each person, wine or grape juice for four cups, salt water, Haggadahs, and an Elijah's cup. Handmade shmurah matzah is the ideal.

### How long does a Seder last?
It depends on how much discussion and singing you do, and the meal is in the middle. Because the afikoman should be eaten before halachic midnight on the first night, plan the pace with that in mind.

### Can I lead a Seder in English?
Yes. Hebrew is preferred if you can read it, but if your Hebrew is limited, English is acceptable. What matters is that everyone understands the story.

### Who asks the Four Questions?
A child traditionally asks them. If no child is present, an adult asks, and if you are alone, you recite them yourself. In Chabad custom everyone recites them after the child.

### Can I host a Seder if I am not very observant?
Yes. Every Jew is welcome to hold a Seder, and doing so is a beautiful mitzvah. Ask the Rabbi about any questions on kashrut or customs.
