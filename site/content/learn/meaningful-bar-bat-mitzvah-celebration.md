A meaningful Bar or Bat Mitzvah puts the child's first steps as a Jewish adult at the center, and lets the party grow out of them: a mitzvah the child takes on, a few words of Torah, an act of giving, and a festive meal that everyone enjoys. A celebration of any size is fine, as long as it is more than a birthday party.

## Start with the mitzvah, not the party

The child becomes Bar or Bat Mitzvah on the Hebrew birthday, with or without any event. So the first question is not "what kind of party?" but "what will my child take on?" The party then celebrates that. Some ways to put the mitzvah first:

- **A boy's tefillin.** His first weekday morning with [tefillin](/learn/tefillin), and the aliyah to the Torah, are the heart of the day. See [starting tefillin before the Bar Mitzvah](/learn/starting-tefillin-before-bar-mitzvah).
- **A girl's Shabbat candles.** Lighting Shabbat candles, separating challah and personal prayer are the mitzvot a girl takes on. A girl who has not yet started can begin on the Shabbat closest to her Bat Mitzvah. A candlestick makes a lovely gift. See [girls lighting Shabbat candles](/learn/girls-lighting-shabbat-candles).
- **A personal mitzvah** the child chooses and keeps, such as daily prayer, a weekly act of kindness or regular charity.

## A talk that the child owns

A speech or *d'var Torah* is the moment the child shines. It can include a thought on the Torah portion, a Torah idea, or the lessons of a notable Jewish woman, and a thank-you to family, teachers and friends. Make it short, personal and rehearsed aloud. See [the Bar Mitzvah speech](/learn/bar-mitzvah-speech).

## Give something

- **Tzedakah.** The Bar Mitzvah boy gives charity before his prayers, and so do the parents. Many families add a gift in the child's honor; see [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).
- **A mitzvah project.** Consider spending an hour or more helping others on the day, such as visiting a nursing home or volunteering at a soup kitchen, and inviting friends to join. This guide does not repeat the full list: see [mitzvah projects](/learn/bat-mitzvah-projects).
- **Ask guests to do a mitzvah too.** Invite everyone to give tzedakah, light a candle or take on a small act. It is a lovely way to share the day.

## Make the meal itself a mitzvah

The festive meal after a Bar Mitzvah is a *seudat mitzvah* (סְעוּדַת מִצְוָה), a meal that is itself a mitzvah. That is a reason to keep the table meaningful:

1. **Keep it kosher,** so that every Jewish guest can eat. See [kosher catering for a celebration in Lamorinda](/learn/kosher-catering-for-a-simcha-in-lamorinda) and the [Bay Kosher program](/programs/bay-kosher), a project of Chabad of Lamorinda, to ask what is available.
2. **Begin with a blessing and a word of Torah.** The parents may thank G-d for reaching this day; guests wish the child success. See [the parents' moment](/learn/parents-blessing-at-bar-mitzvah).
3. **Invite others to share a thought,** short and sweet.
4. **Sing and dance.** The day should have joy like a wedding.
5. **Close with Birkat HaMazon,** the Grace after Meals, which some have the child lead.

## Keep it within your means

A meaningful celebration is not a costly one. A Kiddush after services, a Shabbat dinner at home or a small gathering can carry as much meaning as a large hall. See [a Bar or Bat Mitzvah on a budget](/learn/bar-bat-mitzvah-on-a-budget). A gift of money in multiples of 18, the value of the Hebrew word *chai*, "life," is customary; see [gifts in multiples of 18](/learn/gifts-in-multiples-of-18).

## Common questions

### How can we make a Bar Mitzvah meaningful?
Center the day on a mitzvah the child takes on, a short talk of Torah, giving to others and a kosher festive meal. The size of the party matters far less than its meaning.

### What should a Bat Mitzvah girl do for her special day?
Lighting Shabbat candles, separating challah, prayer and a mitzvah project are the mitzvot a girl takes on. She can also give a talk and invite friends to a mitzvah.

### Does a Bar Mitzvah have to be on Shabbat?
No. The Torah is read on Monday and Thursday as well, and Chabad custom prefers a Monday or Thursday morning, or Shabbat afternoon, for the first aliyah. Ask the Rabbi.

### Does the food have to be kosher?
A *seudat mitzvah* is best served kosher so that every guest can take part. Ask the Rabbi about the kitchen and the caterer.

### What is a good Bar or Bat Mitzvah gift?
Good gifts include religious or educational books, ritual items or money, often in multiples of 18. Ask the family what they would like.

### How do we involve relatives who are new to Jewish customs?
Welcome them warmly and explain the customs in the invitation or a short program. Ask the Rabbi for ideas.
