Christian denominations are the many separate churches within Christianity, and they fall into three main branches: Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox and Protestant. They share belief in Jesus, but differ on authority, worship, the number of sacraments and how salvation works.

## Roman Catholic

The Catholic Church is the largest Christian church, roughly half of all Christians worldwide. It is led by the Pope, the Bishop of Rome, whom Catholics believe is the successor of the apostle Peter. Catholics have priests and bishops, seven sacraments (including baptism, the Eucharist and confession), and honor Mary and the saints. Their worship service is called Mass, and Catholic teaching draws on both the Bible and church tradition.

## Eastern Orthodox

The Eastern Orthodox churches share most of their early history with Catholics, but the two separated in the year 1054. Orthodox churches are organized by country or region, such as the Greek, Russian, Serbian and Romanian Orthodox churches, each led by bishops and a patriarch. Their services (the Divine Liturgy) are rich in chant, incense and icons, and Orthodox priests may marry. Many Russian-speaking Jewish families come from places where Orthodox Christianity is the traditional church.

## Protestant

Protestantism began in the 1500s, when reformers such as Martin Luther questioned certain Catholic teachings and practices. Protestants generally put the Bible at the center of faith, have no pope, and usually recognize two sacraments, baptism and communion. The Protestant world is large and varied. Its main families include:

- **Lutheran,** following Martin Luther.
- **Reformed and Presbyterian,** following John Calvin and others.
- **Anglican and Episcopal,** from the Church of England.
- **Baptist,** which practices baptism of believers, usually older children and adults.
- **Methodist,** which grew out of the teachings of John Wesley.
- **Pentecostal and charismatic,** with lively worship and emphasis on the Holy Spirit.
- **Evangelical and nondenominational,** independent churches found throughout the Bay Area.

## Other Christian groups

There are also the Oriental Orthodox churches (Coptic, Armenian, Ethiopian and others), which have their own ancient traditions. The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (Mormons) considers itself Christian, though many other Christian churches describe its teachings as different from historic Christianity.

## How they differ in plain language

| | Catholic | Orthodox | Protestant |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Leader | The Pope | Bishops and patriarchs | No single leader; each church or denomination governs itself |
| Sacraments | Seven | Seven ("mysteries") | Usually two |
| Clergy | Priests (unmarried) | Priests (may marry) | Pastors or ministers (may marry) |
| Worship | Mass | Divine Liturgy | Sermon, hymns and prayer; varies widely |

## A Jewish note

Jews do not share the beliefs of any of these churches, and Jewish law forbids taking part in their religious rites, whichever denomination it is. Judaism teaches that righteous people of all nations have a place in the World to Come, and that non-Jews are guided by the [seven Noahide laws](/learn/noahide-laws). It also asks us to [welcome and respect others](/learn/welcoming-the-stranger). If you are invited to a church event, ask the Rabbi about the specific case.

## Common questions

### What are the three main types of Christianity?
They are Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox and Protestant. Catholics are led by the Pope, Orthodox churches by bishops and patriarchs, and Protestants by their own denominations.

### What is the difference between Catholic and Protestant?
Catholics have the Pope, seven sacraments and church tradition alongside the Bible. Protestants have no pope, put the Bible at the center, and usually keep two sacraments, baptism and communion.

### What is the difference between Catholic and Eastern Orthodox?
They share most early Christian history and separated in 1054. Orthodox churches have no pope, are organized by region, and their priests may marry.

### Is Protestant the same as Evangelical?
No. Evangelical describes a style of Protestant Christianity that stresses personal faith and the Bible. Many but not all Protestant churches are evangelical.

### Are Mormons and Jehovah's Witnesses Christian?
They consider themselves Christian and follow Jesus, while many other churches say their teachings differ from historic Christianity. It is best to ask people how they describe themselves.

### Which denomination are most Christians in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda?
We do not have local figures to share. The Bay Area has Catholic, Protestant and Orthodox churches, and Jewish neighbors often meet all three.
