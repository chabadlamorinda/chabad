Hafrashat challah means "separating challah": taking a small piece from a batch of dough before baking and setting it aside as a gift to G-d. It is one of the special mitzvot entrusted especially to Jewish women, and many see the moment of separating as a time of great blessing and prayer.

## Where the mitzvah comes from

The Torah says: "Of the first of your dough you shall set apart a challah as a gift" (Numbers 15:20). In Temple times, this portion was given to a Kohen. Today, since we cannot give it to a Kohen in a state of purity, we separate it and burn it, so the mitzvah is still kept.

The word *challah* originally meant this separated portion. Later, the braided Shabbat loaves took their name from it. Read more in our guide to [challah](/learn/challah).

## Which dough needs it

The mitzvah applies to dough made from any of the five grains: wheat, barley, rye, oats or spelt. Whether you bake bread, rolls or even some cakes, it depends mainly on how much flour is in the batch:

- **A small batch** needs no separation at all.
- **A medium batch** is separated **without** a blessing.
- **A large batch** is separated **with** a blessing.

Many follow these approximate amounts: from about 2 lb 11 oz (1.2 kg) of flour, separate without a blessing, and from about 4 lb 15 oz (2.25 kg) of flour, separate with a blessing. Customs differ, and dough made mostly with juice or eggs instead of water has its own rules, so ask the Rabbi for your recipe.

## How to separate challah, step by step

1. **Knead the dough** until it is ready to be shaped.
2. **Say the blessing** (if the batch is large enough).
3. **Pull off a small piece**, about the size of an olive.
4. **Hold it up and say:** "Harei zu challah," meaning "This is challah."
5. **Set it aside**, wrapped in foil.
6. **Burn it.** Place it in the oven or over a flame until it is completely charred, on its own and not together with the bread you are baking. Then wrap it and discard it respectfully.

## The blessing

**Hebrew:** בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו, וְצִוָּנוּ לְהַפְרִישׁ חַלָּה

**Transliteration:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu l'hafrish challah.

**Translation:** Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us to separate challah.

## A time for prayer

There is a beautiful tradition that the moment of separating challah is an auspicious time to pray. Many women pause after the blessing to ask G-d for the health and well-being of their family, for a friend in need, or for a match or a child. Some bake challah together in groups, each praying for someone in need. This is a fitting time to give [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) too.

## Common questions

### Can a man separate challah?
Yes. Anyone who bakes can separate challah, and a bakery separates challah from its dough. It is a mitzvah given especially to women because they traditionally bake for the home.

### What if I forgot to separate challah before baking?
In many cases challah can still be separated from the baked bread. Put all the loaves together, take a small piece and follow the steps. Ask the Rabbi if you are not sure.

### Do I need to separate challah from store-bought bread?
No. A reliable kosher bakery separates challah from its dough. Look for kosher certification. See our guide to [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols).

### Why do we burn the piece?
In Temple times, the portion was eaten by a Kohen in purity. Today we cannot do that, so we burn it, to make sure no one eats something that was given to G-d.
