Jewish tradition surrounds a birth with prayer: the husband says Psalms, a card with Shir Hamaalot (Psalm 121) hangs in the room, and if labor begins on Shabbat, a woman goes to the hospital without hesitation. Preserving life and health overrides Shabbat, and the first rule is to get safely to the doctors.

This guide follows Chabad.org's laws and customs of childbirth. For personal medical questions, ask your doctor, and for Jewish law, ask the Rabbi.

## Prayer before and during labor

It is customary for the husband to recite Psalms (Tehillim) during labor, as time allows, and, during pregnancy, to say Psalm 20 before bed. Chabad.org lists chapters customarily said, and the Rabbi can give you a card. See [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) and [Jewish pregnancy](/learn/jewish-pregnancy).

## Shir Hamaalot in the room

Many families hang a card with Psalm 121, *Shir Lamaalot* ("A Song of Ascents," often called Shir Hamaalot), and other prayers, near the door of the mother's and the baby's room, even in the hospital. The idea is that the baby begins life surrounded by holy words. Ask the Rabbi for a printed card.

## Labor on Shabbat

Life takes priority over Shabbat ([pikuach nefesh](/learn/pikuach-nefesh)). When labor begins on Shabbat or a holiday, according to Chabad.org:

1. Go to the hospital when the birth seems close; do not wait.
2. If possible, ask a non-Jewish driver and settle the fare beforehand; otherwise do not delay.
3. Do only what is needed for safety, and minimize what is not.
4. Call the Rabbi, or have a friend call, who will advise on the details.

Do not feel guilty about anything that is done for the safety of mother and baby. See [Shabbat in the hospital](/learn/shabbat-in-the-hospital) and [the hospital stay in Contra Costa](/learn/hospital-stay-in-contra-costa).

## Husband, wife and the delivery room

Chabad custom has particular guidance on a husband's presence in the delivery room and on the laws of *niddah* after birth. These are private and personal, and best discussed with the Rabbi or, for women, with the Rebbetzin. Please call them before the due date.

## After the birth

- **A boy:** the brit milah takes place on the eighth day ([brit milah](/learn/brit-milah), [preparing for a bris](/learn/preparing-for-a-bris)).
- **A girl:** a naming in the synagogue and a celebration ([baby naming](/learn/baby-naming), [Simchat Bat](/learn/simchat-bat)).
- **The mother:** the blessing of thanksgiving (*HaGomel*) is customarily said after childbirth.
- Shabbat after the birth is a time for a *Shalom Zachar* for a boy ([Shalom Zachar](/learn/shalom-zachar)).

## Common questions

### What are the Jewish customs for giving birth?
The husband recites Psalms, a card with Psalm 121 is placed in the room, and the mother and baby are surrounded by prayer. After the birth, a boy has a brit milah on the eighth day.

### Can you drive to the hospital on Shabbat in labor?
Yes. Saving life and health overrides Shabbat, so go to the hospital when the birth seems close, and call the Rabbi for guidance on the details.

### What is Shir Hamaalot?
Shir Hamaalot is Psalm 121, a prayer for protection that is hung in the room of mother and baby.

### Does Shabbat law apply in the hospital after a birth?
Any need for the mother's or baby's health comes first. Ask the Rabbi about everyday questions, such as meals and candles.
