The Bedtime Shema, Kriat Shema al Hamitah, is a short set of prayers said before going to sleep. Its heart is the first paragraph of the [Shema](/learn/shema), along with forgiveness of others and a blessing asking G-d to let us rest in peace and wake up in the morning.

## Why we pray before sleep

The Sages teach that sleep is a small taste of death. The soul rises up and the body rests. Before we let go, we entrust our soul to G-d, just as King David said, "Into Your hand I entrust my spirit" (Psalms 31:6). In the morning we thank G-d for returning it with [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani).

Bedtime is also a time to look back at the day and end it with a clean slate.

## The main parts

The full Bedtime Shema in the [siddur](/learn/siddur) includes:

1. **Forgiving others.** We begin by saying that we forgive anyone who hurt or angered us that day. It is a powerful way to let go before sleep.
2. **The Shema.** "Hear O Israel, the L-rd is our G-d, the L-rd is One," followed by "Baruch shem" and the paragraph of V'ahavta.
3. **Psalms of protection,** including Psalm 91, "He who dwells in the shelter of the Most High."
4. **The angels.** "On my right is Michael, on my left Gabriel, before me Uriel, behind me Raphael, and above my head the Presence of G-d."
5. **The Hamapil blessing.**

## The Hamapil blessing

Hamapil is a blessing to G-d "who casts the bonds of sleep upon my eyes." It asks for peaceful rest, good dreams, and to wake up to life. It begins: "Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, hamapil chevlei sheinah al einai..."

The custom is to say Hamapil last, right before falling asleep, and not to talk afterward. If you will not fall asleep soon, you can say it without the opening words of the blessing.

## A simple version for children

Many children love a short bedtime routine:

1. **Torah tziva lanu Moshe,** "The Torah that Moses commanded us is the heritage of the congregation of Jacob."
2. **Shema Yisrael** and Baruch shem, with a hand over the eyes.
3. **V'ahavta,** if they know it.
4. **The angels,** "Beyemini Michael..."
5. **Hamapil,** or a parent's blessing.

It is a gentle way to end the day and builds a warm, lifelong habit. A mezuzah on the bedroom door and a small tzedakah box by the bed, a Chabad custom, add to the feeling of a Jewish child's room.

## Common questions

### Do I have to say the whole thing?
No. If you are new to it, start with the Shema itself. Even the first line, said with feeling, is precious. Add more over time.

### What if I fall asleep before finishing?
That is fine. Say what you can. Many people say the Shema while lying in bed, so they drift off with holy words.

### Can I say the Bedtime Shema in English?
Yes. The Shema may be said in any language you understand. Many siddurim include translation and transliteration.

### When should it be said?
Shortly before going to sleep at night. Some say it earlier with young children and again themselves later.
