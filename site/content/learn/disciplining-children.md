Jewish wisdom teaches that discipline should come from love, not anger: be firm and clear about limits, but warm, calm and brief. According to Chabad.org's parenting articles, discipline is meant to teach a child self-control, not to punish.

## Discipline teaches, it does not punish

Chabad.org's article on setting limits describes limits as a railing on a boat: they give a child security so that he or she can explore. The goal is a child who learns self-control and understands that actions have consequences. The same article warns that punishment can breed resentment and teach a child only to avoid getting caught. Parents in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda face the same challenges as parents everywhere, and these ideas help.

## Firm hand, warm hand

The Talmud (Sanhedrin 107b) teaches that the left hand should push away while the right hand draws close. Teachers often explain this to parents as: correct with the weaker hand, embrace with the stronger. Warmth should always outweigh correction. We did not find this teaching on Chabad.org itself, so ask the Rabbi if you want to learn more.

## Practical steps

1. **Wait until you are calm.** Chabad.org says discipline must come from love, not anger, and should be planned, not impulsive. One idea attributed to the Rebbe: imagine the children are a neighbor's, entrusted to you by G-d for a time. You would not lose control with them.
2. **Keep it short.** A scolding of about a minute is enough. Long lectures get tuned out.
3. **Speak about the behavior, not the child.** Say "That was untrue," not "You are a liar."
4. **Stay factual.** Describe what needs fixing and how.
5. **Be firm.** Pleading and begging weaken a limit. Children need a parent who is a loving leader.
6. **Fill the tank.** Playing, listening and affection prevent many problems. Notice and praise good behavior often.

## Parents of young children and teens

With little ones, keep rules few, clear and the same every day. With teens, explain the reasons, listen, and choose your battles; see [parents and teenagers](/learn/parents-and-teenagers). Values are learned mostly by watching the adults a child loves, so model the behavior you hope to see.

## When you lose your temper

Everyone does sometimes, and an occasional lapse in a loving home is human. Apologize, and try again tomorrow. Chabad.org says that if outbursts are frequent, or children are being harmed, seek professional help right away. Read more in [anger: the Jewish view](/learn/anger-jewish-view). This guide gives no halachic rulings; for your family's situation, ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### What is the Jewish view on disciplining children?
Discipline should come from love, not anger, and should aim to teach self-control. It is firm, brief and warm.

### Should parents punish in anger?
No. Chabad.org teaches that discipline must come from love and be planned, not done in the heat of the moment. Wait until you are calm.

### How long should a scolding be?
About one minute. Longer talks lose a child's attention and can harm self-esteem.

### What does "left hand pushes away, right hand draws close" mean?
It is a Talmudic image for parenting: correct gently and briefly, and draw the child close with love. Ask the Rabbi to learn more.

### What if I yell too much?
Occasional lapses are human; apologize and try again. If it is frequent, seek professional help and talk to the Rabbi or Rebbetzin.
