Shiva is the seven-day period of mourning that begins right after a Jewish funeral. Mourners stay at home, set aside daily routines, and receive visitors who come to comfort them and share memories of the person who passed away.

## What is shiva?

Shiva means "seven" in Hebrew. It is observed by the seven closest relatives: father, mother, son, daughter, brother, sister and spouse. The week gives mourners space to grieve fully, surrounded by community, instead of rushing back to normal life.

## How long does shiva last?

Shiva begins as soon as the family returns from the cemetery and continues for seven days, counting the day of the funeral as the first day. It ends on the morning of the seventh day.

- **Shabbat:** mourners do not observe public mourning on Shabbat, but Shabbat counts as one of the seven days.
- **Holidays:** a major Jewish holiday can end or shorten shiva. Ask the Rabbi about your specific dates.

## Customs for mourners

During shiva, mourners traditionally:

- Sit on low stools or cushions, a sign of being brought low by grief.
- Wear the garment torn at the funeral (kriah).
- Refrain from work, entertainment, bathing for pleasure, and wearing leather shoes.
- Do not shave, cut hair or wear new clothing.
- Do not greet visitors with "hello"; visitors wait for the mourner to speak first.

In the shiva home, mirrors are often covered and a memorial candle burns for the full seven days.

## Prayer services

Many families hold daily prayer services in the shiva home so mourners can say [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish). These need a minyan of ten Jewish men over 13. Friends and community members coming to help form a minyan is one of the greatest kindnesses you can offer.

## Visiting a shiva house

Comforting mourners, called nichum aveilim, is a great mitzvah. Here is how to do it:

1. **Walk in quietly.** The door is often left open so mourners do not need to get up.
2. **Sit and let the mourner lead.** It is customary to wait for the mourner to begin speaking.
3. **Share memories.** Stories about the person who passed are a true comfort.
4. **Keep it short.** A visit of 20 to 30 minutes is usually right.
5. **Say the traditional farewell.** As you leave, say: "HaMakom yenachem etchem betoch she'ar aveilei Tzion v'Yerushalayim," meaning "May G-d comfort you among the mourners of Zion and Jerusalem."

## Common questions

### What should I bring to a shiva house?

Food is the most common and helpful gift, especially prepared meals the family can eat without effort. Check that it is kosher if the family keeps kosher. Flowers are not traditional. A donation to tzedakah in the person's memory is also a meaningful gesture.

### Is it okay to visit if I do not know what to say?

Yes. You do not need perfect words. Your presence alone is comforting. Listening is often the best thing you can do.

### Can mourners leave the house during shiva?

Generally mourners stay home for the full week. There are exceptions, such as going to synagogue on Shabbat. Ask the Rabbi about your specific situation.

### What happens after shiva ends?

Mourning continues in gentler stages. Shloshim lasts through the 30th day after burial. For a parent, mourning customs continue for 12 months, and Kaddish is said for 11 months. Read more in our guides to [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish) and [yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit).
