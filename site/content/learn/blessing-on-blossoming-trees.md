Birkat HaIlanot (בִּרְכַּת הָאִילָנוֹת) is the blessing on blossoming trees, said once a year when you first see fruit trees in bloom, traditionally during the Hebrew month of Nissan in the spring. It thanks G-d "Who withheld nothing from His world and created in it good creatures and good trees, to give people pleasure." In Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, where fruit trees blossom early, it is a lovely family outing.

## What is the blessing on trees?

It is a short blessing of gratitude for the beauty of creation. Chabad.org teaches that the blessing is said once a year, over fruit-bearing trees that are in blossom, and that it is not said over a tree that has no fruit. The text, in transliteration: *Baruch atah Adonai, Eloheinu melech ha'olam, she'lo chisar ba'olamo davar, u'vara vo briyot tovot v'ilanot tovim, l'hanot bahem bnei adam.*

## When do you say it?

The blessing is said during Nissan, the month of Passover. Here is the catch for the Bay Area: our mild climate means many fruit trees blossom in February or March, before Nissan begins. If you see blossoms before Nissan, do not worry; the usual advice is to wait for Nissan, if the trees are still in bloom. If the trees have already passed their bloom, or in any case where you are unsure, **ask the Rabbi**. The blessing is said once a year, and the custom is not to say it on Shabbat or a holiday. Check [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays) for the date of Nissan, and call 925-384-7242 if in doubt.

## How to say it, step by step

1. **Find fruit trees in bloom.** Apple, pear, plum, cherry, peach and similar trees count. Ask the Rabbi about any tree you are unsure of.
2. **Look for at least two trees.** It is customary to say it over more than one tree, though one is sufficient according to some. Ask the Rabbi what he recommends.
3. **Say the blessing** aloud, in Hebrew or in English. Children can say it too.
4. **Enjoy.** Take a family walk, sing, and tell the children it is a once-a-year mitzvah.

## Local color: a walk in Moraga

Moraga's pear orchard beside Joaquin Moraga School on Camino Pablo is more than a century old, according to the Lamorinda Weekly, and is a famous local landmark. The newspaper does not say it is open to visitors, so please do not enter without permission. Orinda and Lafayette have many backyard and neighborhood fruit trees that bloom each spring, and a walk through your own neighborhood is all you need.

## Common questions

### What is Birkat HaIlanot?
Birkat HaIlanot is the Jewish blessing on blossoming fruit trees, said once a year in the spring, traditionally in Nissan.

### When do you say the blessing on trees in the Bay Area?
Traditionally in Nissan, over fruit trees in bloom. Because Bay Area trees blossom early, ask Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 how to handle blossoms you see before Nissan.

### Do I need two trees?
It is customary to say it over two or more fruit trees. Ask the Rabbi if you have only one.

### Can children say the blessing?
Yes. Bring your children and let them say it too; it is a lovely first Jewish nature blessing. See [blessings on nature](/learn/blessings-on-nature).

### Do I say it on Shabbat?
The custom is to say it on a weekday, not on Shabbat or a holiday. Ask the Rabbi.

### Where can I learn more about trees in Jewish life?
Read [the Tu BiShvat seder](/learn/tu-bishvat-seder) and [Tu BiShvat](/holidays/tu-bishvat), the Jewish new year of the trees.
