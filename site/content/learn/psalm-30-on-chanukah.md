Psalm 30 begins with the words "Mizmor shir chanukat habayit, l'David," "A psalm, a song for the dedication of the House, of David" (מזמור שיר חנוכת הבית לדוד). The heading uses the word *chanukah*, "dedication," the same word that gives the holiday its name, and this is the link between the psalm, Chanukah and dedicating a new home.

## What does the heading mean?

According to Rashi, whose commentary appears with the psalm, the Levites sang this song at the dedication of the Temple in the days of King Solomon. It is also a psalm composed for the dedication of the Holy Temple, so the "House" in the heading is the Temple, G-d's House. The Hebrew word for dedication, *chanukah*, is the same word as the name of the holiday, which is the link with the festival that celebrates the rededication of the Temple.

You can read more about the festival's name in [what Chanukah means](/learn/what-does-chanukah-mean) and about the home in [Chanukat HaBayit](/learn/chanukat-habayit).

## What does the psalm say?

The psalm is a song of thanks from a person who has been through a hard time and come out the other side. Here are a few of the ideas, as found in a standard translation:

- The writer says he will exalt G-d, because G-d has raised him up and has not let his enemies rejoice over him.
- He cried out to G-d and was healed. Rashi explains that "healed" here means forgiven.
- "For His wrath lasts but a moment; life results from His favor; in the evening, weeping may tarry, but in the morning there is joyful singing."
- At the end: "You have turned my lament into dancing for me; You loosened my sackcloth and girded me with joy." The psalm ends with the writer promising to sing G-d's praise forever.

Rashi also records that the Sages read this psalm as being about Mordechai, Esther and Haman. It is a psalm that moves from darkness to light, a movement we also find in Chanukah.

## A psalm for a new home

Many also say Psalm 30 when inaugurating a new home, which is why it is called a psalm "about inaugurating a new house." If you have recently moved in, our guide to [Chanukat HaBayit](/learn/chanukat-habayit) explains how to put up a mezuzah and celebrate.

## Common questions

### What is Psalm 30?
It is a psalm of David titled "a song for the dedication of the House," a song of thanks that moves from sorrow to joy.

### Why is Psalm 30 connected to Hanukkah?
Its heading speaks of dedication, *chanukah*, and Chanukah celebrates the rededication of the Temple.

### What does "Mizmor Shir Chanukat HaBayit" mean?
"A psalm, a song for the dedication of the House." According to Rashi, the Levites sang it at the Temple's dedication in Solomon's time.

### Can I say Psalm 30 when I move into a new home?
Yes, many do. The psalm is about inaugurating a new house. Ask the Rabbi about the customs for your home.

### When is Psalm 30 said in the morning prayers?
In the siddur it comes right after Hodu. Ask the Rabbi about any special arrangement for Chanukah.
