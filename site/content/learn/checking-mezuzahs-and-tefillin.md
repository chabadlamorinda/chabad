Jewish law requires that mezuzahs and tefillin be checked by a scribe (sofer) twice in every seven years, and the custom, encouraged by the Lubavitcher Rebbe, is to check them every year in the month of Elul, before Rosh Hashanah. Scrolls outside the house are exposed to weather, so they should be checked more often.

## Why do mezuzahs and tefillin need checking?

Mezuzah (מְזוּזָה) and tefillin (תְּפִלִּין) are handwritten parchment scrolls, and a mitzvah is only fulfilled with a valid scroll. Ink fades, parchment cracks and moisture or heat can damage letters, often without any sign on the outside. A case can look perfect while the scroll inside is no longer kosher. Learn the basics of [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) and [tefillin](/learn/tefillin).

## How often should a mezuzah be checked?

- **Twice every seven years** by Jewish law, by a scribe. Chabad.org also notes that the scroll should be removed and inspected every three or four years.
- **Every year in Elul** by ancient custom. The Rebbe encouraged it. See [Elul](/learn/elul) for the month of preparation.
- **More often for outdoor mezuzahs,** such as a front door that gets rain and sun. Some computer scanning can supplement a scribe's check, but it does not replace it.

Tefillin are checked too, and many check them in Elul as well. If you wear yours often, ask the Rabbi how often to check yours.

## What does a scribe look for?

A sofer inspects each letter under good light. These problems make a scroll invalid:

1. **Fading or flaking ink,** or letters that have cracked where the parchment was folded or damaged by weather.
2. **Missing or incorrect letters.** One missing or damaged letter can disqualify the whole mezuzah.
3. **A printed or photocopied scroll** that only looks handwritten. This is a real problem, because many "mezuzahs" are sold this way.
4. **Corrections written out of order,** which are not valid.

If a scroll fails, the scribe can sometimes repair it, and sometimes you need a new one. See [choosing a mezuzah](/learn/choosing-a-mezuzah) and [buying tefillin](/learn/buying-tefillin).

## Taking down and putting back a mezuzah

1. Remove the mezuzah carefully, with the case, and number it with a pencil or a sticky note so you know which doorpost it came from.
2. Take the scrolls to be checked. Nails, screws and tape can be thrown away, but the case and wrapping are sacred objects and are not thrown out. Ask the Rabbi what to do with them.
3. Put each mezuzah back on its own doorpost, or on a similar one. Chabad.org teaches that a new blessing is needed only if the mezuzah was off the doorpost for at least one night. Otherwise, no blessing.
4. Use the placement from the [mezuzah guide](/learn/mezuzah): right side, upper third, tilted inward.

## Common questions

### How often should a mezuzah be checked?
Twice every seven years by Jewish law, and by custom every year in Elul. Mezuzahs outside the house should be checked more often.

### How often should tefillin be checked?
Many check them every year in Elul, together with the mezuzahs. Ask the Rabbi how often to check your set.

### What makes a mezuzah invalid?
Faded or cracked letters, missing or wrong letters, or a scroll that was printed and not handwritten.

### Do I need a new blessing when I put the mezuzah back?
Only if it was off the doorpost for at least one night. If you put it back the same day, no new blessing is said.

### Can I check my mezuzah myself?
You can look at the scroll and the case for obvious damage, but only a trained scribe can judge whether the letters are valid. Ask the Rabbi to help arrange it.
