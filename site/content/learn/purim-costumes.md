Dressing up in costumes on Purim is a joyful Jewish custom that recalls the hidden miracles of the Purim story. Children and adults alike come to the Megillah reading and Purim party as kings, queens, heroes and more.

## Is it a mitzvah?

Wearing a costume is a custom, not one of the four mitzvot of Purim. Those are hearing the [Megillah](/learn/megillah), giving [mishloach manot](/learn/mishloach-manot) (gifts of food), giving gifts to the poor, and eating a festive meal. Costumes add to the joy of the day, and joy is at the heart of Purim. The custom is several hundred years old and is now practiced across the Jewish world.

## Why we dress up

- **G-d's hidden hand.** G-d's name does not appear even once in the Book of Esther. The miracle of Purim was hidden inside natural events. Costumes remind us that G-d is behind the scenes, even when we cannot see Him.
- **Esther's hidden identity.** Queen Esther did not reveal that she was Jewish until the right moment.
- **Everything turned around.** The Megillah says "v'nahafoch hu," the situation was reversed: the day meant for destruction became a day of celebration. Dressing as someone else plays on that reversal.
- **Mordechai's royal clothes.** Mordechai was dressed in the king's royal garments and paraded through the city.
- **The inner Jew.** Chassidic teaching notes that a costume changes only the outside. Every Jew's soul stays pure and connected to G-d, no matter how things look on the surface.

## When to wear costumes

Most people wear costumes on Purim day, and many also at the Megillah reading the night before. Some wear them at the Purim meal and while delivering mishloach manot. Check this year's dates on our [Purim page](/holidays/purim).

## Guidelines to keep in mind

- **Keep it modest and fun.** A costume should fit the spirit of a Jewish holiday.
- **Men and women.** Torah law teaches that men should not wear women's clothing and women should not wear men's clothing. Many authorities say to avoid this even on Purim. Ask the Rabbi if you have a question.
- **Choose positive characters.** Many prefer not to dress as villains, scary or violent figures, or symbols of other religions.
- **Safety first.** For young children, avoid masks that block vision and long costumes that trip.

## Easy costume ideas

1. **Queen Esther:** a crown and a long dress.
2. **Mordechai:** a robe and a crown, or a royal cape.
3. **King Achashverosh:** crown, scepter and a big royal robe.
4. **A Kohen or the High Priest:** white robe and a breastplate made of colored squares.
5. **Biblical figures:** Moses with tablets, Noah with stuffed animals, or King David with a toy harp.
6. **A Torah scroll, a menorah or hamantash:** a fun, creative choice.
7. **Professions:** doctor, firefighter or chef work well for little ones.

## Common questions

### Do adults dress up too?
Yes. Many adults wear at least a hat or a simple costume. It adds to the joy and makes children happy.

### Is it okay to wear a costume to synagogue?
On Purim, yes. Costumes at the Megillah reading are very common. Just be careful that noise and costumes do not stop anyone from hearing every word of the Megillah.

### Why do we eat hamantashen?
These three-cornered pastries hide their filling inside, another reminder of the hidden miracle. See our guide to [Jewish foods](/learn/jewish-foods).

### Can I come to a Purim event if I do not have a costume?
Of course. Everyone is welcome, costume or not. See our [events](/events).
