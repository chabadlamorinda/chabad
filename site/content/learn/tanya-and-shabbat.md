In Chabad teaching, Shabbat is not only a day of rest for the body: the Talmud teaches that every Jew receives an "additional soul" on Shabbat, and the Alter Rebbe explains that this is a revelation of the deepest level of the soul, which is always united with G-d.

This guide draws on the Alter Rebbe's teaching on the additional Shabbat soul in his work Torah Ohr, together with ideas from the Tanya on prayer and joy that help us understand it.

## The additional soul

A discourse of Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi (the Alter Rebbe, who wrote the [Tanya](/learn/tanya)), asks a question. If we receive an additional soul on Shabbat eve, does that mean extra physical strength? The answer is no. The additional soul is a revelation of the yechida, the deepest level of the soul, which is always united with G-d. It is felt as a rise in spiritual life-force, not as physical energy. The discourse notes that the Talmud says it is given on Shabbat eve and taken back when Shabbat ends.

## Two kinds of love

The discourse describes two kinds of love for G-d. The first arises from thinking about G-d's greatness and oneness, and it is limited by a person's understanding. The second goes beyond the intellect and comes from a deep, will-like desire, similar to the will to live. The additional soul brings the second, unbounded love on Shabbat.

This links to the Tanya's idea of prayer. Chapter 12 describes how in the Shema and the Amidah the Divine soul holds sway as the mind meditates on G-d's greatness and kindles love in the heart. See [What Does Tanya Say About Prayer?](/learn/tanya-on-prayer).

## The weekdays prepare the vessel

The discourse says the six workdays are for cultivating the intellectual love through contemplation. That effort prepares a "vessel" that allows the additional soul to be revealed on Shabbat. It quotes the Zohar: "there is no labor like the labor of love."

So Shabbat is not a break from the spiritual life. It is the fruit of the week. For the Tanya's view of the workday, see [Tanya at Work](/learn/tanya-at-work).

## Shabbat prayer reaches into the week

The discourse also says that heartfelt Shabbat prayer can draw a glow of the additional soul into the coming week. Weekday prayer then becomes a "ladder" linking Shabbat's holiness to the workdays, so that weekday actions also take on Shabbat's holiness.

## Joy

Chapter 11 of Igeret HaTeshuvah says that worship should be joyful, and that sorrow (which comes from the body and animal soul) and joy (from the Divine soul) can coexist because they have different sources. This chapter is not about Shabbat, but it helps explain why Shabbat is a day of delight.

## Common questions

### What is the additional soul on Shabbat?
The Talmud teaches that an additional soul is given on Shabbat eve and taken back when Shabbat ends. The Alter Rebbe explains it as a revelation of the deepest level of the soul, the yechida, felt as a rise in spiritual life-force.

### Where does the Alter Rebbe teach about the Shabbat soul?
In his Torah Ohr, a collection of his teachings arranged by the Torah portions. The Tanya's chapters on prayer and joy help explain it.

### Is the additional soul extra physical energy?
No. The Alter Rebbe's discourse says it is a revelation of the soul's deepest level and is felt as a rise in spiritual life-force, not as physical energy.

### How do weekdays connect to Shabbat?
The discourse says that cultivating love of G-d through contemplation on the weekdays prepares a vessel for the additional soul on Shabbat, and heartfelt Shabbat prayer can carry a glow into the week.
