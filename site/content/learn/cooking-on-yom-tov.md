Yom Tov means "a good day": the festival days of Pesach, Shavuot, Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret, Simchat Torah and Rosh Hashanah. Like Shabbat, they are days of rest with candle lighting, Kiddush and festive meals. But there is one big difference: on Yom Tov we may cook and do other work needed for food that day.

## Why cooking is allowed

The Torah says about the festivals: "No work may be done on them, except what is needed for each person to eat; only that may be done" (Exodus 12:16). This is called **ochel nefesh**, work for food. So on Yom Tov you may cook, bake, fry and warm food for the meals of the day.

Yom Kippur is different. It has all the rules of Shabbat, and of course we fast. When Yom Tov falls on Shabbat, all the Shabbat rules apply.

## Fire: use an existing flame

- **You may not create a new fire** on Yom Tov. No striking a match or using a lighter.
- **You may transfer fire** from a flame already burning, such as a candle or a pilot light, to light the stove or a candle.
- **Many families light a 24- or 48-hour candle** before Yom Tov, so a flame is always ready.
- **Turning off a flame** is generally not allowed, and lowering it has its own rules. Electric stoves and ovens raise their own questions too. Ask the Rabbi about your kitchen.

## Only for today

Cooking on Yom Tov is allowed only for that day. You may not cook on the first day of Yom Tov for the second day, or on Yom Tov for the Shabbat after it.

The one exception: when Yom Tov falls on Thursday and Friday, or on Friday alone, you make an [eruv tavshilin](/learn/eruv-tavshilin) before the holiday begins. This lets you cook on Friday for Shabbat.

The same applies to second-night candles: they are lit after nightfall, from an existing flame, not before.

## Carrying

On Shabbat we carry outside only within an [eruv](/learn/eruv). On Yom Tov, carrying outside is generally permitted for something needed that day, such as a house key, a siddur, or food for a meal at a friend's home, even where there is no eruv. If Yom Tov falls on Shabbat, the Shabbat rules apply. Ask the Rabbi about specific cases.

## What is still not allowed

Many melachot remain forbidden on Yom Tov just as on Shabbat: writing, driving, using a phone, switching electricity on or off for non-food purposes, and doing business. Some food-related work, like picking fruit, is also not allowed. [Muktzeh](/learn/muktzeh) applies too. See the [39 melachot](/learn/thirty-nine-melachot).

## Practical tips

1. **Light a long-burning candle** before Yom Tov begins.
2. **Do what you can ahead**: food that tastes just as good made the day before is best made before Yom Tov.
3. **Check the calendar** for a holiday on Thursday or Friday, and remember the eruv tavshilin.
4. **Set timers** for lights before Yom Tov, just as for Shabbat.
5. **Ask early**: if you have a new oven or a hot plate, ask the Rabbi before the holiday.

## Common questions

### Can I cook on Rosh Hashanah?
Yes, for that day's meals, using an existing flame. You may not cook on the first day for the second day.

### Can I strike a match on Yom Tov?
No. Creating a new fire is not allowed. Light from a flame that is already burning.

### Do I need an eruv to carry on a holiday?
Usually not, for things you need on Yom Tov itself. When Yom Tov is on Shabbat, the Shabbat rules apply. Ask the Rabbi about specific cases.

### Can I cook on Yom Tov for Shabbat?
Only if you made an [eruv tavshilin](/learn/eruv-tavshilin) before the holiday. If you forgot, call the Rabbi.
