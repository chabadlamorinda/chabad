Shabbat with kids works best when children are part of every step: helping prepare, lighting candles, receiving a blessing and leading songs at the table. Small traditions done every week give children memories and Jewish identity that last a lifetime.

## Why Shabbat matters for children

Children learn from what we do more than from what we say. A weekly Shabbat table where phones are put away, parents are present, and there is singing and good food teaches that being Jewish is joyful. It is also one of the best ways to build closeness as a family. See our guide to [Jewish parenting](/learn/jewish-parenting).

## Before Shabbat: get kids involved

- **Bake or braid challah** together. Even little hands can roll dough. See our [challah guide](/learn/challah).
- **Set the table,** fold napkins, or make place cards.
- **Make a tzedakah moment.** Let children put coins into a charity box right before candle lighting.
- **Bath and Shabbat clothes,** so everyone feels the day is special.

## Friday night, step by step

1. **Candle lighting.** In Chabad custom, girls light their own candle from about age three, with the blessing, before their mother. It is a beautiful mitzvah for girls to take on. See our [candle lighting guide](/learn/shabbat-candles).
2. **Bless the children.** Parents place their hands on each child's head:
   - For boys: "Yesimcha Elokim k'Efrayim v'chiMenasheh," "May G-d make you like Ephraim and Manasseh."
   - For girls: "Yesimeich Elokim k'Sarah, Rivkah, Rachel v'Leah," "May G-d make you like Sarah, Rebecca, Rachel and Leah."
   - Then for all: the Priestly Blessing, "May G-d bless you and protect you..."
3. **Sing Shalom Aleichem.** Kids love the tune, and it welcomes the angels of Shabbat.
4. **Kiddush.** Children can say their own Kiddush over grape juice in a small cup after the parent. See our [Kiddush guide](/learn/kiddush).
5. **Washing and Hamotzi.** Let kids help pass the salt and the challah.
6. **Parsha time.** Ask a question about this week's Torah portion and offer a small prize for answers. Find ideas on our [parsha page](/parsha).
7. **Songs and stories.** End with favorite Shabbat songs and Birkat Hamazon.

## Shabbat day ideas

- **Synagogue with kids.** Bring a snack, sit near the door, and stay for the part they can enjoy. Many synagogues have a children's program.
- **Shabbat-friendly play.** Board games without writing, puzzles, books, blocks, dolls and walks. Ask the Rabbi about specific games and toys.
- **Visit friends** or invite families with kids for lunch.
- **A Shabbat party.** Some communities hold a weekly children's gathering with songs, Torah stories and treats.
- **Quiet reading time** with Jewish storybooks.

## Keep it positive

- Explain what we do, not only what we do not do. "On Shabbat we rest, so we play with blocks instead of the tablet."
- Make special Shabbat-only treats and toys.
- Let older kids choose a song, invite a friend, or give a short speech.
- Be patient. If a child is tired, a shorter meal is better than a battle.

## Common questions

### At what age should kids start keeping Shabbat?
Children grow into Shabbat gradually. Start from the very beginning with the joyful parts: candles, blessings and songs. As they grow, teach them more of the details in age-appropriate ways.

### Do young children have to stop using screens?
The goal is to train children step by step. Even little ones can learn that Shabbat is a screen-free day. Ask the Rabbi about specific situations.

### What if my kids find the meal too long?
Keep it short and lively, let them leave the table to play between courses, and bring them back for dessert and songs.

### Can kids help light candles if they are boys?
Boys can stand with their mother and sister and watch, answer "Amen," and help set up. The lighting itself is traditionally done by the women and girls.
