A Friday night dinner has a shape to it, and once you know the order it feels natural. Nothing here needs to be perfect. If you light candles and share a meal, you have made Shabbat. Here is the whole evening, step by step, with a few simple words for each part.

## 1. Light the candles

Before sunset (see this week's time at [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times)), the woman of the home, or anyone present, lights two candles, waves her hands toward her face three times, covers her eyes and says: *Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu Melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav v'tzivanu l'hadlik ner shel Shabbat kodesh.* This is a wonderful moment for a quiet prayer for your family. Then say "Good Shabbos" or "Shabbat Shalom." Details: [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).

## 2. Welcome the angels: Shalom Aleichem

When everyone is at the table, sing or say Shalom Aleichem, a song that greets the angels who accompany us home from the synagogue. The words are in any siddur or bentcher; if you do not know the tune, read it slowly together. See [Shalom Aleichem](/learn/shalom-aleichem).

## 3. Eishet Chayil: a song for the woman of the home

Many families then sing Eishet Chayil, "A Woman of Valor" (Proverbs 31), in honor of the wife and mother and of the Shechinah, G-d's presence, that rests in a Jewish home. See [Eishet Chayil](/learn/eishet-chayil).

## 4. Bless the children

In many communities parents place their hands on each child's head and say a short blessing (ask the Rabbi about your family's custom): for boys, "May G-d make you like Ephraim and Menashe"; for girls, "May G-d make you like Sarah, Rebecca, Rachel and Leah," followed by the Priestly Blessing. Add your own whispered words. Children remember this their whole lives. See [blessing your children](/learn/blessing-your-children).

## 5. Kiddush over wine

Stand, fill a cup of kosher wine or grape juice, and say Kiddush, the blessing that declares the day holy. If you are new to it, you can simply say the blessing over wine, *Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu Melech ha'olam, borei pri hagafen*, and read the Kiddush text in English. Everyone takes a sip. See [Kiddush](/learn/kiddush).

## 6. Wash the hands

Before eating bread, each person pours water from a cup over the right hand and then the left (the custom is three times on each), says *...al netilat yadayim*, and dries the hands. From now until the bread is eaten, we do not speak. See [washing the hands](/learn/netilat-yadayim).

## 7. Hamotzi over two challahs

Uncover the two challahs, lift them, and say *Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu Melech ha'olam, hamotzi lechem min ha'aretz.* Slice, dip in a little salt, and pass a piece to everyone. Once you have eaten your piece, you may talk again. See [Hamotzi](/learn/hamotzi) and [challah](/learn/challah).

## 8. The meal: songs and a word of Torah

Eat slowly. Serve what you love; there is no required menu, though fish, soup and chicken are classics. Between courses sing a *zemer* or two ([Shabbat songs](/learn/zemirot)) and share a thought from the week's Torah portion ([the weekly parsha](/learn/weekly-parsha)). Ask each person for one good thing from their week. This is the heart of the evening.

## 9. Birkat Hamazon: thanking G-d for the meal

At the end, say or sing Birkat Hamazon, the Grace after Meals, from a bentcher. If it is long for you, say the first paragraph in English and build from there. See [Birkat Hamazon](/learn/birkat-hamazon). Then sit a little longer. On Shabbat, no one is in a hurry.

## If you only do a few steps

Candles, Kiddush, Hamotzi and a meal together are the core; add the rest over the weeks. One Shabbat dinner is a whole world. For planning the food and the table, see [hosting your first Shabbat dinner](/learn/hosting-your-first-shabbat-dinner) and [Shabbat for beginners](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners).

## Common questions

### What if I do not know the Hebrew blessings?
Say them in English, or read them in transliteration from a bentcher or siddur. G-d understands every language. Come to a Shabbat dinner at Chabad and hear them a few times; they become familiar quickly.

### Can I do Friday night dinner alone or as a couple?
Yes. Every part of the evening works for one person or two. See [Shabbat alone](/learn/shabbat-alone).

### How long does the whole dinner take?
From candle lighting to Birkat Hamazon, most families take an hour and a half to two hours, but there is no clock. The order matters more than the length.

### Do I need special items?
Two candles, a cup, kosher wine or grape juice, two challahs and a cloth to cover them are all you need. A bentcher with the blessings helps; ask the Rabbi if you need one.
