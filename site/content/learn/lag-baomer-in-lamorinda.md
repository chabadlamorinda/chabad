Lag BaOmer (ל"ג בעומר) is the 33rd day of the Omer, the count between Passover and Shavuot, on the 18th of Iyar. It marks the day the plague among Rabbi Akiva's students stopped and the day Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai passed away, and we celebrate with bonfires, outings, music and children's parades. In Lafayette, Chabad of Lamorinda holds a Lag BaOmer BBQ. Call 925-384-7242 or see the [events calendar](/events) for the time and place.

## What is Lag BaOmer?

"Lag" is the number 33 in Hebrew letters: lamed (30) and gimmel (3). The Omer is a 49-day count between Passover and Shavuot, and its weeks are traditionally a time of mourning. On Lag BaOmer, the mourning lifts. Music, dancing, weddings and haircuts are permitted again, according to the custom for the Omer. For the dates, see [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays), and learn about the count in [Counting the Omer](/learn/counting-the-omer).

## Rabbi Akiva's students

The Talmud teaches that thousands of Rabbi Akiva's students died in a plague because they "did not act respectfully towards each other." The deaths stopped on Lag BaOmer. Chabad.org highlights the lesson: love and respect for every fellow Jew. Read about [Rabbi Akiva](/learn/rabbi-akiva) and [Ahavat Yisrael](/learn/ahavat-yisrael).

## Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and the Zohar

Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, known as Rashbi, was a second-century sage who taught the hidden, mystical dimension of the Torah, and the Zohar is the classic book of Kabbalah attributed to him and his students. Chabad.org tells us he asked his students to mark the day of his passing as "the day of my joy." Each year, tens of thousands visit his resting place in Meron, in northern Israel. Hiding from the Romans in a cave, Rashbi and his son were sustained by a carob tree and a spring, which is why carob is a Lag BaOmer food.

## Customs: bonfires, bows and picnics

- **Bonfires:** fires reflect the fiery spirit of the Torah's inner teachings.
- **Bows and arrows:** people go outdoors, and children play with bows and arrows, recalling Rashbi's hiding.
- **Parades:** since the 1950s, the Lubavitcher Rebbe encouraged children's Lag BaOmer parades around the world.
- **Upsherin:** many families give a three-year-old boy his first haircut on Lag BaOmer. See [Upsherin](/learn/upsherin).
- **Weddings and music:** joy returns after the Omer's restrictions.

## Fire safety in a dry spring

Lag BaOmer comes in a California spring when grass can be dry. Please do not light a backyard bonfire or fire pit on your own. Follow the official fire district and utility instructions, and any local burn rules, and ask your fire district if you are unsure. A BBQ or a celebration organized in a proper, permitted place is a much safer way to keep the custom. The point of the fire is the light of Torah, which does not require flames in your yard.

## Common questions

### What is Lag BaOmer?
It is the 33rd day of the Omer, on the 18th of Iyar, when we celebrate the end of the plague among Rabbi Akiva's students and the memory of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai.

### Why do we light bonfires on Lag BaOmer?
Bonfires symbolize the fiery spirit of the Torah's mystical teachings revealed by Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai.

### Is there a Lag BaOmer event in Lafayette?
Yes. Chabad of Lamorinda holds a Lag BaOmer BBQ in Lafayette. Call 925-384-7242 for this year's time and place.

### Can I have a backyard bonfire for Lag BaOmer?
Please check with your local fire district first and follow all official rules. This is not legal or safety advice, so when in doubt, skip the fire and join a community event.

### Why are there bows and arrows on Lag BaOmer?
It is a custom to play outdoors with bows and arrows, recalling the time Rashbi hid from the Romans. Ask the Rabbi if you want to know more.

### Can you get married or have a haircut on Lag BaOmer?
Yes. The Omer's restrictions lift on Lag BaOmer. Ask the Rabbi about your specific date and custom.
