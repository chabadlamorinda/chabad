Kiddush is the blessing over a cup of wine or grape juice that begins the Shabbat and holiday meals. It fulfills the Torah's command to "remember the Shabbat day to keep it holy" by declaring out loud that this day is sacred.

## What Kiddush means

Kiddush (קִידּוּשׁ) means "sanctification." The Torah tells us to remember Shabbat, and the Sages explain that this means saying words that mark the day as holy. They instituted saying these words over wine, a drink that brings joy and is used for important moments in Jewish life.

There are two Kiddushes:

- **Friday night Kiddush** is the main one, said before the evening meal. It includes the Torah verses about G-d finishing creation (Vayechulu), the blessing over wine and a blessing on the holiness of Shabbat.
- **Shabbat day Kiddush** is said before the lunch meal. It is shorter: a few verses about Shabbat followed by the blessing over wine.

Holidays have their own Kiddush text, which you can find in any siddur or holiday prayer book.

## What you need

- **Wine or grape juice.** Kosher wine or grape juice is required.
- **A cup** that holds at least a revi'it, roughly 3 to 5 ounces depending on the opinion. A standard Kiddush cup is fine.
- **Challah** on the table, covered with a cloth while Kiddush is said.
- **A siddur or Kiddush card** with the text.

## How to make Kiddush on Friday night, step by step

1. After candle lighting and evening prayers, gather at the table.
2. Cover the [challah](/learn/challah).
3. Fill the cup to the top. Some add a few drops of water.
4. Hold the cup in your right hand (left if you are left-handed), and stand or sit according to your family custom.
5. Read the Kiddush text. Everyone listening answers "Amen" after each blessing.
6. The person making Kiddush drinks most of a cheekful, ideally most of the cup, while sitting.
7. Pour a little for everyone else at the table.
8. Then wash hands and say Hamotzi over the challah.

## The blessing over wine

**Hebrew:** בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הַגָּפֶן

**Transliteration:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, borei p'ri hagafen.

**Translation:** Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who creates the fruit of the vine.

On Friday night, a second, longer blessing follows, ending "Baruch atah Ado-nai, m'kadesh haShabbat," "Blessed are You, L-rd, who sanctifies Shabbat."

## Who makes Kiddush

Men and women are equally obligated in Kiddush. Usually one person says it on behalf of everyone, and the listeners fulfill the mitzvah by listening carefully and answering "Amen." Children love to say it too, which is a wonderful way to teach them.

## Common questions

### Can I make Kiddush on grape juice?
Yes. Kosher grape juice is fully acceptable for Kiddush, and many families use it, especially for children or anyone who prefers not to drink alcohol.

### What if I do not have wine or grape juice?
On Friday night, you may make Kiddush over the two challah loaves, saying Hamotzi instead of the wine blessing. On Shabbat day, other drinks can sometimes be used. Ask the Rabbi for the details.

### Why do we cover the challah during Kiddush?
Normally bread's blessing comes before wine's, so we cover the challah to "spare its feelings" while it waits. This teaches sensitivity to others' feelings. It also recalls the manna in the desert, which was covered by dew above and below.

### Do I have to eat a meal after Kiddush?
Yes. Kiddush must be followed by a meal in the same place. On Shabbat morning, a snack of cake or crackers counts, which is why synagogues offer a "Kiddush" after services.

### Can I hear Kiddush in synagogue instead of at home?
On Friday night, the Kiddush said in synagogue is a custom, and each household still makes its own Kiddush before the meal. If you will eat at someone else's table, you can fulfill the mitzvah through their Kiddush.
