If you feel you are not good enough, the Tanya answers that your worth does not come from how you feel today or what you have done. The Jewish soul, it teaches, is "a part of G-d above," and the person who keeps struggling to choose well is exactly who the book is written for.

## "Am I a bad Jew?"

Many people in Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda and everywhere carry a quiet question: "Am I Jewish enough? Am I good enough?" Maybe you do not keep Shabbat, do not know the prayers, or feel far from G-d. See [not religious? you are welcome](/learn/not-religious-welcome) and [self-esteem in Judaism](/learn/self-esteem-jewish-view).

The [Tanya](/learn/tanya), by Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi, the founder of Chabad, speaks to this question directly. We share it in our own words, from the Tanya pages on Chabad.org.

## Your soul: a part of G-d above

Chapter 2 teaches that the Jewish soul is truly "a part of G-d above," and the Alter Rebbe says this should be taken literally, not as a poetic image. Jewish souls are described as coming from G-d's own wisdom, as a child comes from a parent.

The chapter also says that souls differ in rank, and that even the lowest soul remains connected to its source. It explains that a soul's level comes from above and not from physical actions or family background, so a high soul can be born to a humble parent. This is one reason why no one is a "lost cause" in this teaching. For more on the soul, see [the soul in Judaism](/learn/the-soul).

## The struggle is normal

The Tanya's main subject is the [beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni), the "intermediate person." Chapter 12 says that he still feels the pulls of his animal soul but does not let them lead his thought, speech or action. He does not have to be a tzaddik. The struggle itself is the picture of an ordinary, healthy spiritual life. It is not proof that something is wrong with you.

## When "not good enough" becomes heavy

Chapter 27 has a gentle and surprising lesson. When unwanted thoughts keep coming and leave a person sad, the Alter Rebbe says this distress is not a sign of piety. It comes from not knowing one's place, as if one should already be a tzaddik. Instead a person should rejoice each time he turns his mind away from a wrong thought, because turning away is itself a mitzvah and a victory.

The chapter says to feel some concern so as not to become indifferent, but not to be sorely troubled, even if the struggle goes on for a lifetime. For many readers this is a relief: being hard on yourself is not holiness.

This is spiritual guidance. If feeling worthless is constant, or you feel hopeless, professional help is also right and good, and the Tanya's words are not a substitute. Ask the Rabbi about your own situation.

## What you do not need

- You do not need to feel holy or spiritual.
- You do not need to be perfect before you begin.
- You do not need to know much. Many Jews start with one mitzvah.

## What you can do

These are suggestions in the spirit of the Tanya, not quotations.

1. Do one mitzvah this week, such as lighting Shabbat candles or giving tzedakah.
2. When an unwanted thought comes, do not argue with it; turn your attention elsewhere, as Chapter 27 describes.
3. Speak with someone who can encourage you.
4. Come to learn, with no pressure and no prior knowledge.

## Common questions

### What does the Tanya say about self-worth?
The Tanya teaches that every Jew's soul is "a part of G-d above" and that the ordinary struggling person, the beinoni, is the book's central figure. Your worth does not depend on how you feel today.

### Is feeling not good enough a Jewish value?
Humility is. Feeling worthless is not the same thing. In Chapter 27 the Alter Rebbe says that being crushed by unwanted thoughts comes from not knowing one's place, and he encourages joy in each small victory instead. See [self-esteem in Judaism](/learn/self-esteem-jewish-view).

### Am I a bad Jew if I do not keep all the mitzvot?
Judaism welcomes every Jew. The Tanya's focus is on the next good choice, not on a score. See [not religious? you are welcome](/learn/not-religious-welcome) and ask the Rabbi for guidance.

### What if I feel depressed, not only "not good enough"?
The Tanya's guidance is spiritual. If you feel low for a long time or feel hopeless, professional help is also right. The Rabbi can help you take the first step.

### What is a beinoni?
It is the "intermediate" person who still feels temptation but does not give in. See [the beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni).
