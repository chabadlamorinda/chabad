Someone stands at a freeway exit with a sign. Someone at the door asks for help with rent. A stranger at the grocery store is a few dollars short. What does Judaism say?

## Don't turn anyone away empty-handed

The Torah says, "Do not harden your heart or close your hand against your needy brother; open your hand to him." Jewish law teaches that when a poor person asks, we should give something, even a little, rather than send them away with nothing. Even a kind word and a smile are a form of giving.

## Dignity first

Maimonides taught that how we give matters as much as what we give. Give with a pleasant face, look the person in the eye, and say something kind. Embarrassing someone in need is a serious wrong. The highest level of giving, he wrote, is helping a person become self-sufficient, with a loan, a job or a partnership.

## What if they might not be honest?

The Talmud says we should be grateful even to those who ask dishonestly, because they keep us in the habit of giving and keep us from becoming hard-hearted. For a small gift, Jewish law does not ask us to investigate. For larger needs, like rent or food for a family, it is fine and wise to check, or to help directly: buy the groceries, pay the bill.

## Practical ideas

- **Keep small bills or coins handy** in the car for people who ask.
- **Carry a gift card or snack bars** if you prefer to give food.
- **Give through someone who knows**: the Rabbi helps local families quietly and with dignity, and can make sure help gets where it is needed.
- **Make a habit of daily giving** with a [pushka](/learn/pushka) at home.

## Common questions

### How much should I give someone on the street?
There is no set amount; a small gift given kindly fulfills the mitzvah. Your yearly giving can be guided by [maaser](/learn/maaser), a tenth of income.

### Is it better to give to an organization?
Both matter. Organizations can help in ways we cannot, and the person in front of you is also a mitzvah. See [tzedakah priorities](/learn/tzedakah-priorities).

### I have nothing to give right now. What do I say?
Say kindly that you can't today, and wish them well. A warm word is never wasted.
