Many Jewish couples today do a simple genetic test before they marry or start a family. It is one of the ways we fulfill the Torah's charge to guard our health and the health of our children. This guide explains the basics. It is not medical advice: for medical questions, please speak with your doctor and a genetic counselor.

## Why screening matters

Some inherited conditions are more common among Ashkenazi Jews, and some among Sephardic and Mizrachi Jews and other groups. The best known is Tay-Sachs disease. Others include Canavan disease, familial dysautonomia, cystic fibrosis and Gaucher disease, among more.

Most of these conditions are recessive. A person can carry a gene and be perfectly healthy. A problem may arise only when both parents carry a gene for the same condition. Screening helps couples know this ahead of time, while they have the most choices.

## Two ways to be screened

### Dor Yeshorim
Dor Yeshorim is a confidential screening program created for the Jewish community, widely used in the Orthodox world. People are tested, often before or early in dating, and receive an identification number instead of their own results. When two people are considering marriage, they can check whether they are compatible. If both carry the same condition, they are told they are not a compatible match. If not, they are not told anything else. The goal is to prevent suffering while avoiding stigma for carriers.

### Through your doctor
Your own doctor or OB-GYN can order carrier screening, often as an expanded panel that covers many conditions. You receive your personal results, and a genetic counselor can explain what they mean. Many couples do this before a pregnancy.

Each approach has advantages. A doctor and genetic counselor can help you decide which tests are right for you.

## When to test

- **Before getting serious,** if you use Dor Yeshorim, so that compatibility can be checked early.
- **Before marriage or before trying to conceive,** for testing through a doctor.
- **If you are already expecting,** speak with your doctor promptly.

## Common questions

### Is screening only for Ashkenazi Jews?
No. Different conditions are more common in different groups, including Sephardic and Mizrachi Jews. Couples from mixed backgrounds may also want screening. Ask your doctor which panel fits your family.

### Does being a carrier mean something is wrong with me?
No. Almost everyone carries some recessive genes. Carriers are usually completely healthy.

### Is Dor Yeshorim confidential?
Yes. It is built so that individual carrier results are not shared, only whether two people are compatible.
