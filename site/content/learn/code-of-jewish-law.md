The Shulchan Aruch (שולחן ערוך), "the Set Table," is the Code of Jewish Law: a clear, practical summary of how to live according to the Torah, from waking up in the morning to Shabbat, kosher food, marriage and business. It was written by Rabbi Yosef Caro in the 1500s and is the foundation of Jewish law today.

## What does "Shulchan Aruch" mean?

Chabad.org explains that the name means "Set Table." The Talmud is a vast sea of discussion of about 2,700 pages; the Shulchan Aruch takes the conclusions and sets them out like a meal on a table, ready to eat. If you want to know what to do, rather than why, this is where to look. For the background of Jewish law itself, see [what is halacha](/learn/what-is-halacha).

## Who wrote it?

Two authors, who never met, made the Shulchan Aruch what it is:

- **Rabbi Yosef Caro (1488-1575)**, a Sephardic sage in Safed in the Land of Israel, wrote the main text. He meant it as a review for students of his larger work, the *Beit Yosef*, which explains the reasoning.
- **Rabbi Moshe Isserles, the Rema**, an Ashkenazi rabbi in Cracow, Poland, was writing a similar book when he saw Rabbi Caro's. He stopped and instead added comments wherever Ashkenazi custom differs. He called them the *Mapah*, "the Tablecloth," a play on a "set table."

The first edition (Venice, about 1565) had only Rabbi Caro's text. Later editions print both, with Rabbi Caro's words in block letters and the Rema's in Rashi script. Today Ashkenazi Jews follow the Rema where he differs, and Sephardic Jews generally follow Rabbi Caro. That is why you may hear "ask the Rabbi what your custom is."

## The four parts

Following an earlier work called the *Arba Turim*, the code has four sections:

1. **Orach Chaim**: prayer, Shabbat, holidays and daily life.
2. **Yoreh Deah**: kosher food, mourning and other matters people often bring to a rabbi.
3. **Even HaEzer**: marriage and divorce.
4. **Choshen Mishpat**: money and civil law.

Chabad.org notes that its opening line urges a person to rise "like a lion" to serve the Creator.

## Versions for everyday readers

- **Kitzur Shulchan Aruch.** Rabbi Shlomo Ganzfried (1804-1886) wrote an original, short and readable version for ordinary people. It is a good first book. See [a Jewish home library](/learn/jewish-home-library).
- **Mishnah Berurah.** The Chafetz Chaim (1839-1933) wrote a six-volume commentary on Orach Chaim, widely used for daily life.
- **Shulchan Aruch HaRav.** Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, the Alter Rebbe and founder of Chabad, wrote a longer version at the request of the Maggid of Mezritch. It explains the reasoning for each law and gives a final ruling that takes later opinions into account. See [the Chabad Rebbes](/learn/chabad-rebbes).

Chabad.org points out that the name has become a word for Jewish law itself, and that someone who lives closely by it is sometimes called a "Shulchan Aruch Yid."

## Why was it controversial?

At first, critics worried that people would only learn the short rulings and skip deeper study. The Rema's glosses answered part of the problem by including the Ashkenazi view. That tension is still true today: a code is a tool for practice, not a substitute for learning with a teacher.

## How can a beginner use it?

1. Start with one topic, such as Shabbat or the blessings, in a simple English book.
2. Learn a short law each day; small steps add up. See [daily Torah study](/learn/daily-torah-study).
3. Remember that real situations are rarely like the book. Ask the Rabbi what to do: a code gives the rules, and a rabbi helps apply them to your life.

## Common questions

### What is the Shulchan Aruch?
The Shulchan Aruch is the Code of Jewish Law by Rabbi Yosef Caro, a practical summary of what to do in every area of Jewish life.

### Who wrote the Shulchan Aruch?
Rabbi Yosef Caro of Safed (1488-1575) wrote it, and Rabbi Moshe Isserles (the Rema) of Cracow added glosses for Ashkenazi custom.

### What are the four parts of the Shulchan Aruch?
Orach Chaim (daily life, prayer, Shabbat, holidays), Yoreh Deah (kosher and more), Even HaEzer (marriage and divorce) and Choshen Mishpat (civil law).

### What is the Kitzur Shulchan Aruch?
It is a short, readable book of law for ordinary readers, written by Rabbi Shlomo Ganzfried.

### What is the Shulchan Aruch HaRav?
It is the version written by the Alter Rebbe, founder of Chabad, which explains the reasoning of each law and gives a final ruling.

### Can I decide my own halacha from the Shulchan Aruch?
It is a great guide to learn from, but practice in a specific case should be decided by a rabbi. Ask the Rabbi.
