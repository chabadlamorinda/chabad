According to the Tanya, you do not have to leave ordinary life to serve G-d: when a Jew uses food, drink, a home or everyday tools in G-d's service, those things are lifted into holiness. Chapter 37 says each Jew elevates a share of the world this way.

For families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, whose days are full of carpools, cooking, work and errands, this is an encouraging teaching. The Tanya does not set holiness against daily life. It says daily life is where holiness is built.

## The world is not the enemy

Chapter 37 of the Tanya says that every Jew elevates a share of the world by using worldly things, for body and soul, in G-d's service: eating, drinking, a home and everyday tools. Before they are used for a mitzvah, the chapter explains, permitted things belong to kelipat nogah, the "mixed" realm that gives life to the physical world. A mitzvah lifts them into holiness.

Some things cannot be lifted. Chapter 37 gives the example of fruit that may not be eaten (orlah): it draws its life from the completely impure forces, so a mitzvah done with it cannot raise it to holiness. The same chapter speaks of tzedakah from money that was not obtained through theft. This is one reason Judaism cares about [honesty](/learn/business-ethics) and what is permitted. The teaching is for permitted things.

## Food that gives strength for a mitzvah

Chapter 37 says that the food and drink that became blood and gave a person strength for a mitzvah are themselves elevated, since they supplied the energy for the act. In the Tanya's picture, a meal is not just fuel: when its energy goes into prayer, learning, kindness or a mitzvah, the meal itself is part of that mitzvah.

That is also why a blessing matters before and after eating. See [Jewish eating](/learn/eating-as-a-mitzvah) for the practical side, including blessings and sharing meals.

## The body as a partner

Chapter 37 says the 248 positive mitzvot correspond to the limbs of the body, drawing G-d's light into them, while the 365 prohibitions correspond to the blood vessels, channeling life-force toward holiness. Chapter 37 also notes that speech mitzvot, such as Torah study and prayer, elevate the animal soul, because speech is a physical act and "thought is not a substitute for speech."

The Tanya's attitude is therefore not ascetic. The body and the animal soul take part in holiness. This fits Chapter 35, which says that practical mitzvot, performed with the body, draw G-d's light onto the body and the animal soul itself. For more on the inner struggle, see [the beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni).

## A home for G-d

Why would G-d want a physical world full of ordinary things? The Tanya's answer is that He wanted a dwelling place in the lowest realm. See [Making a Home for G-d](/learn/making-a-home-for-g-d). Chapter 37 says that when all Israel keeps all 613 mitzvot, the world becomes a "chariot" for G-d, a vehicle for His presence.

## What this looks like in an ordinary day

These are examples of the Tanya's idea, not instructions from the chapter:

- Say a blessing before you eat, and use the energy for something good.
- Make your home a place for [Shabbat](/learn/shabbat) and guests.
- Do your work honestly. See [Tanya at Work](/learn/tanya-at-work).
- Give from what you earn. See [Tanya on Money](/learn/tanya-on-money).

## Common questions

### Can eating be spiritual in Judaism?
Yes. The Tanya says the food and drink that give strength for a mitzvah are themselves elevated, and that each Jew lifts part of the world by using worldly things in G-d's service. A blessing and good intention make it so.

### What does the Tanya say about ordinary things?
Chapter 37 says permitted things, such as food, drink and tools, belong to the "mixed" realm of kelipat nogah and are lifted into holiness when used for a mitzvah. Forbidden things, such as orlah fruit, cannot be lifted.

### Do I have to give up pleasures to be spiritual?
The Tanya does not teach that. Chapter 37 describes permitted physical things being raised through a mitzvah. Ask the Rabbi about specific questions of law and practice.

### What is holiness in everyday life, according to Chabad?
Chapter 37 describes holiness as the result of using the physical world, with its food, homes and tools, in G-d's service. See [what is holiness](/learn/what-is-holiness).

### Is this a halachic ruling about what to eat?
No. This guide explains Tanya's teaching, not law. For what is permitted, see [Kosher](/learn/kosher) or ask the Rabbi.
