Your baby's first Chanukah is simple: light the menorah at home, hold your baby close so the little eyes can see the lights, sing a song, and let the glow become the very first Chanukah memory. A baby is too young to light or understand the holiday, but Jewish tradition values bringing even the youngest into the warmth of a Jewish home.

## Does a baby have to do anything on Chanukah?

No. Babies have no obligations. The parents light, as the household's mitzvah (see [How to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah)), and the baby takes it in through the senses: the light, the songs, the smell of latkes, and being held by people who are happy. Parents are encouraged to involve children in lighting as they grow, and every child begins somewhere. The beginning is simply being there.

Chanukah (חֲנֻכָּה) means "dedication," and a baby's first Chanukah is a lovely time to dedicate a new life to light.

## Simple traditions for the first Chanukah

1. **Light with the baby in your arms.** Choose a calm moment. Let one parent hold the baby at a safe distance from the flame while the other lights.
2. **Sing.** Babies love their parents' voices. Try the simple songs in [Chanukah songs](/learn/chanukah-songs), and sing Haneirot Halalu while the candles burn.
3. **Sit for a moment.** It is customary to stay near the lights for the first half hour. A baby in a lap, a quiet song and the glow make that time easy.
4. **Take a picture of the menorah.** A photo each year lets your child look back one day and see how the family grew.
5. **Give tzedakah in the baby's name.** It is a custom to give more tzedakah during Chanukah and to teach children to give part of their gelt. For a baby, parents can give a little [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) on their behalf, or set aside coins in a box. See [Chanukah gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt).
6. **A first taste.** Babies who have started solids may enjoy a taste of something soft from the holiday table. Check with your pediatrician before offering any new food. See [Chanukah foods](/learn/chanukah-foods).

## Safety first

- **Keep the menorah high and stable.** Use a sturdy, fireproof surface away from curtains and out of children's reach. This matters even more with a crawling baby.
- **Choose steady lights.** Tea lights in a tray are sturdy and short. See [oil and candles](/learn/menorah-oil-and-candles).
- **Never leave the flame alone** with a baby or a toddler.
- **Keep the gelt away.** Chocolate coins and small dreidels are choking hazards for babies. Save the dreidel for later, and see [Chanukah for kids](/learn/chanukah-for-kids) when they are ready.
- **Keep it short if baby is tired.** The lights need to burn about half an hour, but you need not stay glued to them the whole time. If you do step away, someone should stay in the room or nearby.

## Common questions

### Can a baby light the Chanukah menorah?
No. A baby has no obligation to light. The parents light, with the baby nearby. As children grow, they can light their own menorah, with help.

### Is it safe to hold my baby during the lighting?
Hold your baby well away from the flame, ideally with another adult nearby, or let the baby watch from a seat. Keep the menorah on a stable surface out of reach.

### What should I say to my baby at the lighting?
Whatever feels natural. You can say the blessings aloud, tell the baby in a few words what Chanukah is about, or simply sing. The baby learns from your voice and joy.

### Do I need a special menorah for a baby?
No. Any menorah with lights at equal height will do, and many families keep a small one for the baby. See [how to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah). Keep it out of reach.

### What gifts are good for a baby's first Chanukah?
Soft books, wooden toys and music are simple choices. Avoid small parts. Some families also give tzedakah in the baby's name. See [Chanukah gifts](/learn/chanukah-gifts).

### When is the first Chanukah party for a baby?
Whenever you feel up to it. [Light Up Lafayette](/learn/light-up-lafayette) is free and open to everyone, and you can leave when the baby needs to.
