The 19th of Kislev, Yud Tes Kislev in Hebrew, is a joyous day for Chabad communities everywhere. It is called the **Rosh Hashanah of Chassidus**, the "new year" of the teachings of Chassidus, and it is marked with gatherings, singing and Torah study.

## What happened on 19 Kislev

**Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi** (1745 to 1812), known as the **Alter Rebbe**, was the founder of Chabad and the author of the [Tanya](/learn/tanya). In 1798, after false accusations by opponents of the Chassidic movement, he was arrested and taken to St. Petersburg, charged with treason. He was held for 53 days.

On the 19th of Kislev he was cleared and released. The Alter Rebbe saw his release as a sign from Heaven: the accusation in the heavenly court against spreading the inner teachings of the Torah had been dismissed as well. From then on he taught Chassidus more widely and more clearly than ever.

The same day is also the yahrzeit of his teacher, **Rabbi DovBer, the Maggid of Mezritch**, the successor of the [Baal Shem Tov](/learn/baal-shem-tov), who passed away on 19 Kislev 1772.

## How Yud Tes Kislev is celebrated

- **Farbrengens.** Chassidic gatherings with stories, songs (niggunim), words of Torah, and l'chaim, often continuing into the 20th of Kislev.
- **Dividing the Talmud.** Communities divide all the tractates of the Talmud among their members, so that together they study the whole Talmud during the year (Chalukat HaShas).
- **Learning Chassidus.** Many begin a new study, such as a daily portion of Tanya.
- **No Tachanun** is said, as on other festive days.

The traditional greeting is: *"L'shanah tovah b'limud ha'chassidus u'v'darkei ha'chassidus tikateivu v'teichateimu"*, "May you be inscribed and sealed for a good year in the study and ways of Chassidus."

## Why it matters today

Chassidus teaches that every Jew has a G-dly soul, that G-d is found in everyday life, and that serving G-d should be done with joy. The freedom the Alter Rebbe won to teach these ideas is the reason Chabad centers like ours exist around the world.

## Common questions

### When is Yud Tes Kislev?
On the 19th day of the Hebrew month Kislev, usually in late November or early December, about a week before Chanukah. Like every Jewish date, it begins the evening before. See our [Jewish calendar](/learn/jewish-calendar) guide.

### Do I have to be Chassidic to celebrate it?
No. A farbrengen is open to everyone, and the teachings of Chassidus are for every Jew.

### What is a farbrengen?
A warm gathering where people share stories, sing, learn and encourage one another to grow. There is usually food and l'chaim.

### Where can I start learning Chassidus?
The Tanya is the basic book of Chabad Chassidus. Our [Tanya](/learn/tanya) guide explains how to begin, and the Rabbi teaches a weekly Tanya class.
