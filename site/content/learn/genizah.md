Worn-out siddurim, tefillin, mezuzah scrolls and other holy items are not thrown away. Jewish law forbids destroying G-d's names and sacred texts, so they are set aside and buried in a process called genizah (גְּנִיזָה, "storing away"), also known as shaimos (שימוש, "[holy] names").

## What needs genizah

Chabad.org lists these items as needing genizah:

- Torah scrolls, tefillin (with their straps), and mezuzah scrolls
- Siddurim, Chumashim and other books of Torah, Talmud and prayer
- Printed or written material containing G-d's name in Hebrew, or Torah verses, in any language
- Mezuzah cases and tefillin boxes and straps that can no longer be used
- Children's schoolwork that contains such text

Three or more consecutive words from a verse, quoted intentionally, count. Pages from a Torah magazine or a Shabbat flyer may also qualify. If you are unsure, ask the Rabbi.

## What does not need genizah

Items used for a mitzvah are treated with respect but generally are not buried. These include a tallit (once the fringes are removed), a tallit bag, the Four Species, and sukkah roofing. Double-wrap them in paper or plastic before putting them in the trash. Kippot, pictures without text, and audio or video items can be discarded.

Please keep holy items out of regular recycling, and do not mix ordinary items into a genizah collection, since that is disrespectful.

## First: can it still be used?

Before you bury tefillin or a mezuzah, have a scribe check whether it is still kosher. Sometimes a small repair gives a lifetime of use. See [checking mezuzahs and tefillin](/learn/checking-mezuzahs-and-tefillin).

## How genizah works

Many synagogues and Jewish organizations collect holy items and arrange their burial, sometimes for a fee. You can also bury them yourself, covered with about 9.5 inches (24 cm) of earth, in a sturdy container: hard plastic for scrolls and tefillin, thick plastic bags for books.

If an item falls, we pick it up and kiss it. Read about the names of G-d in [shemot, the names of G-d](/learn/shemot-names-of-g-d).

## Common questions

### How do I dispose of an old siddur?
Do not throw it away. Set it aside for genizah burial through a synagogue or Jewish organization, or ask the Rabbi.

### What do I do with old tefillin?
First have a scribe check whether they can still be used. If not, they go to genizah.

### What do I do with an old mezuzah?
Have the scroll checked. If it is no longer kosher, it should be buried. The case can be reused. A case you no longer use is treated as an accessory to a holy item, so ask the Rabbi before discarding it.

### Can I recycle Hebrew books?
Books with Torah content, G-d's names or prayers need genizah, not recycling.

### What is shaimos?
Shaimos means "names," and refers to papers containing G-d's name that must be buried.
