After Chanukah, the leftover oil, wicks and wax from the menorah are treated with respect: the custom is to burn them after the holiday, and not to throw them away or use them for ordinary things. Oil still in the bottle and candles still in the box were never set aside, so you may use them as you like.

## Why leftovers are treated with respect

The fuel put into the menorah for the mitzvah was set aside for it. Because the Chanukah lights are holy, what remains of the fuel meant for the required burning time keeps some of that holiness. This idea is known in Jewish law as something "set aside" for a mitzvah.

## What is set aside, and what is not

According to Jewish law:

- **Fuel meant for the required time.** Oil or wax that was meant to burn during the half hour after nightfall is considered set aside, even if the flame went out early. It should be burned after Chanukah.
- **Extra fuel.** If your cups held more oil than needed, or your candles were larger than needed, the extra was never set aside, and you may use it as you wish. Some authorities are stricter.
- **A stricter view.** Other authorities say that any fuel placed in the menorah counts as set aside. The custom is to burn all leftover wicks, oil or wax after Chanukah.
- **Unused supplies.** Oil still in the bottle and candles still in the box are free to use in any way.

## What people do

The usual custom is to collect the stubs, wicks and leftover oil after Chanukah and burn them safely, for example in a fireproof dish outdoors or in a fireplace. Some have the custom to burn them together with the chametz on the morning before Passover; see [selling your chametz](/learn/selling-your-chametz). Because customs vary, ask the Rabbi if you are unsure.

## Small tips for next year

- Put in only as much oil as you need. If you think about how long you need the lights to burn, you will have less left over. But on Friday, put in enough to burn half an hour after nightfall; see [Chanukah on Shabbat](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat).
- Keep a small container for stubs and used wicks.
- Do not mix leftovers with ordinary trash or use them to light a barbecue or a dinner candle.

## Common questions

### What do I do with leftover Chanukah oil?
Oil that was in the menorah for the required burning time should be burned after Chanukah. Oil still in the bottle can be used for anything.

### What do I do with leftover menorah candles?
Candles still in the box are free to use. Stubs from the lighting are customarily burned after the holiday.

### Can I use Chanukah candle stubs for something else?
It is best to avoid ordinary use, and burn them. If you are unsure, ask the Rabbi.

### Can I throw away Chanukah wicks or candle stubs?
The custom is to burn them rather than throw them away. Some are careful about this more than others. Ask the Rabbi.

### Should leftover Chanukah candles be put in genizah?
Genizah is the respectful storage of worn holy books and writings; see [what genizah is](/learn/genizah). For leftover menorah fuel the custom is different: burn it. Ask the Rabbi if you are unsure.

### When do I burn the leftovers?
After Chanukah ends, at a convenient and safe time. Some people wait and burn them with the chametz on the morning before Passover. Ask the Rabbi what you should do.
