You start keeping Shabbat by choosing one small window, such as an hour or two on Friday evening while the candles burn, keeping it fully, and then adding a little more each week. Chabad.org gives this same advice to someone who wants to keep Shabbat but finds it hard: build up gradually, because trying to do everything at once leads to discouragement.

This is a growth plan for families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda who want to begin. It does not ask you to change your whole life this week. For the meaning of the day itself, see [Shabbat for beginners](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners).

## Step 1: Choose your starting window

Chabad.org suggests picking one dedicated time, roughly an hour or two on Friday evening after the candles are lit. During that window, keep the rules of Shabbat completely: no phone, no screens, and no turning lights on or off. If you use timers, you never need to touch the lights. See [electricity on Shabbat](/learn/electricity-on-shabbat) and [unplugging on Shabbat](/learn/unplugging-on-shabbat).

Light the candles at the right time. Check [this week's candle-lighting time](/shabbat-times) and see [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).

## Step 2: Make that window delicious

Chabad.org says to use the window to savor Shabbat so the restrictions fade into the background: a festive meal with family or friends, a few Shabbat blessings, and some singing. A simple Friday night is enough, with challah, grape juice and a Torah thought. See [Friday night dinner, step by step](/learn/friday-night-dinner-step-by-step) and [an easy Shabbat dinner menu](/learn/easy-shabbat-dinner-menu).

## Step 3: Add one thing for the whole day

Pick one or two activities to avoid for all of Shabbat, such as screens or shopping. Chabad.org notes that practical tools help; for example, timers mean you can leave lights on or off without touching a switch. Choose what is most meaningful for your family, and keep the rest as it is for now.

## Step 4: Grow every few weeks

Chabad.org suggests two ways to grow: keep Shabbat fully for a longer stretch of time, or add to your list of things you avoid. A few examples of next steps:

- Start the window a little earlier, or end it a little later.
- Add Shabbat morning, with a visit to services; see [first time at synagogue](/learn/first-time-at-synagogue).
- Add Shabbat lunch; see [Shabbat day](/learn/shabbat-day).
- Learn the [39 categories of work](/learn/thirty-nine-melachot), one at a time.

Do not rush. A step you can keep is better than a step you drop.

## Step 5: Don't forget the spiritual side

Chabad.org reminds us that Shabbat is more than a list of restrictions. Make room for prayer, a few minutes of quiet and the holiness of the day. See [Shabbat day](/learn/shabbat-day).

## Step 6: Find your community

Chabad.org encourages seeking a local Chabad House. Many people begin by being a guest, as Chabad.org describes: experience Shabbat at someone else's table a few times, then try it at home until it feels "happy and comfortable. Not perfect, just happy and comfortable." You do not need to be Jewishly educated or "religious enough." See [you do not have to be religious to be welcome](/learn/not-religious-welcome) and [starting Jewish observance](/learn/starting-jewish-observance).

## Common questions

### How do I start keeping Shabbat?
Pick one small window, like an hour or two on Friday evening, keep it completely, and add a little more each week, as Chabad.org advises.

### Can I keep Shabbat a little at a time?
Yes. Chabad.org recommends building up gradually rather than all at once. Many people grow slowly over months or years.

### What is the easiest first step for Shabbat?
Lighting candles before sunset and sharing a Friday night meal. Everything else grows from there.

### What if I fail one week?
Start again next Shabbat. Judaism values every mitzvah you do, and a step you took still counts.

### Do I need to stop driving right away?
Driving is part of the Shabbat rules, but you do not have to change everything at once. Many begin with a window at home. Call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242 to talk through your situation.
