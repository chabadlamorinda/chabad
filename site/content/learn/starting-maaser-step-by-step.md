To start giving maaser, open a separate account, move a tenth of each paycheck into it when the money arrives, and give from that account to tzedakah. This simple habit is the heart of keeping maaser, and you can begin with a smaller amount and grow.

## Step 1: Open a separate account (or use an envelope)

Opening a dedicated maaser account is the most important step, one that should be free and make transfers easy. If an account feels like too much, a labeled envelope works in the same spirit: the money is set apart, so it is clear it is not for everyday spending.

## Step 2: Work out your tenth

Follow [How to Calculate Maaser](/learn/how-to-calculate-maaser): your income after mandatory taxes and business expenses. Most people overestimate what they give and underestimate what they spend, so it helps to do the math. If you own a business or investments, read [Maaser for Business Owners and Investors](/learn/maaser-for-business-owners-and-investors).

## Step 3: Make it a monthly habit

The ideal is to set maaser aside as soon as income arrives, such as when your paycheck clears. If you are paid monthly, that is your monthly habit. If you are paid weekly or at irregular times, transfer each time, or at a regular time you choose. Some people also like to choose, in advance, whether to count by the calendar year or the Hebrew year, and review before the year ends.

A simple spreadsheet with two columns, one for earnings and one for giving, makes the yearly review easy.

## Step 4: Give from it

Give from the maaser account to those in need and to Jewish causes you care about. [What Maaser Can and Cannot Pay For](/learn/what-maaser-can-and-cannot-pay-for) will help you choose. A coin in a [pushka](/learn/pushka) is a lovely daily way to give, and you can count it too if you meant it as maaser.

## Starting small, and the limit of a fifth

If a full tenth is too much right now, you do not have to wait. Your basic needs come first, and the Rebbe encouraged giving a partial amount, for example five percent, in some cases keeping a note of what is missing so it can be made up later. Even when you cannot give the full tenth, you can still give tzedakah according to your means. See [Giving When Money Is Tight](/learn/giving-when-money-is-tight).

On the other side, giving up to a fifth, called chomesh, is commendable for those who can afford it, and a fifth is generally the upper limit for giving.

## If you miss a month

It happens, and there is no need for guilt. Missed amounts can be tracked and made up later. At the yearly review, if you find you still owe maaser, send the amount to charity right away. If you find you gave more than a tenth, ask the Rabbi whether you may count the extra toward next year. Just try to give something to tzedakah each year, and begin again.

## Common questions

### How do I start giving maaser?
Open a separate account, move a tenth of your after-tax income into it when you are paid, and give from it to tzedakah. The separate account is the most important step.

### What if I cannot give a full ten percent?
Start with what you can. The Rebbe encouraged giving a partial amount, and you can keep a note of the difference. Ask the Rabbi about your situation.

### What if I forget a month?
Make it up when you can. Missed amounts can be tracked and given later.

### How much is too much?
Up to a fifth is commendable and a fifth is generally the upper limit.

### How often should I review?
At least once a year. It helps to choose in advance whether to count by the fiscal year or the Hebrew year.
