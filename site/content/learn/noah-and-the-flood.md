Noah's ark story is in Genesis 6 to 9: G-d tells the righteous Noah to build an ark, because a flood is coming to wipe out a corrupt world. Noah, his family and a pair of each animal survive, and G-d sets a rainbow in the sky as a sign of His promise never to destroy the world by flood again.

## Why the flood came

The Torah says the earth had become corrupt and filled with violence. People had stopped treating one another with decency. Noah (נֹחַ) was a righteous man who "walked with G-d." Chabad.org notes that only Noah, his family and representatives of every living species survived. You can read the full portion in the weekly reading Noach, see [parsha](/parsha).

## The story, step by step

1. **G-d tells Noah to build the ark** (teivah, תֵּבָה), with specific measurements, of gopher wood.
2. **Noah takes his family and the animals aboard**, and the rains fall for forty days and nights.
3. **The waters cover the earth** and then slowly recede, and the ark comes to rest on the mountains of Ararat.
4. **Noah sends a raven and then a dove.** The dove returns with an olive leaf, a sign that the earth is dry.
5. **Everyone leaves the ark.** Noah offers a thanksgiving offering.
6. **G-d makes a covenant** and sets the rainbow in the clouds as its sign.

## Noah and Abraham

Noah was righteous, yet the Sages compare him with Abraham. Rashi, quoting the Sages, explains that Abraham "walked before G-d" on his own, while Noah needed support. Abraham also prayed for others, while Noah built the ark but did not plead for his generation. It shows how much one person can do. See [Abraham and Sarah](/learn/abraham-and-sarah).

## A Chassidic lesson: the ark is a word

In Hebrew, teivah means both "ark" and "word." Chabad.org shares the Baal Shem Tov's reading: G-d tells Noah to enter the "word," meaning the words of prayer and Torah study, which become a refuge amid the raging waters of life. The Chassidic sources also connect the flood (mabul) with "confusion" (balal). When life feels overwhelming, step into the words of prayer or Torah study; that is your ark.

## The rainbow

The rainbow is the sign of G-d's covenant with Noah. Chabad.org writes that it was set as a sign of the new covenant after the flood. Jewish tradition has a blessing to say upon seeing a rainbow, which acknowledges that G-d remembers His covenant and keeps His word. See [blessings on nature](/learn/blessings-on-nature).

## The seven Noahide laws

After the flood, G-d gave humanity basic laws of decency, which Jewish tradition calls the seven Noahide laws. They apply to all people. Read more in [Noahide laws](/learn/noahide-laws).

## Common questions

### What is the story of Noah's ark?
G-d told the righteous Noah to build an ark, a flood destroyed a corrupt world, and Noah, his family and the animals were saved.

### Why did G-d send the flood?
The Torah says the earth had become corrupt and filled with violence.

### What does the rainbow mean in Judaism?
It is the sign of G-d's covenant with Noah that He will not destroy the world by flood again. There is a blessing to say when you see one.

### What does teivah mean?
Teivah (תיבה) means "ark" and also "word." The Baal Shem Tov taught that we should enter the words of prayer and Torah as a refuge.

### Were Noah and Abraham the same kind of righteous?
The Sages compare them: Abraham prayed for others and walked on his own, while Noah needed support and built the ark. Ask the Rabbi if you want to study this.
