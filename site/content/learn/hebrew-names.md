A Hebrew name is the Jewish name a person receives at birth, at their bris for a boy or at a Torah naming for a girl. It is written as your first name, followed by "son of" (ben) or "daughter of" (bat) and a parent's name, for example Moshe ben Avraham or Leah bat Sarah.

## Why your Hebrew name matters

Jewish mysticism teaches that a Hebrew name is a channel for the soul. The letters of the name are connected to a person's life force, which is why names are chosen with care and why they are used at the most important moments. You will use your Hebrew name:

- When called up to the Torah for an aliyah.
- In prayers for healing (using the mother's name).
- On a ketubah at a [Jewish wedding](/learn/jewish-wedding).
- In memorial prayers like [yizkor](/learn/yizkor) and on a headstone.

## How a Hebrew name is formed

A full Hebrew name has two parts:

1. **Your own name,** such as Yosef, Chana or Yitzchak.
2. **Your parent's name,** connected by ben (son of) or bat (daughter of).

Customs about which parent to use:

- When called to the Torah or on legal documents, the father's name is used: Yosef ben Yaakov.
- When praying for someone's health, the mother's name is used: Yosef ben Rachel.
- A Kohen or Levi adds "HaKohen" or "HaLevi" at the end.

Some people have two names, such as Chaya Mushka or Menachem Mendel. Both are used together.

## How to find your Hebrew name

If you are not sure of your Hebrew name, try:

- Asking parents, grandparents or other relatives.
- Looking at a ketubah, bar or bat mitzvah certificate, or naming certificate.
- Checking a family member's headstone or memorial plaque, which lists their parent's name.

## If you never received one

Many Jews were never given a Hebrew name, and that is perfectly okay. You can receive one at any age. Usually the name is announced at a Torah reading with a short blessing. People often choose:

- A Hebrew version of their English name (Daniel, Miriam, Benjamin as Binyamin).
- The name of a loved relative who has passed away.
- A biblical figure whose story speaks to them.

If your parent does not have a Hebrew name, the Rabbi can guide you on what to use. For a convert, the custom is "ben Avraham Avinu" or "bat Sarah Imeinu."

## Common questions

### Can I change my Hebrew name?

In general, a Hebrew name is not changed. The one well-known exception is adding a name for someone who is seriously ill, a traditional practice done with a Rabbi's guidance. Ask the Rabbi if this applies to you.

### Does my English name need to match my Hebrew name?

No. Many parents pick names that share a sound or first letter, but there is no rule. Your Hebrew name stands on its own.
