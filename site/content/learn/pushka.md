A pushka is a tzedakah (charity) box kept in a Jewish home. Every day, family members drop in a few coins, turning giving into a daily habit and making the home a place of kindness and blessing.

## What is a pushka?

*Pushka* is a Yiddish word for a small box or can. In Hebrew it is called a *kupat tzedakah*. It can be a simple tin, a decorated wooden box, or a craft project made by a child. What matters is not how it looks but that it is used.

Jewish homes have kept a pushka for generations, often placed in the kitchen near the Shabbat candles.

## Why have a pushka at home

- **Daily giving.** [Tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) is one of the most important mitzvot. A pushka makes it easy to give every day, even a little.
- **It changes the home.** The Lubavitcher Rebbe encouraged every Jewish home to have a pushka, teaching that it turns the house into a "house of charity" and brings blessing to everyone who lives there.
- **It teaches children.** Children who put coins in a pushka every day learn that giving is a normal part of life.
- **Small amounts add up.** Maimonides teaches that giving many small gifts trains the heart to be generous, even more than one large gift.

## When to give

1. **Every weekday morning,** before or during prayers.
2. **Before lighting [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles)** on Friday and before holiday candles. Many women and girls drop in coins right before lighting.
3. **Before praying for something,** such as a person who is sick.
4. **On special days,** such as a Hebrew birthday or a yahrzeit.

Money is not handled on Shabbat and major Jewish holidays, so give before they begin and after they end.

## A pushka for every child

The Rebbe also encouraged giving each child a pushka of their own, in their room. A child can decorate it, give a coin each day, and choose, with their parents, where the money will go. Some families give children coins specifically so they can put them in.

## What to do when it is full

1. **Count the money** together as a family.
2. **Give it to a worthy cause:** your synagogue, Jewish education, a family in need or another charity you care about.
3. **If the pushka is labeled for a specific charity,** the money should go to that charity.
4. **Start again.**

If you are not sure where to send the money, call us and we will be happy to suggest a good cause.

## Common questions

### How much should I put in each day?
Any amount is meaningful. Even a single coin given every day builds a habit of giving. What matters most is consistency.

### Where should I keep my pushka?
Many keep it in the kitchen, near where Shabbat candles are lit, so it is seen and used every day. A pushka in each child's room is also a beautiful idea.

### Can I count pushka money as part of my maaser?
Yes, if you are giving from your income and it goes to tzedakah. Read more in our guide to [maaser](/learn/maaser).

### Can I use pushka money for something else and pay it back?
Once money is placed in a pushka, it is set aside for tzedakah. If you need to use it, ask the Rabbi first.
