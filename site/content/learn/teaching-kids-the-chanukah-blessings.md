On Chanukah we say two blessings every night before lighting the menorah, and a third, Shehecheyanu, only on the first night. Children learn them best a few words at a time, with the meaning explained and the tune sung over and over, just like a song.

## The three blessings, in plain words

All three begin the same way: "Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam" (בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה׳ אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם), "Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe." Then each continues differently.

**1. Every night, for the mitzvah.**
*asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav v'tzivanu l'hadlik ner Chanukah.*
"Who made us holy with His commandments and commanded us to kindle the Chanukah light." Some prayer books add the word "shel" before Chanukah; follow the text in your prayer book.
*Tell your child:* "We say thank you to G-d for giving us this mitzvah, and then we do it."

**2. Every night, for the miracle.**
*she'asah nisim la'avoteinu, bayamim hahem bazman hazeh* (שֶׁעָשָׂה נִסִּים לַאֲבוֹתֵינוּ בַּיָּמִים הָהֵם בִּזְמַן הַזֶּה).
"Who made miracles for our ancestors in those days, at this time."
*Tell your child:* "The Maccabees' miracle happened long ago, and every year the same light comes back."

**3. First night only, Shehecheyanu.**
*shehecheyanu v'kiy'manu v'higi'anu lizman hazeh* (שֶׁהֶחֱיָנוּ וְקִיְּמָנוּ וְהִגִּיעָנוּ לִזְמַן הַזֶּה).
"Who has kept us alive, sustained us and brought us to this season." On the first night (or the first time you light the menorah this year) this blessing is added. See [Shehecheyanu](/learn/shehecheyanu) for the meaning.
*Tell your child:* "We are happy to be here together again this year."

The blessings are said before lighting, standing near the menorah, usually while holding the shamash, the helper candle. For the full lighting steps see [how to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah).

## How to teach it, step by step

1. **Start with the meaning.** Children remember what they understand. Use the "Tell your child" lines above.
2. **Teach one phrase at a time.** First "Baruch atah Ado-nai." The next night, add "Elo-heinu melech ha'olam." Add a little more each night, just as we add a candle each night.
3. **Sing it.** A melody makes the words easy to hold on to. Ask the Rabbi for the tune you will hear in synagogue.
4. **Say it slowly with the child, then let the child lead.** A four-year-old can say the opening alone; a seven-year-old can say the whole first blessing.
5. **Answer "Amen."** Even the youngest can say Amen after a blessing. Answering Amen to a blessing is itself a mitzvah.
6. **Use a card.** Print the blessings with transliteration, put them next to the menorah and let the children point as the family says them.

## Fun ways to practice

- **Talk about the miracle at dinner.** When the idea of "miracles for our ancestors" is familiar, the words sound familiar too.
- **Listen to songs.** Look for kids' videos with the lighting steps and children's songs. See also [Chanukah songs](/learn/chanukah-songs).
- **Rehearse before Chanukah.** A pretend lighting with an unlit menorah, practicing the order of the candles, makes the real night calmer.
- **Haneirot Halalu.** As the lights are kindled, many families sing a short hymn called Haneirot Halalu, "These lights we kindle." Ask the Rabbi for the words and the tune; children pick them up quickly.

## Common questions

### What are the Chanukah blessings?
There are three: one for the mitzvah of kindling the Chanukah light, one for the miracles of those days, and Shehecheyanu, thanking G-d for bringing us to this season. The first two are said every night, and Shehecheyanu is added on the first night.

### How do you teach a child the Chanukah blessings?
Explain the meaning, teach one phrase per night, sing the words and let the child say Amen. By the fourth or fifth night, most children can say a good part.

### Do children have to say the blessings?
Children can take part by saying them with a parent or by answering Amen. At what age a child lights and says the blessings on their own varies, so ask the Rabbi.

### Do girls say the blessings too?
Yes. Women and girls are obligated in the Chanukah lights and say the blessings. Families differ on who lights; ask the Rabbi about your household.

### Which nights do you say Shehecheyanu?
Only the first night, or the first time you light the menorah this Chanukah. It is not repeated on the other nights.
