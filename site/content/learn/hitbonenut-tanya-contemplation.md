Hitbonenut (הִתְבּוֹנְנוּת, also spelled hisbonenus) is deep, focused thought about G-d's greatness and closeness, until the mind's understanding reaches the heart as love and awe. It is the Tanya's main tool for feeling connected to G-d, and it needs no special skill: just time, quiet and an honest mind.

## What is hitbonenut?

The word comes from the Hebrew for understanding. In Chassidic teaching it means not only knowing an idea but staying with it until it becomes real to you. The [Tanya](/learn/tanya), by Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi, the Alter Rebbe, gives it a central place. For the wider subject, see [Jewish meditation](/learn/jewish-meditation), which this guide does not repeat.

## From the mind to the heart

Several chapters of the Tanya, on Chabad.org, describe this path in their own words.

- **Chapter 3** describes how an idea moves through the soul: a first flash of understanding is developed, and then the mind binds itself to the idea until it produces real emotion. It says that contemplating G-d's greatness gives rise to awe and love. It also says the intellect keeps that love from burning so strongly that the soul would leave the body, since we are here to do mitzvot.
- **Chapter 17** reads the verse that the matter is "very near to you, in your mouth and in your heart." It asks how loving G-d can be near to everyone, since feelings are hard to command. The answer is that the mind can be directed at will. By thinking about G-d's greatness, a person can reach at least an intellectual love, and that love can guide the heart, speech and action.
- **Chapter 41** says that before a mitzvah or Torah study, a person should stir up awe of G-d. It teaches that if one cannot feel this in the heart, awakening it in the mind still counts, and that love and awe are like two wings of a bird. It also encourages fixed times to contemplate G-d's greatness.

The message is encouraging: you do not need to feel an emotion on demand. You need to give your mind the time, and let the heart follow as it will.

## A simple five-minute practice before prayer

This is a practical suggestion in the spirit of the Tanya, not a quotation from it. If you want a formal method, ask the Rabbi.

1. **Sit quietly for a minute.** Put the phone away.
2. **Choose one idea.** For example: G-d is giving life to the world, and to me, at this moment. Or: G-d is everywhere, including here. These ideas come from the Tanya's teaching that life flows to us from G-d every moment (see [Tanya on worry and trust](/learn/tanya-on-worry-and-trust)).
3. **Think it through slowly.** Picture the sky, a baby, your own breath.
4. **Notice what you feel,** wonder, thanks, calm. Do not force anything. Even a small feeling is a start.
5. **Begin to pray or learn from there.** Chapter 41 says to prepare before morning prayers and before each period of Torah study. See [kavanah in prayer](/learn/kavanah-in-prayer).

## Love and awe in simple terms

[Love of G-d](/learn/loving-g-d) is the wish to be close to Him. [Awe of G-d](/learn/fear-of-g-d) is a respectful sense that we stand before the King. In Chapter 41, the Alter Rebbe teaches that both are needed for service to rise, like two wings. Neither needs to be dramatic. Start small.

## If it does not "work"

Some days you will feel nothing. The Tanya's teaching is that the effort itself counts, and that mind-based awe still carries the mitzvah. See [the beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni): the Tanya's ideal is the person who keeps choosing, not the one who always feels.

## Common questions

### What is hitbonenut?
Hitbonenut is deep thought about G-d until it moves the heart to love and awe. It is the Tanya's practice for connecting mind and feeling, and it is an ordinary skill anyone can start.

### How do I contemplate G-d if I am a beginner?
Pick one simple idea, such as G-d giving life every moment, sit quietly for a few minutes and think it through slowly. You can do this before prayer. Ask the Rabbi for guidance on how to begin.

### Is hitbonenut the same as meditation?
It shares the quiet and focus but differs in aim: it is thinking about G-d's greatness to awaken love and awe, not emptying the mind. See [Jewish meditation](/learn/jewish-meditation).

### What if I cannot feel love of G-d?
Chapter 17 of the Tanya says the mind can lead even when the heart does not feel it, and Chapter 41 says awe awakened in the mind still counts. Keep going gently.

### Does the Tanya say to do this daily?
Chapter 41 encourages fixed times to contemplate G-d's greatness, and preparing before prayer and before each period of study. Ask the Rabbi how much is right for you.
