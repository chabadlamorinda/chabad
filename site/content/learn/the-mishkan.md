The Mishkan (מִשְׁכָּן, "dwelling place") was the portable sanctuary, or Tabernacle, that the Jewish people built in the desert soon after the Exodus, so that G-d's presence could dwell among them. It traveled with them for forty years and was the forerunner of the Holy Temple in Jerusalem.

The Torah describes it in the portions Terumah through Pekudei in the book of Exodus. Here is what it was, what was inside it, and what it means for a home in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda today.

## Why a home for G-d?

After the Torah was given at Mount Sinai, G-d asked Moses for a home in which He could dwell among His people. "They shall make Me a sanctuary, and I will dwell among them" (Exodus 25:8).

Chabad.org notes that the Mishkan was both a building and an idea: that G-d is not aloof in heaven, but can be found within the physical world. Its two names express this. *Mishkan* means "dwelling place," and *Mikdash* means "sanctity."

## What was inside the Mishkan?

Chabad.org describes the Mishkan as a portable holy structure with a courtyard around it. Inside:

- **The Ark (Aron).** Made of wood covered in gold, with two cherubim on top. It stood in the inner chamber behind a woven curtain and held the two tablets G-d gave Moses at Sinai.
- **The menorah.** A candelabra of pure gold, lit daily by the priest.
- **The table.** It held the special bread.
- **The incense altar.** A small square altar for the spice offering.
- **The walls.** Forty-eight wooden boards fitted together, covered with tapestries and coverings of skin. A woven curtain formed the entrance.

Every item had rings and poles, so it could be carried wherever the people traveled. A lasting tradition holds that the Mishkan was never destroyed. Read more about its successor in [the Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple).

## Everyone gave

The Torah begins the project with a call: "Take for Me a gift" from every person whose heart moves them (Exodus 25:2). The sanctuary was built from gifts that people chose to give, not from a tax.

That teaches a lesson Chabad communities still live by: a holy place is built together. Everyone's gift counts. You can see this in [giving to Chabad of Lamorinda](/donate) and in the [Chai Club](/chai-club).

## "Within them," not "within it"

One of the best-known Chassidic teachings is about a single word. The verse does not say, "and I will dwell within *it*," but "within *them*." Chabad.org explains, citing Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, that G-d wants to dwell within each and every person.

The sanctuary was the model. The real home for G-d is in us, our homes and our daily lives.

Chabad.org adds that G-d makes His home in whatever people sanctify. This is why the Torah uses physical objects in mitzvot: wool becomes tzitzit, parchment becomes a mezuzah or a Torah scroll. Even ordinary parts of life, done according to the Torah, can become a dwelling place. A Jewish home with a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) and a [chanukat habayit](/learn/chanukat-habayit) is a small mishkan.

## Making your home a sanctuary

You do not need to build anything large. Some small ways:

1. Put up a mezuzah on your door.
2. Light Shabbat candles on Friday afternoon. See [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times).
3. Give a little tzedakah each day.
4. Set aside a time to learn the weekly [Torah portion](/learn/weekly-parsha).
5. Welcome a guest to your table.

## Common questions

### What is the Mishkan?
The Mishkan was the portable Tabernacle that the Jewish people built in the desert as a place for G-d's presence. It accompanied them for forty years.

### What was inside the Tabernacle?
It contained the Ark with the two tablets, a golden menorah, a table for the special bread and an incense altar. A courtyard surrounded it.

### What does "I will dwell within them" mean?
It means G-d wants to dwell in every person, not only in a building. Chabad.org points out the verse says "within them," not "within it."

### What is the difference between the Mishkan and the Temple?
The Mishkan was a portable tent used in the desert. The Holy Temple in Jerusalem was a permanent building that came later. See [the Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple).

### Which Torah portions describe the Mishkan?
The portions Terumah, Tetzaveh, Ki Tisa, Vayakhel and Pekudei in the book of Exodus. See the [weekly parsha](/parsha) for this week's reading.

### How can a home in Lafayette or Orinda be a sanctuary?
