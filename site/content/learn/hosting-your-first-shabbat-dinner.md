You do not need a big house, fancy dishes or perfect Hebrew to host a Shabbat dinner. You need a table, a few friends or family, and the wish to make Friday night special. Here is everything, step by step.

## What to have ready

- **Two candles** and candlesticks (any will do; tea lights work too).
- **Wine or grape juice** and a cup for Kiddush.
- **Two challahs** (store-bought is perfect), on a plate or board, covered with a napkin or a challah cover.
- **A white tablecloth** if you have one, and flowers if you like.
- **The meal:** soup, a main dish, sides, dessert. Keep it simple and cook ahead, so you are not working after Shabbat begins. If guests keep kosher, see [kosher](/learn/kosher) or ask the Rabbi.
- **A copy of the blessings**, printed or in a small booklet.

## The order of the evening

1. **Light Shabbat candles** before sunset, at candle-lighting time. See [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).
2. **Sing Shalom Aleichem**, welcoming the Shabbat angels. See [Shalom Aleichem](/learn/shalom-aleichem).
3. **Bless the children**, if there are any at the table. See [blessing your children](/learn/blessing-your-children).
4. **Make Kiddush** over the wine or grape juice and pass it around. See [Kiddush](/learn/kiddush).
5. **Wash hands** for bread with the blessing. See [washing hands](/learn/netilat-yadayim).
6. **Hamotzi:** uncover the challahs, say the blessing, slice, dip in salt and share.
7. **Enjoy the meal.** Between courses, share a thought from the [weekly parsha](/learn/weekly-parsha), a story, or a question for everyone.
8. **Sing**: any Shabbat song you know. See [zemirot](/learn/zemirot).
9. **Birkat Hamazon**, the blessing after the meal. See [Birkat Hamazon](/learn/birkat-hamazon).

## Making guests feel at home

Explain each step in a sentence before you do it; it puts everyone at ease. Invite guests to join in, but never put anyone on the spot. Phones away makes a big difference. And invite someone new each time: a neighbor, a colleague, a student. See [welcoming guests](/learn/hachnasat-orchim).

## Common questions

### I don't read Hebrew. Can I still host?
Of course. Say the blessings in transliteration or in English. What matters is the warmth and the intention.

### Do I need to cook everything myself?
Not at all. Many hosts order food, ask guests to bring a salad or dessert, or keep the menu to soup, chicken and challah.

### Can I host if I am not keeping all of Shabbat?
Yes. Every Shabbat dinner is a mitzvah and a beautiful step. Start where you are; the Rabbi is glad to help with anything you would like to add.
