In Jewish tradition, dating is not a pastime. It is the serious, hopeful search for a soulmate with whom to build a Jewish home. The goal is marriage, and the approach is thoughtful, respectful and full of prayer.

## Why dating matters so much in Judaism

The Torah says, "It is not good for man to be alone" (Genesis 2:18). Jewish mysticism teaches that husband and wife are two halves of one soul, separated before birth and reunited at marriage. The Talmud says that forty days before a child is formed, a heavenly voice already announces whom they will marry.

So finding your match, called your *bashert* (destined one), is seen as both a gift from G-d and something you must actively pursue. The Sages even say that matching couples is as difficult as splitting the Red Sea.

## What is a shidduch?

A *shidduch* is a match, usually suggested by a friend, relative, rabbi or matchmaker (*shadchan*) who knows both people. In many traditional communities this is the main way people meet. Even outside those communities, a thoughtful introduction from someone who cares about you is often more effective than an app.

Whatever way you meet, the Jewish approach has the same core ideas:

- **Date with purpose.** Dating is for finding a spouse, so it begins when you are ready to marry.
- **Get to know the person, not just the chemistry.** Values, character and life goals come first.
- **Keep it respectful.** Traditional Jewish dating avoids physical contact before marriage, which keeps the focus on who the other person truly is.
- **Pray.** Many singles say [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) and ask for blessings to find their match.

## What to look for in a spouse

Jewish sources stress character above all. Look for:

1. **Kindness.** When Abraham's servant searched for a wife for Isaac, he looked for someone who showed generosity to strangers. Rebecca offered water to him and his camels.
2. **Shared values.** Agreement on the kind of home you want, including Shabbat, kosher, and how you will raise children, matters far more than shared hobbies.
3. **Good middot (character traits).** Honesty, patience, humility and how someone treats others, including waiters and family.
4. **Emotional connection.** Attraction and friendship matter too. Judaism does not ask anyone to marry a person they do not like.

## How a traditional date works

Dates are usually in public places like a hotel lobby, a cafe or a park, where it is easy to talk. Conversation explores goals, family, beliefs and dreams. After a few dates, each person reflects, often with a mentor, and decides whether to continue. When both feel ready, they become engaged.

Jewish law also forbids a man and woman who are not married to each other from being alone together in a closed private space (*yichud*). Meeting in public places keeps this simple.

## Common questions

### Is it okay to use dating apps?
Yes, many Jewish couples meet online. The same values apply: be honest, date with a goal of marriage, and take time to learn about the person's character and values.

### Does it matter if the person I date is Jewish?
Judaism places great importance on marrying another Jew, so that the Jewish home and heritage continue. If you have questions about your own situation, the Rabbi is happy to talk privately and without judgment.

### What if I have been searching for a long time?
You are not alone, and it is not your fault. Keep praying, ask friends and mentors to think of you, and stay open. Many people find their match when they least expect it.

### How long should we date before getting engaged?
There is no fixed rule. In traditional circles engagements often come after a few months or less, once both know clearly. What matters is that both feel sure and at peace with the decision.
