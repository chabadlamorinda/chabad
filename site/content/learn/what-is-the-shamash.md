The shamash (שַׁמָּשׁ, "attendant" or "helper") is the extra candle on the Chanukah menorah, used to light the other candles. That is why a menorah has nine lights, even though there are eight nights: eight lights are the mitzvah, and the ninth serves them.

## Why a ninth light?

The Chanukah lights are holy. Jewish law says that we may not use them for any practical purpose, such as reading, working or lighting the room. We may only look at them, to remember the miracle. After the blessings, we say in Haneirot Halalu that these lights are only for seeing. See [how to light](/learn/chanukah-menorah).

If you need light for something, the shamash is there. It is kept burning beside the other lights, so that any benefit comes from it and not from the holy lights. Its name, "attendant," is just what it does: it serves the others.

## How the shamash is used

On each night:

1. Light the shamash first.
2. Holding it, say the blessings.
3. Use it to light the other lights, starting with the newest on the left.
4. Set it back in its place, lit, beside the others.

Jewish tradition notes that many people use a beeswax candle for the shamash.

## Where does the shamash go?

The shamash must be set apart from the eight lights, so that it is obvious which are the mitzvah lights and so that nobody thinks the ninth is one of them. Many menorahs have it higher than the rest, or on a different level, and some place it a bit to the side. See [choosing a kosher menorah](/learn/choosing-a-kosher-menorah). Its position is a custom, but its being set apart is part of the standard design.

## The lesson of the lamplighter

Chassidic thought sees in the shamash a model of a good person: someone who lights up others. The shamash does not lose anything by lighting the other candles, and in the end the whole menorah shines brighter because of it. A good teacher, friend or parent is like that. They give light and still burn bright. This is also a thought for those who help others in our community, such as the volunteers who visit the sick and the elderly; see [help for seniors and families](/help/help-for-seniors-and-families).

## Common questions

### What is the shamash?
The shamash is the helper candle used to light the other Chanukah candles. It is the ninth light on the menorah, set apart from the eight mitzvah lights.

### Why does the menorah have 9 candles?
Eight are for the eight nights, and the ninth is the shamash, which lights them and provides light for any ordinary use.

### Why can't I use the Chanukah lights to read?
The lights are holy and are meant only to be seen. If you need light, use the shamash or another lamp.

### Where does the shamash go on the menorah?
Set apart from the other eight, often higher or on a different level. Say the blessings holding it and then place it in its spot.

### Do I count the shamash as one of the candles of the night?
No. On the first night you light one candle plus the shamash. The shamash is extra. See [night by night](/learn/chanukah-night-by-night).

### What if the shamash goes out?
It is not one of the mitzvah lights, but it is good to keep it lit. Read [What If the Chanukah Candles Go Out?](/learn/what-if-chanukah-candles-go-out).
