The best-known Jewish symbols are the seven-branched menorah, the Star of David, the word chai, and the two Tablets of the Ten Commandments. Some symbols are actual mitzvah objects, like the mezuzah and tefillin, while others are simply traditional signs of Jewish identity.

## Symbols and mitzvah objects

It helps to know the difference:

- **A symbol** expresses an idea or identity. Wearing it is a nice custom, but it is not a commandment.
- **A mitzvah object** is used to fulfill a commandment from the Torah, such as a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah), [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) or [tzitzit](/learn/tzitzit). These have detailed rules about how they are made.

## The seven-branched menorah

The menorah was the golden lamp that stood in the Tabernacle and later in [the Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple) in Jerusalem. Its seven branches were lit every day by the priests. It is one of the oldest Jewish symbols and stands for spreading the light of Torah and goodness to the world. The Chanukah menorah, with eight branches plus a helper candle, recalls the miracle of the oil.

## The Star of David

The six-pointed [Magen David](/learn/star-of-david), "Shield of David," became the most widely recognized Jewish symbol in recent centuries.

## Chai

The Hebrew word [chai](/learn/chai-symbol), "life," has the number value 18. That is why Jewish gifts are often given in multiples of 18.

## The Tablets

Two rounded tablets with Hebrew letters represent the [Ten Commandments](/learn/ten-commandments), given by G-d at Mount Sinai. They are often shown above the ark in a synagogue.

## The mezuzah

A small scroll on the doorpost, the mezuzah contains the Shema. It is both a mitzvah and a sign that a home is Jewish.

## Other familiar symbols

- **Shofar.** The ram's horn blown on Rosh Hashanah, a call to return to G-d. See [shofar](/learn/shofar).
- **Tallit.** The prayer shawl with fringes, worn during morning prayers.
- **Kippah.** The head covering that reminds us G-d is above us.
- **Lion of Judah.** The tribe of Judah, from which King David came, is compared to a lion in the Torah.
- **Pomegranate.** A traditional image of a life full of mitzvot, "as full of mitzvot as a pomegranate is full of seeds."
- **Olive branch and dove.** From the story of Noah, a sign of peace.
- **Hamsa.** A hand-shaped design popular in some Sephardic and Middle Eastern communities. It is a folk custom, not part of Jewish law.

## The best symbol of all

Symbols are beautiful, but the Jewish tradition teaches that a Jew's truest sign is how they live: lighting Shabbat candles, giving tzedakah, learning Torah and treating others with kindness.

## Common questions

### What is the oldest Jewish symbol?
The seven-branched menorah is among the oldest. It is described in detail in the Torah and was carved on the Arch of Titus in Rome, which shows the Temple's menorah being carried away after the destruction.

### Is the hamsa a Jewish symbol?
The hamsa is a cultural design used by some Jews, especially from North Africa and the Middle East. It is not a mitzvah and has no source in the Torah. For protection, the Jewish tradition points to a kosher mezuzah and doing mitzvot.

### What symbols are on Jewish gravestones?
Common ones include the Star of David, a menorah, hands raised in blessing for a Kohen, or a pitcher for a Levite. Many gravestones also bear the Hebrew letters for "May his (or her) soul be bound in the bond of life."
