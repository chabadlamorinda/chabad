Judaism teaches that there is one G-d, who created the world, keeps it in existence every moment, and cares about each person. Belief in G-d is the foundation of the Torah and the first of the Ten Commandments: "I am the L-rd your G-d."

## The core beliefs

Jewish tradition describes G-d in a few key ways:

- **One.** "Hear O Israel, the L-rd is our G-d, the L-rd is One." The [Shema](/learn/shema) is the central declaration of Jewish faith. G-d is not one of many, and G-d has no parts.
- **Without a body.** G-d has no physical form. When the Torah speaks of G-d's "hand" or "eyes," it uses human language to help us understand.
- **Eternal.** G-d has no beginning and no end, and is beyond time.
- **The Creator.** G-d created the world from nothing.
- **Involved.** G-d knows and cares about every person and every detail of the world.

Maimonides listed these ideas among his Thirteen Principles of Faith, which appear in many prayer books.

## Creation is happening now

Chassidic teaching adds a beautiful idea. G-d did not just create the world long ago and step back. G-d's creative word flows into every creature at every moment, keeping it in existence. If that flow stopped for an instant, the world would cease to be.

This means G-d is not far away. G-d is present right here, in every moment of your life.

## Divine Providence

The Baal Shem Tov taught that G-d directs every detail of creation, even the way a single leaf turns in the wind. This is called *hashgachah pratit*, Divine Providence. Nothing that happens to us is random. Every encounter and situation has a purpose, even when we cannot see it.

## Faith and questions

In Hebrew, faith is *emunah*. Jewish tradition teaches that every Jew has faith deep within the soul, even if it is sometimes hidden. Learning and experience help bring it into the open.

Questions are not the opposite of faith. Abraham questioned G-d. Moses asked to understand G-d's ways. The Talmud is full of debate. Asking honest questions and seeking answers is a very Jewish way to grow.

## How to strengthen your connection

1. **Start the day with [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani),** thanking G-d for returning your soul.
2. **Say the Shema** morning and night.
3. **Talk to G-d** in your own words. Prayer is a conversation.
4. **Learn.** Study Torah, especially Chassidic teachings that explain G-d in clear, deep ways.
5. **Do a mitzvah.** Each mitzvah is a point of connection with G-d. Read more about [why we do mitzvot](/learn/why-mitzvot).
6. **Notice the good.** Saying blessings before eating trains us to see G-d in everyday things.

## Common questions

### Why do Jews write "G-d" instead of "God"?
Out of respect for G-d's name. Jewish law forbids erasing or discarding G-d's name in Hebrew, and many extend this respect to English, so a printed page with the name is not thrown away casually.

### Can I be Jewish if I am not sure I believe in G-d?
Yes. Being Jewish depends on birth or conversion, not on feelings of belief at any given moment. Many people find that faith grows through doing mitzvot and learning.

### If G-d is good, why is there suffering?
This is one of the oldest and hardest questions, asked by Moses and Job. Judaism does not offer easy answers, but teaches that G-d is with us in pain, and that we must respond with kindness and help. The Rabbi is always happy to talk.

### Do Jews believe G-d hears every prayer?
Yes. Every prayer is heard, even if the answer is not what we hoped. Prayer also changes the person praying.
