*Barchi Nafshi* (בָּרְכִי נַפְשִׁי), "Bless, my soul," is Psalm 104, the psalm of creation. It praises G-d for light, water, mountains, springs, trees, animals, the moon and the sea, and Chabad.org notes it is said in the morning prayers on Rosh Chodesh, the start of the Jewish month.

For anyone who loves the hills around Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, it may be the most natural psalm there is. Here is a simple guided reading, in the order of the verses.

## What the psalm says

According to Chabad.org's text, Psalm 104 moves through creation like a walk through the world:

1. **Opening (verse 1).** The psalmist calls on his soul to bless G-d, who is clothed in majesty and splendor.
2. **Light, heavens and winds (verses 2 to 4).** G-d is wrapped in light and spreads out the heavens like a curtain. Clouds are His chariot, and the winds His messengers.
3. **The earth and the waters (verses 5 to 9).** The earth is set on firm foundations. The waters once covered it, but they fled at G-d's word and were given a boundary they cannot cross.
4. **Springs and streams (verses 10 to 13).** Springs flow between the mountains and give drink to the animals and birds. G-d waters the mountains, and the earth is satisfied.
5. **Plants and food (verses 14 and 15).** Grass for the animals, crops for people to work, and wine, oil and bread.
6. **Trees and homes of animals (verses 16 to 18).** The trees are well watered, birds nest in them, and the mountains shelter wild goats.
7. **The moon, sun, night and day (verses 19 to 23).** The moon marks the festivals and the sun knows when to set. At night the forest animals come out, and at sunrise people go out to work until evening.
8. **"How manifold are Your works" (verse 24).** The psalmist stops in wonder, and says that G-d made them all with wisdom.
9. **The sea (verses 25 and 26).** The sea teems with creatures, ships sail on it, and the Leviathan plays in it.
10. **All creatures depend on G-d (verses 27 to 30).** All look to G-d for food. When He gives, they are satisfied, and when He sends forth His spirit, they are renewed and so is the earth.
11. **Closing praise (verses 31 to 35).** The psalmist prays that G-d's glory endure, and sings to G-d as long as he lives, and ends where he began, with "Bless G-d, my soul."

## Why it is said on Rosh Chodesh

Chabad.org explains that on Rosh Chodesh the Song of the Day is followed by Psalm 104, which includes the line "He made the moon to mark the seasons" (verse 19). The new Jewish month begins with the new moon, so the psalm is a good fit. Read more in [Rosh Chodesh](/learn/rosh-chodesh).

Some communities say the psalm at other times as well, for example on Shabbat afternoons in the winter. We did not find this custom confirmed on Chabad.org, so if you want to know what Chabad does, ask the Rabbi.

## How to read it

You do not need to read the whole psalm to enjoy it. Try this:

- **Read it slowly** in English, one section at a time, and notice which picture speaks to you.
- **Read it outdoors.** The psalm is full of mountains, springs, trees and birds. It is wonderful on a trail, and you can read it on a summit; see [a Jewish hiker's guide](/learn/jewish-hiking-in-the-east-bay-hills).
- **Read it with children.** Ask: which animal in the psalm is your favorite?
- **Say the final line to yourself:** "Bless G-d, my soul." Gratitude is the heart of the psalm.

## Common questions

### What is Barchi Nafshi?

*Barchi Nafshi* is Psalm 104, which means "Bless, my soul." It is a psalm about the creation of the world.

### What is Psalm 104 about?

It praises G-d for creation, from light and the heavens, to the mountains and springs, the trees and animals, the moon and the sun, and the sea.

### When do we say Psalm 104?

Chabad.org explains that it is said on Rosh Chodesh after the Song of the Day. Other customs exist, so ask the Rabbi.

### Why is Psalm 104 said on Rosh Chodesh?

Because it mentions the moon marking the seasons, and Rosh Chodesh begins with the new moon.

### Is there a good psalm to say on a hike?

Psalm 104 is the psalm of creation, so it fits well on a hill or by a stream. Read it on weekdays, and see [a Jewish hiker's guide](/learn/jewish-hiking-in-the-east-bay-hills).

### Where can I read Psalm 104 in English?

The Hebrew and English text is on Chabad.org, or in any siddur or Tehillim book. Ask the Rabbi if you would like a copy.
