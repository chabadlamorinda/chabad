After King Solomon died, the Jewish people split into two kingdoms: the northern Kingdom of Israel, made up of ten tribes, and the southern Kingdom of Judah, with the tribes of Judah, Benjamin and Levi. For about two centuries they were two parallel monarchies, until the north was exiled by Assyria and, later, Judah and the First Temple fell to Babylon.

## The split: Rehoboam and Jeroboam

Solomon built the Holy Temple and ruled over a united people. But, as Chabad.org retells, G-d had told Solomon that the kingdom would be torn away because of his wives' idolatry. When Solomon died, his son Rehoboam became king. The people asked him to lighten their burden. Rehoboam rejected the advice of the elders, who told him to listen, and the people rebelled and appointed Jeroboam of the tribe of Ephraim as their ruler. The prophet Ahijah had earlier told Jeroboam that he would rule part of the kingdom. See [King Solomon](/learn/king-solomon).

Rehoboam kept the south, Judah, with Jerusalem, the Temple and the line of King David. Jeroboam ruled the north.

## The northern kingdom: Israel

Jeroboam feared that if his people kept going up to Jerusalem for the festivals they would return to Rehoboam. So he set up shrines in Dan and Bethel with golden calves, and proclaimed new holidays. It was a tragic mistake that shaped the whole history of the north.

Chabad.org says that the northern kings were universally wicked, and that Ahab's wife Jezebel introduced the worship of Baal. Prophets such as Elijah, Elisha, Hosea, Amos and Jonah called the people back. Under Jeroboam II the kingdom reached its largest size in Jewish history, but the people did not repent, and decline followed. The kingdom lasted 241 years. Read more in [what is a prophet](/learn/what-is-a-prophet).

## The end of the north

Assyria rose to power. The last king, Hoshea, turned to Egypt for help, and Shalmaneser of Assyria imprisoned him and besieged the capital, Samaria, for three years. Chabad.org tells us that Hoshea even removed the guards that blocked the road to Jerusalem, but the people did not go up. Samaria fell, and the Kingdom of Israel ended. Its people were exiled in stages and disappeared from history. Those are the [Ten Lost Tribes](/learn/ten-lost-tribes). The land was resettled with other peoples.

## The southern kingdom: Judah

Judah survived longer, with the Temple in Jerusalem and kings of the House of David. Its kings varied: some, like Jotham, faithful to the prophets; some, like Ahaz, who asked Assyria for help. In the end, Chabad.org explains, Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon installed Zedekiah as king after taking earlier exiles, and Zedekiah rebelled against Babylon despite the warnings of the prophet Jeremiah. Jerusalem was besieged beginning on the tenth of Tevet, and the walls were breached in Tammuz. On the ninth of Av, the First Temple was set on fire.

The First Temple had stood about 410 years. Jeremiah taught the exiles to build homes and families in Babylon and promised that after seventy years G-d would remember them. The Davidic kingdom ended but, as the prophets promised, will be restored. See [the Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple) and [Tisha B'Av](/learn/tisha-bav-for-beginners), when we remember the destruction.

## What does it teach us?

The splitting of the Jewish people was a tragedy that began with a refusal to listen and ended with exile. The lesson that our sages draw is the value of unity: the Temple fell and the north vanished when the Jewish people were divided. Today it is the reason we say that every Jew counts; see [love your fellow Jew](/learn/ahavat-yisrael).

## A simple timeline

1. Solomon builds the Temple and rules over all the tribes.
2. After his death, the kingdom splits in two: Israel in the north (ten tribes) and Judah in the south.
3. The north lasts 241 years, then Assyria destroys it and exiles its people.
4. Judah continues for a time, until Babylon destroys the First Temple.
5. The exiles return after seventy years, and the Second Temple is built.

## Common questions

### Why did Israel split into two kingdoms?
After Solomon's death Rehoboam rejected the people's request and they rebelled and made Jeroboam king. The deeper cause, the sages say, was Solomon's wives' idolatry.

### What were the two kingdoms called?
The northern kingdom was Israel (the ten tribes) and the southern kingdom was Judah (Judah, Benjamin and Levi).

### Who were Rehoboam and Jeroboam?
Rehoboam was Solomon's son and king of Judah. Jeroboam, of the tribe of Ephraim, became king of the northern kingdom of Israel.

### What were the golden calves of Jeroboam?
He placed golden calves at Dan and Bethel so that people would not go to Jerusalem. It became a lasting sin of the northern kingdom.

### How did the Kingdom of Israel end?
Assyria besieged Samaria for three years, took it, and exiled the people in stages. They are known as the Ten Lost Tribes.

### How did the Kingdom of Judah end?
Babylon, under Nebuchadnezzar, besieged Jerusalem and destroyed the First Temple on the ninth of Av, and the people were exiled.
