When a Jewish hiker sees an awe-inspiring mountain, hills or a vast landscape after not seeing it for thirty days, the blessing is "Oseh Maaseh Bereishit," "Who performs the work of creation." Chabad.org also lists blessings for thunder and lightning, a rainbow and blossoming trees, so a walk in the hills can be filled with prayer.

Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda sit at the edge of open hills, and a few of the East Bay's best-known parks are a short drive away on a weekday. Here are the blessings, a psalm worth reading at the top, and a few trail ideas.

## The blessings of the trail

Chabad.org lists these blessings for what a hiker may see:

- **Mountains, hills, deserts and rivers.** When you see them after an interval of thirty days, say "Oseh Maaseh Bereishit" (עוֹשֵׂה מַעֲשֵׂה בְרֵאשִׁית), "Who performs the work of creation." The thirty-day rule matters: the blessing is for a sight you have not seen in a month. Which mountains or views are impressive enough to count is a question for the Rabbi Ask whether a view you see every day near home counts.
- **Thunder and lightning.** Chabad.org gives "Whose power and might fill up the world," and notes that it is customary to say both that and "Oseh Maaseh Bereishit."
- **A rainbow.** The blessing begins "Who remembers the covenant."
- **Blossoming trees in Nisan.** When you go out in the month of Nisan and see trees in bud, you say the blessing that G-d "did not leave anything lacking in His world." See [the blessing on blossoming trees](/learn/blessing-on-blossoming-trees).

More blessings are in [blessings on nature](/learn/blessings-on-nature). If you are not sure whether a blessing applies, ask the Rabbi at 925-384-7242 and he will help.

## Where to hike in the East Bay hills

These are public places, and the facts below come from the agencies and references that describe them. Check the park's own website for hours, fees and trail conditions before you go.

- **Briones Regional Park.** Run by the East Bay Regional Park District, it covers about 6,117 acres of the Briones Hills in central Contra Costa County. Its highest point, Briones Peak, is 1,483 feet and has views of Mount Diablo and the Diablo Valley to the east.
- **Lafayette Reservoir.** EBMUD describes a 4.7-mile unpaved Rim Trail around the reservoir, as well as a 2.7-mile paved Lakeside Nature Trail.
- **Mount Diablo.** The summit of Mount Diablo State Park, also in Contra Costa County, rises to 3,849 feet and is known for one of the largest views in the western United States. The Sierra Nevada can be seen from the summit on a clear day.

Go on a weekday. Shabbat is for rest, and we do not drive on it; for what is possible on foot on Shabbat, see [Shabbat walks](/learn/shabbat-walks-in-lamorinda).

## Psalm 104 on the summit

When you reach the top, consider reading [Psalm 104](/learn/barchi-nafshi), "Barchi Nafshi," "Bless, my soul." It describes God wrapped in light, the waters and the mountains, springs flowing between the hills, the trees where birds nest, the moon marking the seasons, and ends in a call to bless G-d. It is the best psalm to read when the whole landscape is in front of you. It is also said at morning prayers on Rosh Chodesh, the new month.

## Safety is a mitzvah

Jewish tradition treats protecting life and health as a serious obligation. Bring water, tell someone your route, check the weather and turn back if the trail is unsafe. Follow the park's own posted rules. If you take a longer trip, you may want to say [Tefilat Haderech](/learn/tefilat-haderech), the Traveler's Prayer, which Chabad.org says is for trips of more than about 2.36 miles beyond your city's limits. Ask the Rabbi whether it applies to your drive. For health in general, see [Jewish health and body](/learn/jewish-health-and-body).

## Leave the trail as you found it

Judaism teaches us not to waste or destroy needlessly, a law called bal tashchit. On a trail, that means taking out whatever you bring in, staying on the path and leaving the plants and animals alone. See [bal tashchit](/learn/bal-tashchit).

## Common questions

### What blessing do you say when you see a mountain?

"Oseh Maaseh Bereishit," "Who performs the work of creation," when you see mountains, hills or deserts after thirty days. Ask the Rabbi whether a particular mountain qualifies.

### What blessing do you say on a rainbow?

The blessing begins "Who remembers the covenant," according to Chabad.org.

### What do you say on thunder and lightning?

Chabad.org gives "Whose power and might fill up the world," and says it is customary to say both that and "Oseh Maaseh Bereishit."

### Is there a blessing on a view of the Bay or the ocean?

For the ocean there is a different blessing, "Who created the ocean," when you see it after thirty days. For the Bay and its views, ask the Rabbi.

### Can I go hiking on Shabbat?

You can walk calmly within the Shabbat boundary and without carrying outside an eruv, but you cannot drive to a trailhead. See [Shabbat walks](/learn/shabbat-walks-in-lamorinda).

### What psalm is good to read on a hike?

Psalm 104, "Barchi Nafshi," which is about the creation of the world, from light to mountains to the sea. See [Barchi Nafshi](/learn/barchi-nafshi).
