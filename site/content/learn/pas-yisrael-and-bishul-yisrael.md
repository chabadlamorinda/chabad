Pas Yisrael (פַּת יִשְׂרָאֵל) means bread baked with a Jew's participation, and bishul Yisrael (בִּשּׁוּל יִשְׂרָאֵל) means food cooked with a Jew's participation. Jewish law asks that a Jew take part, even by simply lighting the flame, in baking bread and cooking certain foods, so that these foods are not prepared entirely by a non-Jew. This is a separate layer from the basic kosher rules.

## What are pas Yisrael and bishul Yisrael?

These two rules were instituted by our Sages. They also protect kashrut, since a non-Jew may not know the kosher rules. The rules have two parts:

- **Pas Yisrael:** bread and baked goods should be baked by a Jew, or with a Jew's help. Lighting the oven counts as help.
- **Bishul Yisrael:** certain foods cooked entirely by a non-Jew may not be eaten, even if all the ingredients and pots are kosher. This applies to foods that cannot be eaten raw, such as meat and grains, and that are fit for a royal table. Foods eaten raw, such as fruit or a carrot, are not affected.

What counts as "fit for a royal table" is a technical question, so ask the Rabbi about a specific food.

## What does "pas Yisrael" mean on a label?

Many store-bought breads are kosher certified but are made in non-Jewish bakeries. This is called **pas palter** (פַּת פַּלְטֶר), commercial bread. Chabad.org explains that a hechsher alone does not make bread pas Yisrael unless the package says so. Commercial bread is allowed in some cases, when the bakery is under kosher supervision and bread baked with a Jew's help is not available, and many people avoid it even so. Everyone should avoid it between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur. If bread is baked with dairy, ask the Rabbi.

Read [kosher certification](/learn/kosher-certification) to see what a symbol on the package does and does not tell you.

## Housekeepers and caregivers: who lights the flame?

This comes up often in families with a caregiver, cleaner or cook, including many in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda. If a non-Jew cooks in your kosher kitchen, the food may need a Jew to take part. The simple way to do that:

1. **A Jew lights the burner or turns on the oven** (or turns up the flame), before the cooking begins.
2. **The non-Jewish helper then cooks.** Under the Ashkenazi custom, this symbolic participation is enough. Stirring the pot also counts.
3. **Sephardi custom is stricter:** a Jew should actually place the food on the fire. If you follow Sephardi practice, ask the Rabbi.

A housekeeper also needs to know the basics, such as which pots and dishes are meat and which are dairy, so [setting up a kosher kitchen](/learn/kosher-kitchen) with clear labels is the first step.

## Making it easy at home

- Keep a routine: a Jew turns on the oven or stove for the day, or for each meal.
- Prepare meals that need no cooking, such as salads, with the helper.
- Leave short, labeled instructions in the kitchen.
- For a one-time event with a caterer, ask for kosher supervision. See [eating out kosher](/learn/eating-out-kosher).

## Common questions

### What is pas Yisroel?
Pas Yisroel is bread baked by a Jew, or with a Jew's help, even if the Jew only lit the oven. It is kept in addition to the basic kosher rules.

### What is bishul Yisrael?
Bishul Yisrael is food cooked with a Jew's participation. Certain foods cooked entirely by a non-Jew are not eaten.

### Can a non-Jewish housekeeper cook in a kosher kitchen?
Yes, if a Jew turns on the flame first and the helper knows the kosher rules. Ask the Rabbi about your kitchen.

### Do I need pas Yisrael if my bread has a hechsher?
A hechsher alone does not make bread pas Yisrael. Many people eat commercial bread under supervision when pas Yisrael is not available, and many keep to pas Yisrael whenever they can.

### Is a food eaten raw affected?
No. Foods like fruit, salad and raw vegetables are not covered by bishul Yisrael.

### Do Ashkenazim and Sephardim differ?
Yes. Ashkenazim rely on symbolic participation, such as lighting the fire, while the Sephardi custom asks that a Jew place the food on the fire. Ask the Rabbi which applies to you.
