The Seven Noahide Laws are a set of universal moral commandments that, according to the Torah, G-d gave to all of humanity through Noah after the Flood. Judaism teaches that a non-Jew who keeps these seven laws lives a righteous life and has a share in the World to Come.

## Where do the Noahide laws come from?

The Talmud (Sanhedrin 56a) teaches that G-d gave six of these laws to Adam, and a seventh to Noah. Since every person alive is descended from Noah, they are called the Noahide laws (in Hebrew, sheva mitzvot bnei Noach).

Judaism does not require non-Jews to become Jewish. Instead, it teaches that every person has a unique relationship with G-d and a role in making the world good.

## The seven laws

1. **Do not worship idols.** Recognize the One G-d, Creator of the world.
2. **Do not curse G-d.** Show respect for the Creator.
3. **Do not murder.** Every human life is sacred.
4. **Do not commit sexual immorality.** This includes adultery and other forbidden relationships.
5. **Do not steal.** Respect other people's property and rights.
6. **Do not eat flesh taken from a living animal.** Show compassion and avoid cruelty to animals.
7. **Establish courts of justice.** Societies should have fair laws and systems to uphold them.

Each of these laws includes many details, and together they form a complete moral framework.

## Positive in spirit

Although most are phrased as prohibitions, the Noahide laws are about building a good world. The seventh law, establishing justice, is a positive responsibility. Many teachers also explain that the laws imply positive values, such as belief in G-d, giving charity and treating others with kindness.

Maimonides writes that a non-Jew who keeps these laws because G-d commanded them in the Torah is counted among "the pious of the nations of the world."

## The Rebbe and the Noahide laws

In the 1980s, [the Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe) launched a worldwide effort to teach the Noahide laws, encouraging Jews to share them with their non-Jewish neighbors. He saw them as the basis for a moral society and a step toward the era of [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach), when "the world will be filled with the knowledge of G-d."

## Who keeps the Noahide laws?

All non-Jewish people are encouraged to keep them. Some people around the world identify as Noahides and build their lives around these laws, often studying Torah topics that relate to them.

Jews, meanwhile, are commanded in the [613 mitzvot](/learn/613-mitzvot), which include the principles of the seven Noahide laws.

## Common questions

### Do non-Jews need to convert to be close to G-d?

No. Judaism teaches that righteous people of all nations have a share in the World to Come. Keeping the Noahide laws is a full and meaningful spiritual path.

### Are the Noahide laws a religion?

They are a universal moral code, not a separate religion. People from many backgrounds keep them as a foundation for their faith and ethics.

### Can a non-Jew keep Shabbat or other Jewish mitzvot?

Some mitzvot are specific to Jews. Non-Jews may perform many good deeds, such as prayer and charity, but certain observances like keeping Shabbat in full are reserved for Jews. Ask the Rabbi for guidance on specifics.
