In Jewish tradition a mother is the pillar of the home: Chabad.org describes the woman as the "backbone" who sets the tone and spiritual mission of the household and passes the chain of Jewish continuity to the next generation. That does not mean fathers are excused; it means that a mother's warmth, example and small daily acts shape a Jewish home more than any speech.

## The woman and the home

Chabad.org's article on women's special mitzvot says that the woman nurtures her inner space and her home, and in doing so shapes the values and attitudes of her family. Chassidic teaching describes the home as a "mini-Temple," and the mother as the one who builds it, by bringing in light, holiness and nourishment. See [Jewish women](/learn/jewish-women).

The three mitzvot traditionally given special emphasis to women, candles, challah and family purity, are all about home life. Read [the three special mitzvot of Jewish women](/learn/three-mitzvot-for-jewish-women).

## Teaching children, softly

The Chabad.org article on Jewish education explains that formally the obligation to teach Torah is the father's, but mothers often do the most effective teaching. They usually spend more time with the children and have a softer, nurturing approach, and so they are well placed to pass on morals and Jewish values. See [being a Jewish father](/learn/being-a-jewish-father) and [chinuch](/learn/chinuch).

A mother teaches her daughter to light Shabbat candles from about age three, when she can say the blessing, and sees her own contribution to the light of the home. Learn more in [girls lighting Shabbat candles](/learn/girls-lighting-shabbat-candles).

## A story: bread in exile

Rebbetzin Chana Schneerson, the Rebbe's mother, joined her husband, Rabbi Levi Yitzchak Schneerson, when he was exiled in 1939 to a place called Ch'ili. According to her diaries, as described on Chabad.org, bread was scarce, and she stood in line for hours to get bread for Shabbat. Her husband encouraged her as she left, reminding her that G-d had given them Shabbat as "a fragrant spice" and that they needed to prepare for it. Before holidays she also worked to create a festive mood, even in exile.

## Not stereotypes

Chabad.org's "Different But Equal" presents men and women as having the same purpose, to join body and soul and make the world better, with differences in how each expresses it. The Rebbe is quoted in that article saying the world is entering a "feminine era" in which women rise to their proper prominence. The article does not prescribe fixed household roles. Jewish women also study Torah: see [Torah study for women](/learn/torah-study-for-women).

## Praying and blessing

The candle-lighting moment, with eyes covered, is especially auspicious for private prayer, and many mothers use it for their children. See [praying for your children](/learn/praying-for-your-children). For the Friday-night blessing of children, see [blessing your children](/learn/blessing-your-children).

## Common questions

### What is the role of a Jewish mother?
She is the pillar of the home, nurturing its Jewish atmosphere through Shabbat candles, challah, family purity and the values she teaches. Chabad teaching adds that the father has his own duties too.

### Why do women light the Shabbat candles?
Chabad.org says the woman sets the tone of the household, and the candles set the tone of Shabbat. If no adult woman is present, a man lights them.

### Does a Jewish mother have to be religious?
Take one mitzvah at a time, and ask the Rabbi or Rebbetzin where to begin. Every Jewish mother is welcome at Chabad of Lamorinda with no membership.

### Can a working mother be a Jewish mother?
Yes. Chabad.org's teaching is about the tone and spirit a woman brings to her home. Ask the Rebbetzin how to begin in a way that fits your life.
