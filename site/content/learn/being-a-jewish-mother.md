In Jewish tradition a mother is the pillar of the home: Chassidic philosophy describes the woman as the "backbone" who sets the tone and spiritual mission of the household and passes the chain of Jewish continuity to the next generation. That does not mean fathers are excused; it means that a mother's warmth, example and small daily acts shape a Jewish home more than any speech.

## The woman and the home

Chassidic teaching on women's special mitzvot says that the woman nurtures her inner space and her home, and in doing so shapes the values and attitudes of her family. It describes the home as a "mini-Temple," and the mother as the one who builds it, by bringing in light, holiness and nourishment. See [Jewish women](/learn/jewish-women).

The three mitzvot traditionally given special emphasis to women, candles, challah and family purity, are all about home life. Read [the three special mitzvot of Jewish women](/learn/three-mitzvot-for-jewish-women).

## Teaching children, softly

Jewish tradition explains that formally the obligation to teach Torah is the father's, but mothers often do the most effective teaching. They usually spend more time with the children and have a softer, nurturing approach, and so they are well placed to pass on morals and Jewish values. See [being a Jewish father](/learn/being-a-jewish-father) and [chinuch](/learn/chinuch).

A mother teaches her daughter to light Shabbat candles from about age three, when she can say the blessing, and sees her own contribution to the light of the home. Learn more in [girls lighting Shabbat candles](/learn/girls-lighting-shabbat-candles).

## A story: bread in exile

Rebbetzin Chana Schneerson, the Rebbe's mother, chose to join her husband, Rabbi Levi Yitzchak Schneerson, when the Soviet authorities, after arresting him in 1939 and holding him for nearly a year, sent him into exile in the remote village of Ch'ili in Central Asia. In her diary, as the story is told, she wrote that bread was so scarce that before Shabbat she often had to wait in line until late to receive it. As she left for the bread line, her husband would say with feeling: G-d has given us a fragrant spice called Shabbat, and we need to prepare ourselves for Shabbat.

## Not stereotypes

The Chassidic view presents men and women as having the same purpose, to join body and soul and make the world better, with differences in how each expresses it. The Rebbe taught the world is entering a "feminine era" in which women rise to their proper prominence. This view does not prescribe fixed household roles. Jewish women also study Torah: see [Torah study for women](/learn/torah-study-for-women).

## Praying and blessing

The candle-lighting moment, with eyes covered, is especially auspicious for private prayer, and many mothers use it for their children. ## Common questions

### What is the role of a Jewish mother?
She is the pillar of the home, nurturing its Jewish atmosphere through Shabbat candles, challah, family purity and the values she teaches. Chabad teaching adds that the father has his own duties too.

### Why do women light the Shabbat candles?
Chassidic thought says the woman sets the tone of the household, and the candles set the tone of Shabbat. If no adult woman is present, a man lights them.

### Does a Jewish mother have to be religious?
Take one mitzvah at a time, and ask the Rabbi or Rebbetzin where to begin. Every Jewish mother is welcome at Chabad of Lamorinda with no membership.

### Can a working mother be a Jewish mother?
Yes. The teaching is about the tone and spirit a woman brings to her home. Ask the Rebbetzin how to begin in a way that fits your life.
