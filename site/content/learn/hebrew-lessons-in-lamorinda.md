## Two kinds of Hebrew learning

Hebrew is called *lashon hakodesh* (לשון הקודש), the "Holy Tongue." Chabad.org describes it as the language of the Bible, the language Jews have prayed, studied and written in for thousands of years. Tradition teaches that its 22 letters hold deep secrets of creation. You can read more in [Lashon Hakodesh](/learn/lashon-hakodesh).

Most people who search for "Hebrew lessons near me" mean one of two goals:

- **Reading Hebrew for prayer and Torah.** You learn the letters and vowel signs, then sound out words in the [siddur](/learn/siddur) (prayer book). This is the fastest path, and it opens up services, blessings and a bar or bat mitzvah.
- **Speaking modern Hebrew.** This means conversation, vocabulary and grammar, as spoken in Israel. It takes longer and usually needs regular practice with a teacher or speakers.

The two overlap, since the letters are the same. Decide which goal matters most to you, and start there.

## Tips for getting started

1. **Pick one goal** (reading prayers, or speaking) and say it out loud to your teacher.
2. **Practice ten minutes a day.** Short and steady beats long and rare.
3. **Read aloud.** Hearing yourself helps letters stick.
4. **Start with words you know:** Shabbat, shalom, Torah, mitzvah.
5. **Learn alongside others.** A class or study partner keeps you going.

### Can adults learn to read Hebrew from scratch?
Yes. The alef-bet has 22 letters plus a few vowel signs, and adults who practice a little each day can begin following the siddur in weeks. See the [adult Hebrew guide](/learn/hebrew-reading-for-adults).

### What is the difference between prayer Hebrew and modern Hebrew?
Prayer Hebrew is the Hebrew of the siddur and Torah, usually learned by reading. Modern Hebrew is the spoken language of Israel, with everyday vocabulary. Most beginners start with reading for prayer.
