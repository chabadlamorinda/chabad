Jewish women traditionally do not do work while the Chanukah lights are burning, and the custom honors Yehudit (Judith) and the women who played a central role in the Chanukah miracle. The lights are holy and are there only to publicize the miracle, so for that half hour the women of the home simply sit, watch and enjoy.

## Why women pause from work

Chabad.org gives two reasons. First, the Chanukah lights exist only to remind us of the miracle and are not to be used for any other purpose. By not working in their glow, women make sure the light is never mistaken for ordinary lighting.

Second, women are obligated to light, but many fulfill the mitzvah through a menorah lit by the family. Pausing from work once the lights are lit shows that the mitzvah was not forgotten or neglected. Chabad.org adds that women across the generations felt a special affinity for the Chanukah lights and found their own ways to honor them.

## How long, and which work?

The custom applies for as long as the lights burn, which is at least a half hour after nightfall. If you are wondering exactly which tasks count, such as cooking, cleaning or the office, ask the Rabbi, because the details are best answered case by case. Many women simply stay away from ordinary housework and enjoy the lights with their children.

## Yehudit's story

The Chanukah story is not only about Maccabee soldiers. According to the tradition, Yehudit, a daughter of the Hasmonean family, went to the enemy general who was threatening the Jews. She fed him salty cheese so that he grew thirsty and drank wine, and when he fell asleep she took his sword. When the soldiers saw the fall of their commander, they fled, and the Jews gained courage.

This is why many families eat dairy foods on Chanukah, as a reminder of Yehudit's cheese. It is a custom, not an obligation, so enjoy latkes with sour cream and cheese. See [Chanukah foods](/learn/chanukah-foods) and [the Maccabees](/learn/the-maccabees) for more of the story.

## Making the half hour special

Instead of "not working," think of it as a gift. Here are ideas for a mother, a grandmother or a daughter:

1. Sit near the menorah and just watch the flames.
2. Sing a Chanukah song with the children; see [Chanukah songs](/learn/chanukah-songs).
3. Tell the story of Yehudit in your own words.
4. Share one good thing from your day and one thing you hope for.

## Women are part of the miracle

Our sages say that women were involved in that miracle, and Chabad teaching stresses that the lights of Chanukah shine out into the street so that everyone can see them. The woman who runs the home is often the one who makes that light shine, week after week. ## Common questions

### Why don't women work while the Chanukah candles are burning?
The Chanukah lights are meant only to publicize the miracle, so women do not work in their presence. It also shows that the mitzvah of lighting was not neglected.

### How long is the half hour without work?
It lasts while the lights burn, at least a half hour after nightfall. Ask the Rabbi about specific tasks.

### Who was Yehudit in the Chanukah story?
Yehudit (Judith) was a brave Jewish woman who, according to the tradition, helped bring down an enemy general and gave the Jews courage. Many families remember her on Chanukah.

### Why do we eat cheese on Chanukah?
Many eat dairy foods on Chanukah to remember the cheese Yehudit gave the enemy general. It is a custom, and it is a good excuse for cheese latkes.

### Are women obligated to light Chanukah candles?
Yes, women are obligated, though many fulfill the mitzvah through a menorah lit by their family. Girls and women can light their own too; ask the Rabbi about your home.
