Hosting guests for Shabbat means inviting someone to share your Friday night or Shabbat lunch table, a mitzvah called hachnasat orchim (הכנסת אורחים, "welcoming guests"). In Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, a single extra chair can change a newcomer's week, and you do not need a perfect home or a fancy meal to do it.

## Why an open table matters

Our tradition begins with a tent open on all sides. The Torah describes Abraham sitting at the entrance of his tent in the heat of the day, looking for travelers, and Sarah's tent was known for its blessings: a candle that burned from one Shabbat to the next, dough that stayed blessed, and a cloud of Divine presence over the tent. Chabad teaches that welcoming a guest is great enough that Abraham left even a Divine visit to run and greet the strangers (see [Hachnasat Orchim](/learn/hachnasat-orchim)).

Shabbat is the perfect time for this. After a week of rushing, the table becomes the place where people slow down and feel they belong.

## Who might need a Shabbat table

Lamorinda is a warm place, but many people here are far from their families. Think of:

- **Newcomers** who just moved to Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda and have not met their neighbors. See [moving to Lamorinda](/learn/moving-to-lamorinda).
- **College students.** Saint Mary's College is in Moraga, and students from other campuses live in the East Bay too.
- **Israelis and Russian speakers** far from relatives, who often miss a real Shabbat table in their own language.
- **Seniors** who now eat alone, and widows and widowers.
- **Singles and young professionals** commuting on BART, and families new to Jewish life.

You do not need to know someone's level of observance. Every Jew is welcome at your table, just as every Jew is welcome at Chabad.

## How to invite without pressure

1. **Invite early and warmly.** "We would love to have you for Friday night. It is simple and friendly."
2. **Say what to expect.** Tell guests the start time, that there is no dress code, and how long it usually lasts. For this week's candle lighting and Kabbalat Shabbat time, see [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times); the Kabbalat Shabbat time this week is {kabbalat-shabbat}.
3. **Promise no pressure.** A guest should never feel tested. Nobody has to know the blessings or sing.
4. **Offer a ride or a walk together** if the guest does not live close. If a guest observes Shabbat, they will need to walk or arrive before it begins.
5. **Ask about food needs** such as allergies or vegetarian, and about kosher (below).

## A simple menu and a simple evening

Keep it easy so you can enjoy it:

- Two challahs, wine or grape juice, and a simple soup or salad.
- One main dish prepared before Shabbat, such as chicken or a vegetarian casserole, plus two sides.
- Fruit or a cake for dessert.

A relaxed rhythm works well: candles, Kiddush, washing and the challah, the meal with a song or two between courses, and a short Torah thought on the weekly portion (see this week's [parsha](/parsha)). If you are nervous, read [Hosting Your First Shabbat Dinner](/learn/hosting-your-first-shabbat-dinner), and use the [Shabbat guest guide](/learn/shabbat-guest-guide) as a handout.

## Making everyone comfortable

- **Non-observant guests:** explain each step in one sentence. "We cover the challah because..." Invite, but never insist, that they join in.
- **Kosher guests:** if you are not keeping kosher yourself, a guest may prefer to bring food or eat only certain things. Read [hosting a kosher guest](/learn/hosting-a-kosher-guest) and ask the Rabbi if you are unsure.
- **Children:** give them a job, like passing the challah or lighting a candle with a parent. A kids' table with a quiet activity helps adults talk.
- **Conversation:** ask questions that welcome people: "Where is your family from?" "What was Shabbat like in your home?" Avoid putting anyone on the spot about their Jewish knowledge.

## Common questions

### How do I host Shabbat guests for the first time?
Invite one or two people, cook a simple meal before Shabbat, light candles, say Kiddush and eat together. You do not need to know everything: a warm welcome is the mitzvah. Our guide to [hosting your first Shabbat dinner](/learn/hosting-your-first-shabbat-dinner) walks you through it.

### Do I have to keep kosher to host Shabbat guests?
Hosting is a mitzvah in any home. If a guest keeps kosher, ask what they need, and consider a vegetarian meal on disposable or new dishes. For your own kitchen, ask the Rabbi at 925-384-7242.

### How do I find people to invite in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda?
Start with neighbors, coworkers, newcomers and seniors you know. You can also tell Chabad of Lamorinda that you have room and we will be glad to help you meet a guest.

### What if my guests are not observant?
Welcome them as they are. Explain each custom briefly and let them take part as they like. Every Jew belongs at the Shabbat table.

### Can children come to a Shabbat dinner?
Yes. Children make the table lively. Give them a small role and keep the evening short enough for them.

### What if I cannot cook a big meal?
Hosting does not mean cooking everything. Share the cooking, order kosher food in advance, or invite guests for a simple Friday-night dessert and songs.
