Iyar (אִיָּר) is the second month of the Jewish calendar, falling in April or May, and it is known as the Jewish month of healing. Its Hebrew letters form the first letters of the verse "I am the L-rd, your healer" (Exodus 15:26), and every one of its 29 days is a day of counting the Omer on the way from Passover to Shavuot.

## What does Iyar mean?

Iyar is the second month counting from Nissan and the eighth counting from Tishrei, since the Jewish calendar has two "starts" (see [the Jewish calendar](/learn/jewish-calendar)). The Torah calls it simply "the second month," and the Prophets call it "the month of radiance" (Chodesh Ziv), the time when the trees blossom. Chabad.org notes a connection to the Hebrew word for light, "ohr," and to the manna that began to fall in this month. The name itself, as the Talmud teaches, came back with the Jews from the Babylonian exile.

## Healing after the excitement of Nissan

[Nissan](/learn/nissan) is full of dramatic events: the Exodus, the Seder, the miracles. Iyar is the quiet month that follows. Chabad.org teaches that this is its challenge: after the high of the holiday, to keep our Torah and mitzvot fresh and alive. Jewish tradition says G-d gives us "preventative medicine" for this, and we need only the effort to stay curious and engaged. It is the right month to ask yourself, what small habit would heal or strengthen me? Perhaps lighting [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles), saying a short prayer in the morning, or calling someone who is lonely.

## The Omer: every day counts

The Omer is counted from the second night of Passover until Shavuot, 49 days. Iyar is the only month in which every day is counted. Each night we say a blessing and count aloud, and ask G-d to help us refine a part of our character. Chabad.org adds that Iyar's zodiac sign is Taurus, the bull, which stands for our animal soul, strong and in need of direction. Counting the Omer is how we prepare to receive the Torah on Shavuot. See [counting the Omer](/learn/counting-the-omer).

## Days of Iyar

- **Pesach Sheni (14 Iyar).** In the days of the Temple, someone who could not bring the Passover offering was given a second chance one month later. Today we eat a piece of matzah to mark it, and its message is that it is never too late to fix a mistake. See [Pesach Sheni](/learn/pesach-sheni).
- **Lag BaOmer (18 Iyar).** The 33rd day of the Omer is a day of joy, bonfires and outings for children. It marks the end of a plague among the students of Rabbi Akiva, and the day Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai asked that his passing be celebrated. In Chabad communities there are children's parades. See [Lag BaOmer](/learn/lag-baomer) and the [Lag BaOmer holiday page](/holidays/lag-baomer).
- **Israel's days.** Iyar also holds Israel's Independence Day and Jerusalem Day. Ask the Rabbi how these are marked in your community.

## Common questions

### What is the month of Iyar?
Iyar is the second month of the Jewish calendar, in April or May, and it is known as the month of healing. It has 29 days, every one of which is a day of the Omer count.

### Why is Iyar called the month of healing?
Its Hebrew letters make up the first letters of "I am the L-rd, your healer." The month follows the excitement of Nissan, and its challenge is to keep our Jewish life fresh and alive.

### What holidays are in Iyar?
Iyar contains Pesach Sheni on the 14th, Lag BaOmer on the 18th, and Israel's Independence Day and Jerusalem Day. Dates are on the [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays) page.

### Do you count the Omer every day in Iyar?
Yes. Iyar is the only month in which all days are days of the Omer count. If you forget one night, ask the Rabbi what to do.

### Are there any restrictions in Iyar?
Customs of mourning during part of the Omer vary by community, so ask the Rabbi what applies to you.
