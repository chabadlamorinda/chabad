Yes, it is normal to keep struggling, and Tanya says the struggle is not a sign that something is wrong with you. In Chapter 27, Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi teaches that a person should not be deeply discouraged even if the battle with his inner pull lasts a lifetime, since this struggle may be exactly what he was created for.

If you feel like a hypocrite because you pray, keep Shabbat or give charity and still feel pulled in the wrong direction, this guide is for you.

## "I thought I would be over this by now"

Many people expect that sincere effort will make temptation disappear. Then, years later, the same impulse returns, and they think, "I must be fake."

Tanya sees it differently. According to Chapter 12 of the Tanya, even the beinoni (see [the beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni)) has an animal soul that is still active and still stirs up desires, especially after prayer, when the closeness of prayer fades. The difference is what he does with them. He does not act on them. The struggle remains, and so does his choice.

## The struggle may be the point

Chapter 27 goes further. It says that being somewhat troubled by wrong thoughts is right, since complete indifference would end the struggle, but deep depression is not. The chapter reads the words "You have created wicked men" to mean that people are created so that wrong thoughts come to them, not so that they actually act wickedly. Only tzaddikim fully remove the evil from within.

It also describes two kinds of Divine pleasure: the first when darkness is completely turned into light, as tzaddikim do, and the second when the negative force is subdued while it is still at full strength, as beinonim do. The chapter compares them to sweet foods and to sharp or sour foods that are spiced until they become delicious. In this picture, your effort is not a failure to be perfect; it is its own kind of gift.

## You do not have to feel it

Chapter 14 adds relief. The rank of beinoni, it says, is open to every person "at any time or hour," and it does not depend on feeling. A person may not yet hate evil in his heart, because feelings change with the day. What is in our control is choice: to turn away from evil and do good in action, speech and thought. So the person who struggles, and keeps choosing, is doing exactly what Tanya asks.

## Honest, but not harsh

None of this excuses carelessness. Chapter 30 teaches that we should honestly measure our own effort against the struggle another person might be facing, and not judge others until we are in their place. See [Tanya on humility](/learn/tanya-on-humility). When we do fall, [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah) is always open, and [success and failure](/learn/success-and-failure) looks at how a fall can become a step forward.

## A word of care

Tanya speaks about a spiritual struggle. If your struggle involves addiction, constant hopelessness or thoughts of harming yourself, please get professional help right away; that is also what Jewish tradition asks of us. See [addiction and recovery](/learn/addiction-and-recovery). For a personal situation, ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### Is it normal to struggle with the same sin again and again?
Yes. Tanya says the inner struggle may last a lifetime, and that one should not be deeply discouraged. Falling does not cancel the effort; teshuvah is always possible.

### Does struggling mean I am a hypocrite?
No. Tanya's beinoni feels the pull and does not act on it. Feeling a wrong desire is not hypocrisy; choosing right while feeling it is the point.

### Why doesn't G-d just remove my temptation?
Chapter 27 suggests that the struggle may be exactly what a person was created for. Only tzaddikim fully remove the evil within, and the beinoni's battle has its own value.

### Do I have to feel spiritual to be on the right path?
No. Chapter 14 says the beinoni level does not depend on feeling, but on choosing in action, speech and thought.

### What if I feel hopeless about changing?
Tanya's guidance is spiritual, and professional help is also right when needed. Ask the Rabbi, and see [changing a bad habit](/learn/changing-a-bad-habit).
