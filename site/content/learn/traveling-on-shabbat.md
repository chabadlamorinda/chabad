Business trips, vacations, college visits and family simchas often land on a weekend. With some planning, Shabbat on the road can be one of the most memorable Shabbats of the year.

## Plan to arrive early

- **Book travel so you arrive well before candle lighting** where you are going, with time to spare. Shabbat begins at sunset in the place you are, not at home.
- **Flights get delayed.** Avoid the last flight before Shabbat; leaving a day early is safest.
- **Check the local times** for your destination before you leave.
- **Crossing time zones or the date line?** Ask the Rabbi before booking.
- If you are delayed and Shabbat is about to start, call the Rabbi as soon as you know.

## Find Chabad wherever you go

There are Chabad centers in cities, college towns and vacation spots around the world. Most welcome travelers for Shabbat services and meals, often with no charge, and many can help with kosher food. Search Chabad.org for the nearest center and contact them a few days ahead. Rabbi Labkowski is also glad to help you connect.

## What to pack

- **Candles:** tea lights or small candles with a holder. Many hotels do not allow open flames; ask the hotel, and ask the Rabbi about electric lights. See [Shabbat Candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).
- **Kiddush:** a small bottle of kosher wine or grape juice and a cup.
- **Challah:** two rolls or matzah for [Hamotzi](/learn/hamotzi).
- **Food:** sealed kosher meals or snacks that need no cooking. See [Kosher When Traveling](/learn/kosher-when-traveling).
- **A siddur** and a printed copy of the Shabbat times.

A man traveling alone lights Shabbat candles himself, just as a woman would at home.

## Hotels

Modern hotels raise Shabbat questions:

- **Electronic key cards** and door locks.
- **Elevators,** automatic doors and motion-sensor lights in halls and rooms.
- **Carrying** your key outside the room where there is no [eruv](/learn/eruv).

There are practical solutions, such as asking for a low floor, a mechanical key, or for staff to open the door. The details matter, so **ask the Rabbi before your trip.**

## On the way

Say [Tefilat Haderech](/learn/tefilat-haderech), the traveler's prayer, when you set out. Give a coin to tzedakah before you leave; you become a "shliach mitzvah," a messenger to deliver it.

## Common questions

### My flight is delayed and Shabbat is starting. What do I do?
Call the Rabbi right away if you can. In general, plan to stop wherever you are when Shabbat begins, so leave a big margin.

### Can I keep Shabbat in a hotel with key cards?
Yes, with some planning. Ask the Rabbi what to arrange with the hotel before you go.

### Will a Chabad center host me if I don't know anyone?
Almost always. Chabad welcomes every Jew. Contact them a few days ahead so they can plan.

### What about holidays away from home?
The same planning applies, and some holidays last two or three days. Pack more food and call the Rabbi.
