To prepare a child for Jewish camp, talk about what a day will look like, pack simply and clearly labeled, check the camp's kosher and Shabbat arrangements ahead of time, and practice short separations at home. Most children settle in quickly when they know what to expect and feel that the people at camp are warm and kind.

## Day camp or overnight camp?

Day camp means your child is home at night; overnight camp means sleeping away for a week or more. Day camps are a gentle start, while overnight camps ask more of a child and of parents. For the thinking behind overnight camps, see [Jewish overnight camp](/learn/jewish-overnight-camp). In Lamorinda, see [Jewish summer camp in Lamorinda](/learn/jewish-summer-camp-in-lamorinda).

Lamorinda Jewish Summer Camp, run by Chabad of Lamorinda, is for ages 2 to 4. It runs seven weeks in the summer, 8:30 AM to 5:00 PM, with Jewish songs and movement, Shabbat celebrations, water play, arts and crafts, sensory play, Hebrew and traditions, mitzvahs and kindness. Spaces are limited. See [the camp page](/programs/summer-camp) for details.

## Talking with your child before camp

1. **Describe the day.** "You will sing, play outside, eat lunch with friends and have a Shabbat party." Specific pictures calm worries.
2. **Visit if you can.** Seeing the place and meeting a teacher or counselor makes it familiar.
3. **Read a book about camp.** Stories help children imagine themselves there.
4. **Practice small separations.** A morning with a grandparent or a friend helps a child learn that parents always come back.
5. **Let your child help pack.** Choosing a water bottle or a favorite shirt gives a sense of control.

## What to pack

Every camp gives its own list, so follow it first. A general packing checklist for a Jewish camp often includes:

- **Clothes for the weather,** closed shoes, sun hat, swimsuit and towel, and a change of clothes for younger children.
- **Sunscreen** and a water bottle, labeled with your child's name.
- **Shabbat clothes.** Many camps ask for something a little nicer for Friday.
- **Tzitzit and a kippah** for a boy who wears them. Chabad teaches that boys begin wearing tzitzit and a kippah around age three. See [tzitzit](/learn/tzitzit) and [kippah](/learn/kippah). Bring a spare kippah in the bag.
- **Pocket tzedakah money** if your child likes to give. See [children, money and tzedakah](/learn/children-money-and-tzedakah).
- **A comfort item** such as a small stuffed animal or a family photo, if the camp allows.
- **For overnight camp:** pajamas, a flashlight, a prayer book if the camp suggests one, and stationery for letters home.

Label everything. Children lose things quickly.

## Kosher food at camp

If your family keeps kosher, ask the camp before enrolling: Is all the food kosher? Does it have kosher certification? What about snacks and food that families bring? Chabad of Lamorinda's own kosher program, [Bay Kosher](/programs/bay-kosher), is the local source for kosher food. If your child has allergies or dietary needs, tell the camp director in writing. For any halachic question, such as milk and meat or food brought from home, ask the Rabbi. See [kosher in Lamorinda](/learn/kosher-in-lamorinda) as well.

## Shabbat at camp

Many Jewish camps celebrate Shabbat with candles, challah, songs and a special meal. At a day camp, this is often done on Friday as a Shabbat party. Overnight camps may keep Shabbat for the whole day; ask the camp what its Shabbat looks like and what children should wear or bring. Prepare your child at home with candles and blessings; see [Shabbat with kids](/learn/shabbat-with-kids).

## Homesickness: what really helps

- **Normalize it.** Say that it is common to miss home and that it passes.
- **Keep goodbyes short and warm.** A long goodbye makes it harder. Smile, hug, say you will be back, and go.
- **Write notes.** For overnight camp, write ahead of time and send cheerful letters. Avoid saying "come home if you are sad," which can add pressure.
- **Ask the staff.** A counselor can tell you how your child is really doing.
- **Give it time.** Many children feel homesick for a day or two and then they love it.

If your child is really struggling, speak with the camp director together. Parents of young children can also call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242 for advice.

## Common questions

### What should my child bring to Jewish camp?
Follow the camp's list. Usually this includes weather-appropriate clothes, sunscreen, a water bottle and, for boys, tzitzit and a kippah. Label everything.

### Do I need to ask whether the camp is kosher?
Yes. Ask whether all food is kosher, who supervises it and what to do about snacks. For questions of halacha ask the Rabbi.

### How do I help my child with homesickness?
Prepare ahead, keep goodbyes short, write cheerful notes and trust that the feeling passes. If it does not, talk to the camp director.

### What is Shabbat like at camp?
Many Jewish camps celebrate Shabbat with candles, challah and songs. Ask the camp how it is done, and prepare your child at home.

### At what age can a child go to day camp?
It depends on the camp. Lamorinda Jewish Summer Camp is for ages 2 to 4. Overnight camps usually start at an older age; each camp sets its own rules.

### Does Chabad of Lamorinda have a camp for older children?
The Lamorinda Jewish Summer Camp is for ages 2 to 4. For older children, call 925-384-7242 to hear what is planned.
