Maimonides, known in Hebrew as the Rambam, was Rabbi Moshe ben Maimon, one of the greatest Jewish scholars of all time. He lived in the 12th century, wrote the first complete code of Jewish law, and was also a philosopher and a renowned physician.

## His life

Maimonides was born in Cordoba, Spain, in 1138 (some sources say 1135). When he was young, a harsh regime took power and persecuted Jews, and his family was forced to flee. They wandered through Spain and North Africa before settling in Egypt.

In Egypt, Maimonides became the leader of the Jewish community. After his brother David, who supported the family as a merchant, drowned at sea, Maimonides began working as a physician. He became a doctor to the royal court, while still answering questions from Jews around the world and writing his great works.

He passed away in 1204, on the 20th of Tevet. He is buried in Tiberias, in the Land of Israel.

A famous saying captures his greatness: "From Moses to Moses, there was none like Moses."

## His major works

- **Commentary on the Mishnah.** Written while still young, it explains the [Mishnah](/learn/mishnah) clearly and includes his Thirteen Principles of Faith.
- **Sefer HaMitzvot (Book of the Commandments).** A list and explanation of all [613 mitzvot](/learn/613-mitzvot).
- **Mishneh Torah.** His masterpiece: fourteen books covering all of Jewish law, written in clear Hebrew and organized by topic. It is also called *Yad HaChazakah*, "the strong hand," since the Hebrew letters of *yad* equal fourteen. Read more in our guide to the [Rambam](/learn/rambam).
- **Guide for the Perplexed.** A philosophical work for thinkers struggling to reconcile faith and reason.
- **Medical writings** on health, diet and healing.

## The Thirteen Principles of Faith

Maimonides summarized Jewish belief in thirteen principles, including that G-d exists, is One, has no body, and is eternal; that we pray only to G-d; that Moses was the greatest prophet; that the Torah is from G-d and will not be changed; that G-d knows our deeds and rewards and punishes; that [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach) will come; and that the dead will be resurrected. Many prayer books include these as the hymn *Yigdal* and as a list beginning "Ani maamin" (I believe).

## Famous teachings

- **The eight levels of tzedakah.** The highest is helping someone become self-supporting, through a loan, a partnership or a job.
- **The middle path.** Good character means balance: not stingy and not wasteful, not proud and not self-deprecating, with special care to avoid anger and pride.
- **Every person can change.** He wrote that any person can become as righteous as Moses.

## Daily Rambam study

In 1984, the Lubavitcher Rebbe launched a daily study cycle of the Mishneh Torah, so Jews everywhere could learn all of Jewish law together. People can study three chapters a day, one chapter a day, or the Sefer HaMitzvot.

## Common questions

### Is "Rambam" the same as Maimonides?
Yes. Rambam is an acronym of **R**abbi **M**oshe **b**en **M**aimon. Maimonides is the Greek form of his name.

### Was Maimonides really a doctor?
Yes. He was a respected physician who served the royal court in Egypt and wrote medical works that were studied for centuries.

### Where should I start reading Maimonides?
His laws of character traits (Hilchot De'ot) and laws of teshuvah are clear and inspiring places to begin. The Rabbi can help you choose.
