The story of Joseph and his brothers is the Torah's account of Jacob's favorite son, who is sold by his jealous brothers, rises from slavery and prison to rule Egypt, and finally forgives them and reunites the family. We read it in the Torah portions Vayeshev, Miketz and Vayigash, which usually fall in the weeks of Chanukah, so the light of Chanukah and the story of Joseph are read side by side.

## The story, in plain words

**The coat and the dreams.** Jacob (Yaakov) loved Joseph (Yosef), the first son of his beloved Rachel, and gave him a special striped coat. Joseph, seventeen, told his brothers dreams in which their sheaves, and then the sun, moon and eleven stars, bowed to him. The brothers' resentment grew.

**The pit and the sale.** When Joseph came to find them in the fields, the brothers threw him into an empty pit. Judah suggested selling him instead, and Joseph was taken to Egypt by passing traders. The brothers dipped his coat in blood and brought it to their father, who mourned for many years.

**Egypt and prison.** In Egypt Joseph served in the house of Potiphar and was trusted with everything. Falsely accused, he was thrown into prison, where he interpreted the dreams of Pharaoh's cupbearer and baker. The cupbearer forgot him for two years.

**From prison to palace.** Pharaoh dreamed of seven fat cows eaten by seven thin ones. Joseph, then thirty, explained that Egypt would have seven years of plenty and then seven of famine, and advised storing grain. Pharaoh made him viceroy.

**The brothers return.** Famine reached Canaan, and the brothers came to buy food. Joseph recognized them; they did not recognize him. He tested them, and when Judah offered himself as a slave in place of young Benjamin, Joseph could no longer hold back. "I am Joseph," he said. "Is my father still alive?"

**Forgiveness.** Joseph told his brothers not to be distressed: everything had been part of a Divine plan to save many lives. Jacob came down to Egypt with his family and saw his son after twenty-two years. That is how the Jewish people came to Egypt, and the book of Genesis closes with the stage set for [the Exodus](/learn/exodus-story).

## Why do we read it around Chanukah?

The Torah reading follows the calendar, so the timing is not an accident of the schedule alone: the Jewish calendar and the Torah portions are arranged together. Chabad teaching draws a few connections:

- **Light in the darkest place.** Joseph lived in Egypt, then the most spiritually dark place, and remained Joseph, true to his father's house. Chanukah is about keeping a Jewish flame alive in a foreign culture. See [the lessons of Chanukah](/learn/lessons-of-chanukah).
- **From the pit to the palace.** Chanukah celebrates that a small flame can overcome a great darkness. Joseph's own life, from a pit to Pharaoh's palace, is the same message.
- **Unity.** The story ends with brothers reconciled. A family or a people whose flame is shared is stronger than one divided.

For the Chanukah story itself, see [the story of Chanukah](/learn/chanukah-story).

## What the story asks of us

Joseph could have lived in bitterness. Instead he saw a larger picture: "G-d sent me before you to preserve life." That does not excuse the brothers' act, but it teaches us to look for the Divine plan in hardship. His forgiveness of his brothers is a model for any family hurt; for more, see [forgiveness and making peace](/learn/forgiveness-and-making-peace) and [sibling rivalry](/learn/sibling-rivalry). Joseph is also the Torah's great interpreter of [dreams](/learn/dreams-jewish-view).

## Common questions

### What is the story of Joseph and his brothers?
It is the Torah's account of Jacob's son Joseph, sold into slavery by his brothers, who rises to become ruler of Egypt and forgives them when famine brings them to him. It is told in Genesis, from Vayeshev through Vayigash.

### Which Torah portions tell the story of Joseph?
Vayeshev, Miketz and Vayigash, and the story continues into Vayechi. Check [the parsha page](/parsha) for the date each is read this year.

### Why do we read Miketz on Chanukah?
Miketz is usually read on the Shabbat of Chanukah. The calendar places Joseph's rise from darkness to light in the season of Chanukah, and Chabad teaching sees in both the message that a small light overcomes darkness.

### Why did Joseph's brothers sell him?
The Torah says they were jealous of their father's love and of Joseph's dreams. Our commentators discuss how they saw the matter, and the Torah itself makes clear that they later regretted it.

### Did Joseph forgive his brothers?
Yes. He told them that G-d had sent him ahead to save lives, and he cared for the whole family in Egypt. Jewish tradition also teaches that forgiveness is a path to peace.
