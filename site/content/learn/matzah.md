Matzah is flat, unleavened bread made only of flour and water, baked quickly before it can rise. Eating matzah on the first night of Passover is a mitzvah from the Torah, and we eat it in place of bread for the whole holiday.

## What is matzah?

Matzah is made from one of five grains, usually wheat, mixed only with water. From the moment the water touches the flour until the matzah comes out of the oven, the dough is kneaded and worked constantly, and the whole process takes less than 18 minutes. This keeps it from becoming [chametz](/learn/chametz).

## Why we eat matzah

The Torah calls matzah "the bread of affliction" (Deuteronomy 16:3), the simple food of slaves. It is also the bread of freedom. When the Jewish people left Egypt, they left so quickly that their dough did not have time to rise.

The Zohar calls matzah "the food of faith" and "the food of healing." Chassidic teaching explains that matzah, flat and humble, represents setting aside our ego so G-d's light can enter.

## What is shmurah matzah?

Shmurah means "guarded." Shmurah matzah is made from wheat watched from the time it was harvested, to make sure no water touched it. It is usually round and baked by hand, as matzah was baked for centuries.

- Each matzah is made with the intention that it is for the mitzvah.
- Many have the custom to use only handmade shmurah matzah for the Seder, and Chabad custom is to eat only shmurah matzah throughout Passover.
- Machine-made shmurah matzah also exists, and all kosher for Passover matzah is supervised. But the matzah at the Seder should be shmurah.

## How much to eat at the Seder

At the [Seder](/learn/passover-seder), there are several times we eat matzah:

1. **Matzah:** after the blessings, a portion of about one ounce or more (a kezayit, the size of an olive by halachic measure). Many eat more.
2. **Korech:** a sandwich of matzah with bitter herbs.
3. **Afikoman:** a final piece at the end of the meal.

Lean to the left while eating matzah, as a sign of freedom. Try to eat each portion within a few minutes.

## The blessing

Before eating the matzah at the Seder, we say Hamotzi and then this blessing:

**Hebrew:** בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו, וְצִוָּנוּ עַל אֲכִילַת מַצָּה

**Transliteration:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu al achilat matzah.

**Translation:** Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us concerning eating matzah.

## Matzah during the rest of Passover

After the Seder nights, eating matzah is not required, but it is a mitzvah to enjoy it, and it takes the place of bread. Whenever you eat a meal with matzah, wash your hands as you would for bread, say Hamotzi, and say Grace after Meals.

## Common questions

### Why is matzah not eaten before Passover?
Many have the custom not to eat matzah from Rosh Chodesh Nissan, or at least on the day before Passover, so that it is eaten with appetite and freshness at the Seder.

### What is egg matzah?
Egg matzah is made with fruit juice or eggs instead of water. Ashkenazi custom is not to eat it on Passover except for the elderly or sick, and it cannot be used for the Seder.

### Can I use gluten-free matzah?
Matzah for the mitzvah must be made from one of the five grains. Oat matzah exists for those with gluten sensitivities. People with celiac disease or other conditions should ask the Rabbi.

### What is gebrokts?
Gebrokts means matzah that has come into contact with water, such as matzah balls. Chabad and many Chassidic communities avoid gebrokts on the first seven days of Passover.
