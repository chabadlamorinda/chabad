Jews eat dairy foods on Shavuot, like cheesecake, blintzes and cheese kreplach, as a sweet reminder of receiving the Torah at Mount Sinai. Many families have a dairy meal or kiddush and also a festive meat meal, keeping them properly separate.

## The holiday

Shavuot celebrates the day G-d gave the Torah to the Jewish people at Mount Sinai, seven weeks after the Exodus. We count the days from Passover to Shavuot with the [Counting of the Omer](/learn/counting-the-omer). On Shavuot, everyone, including children, comes to synagogue to hear the [Ten Commandments](/learn/ten-commandments). See our [Shavuot page](/holidays/shavuot).

## Why dairy? Several reasons

- **New kosher laws.** When the Jewish people received the Torah, they learned the laws of kosher slaughter and of [meat and milk](/learn/meat-and-milk). Their pots were not yet kosher, and preparing kosher meat takes time, so they ate dairy.
- **Torah is like milk.** King Solomon wrote, "Honey and milk are under your tongue" (Song of Songs 4:11), comparing Torah to sweet, nourishing milk.
- **Forty days.** The Hebrew word for milk, chalav, has the numerical value of 40, recalling the 40 days Moses spent on Mount Sinai.
- **Mount of cheese.** One of the names of Mount Sinai is Har Gavnunim, which sounds like gevinah, cheese.

## Dairy and meat on the same day

Shavuot is a festival, and the joy of a festival is traditionally expressed with meat and wine. So many families, including Chabad, have a dairy kiddush or dairy meal after the morning service and later a separate meat meal. To do this properly:

1. **Finish the dairy food** and clear the table.
2. **Change the tablecloth** and use separate dishes.
3. **Eat something pareve** like bread or crackers, and rinse your mouth or drink something.
4. **Wash your hands.**
5. **Wait** before the meat meal according to your custom. After hard, aged cheese, people wait the same as after meat (six hours in common practice), so many choose soft cheeses on Shavuot.

When a detail is unclear, ask the Rabbi.

## Favorite dairy dishes

- **Cheesecake**, the best-known Shavuot dessert.
- **Blintzes**, thin pancakes filled with sweet cheese. Two blintzes side by side are said to look like the two tablets.
- **Cheese kreplach** and dairy kugels.
- **Quiches, lasagna and pasta bakes**, easy to prepare before the holiday.

Remember that cooking on a festival is allowed only for that day's meals, and only from an existing flame. If Shavuot is followed by Shabbat, you need an eruv tavshilin to cook for Shabbat.

## Other Shavuot customs

- **Staying up all night** on the first night to learn Torah.
- **Decorating with flowers and greenery**, recalling that Mount Sinai blossomed.
- **Reading the Book of Ruth**, the story of a woman who embraced the Torah.
- **Remembering King David** and the Baal Shem Tov, whose yahrzeits are on Shavuot.

## Common questions

### Do I have to eat dairy on Shavuot?
It is a widespread custom, not an obligation. Many also eat meat at the festive meal, since meat and wine bring joy on a festival.

### How long do I wait between dairy and meat on Shavuot?
After soft cheeses and milk, it is enough to eat something pareve, rinse your mouth, wash your hands and use a separate tablecloth. After hard, aged cheese, the custom is to wait as you would after meat. Ask the Rabbi about specific cheeses.

### Why are blintzes eaten on Shavuot?
They are an easy dairy dish, and two rolled blintzes resemble the two tablets of the Ten Commandments.

### Can children come to hear the Ten Commandments?
Yes. Bringing children, even babies, is a special Shavuot custom, because the whole people stood together at Sinai.
