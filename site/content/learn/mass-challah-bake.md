A mass challah bake is a gathering where many women, often of every age and background, bake challah dough together, take challah with the blessing and learn what the mitzvah means. It turns a task usually done alone in a kitchen into an evening of friendship, learning and Shabbat spirit.

## What happens at a challah bake

The format varies from place to place. Jewish tradition describes one weekly challah bake where people of all ages gathered at long tables. Someone gave an overview of the meaning of challah, the participants baked challah for Shabbat, gave charity and recited a blessing, and learned about the weekly holiday. At a larger "mega" bake, the same pieces happen with more people.

Chabad of Lamorinda's Jewish Women's Circle lists the Mega Challah Bake among its past evenings, along with creative workshops and Sip & Paint. To hear when the next gathering is, call 925-384-7242 or join the [Women's Circle](/programs/womens-circle) list.

## The mitzvah underneath: taking challah

The heart of a challah bake is the mitzvah of *hafrashat challah* (הפרשת חלה). The Torah says to give "the first of your kneading" (Numbers 15). The tradition explains the steps:

1. Knead a dough from wheat, barley, spelt, oat or rye flour.
2. A piece of about an ounce is separated and said to be challah, after the blessing.
3. Because the Temple is not standing and the challah is not given to a *kohen*, the piece is not eaten: because of its holiness it may not be used, so it is burned (many wrap it in foil first).

The blessing is said when the dough is large enough. Traditional guides to women's mitzvot give 59 ounces of flour for a blessing; for a smaller amount, ask the Rabbi or Rebbetzin. See the full steps in [taking challah](/learn/hafrashat-challah) and [baking challah](/learn/baking-challah).

## What baking together means

Chabad teaching gives several ideas:

- **Everything is a gift.** Setting aside the first portion of the dough acknowledges that everything we have comes from G-d, and that serving Him comes first.
- **A link to Shabbat.** The challah brings the Shabbat feeling into the meal. See [challah](/learn/challah).
- **A link to the past.** Traditional teaching tells of a care center where baking challah brought elderly residents from Eastern Europe, who had been hard to engage, to life, as they remembered baking in their youth.

Challah is also one of the three special mitzvot given to women, alongside Shabbat candles and family purity: [the three mitzvot of Jewish women](/learn/three-mitzvot-for-jewish-women).

## Praying at a challah bake

Some women use the moment of taking challah for personal prayer. The traditional description of the mitzvah mentions the blessing; any further prayers are a matter of personal custom, so ask the Rabbi or Rebbetzin if you want guidance. You may also hear that there is a special power when forty or more women bake together. That is a popular custom that the traditional sources do not describe; ask the Rabbi what Chabad teaching says. Read [praying for others](/learn/praying-for-others).

## Common questions

### What is a mass challah bake?
It is an event where many women bake challah together, take challah with a blessing and learn about the mitzvah. Often they bake challah for Shabbat.

### Do I need to know how to bake?
You do not need to be an expert to take part; ask the organizers what to bring when you sign up. For home baking see [baking challah](/learn/baking-challah).

### Why is part of the dough burned?
Challah was once given to the *kohen*. Today that is not possible, and because the piece is holy it may not be used, so, as Jewish tradition explains, it is burned.

### Can I bring a friend or my daughter?
The tradition describes a bake where people of all ages gathered. Ask about a particular event when you call 925-384-7242.

### Is there a challah bake in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda?
Chabad of Lamorinda's Women's Circle has held a Mega Challah Bake. Call 925-384-7242 to hear what is planned.

### Can I take challah at home?
Yes. Read [taking challah](/learn/hafrashat-challah) and ask the Rebbetzin if you have questions.
