A kosher sukkah is a hut with at least two and a half walls that stand firm in ordinary wind, a roof of cut plant material (schach) that gives more shade than sun, and open sky above. In hilly, wooded Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, the main challenges are overhanging trees, wind and the slope of your yard.

The sukkah (סֻכָּה) is where we eat and spend time during Sukkot. For the holiday itself, see [Sukkot for beginners](/learn/sukkot-for-beginners) and [the sukkah](/learn/sukkah). Dates are on [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays).

## Choose the spot

Chabad.org's "Sukkology 101" teaches that any area under a tree, a house roof or another covering is not part of the sukkah. So:

- **Open sky.** Pick a flat, open place away from oak branches and eaves. A tree that looks high now may reach over the schach, so stand under the spot and look up.
- **Level ground.** On a hillside, build on the flattest part of the yard or deck; a sloped floor makes the walls and table hard to steady.
- **Decks.** A deck is fine if the sky is open above it. A balcony with another balcony or an eave directly above does not work (see below).
- **Easy to reach the kitchen,** so that meals are easy to carry.
- **Away from fire risk:** not near dry brush or the house's vents, and clear from grills, candles and lights.

If an overhanging branch is the problem, the options are to move the sukkah, trim the branch (checking with your neighbors and any rules), or ask the Rabbi.

## Walls that stay up in the wind

According to Chabad.org, a sukkah needs at least two walls and a small part of a third, though three or four are better, and the walls must stand in ordinary wind. Fabric walls need to be tied tightly, so they do not flap. Practical tips for windy hills:

1. Use a sturdy frame, such as metal pipes with connectors, or wood, anchored with sandbags or stakes (where rules allow).
2. Tie fabric tightly at the bottom and corners so it does not billow.
3. Add walls on the side that gets the prevailing wind.
4. Check the frame before Shabbat and each yom tov, since you may not build on Shabbat or the holiday.

The minimum interior height is 10 handbreadths (about 40 inches) and the sukkah must be at least 7 by 7 handbreadths; ask the Rabbi for exact measures for your sukkah kit.

## The roof (schach)

Schach must be plant material that grew from the ground and was cut: for example bamboo poles, cedar or palm branches. Not allowed are metal, plastic, cloth, food or anything made by people (Chabad.org). Spread it so there is more shade than sun, but thin enough that you can see stars at night, and so rain can come through. Place the schach after the walls are up. A tarp or umbrella below the schach disqualifies the sukkah, so don't sit under one.

## Rain and early fall weather

Rain can come in early fall in the East Bay. Chabad.org says the sukkah is the one mitzvah you may leave if it truly bothers you, such as in wet weather: you may go inside and eat there. Many families stay if it is only a drizzle, and many step in at a downpour. Ask the Rabbi whether to say a blessing when you return. Cover the table, keep chairs dry, and put the cushions away.

## Fire season care

Early fall can still be dry and warm. Keep lights and extension cords away from the schach, avoid candles inside the sukkah unless you are sitting nearby, and never leave flames alone. Follow your fire district's instructions during red flag warnings; see [wildfire readiness](/learn/wildfire-readiness-for-jewish-families).

## Neighbors, HOAs and renters

Check your lease, your HOA's rules and your neighbors' view before building, especially on a shared balcony. Speak early and kindly; offer a time frame and a safe design. California law protects a mezuzah on your entry door but a sukkah is a different question; read [your mezuzah, HOA and landlord](/learn/mezuzah-and-religious-displays-hoa-california) and ask the Rabbi for ideas.

## Common questions

### How do I build a sukkah in my backyard?

Build at least two and a half walls that stand in ordinary wind, put cut plant material on top with more shade than sun, and leave the sky open above. Ask the Rabbi for details.

### Can I build a sukkah under a tree?

No. Any area under a tree or other covering is not part of the sukkah. Move the sukkah or trim the branches.

### Can I build a sukkah on a deck or balcony?

Yes, if the sky is open above the schach. A balcony directly under another balcony or eave does not work. Ask the Rabbi about your home.

### What if it rains on Sukkot?

You may go inside if the rain bothers you; it is the one mitzvah you can leave when it is uncomfortable. Return when it stops. Ask the Rabbi whether to bless on returning.

### Can my HOA stop me from building a sukkah?

Rules vary. Speak to the board early and offer a safe, temporary design; this is not legal advice. Ask the Rabbi for help.
