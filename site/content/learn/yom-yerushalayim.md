Yom Yerushalayim, or Jerusalem Day, is observed on the 28th of the Hebrew month of Iyar. It marks the day in 1967, during the Six-Day War, when the Old City of Jerusalem, the Temple Mount and the Western Wall came back under Jewish control for the first time in nearly two thousand years. It is a day of thanks for being able to pray at the Western Wall again.

## What happened in 1967

For nineteen years before 1967, Jews could not reach the Old City or the Western Wall. During the Six-Day War, Israeli soldiers reached the Old City, and on 28 Iyar the Western Wall was in Jewish hands again. A photograph of paratroopers standing in front of the Wall became famous around the world.

In Israel the day was later declared Jerusalem Day, and the Chief Rabbinate of Israel set it as a day of thanks. Many Jews all over the world mark it as well.

## Why Jerusalem matters

Jerusalem has been the heart of Jewish prayer since King David and King Solomon built the Holy Temple. Three times a day Jews face Jerusalem when we pray, and at every Jewish wedding we recall it. Read [Jerusalem in Judaism](/learn/jerusalem) and [the Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple).

## The Western Wall

The Western Wall, or Kotel, is a retaining wall of the ancient Temple Mount, and the place where Jews pray most often today. Visitors place folded notes with prayers in its cracks. See [the Western Wall](/learn/western-wall). On Yom Yerushalayim, large crowds come to pray and sing there.

## How Jews mark the day

Observance differs, and every approach is sincere:

- In Israel there are parades, songs and gatherings, and many pray at the Wall.
- Many communities say Hallel, some with a blessing and some without.
- Many Jews simply say Psalms and give thanks privately.

In Chabad communities the day is marked by reflecting on the miracle of Jerusalem and by mitzvah activity. During the Six-Day War, the Rebbe called on Jewish men to put on tefillin; see [tefillin](/learn/tefillin). Our customs about prayers for the day can vary, so ask the Rabbi.

## Ways anyone can mark it

- Read a few psalms (see [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim)); Psalm 122 is a song about Jerusalem.
- Learn about the Western Wall and the Temple together with your family.
- Send a note for the Wall through a friend visiting Israel, or [plan a first trip to Israel](/learn/first-trip-to-israel).
- Give charity in honor of Jerusalem.

## Common questions

### What is Yom Yerushalayim?
Jerusalem Day, the day marking the reunification of Jerusalem and the return of the Old City and the Western Wall to Jewish control in the Six-Day War in 1967.

### When is Yom Yerushalayim?
On the 28th of Iyar. See the [Jewish calendar](/learn/jewish-calendar) for this year.

### How do you celebrate Yom Yerushalayim?
Many say Psalms and Hallel, sing, learn about Jerusalem and give thanks. In Israel there are parades and gatherings, and many go to the Western Wall.

### Is Yom Yerushalayim a religious holiday?
The state established it and the Chief Rabbinate set it as a day of thanksgiving. How it is observed in prayer varies by community, so ask your Rabbi.

### What is the Western Wall?
A wall that held up the ancient Temple Mount in Jerusalem, and the holiest place where Jews pray today.

### What is the difference between Yom Yerushalayim and Tisha B'Av?
Tisha B'Av mourns the destruction of the Temple; Yom Yerushalayim gives thanks for Jewish access to Jerusalem.
