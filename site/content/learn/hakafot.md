Hakafot are the joyful circles of singing and dancing with the Torah scrolls on Simchat Torah. All the scrolls are taken out of the ark and carried around the synagogue seven times, with everyone, young and old, dancing along.

## What hakafot are

Hakafah means "circling." On Simchat Torah, we celebrate finishing the yearly cycle of reading the Torah and starting again from the beginning. Instead of studying the Torah, we dance with it, closed and wrapped in its cover.

Chassidic teaching explains why: on Simchat Torah, our joy does not depend on how much we know. A scholar and a person who cannot read Hebrew dance with the same scroll in the same way. We celebrate our simple, essential bond with G-d and His Torah, which every Jew shares equally.

## When hakafot take place

- **Simchat Torah night** and **Simchat Torah day** are the main hakafot.
- Outside Israel, Chabad and many others also hold hakafot on the night of **Shemini Atzeret**, the evening before.
- In Israel, Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah are one day, and hakafot are held then.

## What happens, step by step

1. **Atah Hareisa.** The evening begins with a series of verses praising G-d. In many synagogues, each verse is given as an honor to a different person, who says it aloud and the congregation repeats.
2. **The ark is opened** and all the Torah scrolls are taken out and handed to members of the community.
3. **The first hakafah begins.** The leader says a short set of verses, starting "Ana Hashem hoshi'a na," and then everyone circles the bimah (reading table) singing and dancing.
4. **The scrolls are passed around.** Between and during the hakafot, the scrolls change hands so that as many people as possible get to hold one.
5. **Seven hakafot** are completed, each with its own verses and songs. Often the dancing spills out into the street.
6. **The Torah is read.** In Chabad custom, the Torah is also read on Simchat Torah night, from the last portion of the Torah.

## Customs

- **Children** wave flags, ride on shoulders and join the circles. Many synagogues give out treats.
- **Everyone gets a turn** to hold a Torah or dance near it.
- **Women and girls** watch, sing and celebrate, often with their own festive gathering nearby.
- **Kiddush and food** keep the energy going for hours.

## Common questions

### Do I need to know the songs?
No. Most songs repeat a single line many times. Clap, sing along and join the circle. Your presence is the point.

### Why seven hakafot?
Seven is a number of completion in Jewish thought, like the seven days of creation. Kabbalah links the seven circles to the seven divine attributes and to the seven days of Sukkot.

### Can I hold a Torah if I am not observant?
Yes. On Simchat Torah, every Jewish man is encouraged to dance with a Torah. Holding it with care and joy is what matters. Read more in our [Torah](/learn/torah) guide.

### Can kids come?
Hakafot are one of the best Jewish experiences for children. Bring them with flags and energy, and keep an eye on them in the crowd.
