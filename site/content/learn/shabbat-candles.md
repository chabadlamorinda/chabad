Shabbat candles are lit by Jewish women and girls 18 minutes before sunset every Friday, and before Jewish holidays. Lighting them welcomes Shabbat into the home and brings light, peace and blessing to the whole family.

## What it is and why we do it

The Sages instituted lighting candles before Shabbat so the home would be bright and peaceful during the Shabbat meal. Light stands for shalom bayit, peace in the home. Jewish tradition calls the Shabbat candles a source of blessing for the family, and many women take a moment after lighting to pray for their loved ones.

Lighting is a special mitzvah of Jewish women, going back to Sarah, whose Shabbat candles, our Sages say, burned from one Friday to the next. If no woman is home, a man lights.

## How many candles?

- **Married women** light at least two candles, for the two commands "remember" and "keep" Shabbat. Many add one candle for each child.
- **Girls**, in Chabad custom, light one candle from about age three, with the blessing. The Rebbe encouraged this in 1974 as a way to add light to the world.
- Candles should be large enough to burn at least until the meal begins.

## How to light, step by step

1. **Check the time.** Find candle lighting time for your area on our [Shabbat page](/shabbat). Never light after sunset.
2. **Give tzedakah.** Many put a coin in a charity box first.
3. **Light the candles.** Use a match or a separate candle. Girls light before their mother so she can help them.
4. **Draw in the light.** Wave your hands around the flames toward you three times.
5. **Cover your eyes** and say the blessing (below).
6. **Pray.** This is a special moment for personal prayer.
7. **Uncover your eyes**, look at the candles and wish everyone "Shabbat Shalom."

Once the blessing is said, the woman has accepted Shabbat and does not do any work forbidden on Shabbat. Leave the candles where they are and let them burn out on their own.

## The blessing

**Hebrew:**
בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו, וְצִוָּנוּ לְהַדְלִיק נֵר שֶׁל שַׁבָּת קֹדֶשׁ

**Transliteration:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu l'hadlik ner shel Shabbat kodesh.

**Translation:** Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us to light the candle of the holy Shabbat.

Before holidays, the blessing ends differently, and on some nights the Shehecheyanu blessing is added. See our [holidays page](/holidays) or ask the Rabbi.

## Why cover the eyes?

Usually we say a blessing before doing a mitzvah. With Shabbat candles, saying the blessing first would mean accepting Shabbat and then no longer being allowed to light. So we light first, cover our eyes, say the blessing, and then open our eyes to enjoy the light, as if it were just lit.

## Common questions

### What time do I light Shabbat candles?
Light 18 minutes before sunset on Friday. The exact time changes each week, so check our [Shabbat page](/shabbat). If you are running late, you may light until sunset, but not after.

### Can I use electric or tea light candles?
Use real flames, such as wax candles, tea lights or oil cups. If you are in a hospital or a place where open flames are not allowed, ask the Rabbi about using an electric light.

### Can I light if I am not religious?
Yes. Lighting Shabbat candles is a beautiful mitzvah on its own, whatever else you do on Shabbat. Every candle adds light to your home and to the world.

### Where should the candles be?
Light where you will eat the Shabbat meal, or at least where you will see and enjoy the light. Place them somewhere safe, away from curtains and little hands.

### What if I am traveling or away from home?
Light where you will be staying and eating that night. In a hotel, ask the Rabbi for practical advice, since many hotels limit open flames.
