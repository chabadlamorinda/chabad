If you or your parents came from Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, Moldova or anywhere in the former Soviet Union, and now live in Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda, Pleasant Hill or Martinez, Chabad of Lamorinda would love to meet you. Many Jewish families grew up with little chance to practice Judaism openly. Whatever your story, you are welcome here, just as you are. There is no membership, no dues and no test.

## The website in Russian

This whole website is available in Russian. Tap **RU** at the top of any page to switch. Shabbat times, events, holidays, the help pages and the forms all appear in Russian, so parents and grandparents can read them too. [Create a free account](/account) and you can RSVP for the whole family in one tap.

## Come for Shabbat

Shabbat is the heart of Jewish life, and the easiest way to begin. **Kabbalat Shabbat** is on Friday evening at 6:30 PM (the time may change with the season). On **Saturday from 10:00 AM to 12:00 PM** there are Shabbat morning services with the Torah reading, a short talk and children taking part, and then a hot Kiddush lunch. The prayer book is in Hebrew and English, and the Rabbi announces the pages, so no one feels lost.

Rabbi Yisroel and Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski and their family also host Shabbat dinners, any week of the year: a traditional Friday night meal with songs and warm conversation. [Let us know you are coming](/forms/shabbat-meal). Candle lighting times for our area are on the [Shabbat page](/shabbat).

## Help with life events

Many families come to us at the big moments of life. The Rabbi helps with every one of them, whatever your background:

- **A new baby:** a bris for a boy, a naming at the Torah for a girl, and choosing a Hebrew name. See [new baby](/help/new-baby).
- **Bar and bat mitzvah,** for children and also for adults who never had one. See [bar and bat mitzvah](/help/bar-bat-mitzvah).
- **A Jewish wedding.** See [getting married](/help/getting-married).
- **Illness and loss:** visits, prayers, a Jewish funeral, shiva and Kaddish. See [someone is sick](/help/someone-is-sick) and [when a loved one passes away](/help/someone-passed-away).
- **Remembering parents and grandparents:** find the yahrzeit date with our [yahrzeit calculator](/tools/yahrzeit), arrange [Kaddish](/kaddish), and add names to the [Yizkor booklet](/forms/yizkor).

## Questions about Jewish roots

Sometimes documents were lost, names were changed, or a grandmother only spoke about being Jewish late in life. If you have questions about your family's Jewish roots, the Rabbi is glad to talk with you privately and without judgment. Our page [Am I Jewish?](/help/jewish-identity) explains how it works and which family documents can help.

## Common questions

### Is there a place for Russian-speaking Jews in Lamorinda?
Yes. Chabad of Lamorinda in Lafayette welcomes every Jew, including Russian-speaking families from Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda, Pleasant Hill and Martinez. The website is available in Russian.

### I did not grow up religious. Will I feel out of place?
No. Many people at our services are just starting. The Rabbi explains as we go, and questions are always welcome.

### How do I find the yahrzeit date for a parent or grandparent?
Use the [yahrzeit calculator](/tools/yahrzeit) and save it in [your account](/account) for a reminder every year. Call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242 if you would like Kaddish said.

### My grandmother was Jewish, but I have no documents. Am I Jewish?
If it was your mother's mother, you may well be Jewish. Gather what you can and talk to the Rabbi. See [Am I Jewish?](/help/jewish-identity)
