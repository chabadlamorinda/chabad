Every Jewish holiday has its foods, and Chanukah's are among the most loved: crispy potato latkes, jelly doughnuts and cheese treats. Each one tells part of the Chanukah story.

## Fried in oil: remembering the miracle

The miracle of Chanukah happened with oil. When the Maccabees rededicated the Holy Temple, they found one small jar of pure olive oil, enough to light the Menorah for one day, and it burned for eight. So we eat foods fried in oil.

- **Latkes** (in Hebrew, levivot) are pancakes of grated potato, egg and onion, fried until golden. Potatoes came to Europe much later; earlier latkes were made of cheese or other vegetables.
- **Sufganiyot** are round doughnuts filled with jam and dusted with sugar, the favorite Chanukah treat in Israel.
- Other families make fried fish, fritters, or Sephardic bimuelos and zalabia.

## Cheese and dairy

There is also a custom to eat dairy foods on Chanukah. It recalls Yehudit (Judith), a brave woman who fed a Greek-Syrian general salty cheese and wine until he fell asleep, and then saved her city. Cheese latkes, blintzes and cheesecake are popular for this reason.

## Easy potato latkes

1. Grate 4 large potatoes and 1 onion. Squeeze out as much liquid as you can.
2. Mix in 2 eggs, 3 tablespoons flour or matzah meal, salt and pepper.
3. Heat a generous layer of oil in a pan. Drop in spoonfuls and flatten.
4. Fry until golden on both sides and drain on paper towels. Serve with applesauce or sour cream.

Kosher tips: use oil and flour with a reliable kosher symbol, and if you serve sour cream, keep the whole meal dairy.

## Chanukah food and Jewish values

The meal is part of the celebration. Invite a friend who is alone for the holiday, bring latkes to an elderly neighbor, or share sufganiyot at work or school. Spreading light includes spreading warmth.

## Common questions

### Do I have to eat latkes on Chanukah?
There is no obligation, but eating foods made with oil is a long-standing custom that helps the whole family feel the holiday.

### Can I make latkes ahead and reheat them?
Yes. Cool them on a rack, freeze in a single layer, and reheat in a hot oven so they stay crisp.

### Is there a special Chanukah meal?
Chanukah has no required festive meal like Purim, but a family meal with songs, stories of the miracle and words of Torah turns it into a mitzvah celebration.

### Are store-bought sufganiyot kosher?
Only if they come from a kosher bakery or carry a reliable kosher symbol. See our [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols) guide.
