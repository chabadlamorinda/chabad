The best way to help new parents is to bring a meal they can eat right away, ask what they need, keep your visit short, and, if they keep kosher, bring food that is kosher. Helping a mother who has just given birth is an act of chesed (חֶסֶד, kindness) and a mitzvah, and it is often the thing that parents remember most.

Many families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda live far from relatives, so a friend with a casserole can make a very big difference. Here is how to do it well.

## Why it is a mitzvah

Jewish tradition values [chesed](/learn/chesed), acts of kindness that give of ourselves. A good plan for new parents is to arrange for friends to bring food and for one person to coordinate the meals, and to arrange help in advance, including cleaning help, rides and care for older children. A mother in her first weeks after birth is recovering, and for about the first two weeks she should stay mostly off her feet and let others take care of her.

## Before you bring food

1. **Ask first.** Send a message: "Can I bring dinner? Any allergies or dislikes?" Ask about food allergies and what days suit.
2. **Ask about kosher.** If the family keeps kosher, ask how. Some families keep a kosher home and will eat only food from certain sources. If you are not sure, ask rather than guess. Our guide to [hosting a kosher guest](/learn/hosting-a-kosher-guest) and [the kosher kitchen](/learn/kosher-kitchen) explain the basics, and the Rabbi can answer any question. Bay Kosher, a project of Chabad of Lamorinda, is always kosher; see [Bay Kosher](/programs/bay-kosher).
3. **Coordinate.** Ask a friend to set up a schedule, so the family does not get five lasagnas on one day.

## What to bring

- **Food that is easy to heat and eat with one hand.** Soups, pasta, chicken, rice and vegetables, and fruit and snacks.
- **Disposable containers**, so nobody has to return a dish.
- **Labels with ingredients** and reheating instructions.
- **Food that fits Shabbat.** A meal that can be heated before Shabbat is a gift. See [first Shabbat with a newborn](/learn/first-shabbat-with-a-newborn).
- **A treat for older siblings.**

## How to visit

New mothers should choose company that is relaxed and supportive, not critical. So drop off the food at the door, or stay only for a few minutes if invited, offer to hold the baby so mom can shower or nap, and leave when the baby is hungry or tired. Do not ask to see the baby unless the parents invite you. Wash your hands, and stay home if you are sick.

## Other ways to help

- Take out the garbage, do a load of laundry, or wash dishes.
- Drive older children to school or activities.
- Pick up groceries from a list.
- Pray for the family. Our [Tehillim page](/tehillim) has Psalms for mother and child.

## Common questions

### What should I bring to new parents?
A ready-to-eat meal in disposable containers, with a note of ingredients. Ask first about allergies and kosher.

### Does the food have to be kosher?
If the family keeps kosher, yes. Ask them how they keep it, or bring food from a kosher source. Ask the Rabbi if you have a question.

### How long should I visit new parents?
Keep it short, often only a few minutes, unless they ask you to stay. Let the mother rest.

### Is bringing a meal a Jewish custom?
Yes, it is part of the mitzvah of chesed. Friends are encouraged to arrange meals and help for a new mother.
