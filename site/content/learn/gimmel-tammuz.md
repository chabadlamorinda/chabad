Gimmel Tammuz, the third day of the Hebrew month of Tammuz, is the yahrzeit of the Lubavitcher Rebbe, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson, who passed away on 3 Tammuz 5754 (June 12, 1994). It falls in early summer each year. For Chabad and for countless Jews the world over, it is a day of reflection, learning and renewed commitment.

## Who was the Rebbe?

The Rebbe led the Chabad movement for over four decades, from 1950 until his passing. He sent young couples, called shluchim (emissaries), to every corner of the world to care for Jews wherever they live, which is how Chabad houses came to be in thousands of cities, including here in Lamorinda. He taught that every Jew is precious, that one more mitzvah can change the world, and that we must prepare the world for Moshiach through acts of goodness and kindness. See [the Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe).

## A day of connection, not only of loss

Chassidic teaching explains that a tzaddik, a righteous person, "is found in all the worlds even more than in his lifetime." His influence continues and even grows through those who follow his teachings. So Gimmel Tammuz is marked not only with sadness, but by doing more: learning his teachings, adding a mitzvah, and reaching out to another Jew.

## How people mark the day

- **Learn** a teaching of the Rebbe: a talk, a letter or a short thought.
- **Do an extra mitzvah**: put on tefillin, light Shabbat candles, give tzedakah, or [do a mitzvah this week](/mitzvah).
- **Give tzedakah** in the Rebbe's merit.
- **Write a letter.** Many people write a short letter, with their Hebrew name and their mother's Hebrew name, asking for a blessing. It is read at the Ohel.
- **Gather and farbreng**: share stories and songs with friends.

## The Ohel

The Rebbe is buried in the Montefiore Cemetery in Cambria Heights, Queens, New York, beside his father-in-law, the previous Rebbe. The resting place is called the Ohel. Tens of thousands of people of every background visit each year, especially around Gimmel Tammuz, to pray and to ask for blessings. If you cannot go, you can send a letter to be read there.

## Common questions

### When is Gimmel Tammuz?
On the third day of Tammuz in the Hebrew calendar, usually in June or early July. The date changes each year on the regular calendar; see our [Jewish calendar](/calendar).

### Do I need to be part of Chabad to visit the Ohel or send a letter?
No. Every Jew, and many non-Jews, visit and write. You are welcome.

### What do I write in a letter to the Ohel?
Write your Hebrew name and your mother's Hebrew name, what you are asking a blessing for, and ideally a good resolution, a mitzvah you will add. Keep it simple and from the heart. Ask the Rabbi if you would like help.

### Why do people say "the Rebbe" and not his name?
"Rebbe" means teacher and leader. For his followers he was simply the Rebbe, the way a family speaks of "Dad."
