If this is your first Yom Kippur, or your first in many years, welcome. You do not need to know Hebrew, own a prayer book or have been to synagogue before. Here is what the day is, what happens, and how to make it yours.

## What Yom Kippur is

Yom Kippur, the Day of Atonement, is the holiest day of the Jewish year. It comes ten days after Rosh Hashanah, on the 10th of Tishrei, and closes the [Ten Days of Repentance](/learn/ten-days-of-repentance). On this day, the Torah says, G-d forgives. Our part is to show up honestly: to look at the year behind us, to make peace with people we have hurt, and to decide who we want to be. See [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah).

Chassidic teaching describes Yom Kippur as the day when the deepest part of the soul, the part that was never touched by any mistake, shines through. For twenty-five hours we live like angels, without food, drink or everyday comforts, so that this inner self can be heard.

## The five things we refrain from

From before sunset until nightfall the next day, Jewish law asks us to set aside five things:

1. **Eating and drinking**, including water. See [the Yom Kippur fast](/learn/yom-kippur-fast).
2. **Washing** for pleasure. Rinsing fingers after the bathroom or washing dirt off is fine.
3. **Anointing**: lotions, creams, perfume and cosmetics.
4. **Leather shoes.** Canvas sneakers, rubber or cloth shoes are worn instead; this is why you will see suits and sneakers together.
5. **Marital relations.**

Work is also forbidden, as on Shabbat: no driving, cooking, phones or writing.

## Who should not fast

Fasting is a mitzvah for healthy adults. It is also a mitzvah *not* to fast when fasting is dangerous. Pregnant and nursing women, people with diabetes, heart conditions or eating disorders, anyone on medication that must be taken with food, and anyone who is ill should speak with their doctor and then with the Rabbi before the holiday. There are ways to eat or drink in small measured amounts that are themselves part of Jewish law. Children under Bar or Bat Mitzvah do not fast. Guarding your health is G-d's will too. See [pikuach nefesh](/learn/pikuach-nefesh).

## The prayers, in order

- **Kol Nidre**, just before sunset as the day begins: the Torah scrolls are held, and a haunting melody releases vows we could not keep. Arrive early; this is the moment nobody wants to miss. See [Kol Nidre](/learn/kol-nidre).
- **Morning prayers and the Torah reading**, with the Viduy confession, said together, quietly tapping the heart.
- **Yizkor**, late in the morning service, a memorial prayer for parents and loved ones who have passed. Those whose parents are living traditionally step out. See [Yizkor](/learn/yizkor).
- **Mincha**, the afternoon service, with the Book of Jonah: a story about second chances.
- **Neilah**, "the closing of the gates," as the sun sets: the most intense hour of the year, ending with the Shema, then "The L-rd is G-d" called out seven times, and one long shofar blast. Then everyone breaks the fast together.

## What to wear

White, if you can: it is the color of purity and of angels, and many men wear a white *kittel*. Dress respectfully, as for an important occasion, with non-leather shoes. Married women cover their hair in synagogue; men wear a kippah and, in the morning, a [tallit](/learn/tallit).

## If you cannot stay all day

Come for what you can. Kol Nidre at the start and Neilah at the end are the two most powerful hours. If you are home for part of the day, rest, read, think about the year, and say your own words to G-d. See [talking to G-d in your own words](/learn/talking-to-g-d-in-your-own-words).

Before the holiday, eat a good meal, ask forgiveness from anyone you have hurt, give [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah), and light the candles. Dates and times are on our [Yom Kippur page](/holidays/yom-kippur).

## Common questions

### Do I need to be a member to attend?
No. At Chabad of Lamorinda every Jew is welcome, no membership needed. Call the Rabbi with any questions.

### I do not read Hebrew. Will I be lost?
No. Prayer books with English on the facing page are widely available, and in most synagogues the pages are announced along the way; ask the Rabbi what to expect. Sitting quietly and thinking is also prayer.

### What if I feel faint during the fast?
Sit down, tell someone, and if you feel truly unwell, drink. Health comes first, always, and the Rabbi will tell you the same.
