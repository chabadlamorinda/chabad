Yes, bring your children to synagogue. Children are a natural part of Jewish community life, and a little noise from a toddler does not ruin anyone's prayers. The goal is not perfect behavior but a warm, happy first impression, so keep visits short at first, let your child take part in simple ways, and step out for a break whenever you need to.

## Why children belong in synagogue

Parents have a duty to educate their children in Jewish life from an early age, beginning once a child can do a given mitzvah. Teaching begins around age three with blessings over food and basic prayers, and the Shema is taught as soon as a child speaks. A child who sees the synagogue as a happy, welcoming place is learning that Judaism belongs to them. Synagogues also often serve as centers of Jewish life, with classes, celebrations and Hebrew school for children.

## Before you go

- **Set expectations.** Tell your child where you are going and what it will look like: the Torah scroll, singing, people standing and sitting.
- **Pick the right part.** Little ones often do best arriving for part of the service, such as the Torah reading, the singing, or the Kiddush afterward. Coming partway through is normal.
- **Pack quietly.** A snack, a small quiet book or a Jewish picture book can help. Avoid anything noisy or any screen, especially on Shabbat. See [Babies and Toddlers on Shabbat](/learn/babies-and-toddlers-on-shabbat).
- **Dress comfortably,** neat and modest. See [Synagogue Etiquette](/learn/synagogue-etiquette).

## Simple things children can do

Most children can join in more than you expect:

- **Answer "Amen"** after a blessing. This is a real mitzvah, and young children love it.
- **Touch and kiss the Torah** with a siddur or the corner of a tallit as it passes, if it is carried near them.
- **Say the Shema** along with you. See [Shema](/learn/shema).
- **Follow in a children's siddur** with pictures, or find the page with you. See [Finding Your Place in the Siddur](/learn/finding-your-place-in-the-siddur).
- **Sing,** especially at the end of the service, and enjoy the Kiddush with everyone else.
- **Help a little.** Ask the gabbai first whether a child can open the ark or hold something. See [Synagogue Honors](/learn/synagogue-honors).

## What to do when a child gets restless

1. **Step out for a few minutes** to the lobby or the hall, then come back. This is perfectly fine.
2. **Stay calm.** The people around you have mostly been there too.
3. **Keep it short.** Ten happy minutes beat an hour of tears.
4. **Praise afterward.** Tell your child something specific that they did well.
5. **Do not leave a young child alone.** Stay with them or ask a trusted adult.

## One more thing about separate seating

In a traditional synagogue, men and women sit separately. If you have little ones, ask the Rabbi in advance about how families handle this. Read the [mechitzah guide](/learn/mechitzah).

## Common questions

### Can I bring my baby or toddler to synagogue?
Yes. Children are welcome, and many families bring them. If your child gets restless, step out for a bit and come back.

### How do I keep my child quiet in synagogue?
You may not need to. Keep visits short, bring a quiet book or snack, and step out when needed. A little noise is understood.

### What can my child do during the service?
Answer "Amen," say the Shema, sing, touch and kiss the Torah as it passes, and follow along in a children's siddur.

### At what age should children start coming to synagogue?
There is no set age to start. Teaching usually begins around age three with blessings and basic prayers, and bringing a child sooner is also fine.

### Is there a children's service at Chabad of Lamorinda?

### Can children speak to the Rabbi?
Of course. Children often love meeting the Rabbi and Rebbetzin. Say hello at Kiddush.
