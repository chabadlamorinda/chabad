A last name can hint at Jewish ancestry, but it cannot prove it. Most Jews took family names only about 200 years ago, and Jewish status in Jewish law passes through the mother, so a surname alone neither proves nor disproves that someone is Jewish.

## When did Jews get last names?

For most of Jewish history a person was known by their own name plus their father's name, such as Moshe ben Amram, or their mother's name in prayer. Among Ashkenazi Jews, surnames spread after 1787, when Emperor Joseph II of Austria required Jews to adopt them. Other countries followed, and by the middle of the 19th century most Jewish families had one. Officials sometimes assigned names carelessly, and some families took new names to avoid army service or to cross borders.

## Kinds of Jewish surnames

According to Chabad.org's overview of Ashkenazi surnames, they fall into several groups:

- **Priests and Levites:** Cohen (כהן) and Levy, with variants like Kohn, Kahn, Kagan, Levi, Levin and Levine.
- **A father or mother:** Abramson and Jacobowitz come from fathers. Rivkin and Tamarkin come from mothers.
- **A place:** Brody, Horowitz, Pollack and Minsky.
- **A trade:** Schmidt (smith), Schneider (tailor) and Kantor (cantor).
- **A look or feature:** Klein (small), Gross (large) and Weiss (fair).
- **Nature compounds:** Goldberg, Rosenthal and Adler (eagle).
- **Hebrew acronyms:** Katz, from the words for a righteous priest, and Segal, a name tied to the Levites.

## Cohen, Levy and Katz

Cohen means "priest." Kohanim (priests) descend in the male line from Aaron, brother of Moses. Levy generally, but not always, shows descent from the tribe of Levi. A family might also have the name because a recent ancestor was called Levi, because it passed through a woman, or because a name was changed long ago. Having the name does not by itself make someone a kohen or Levite, so ask a rabbi. See [Kohen, Levi and Yisrael](/learn/kohen-levi-yisrael).

## Russian-speaking families

Russian has no "h" sound, so the "h" in Cohen often became a "g" and Cohen turned into Kagan. Many Russian Jewish names ended up changed, shortened or replaced during the Russian Empire and the Soviet years, so a family's current name may hide the old one. Those stories are common among our neighbors in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda.

## So am I Jewish?

Look beyond the surname. Jewish status comes through a Jewish mother or by conversion, as explained in [Who is a Jew?](/learn/who-is-a-jew). To find out, ask your family, gather documents and read [Proving Jewish ancestry](/learn/proving-jewish-ancestry). A DNA result can be interesting but it does not decide the question under Jewish law. If you have only discovered it recently, read [Discovered you are Jewish](/learn/discovered-you-are-jewish).

## Common questions

### Is my last name Jewish?
It might be, but a name alone cannot tell. Many Jewish names such as Cohen, Katz or Levy are common, yet non-Jews also carry some, and many Jews have names that sound non-Jewish.

### Why did Jews not have last names until recently?
For most of history Jews used their own name and their father's name. Surnames became general among Ashkenazi Jews from 1787 when Austria required them.

### Does the name Cohen mean I am a kohen?
Not necessarily. Cohen usually points to priestly ancestry in the male line, but it can also come from a name change or other reasons. Ask the Rabbi.

### What does Levy mean?
Levy generally, but not always, shows descent from the tribe of Levi, who served in the Temple.

### What do names like Katz and Segal mean?
They are Hebrew acronyms. Katz stands for a righteous priest and Segal is tied to the Levites.

### Can a surname prove I am Jewish?
No. In Jewish law a person is Jewish through a Jewish mother or conversion. Call 925-384-7242 and ask the Rabbi about your situation.
