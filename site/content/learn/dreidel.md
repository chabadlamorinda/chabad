A dreidel is a four-sided spinning top with a Hebrew letter on each side, played with on Chanukah. The letters stand for "Nes Gadol Hayah Sham," meaning "A great miracle happened there."

## What is a dreidel?

Dreidel is a Yiddish word meaning "to turn." In Hebrew it is called a sevivon. Each side has one Hebrew letter:

- **Nun (נ)**
- **Gimmel (ג)**
- **Hei (ה)**
- **Shin (ש)**

Together they spell out the first letters of Nes Gadol Hayah Sham, "A great miracle happened there." In Israel, the last letter is Pei (פ) instead of Shin, for "Nes Gadol Hayah Po," "A great miracle happened here."

## The history of the dreidel

A popular tradition teaches that when the Greek rulers banned Torah study, Jewish children studied in secret. When soldiers came by, they took out tops and pretended to be playing a game. Whatever its exact origin, the dreidel has been part of Chanukah for centuries.

## How to play dreidel, step by step

You need a dreidel, two or more players, and a pile of tokens for each player: chocolate coins (gelt), nuts, raisins or pennies.

1. **Everyone puts one token in the middle** to start the pot.
2. **Take turns spinning the dreidel.** When it falls, look at the letter facing up.
3. **Follow the letter:**
   - **Nun:** "nisht," nothing. You get nothing and give nothing.
   - **Gimmel:** "gantz," everything. You take the whole pot.
   - **Hei:** "halb," half. You take half the pot.
   - **Shin:** "shtel," put in. You add one token to the pot.
4. **When the pot is empty or has one token**, everyone puts in another token.
5. **When a player runs out of tokens**, they are out. The game ends when one player has everything, or when it is time for latkes.

## The deeper meaning

Chassidic teaching finds beautiful lessons in the dreidel:

- **The numbers.** In Hebrew, each letter has a number value. Nun, Gimmel, Hei and Shin add up to 358, the same as the word Moshiach. The miracle of Chanukah points to the final redemption.
- **Spun from above.** A Chanukah dreidel is spun from the top, unlike the Purim gragger, which is turned from below. On Chanukah, the miracle came from G-d's initiative above, after the Jewish people's own efforts.
- **It all turns.** History can feel like a spinning top, but in the end, G-d is guiding it, and the outcome is always for good.

## Gelt and giving

Chanukah gelt, coins given to children, goes hand in hand with the dreidel. It is a great time to teach children about [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) too: many families encourage children to give part of their gelt to charity. Some give gelt on each night, with extra on the fifth night.

## Common questions

### Is it okay to play dreidel for money?
Families often play with coins or chocolate as a lighthearted game. Many prefer to use candy or nuts, especially with children, to keep it about fun and not gambling.

### When do we play dreidel?
Traditionally after lighting the [menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah), while the candles burn. Since we do not do work or use the lights during the first half hour, games like dreidel are a fun way to stay near the menorah.

### What do the Yiddish words have to do with the Hebrew letters?
The Hebrew letters stand for "A great miracle happened there." The game rules use Yiddish words that begin with the same letters: nisht, gantz, halb and shtel. It is a clever double meaning.

### Can we play dreidel on Shabbat during Chanukah?
Customs differ about spinning games on Shabbat. Many avoid playing for money or prizes on Shabbat. Ask the Rabbi about your family's practice.
