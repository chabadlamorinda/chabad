Hebrew school is not only about letters and holidays. It is where children learn the values that make a good person: kindness, honesty, tzedakah, respect, welcoming guests and gratitude. Jewish tradition teaches these values through real stories and real mitzvahs, so children learn not only what is good, but how to do it.

## Values children can picture

The Torah teaches values through people. Children do not just hear "be kind"; they meet Abraham running in the heat to welcome three strangers. They do not just hear "forgive"; they see Joseph forgive the brothers who sold him. A value with a face and a story stays with a child.

## Six Jewish values every child can learn

**1. Kindness (chesed).** The Mishnah says the world stands on three things: Torah, prayer and acts of kindness. Children learn to visit the sick, help a friend, share and include the child who is alone. See [raising kind children](/learn/raising-kind-children).

**2. Honesty (emet).** "Keep far from a false word" (Exodus 23:7). Children learn to tell the truth, return what is not theirs and keep their promises.

**3. Tzedakah.** The Hebrew word means "justice": sharing with others is simply right. Children put coins in a pushka, choose a cause and learn the joy of giving. See [children, money and tzedakah](/learn/children-money-and-tzedakah).

**4. Respect for parents.** "Honor your father and your mother" is one of the Ten Commandments. Children learn what that looks like: listening, helping and speaking kindly. See [honoring parents](/learn/honoring-parents).

**5. Welcoming guests (hachnasat orchim).** Like Abraham and Sarah's open tent, a Jewish home welcomes people. Children learn to greet a guest, offer a drink and make a newcomer feel at home.

**6. Gratitude.** A Jewish child's day begins with *Modeh Ani*, "I thank You," and every bite of food comes with a blessing. Gratitude becomes a habit, and grateful children are happier children.

And one rule that holds them all: "Love your fellow as yourself" (Leviticus 19:18). Hillel called "What is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow" the whole Torah, with the rest its explanation.

## Learning by doing

Values are learned best through practice. A class that collects tzedakah, makes cards for someone who is sick, or prepares a holiday gift for a neighbor turns values into habits. See [hands-on Jewish learning for kids](/learn/hands-on-jewish-learning-for-kids).

## Growing values at home

- **Notice the good.** When your child shares or tells the truth, say so.
- **Keep a pushka** where everyone can see it, and give before Shabbat.
- **Invite guests** and let your child help host.
- **Talk at dinner**: "Who was kind to you today? Who were you kind to?"
- **Be the example.** Children copy what they see far more than what they hear. See [being a mensch](/learn/being-a-mensch).

## Common questions

### What values do children learn in Hebrew school?
Kindness, honesty, tzedakah, respect for parents, welcoming guests and gratitude, all rooted in "love your fellow as yourself", taught through Torah stories and mitzvahs.

### How does Judaism teach values to children?
Through stories of real people in the Torah, and through doing mitzvahs: giving tzedakah, saying blessings, helping others. Values become habits by practice.

### What is a mitzvah?
A mitzvah is a commandment from G-d, a good deed that connects us to Him. Giving tzedakah, lighting Shabbat candles and honoring parents are all mitzvahs.

### How can I teach my child tzedakah?
Give them a pushka, let them add a coin each day or before Shabbat, and choose together where the money goes.
