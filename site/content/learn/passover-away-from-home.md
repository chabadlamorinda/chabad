If you travel for Passover, sell your chametz through a rabbi before you go, bring shmurah matzah for the Seder, eat only food certified kosher for Passover, and contact the local Chabad center at your destination to join a Seder. Chabad centers around the world welcome Jewish travelers.

## 1. Before you leave: your chametz

Chametz is leavened grain that a Jew may not own on Passover. See [chametz](/learn/chametz).

Chabad.org advises:

- **Selling.** Sell all your chametz through a rabbi. If you stay in your own time zone, your local rabbi can handle it: use [the chametz sale form](/forms/sell-chametz) or read [selling your chametz](/learn/selling-your-chametz). If you cross time zones, ask your rabbi, since timing depends on where the owner is located.
- **Coming home before Passover ends.** Clean and search for chametz the night before you leave; ask the Rabbi how to handle your home.
- **Away for the whole holiday.** Sell all the chametz and skip the home search; the search in your Passover accommodation is enough.

## 2. Hotels and rentals

- Paying guests must search for chametz in the rooms they rent.
- Non-paying guests are covered by the host's search.
- Whether you must search a rented room depends on the arrangement. Ask the Rabbi about your case.

## 3. Matzah

Your host or hotel will probably provide matzah, but handmade shmurah matzah is the best choice for the Seder. Bring it from home or order it ahead. See [matzah](/learn/matzah).

## 4. Finding a Seder

Get the contact information of the local Chabad center before you travel. Ask about communal Seders and service times. Chabad centers can also help with urgent needs, such as kosher-for-Passover products or a mikvah. See [Passover Seder](/learn/passover-seder).

## 5. Eating kosher for Passover on the road

- All food must be certified kosher for Passover by a reliable agency.
- Tell your hosts your standards in advance.
- At a hotel, find out which rabbi or agency certifies the kitchen. If unsure, ask your rabbi to check.

See [kosher when traveling](/learn/kosher-when-traveling) and [kosher for Passover](/learn/kosher-for-passover).

## 6. Yom Tov on the road

Outside Israel, Passover is two days at the start and two at the end; in Israel it is one day each. Rules for an Israeli visiting abroad, or the reverse, are complex, so ask your rabbi. If you plan to cross the International Date Line between the second day of Passover and Shavuot, ask the Rabbi before you book. Halachic times differ by location. The dates of Passover are on [the holidays page](/holidays/passover).

## Common questions

### Can I travel on Passover?
Many families do. Plan for chametz, matzah, kosher-for-Passover food and a Seder. Ask the Rabbi about the holiday days.

### Do I still sell my chametz if I am away?
Yes. If you are away for all of Passover, sell it and skip the home search.

### How do I find a Seder while traveling?
Contact the local Chabad center before you go.

### What matzah should I bring?
Handmade shmurah matzah is best for the Seder; bring or order it.

### Is hotel food kosher for Passover?
Only if certified. Ask who certifies it, and check with your rabbi if unsure.
