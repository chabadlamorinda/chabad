A good Bar or Bat Mitzvah invitation names the child with his or her Hebrew name, gives the date in both calendars, says clearly where the celebration is and what to expect, and begins with a short blessing line such as בעזרת ה' (b'ezrat Hashem, "with G-d's help"). Keep it warm and simple, and ask the Rabbi to check it before you print.

## What to include

1. **The child's name,** English and Hebrew. Hebrew names follow the pattern "Shmuel ben Avraham v'Sarah" for a boy (son of) or "Rivka bat Avraham v'Sarah" for a girl (daughter of). See [Hebrew names](/learn/hebrew-names).
2. **The date,** with the English date and the Hebrew date. Jewish dates are counted by the Hebrew calendar; see [finding the Hebrew date](/learn/bar-mitzvah-hebrew-birthday-date). For a Shabbat celebration, add the Torah portion (the parsha). A letter from the Rebbe acknowledging a Bar Mitzvah invitation, on Chabad.org, gives the day as Shabbat, the portion, and the Hebrew date.
3. **The place and time** of the service and of the meal, with the address. Say clearly if guests are welcome at both.
4. **What to expect** if the celebration is on Shabbat (see below).
5. **A line about food:** if the meal is kosher, say so, which helps guests who keep kosher.
6. **How to respond:** an RSVP date and a phone number or email.
7. **A word of welcome** to family members who are not familiar with the customs.

## The blessing line at the top

Many Jewish invitations begin with ב״ה (B"H) or בס״ד (B"S"D). Chabad.org explains that B"H stands for *baruch Hashem*, "blessed is G-d," or *b'ezrat Hashem*, "with the help of G-d," and that B"S"D stands for the Aramaic *b'siyata d'shmaya*, "with the help of heaven." For an event still in the future, "b'ezrat Hashem" fits well. It is customary to say that planned events will happen with G-d's help.

## What to avoid on the printed page

- **Spelling out G-d's name.** In English write "G-d," and in Hebrew use ה' for the Divine name. A printed invitation may be thrown away, so ask the Rabbi before adding Hebrew verses or prayers.
- **Printing the date of a Shabbat or holiday for a mixed group** without telling them what to expect.
- **Leaving out the Hebrew date.** It takes a line and adds meaning.

## Sample wording

**For a boy:**

> With G-d's help (בעזרת ה')
> Rachel and David Cohen invite you to celebrate the Bar Mitzvah of their son
> Benjamin (Binyamin ben David v'Rachel)
> Shabbat, [portion], [Hebrew date] / [English date]
> Services at [time] at [place]. Kiddush luncheon to follow.
> RSVP by [date] to [phone or email].

**For a girl:**

> With G-d's help (בעזרת ה')
> Sarah and Michael Levy invite you to join them in celebrating the Bat Mitzvah of their daughter
> Hannah (Chana bat Michael v'Sarah)
> Sunday, [Hebrew date] / [English date], at [time], at [place]
> Dinner and a few words of Torah from Hannah. RSVP by [date].

## If it is on Shabbat

Tell your guests what to expect so that everyone is comfortable. A line such as "Shabbat is a day of rest: please leave phones and cameras at home and walk to the service if you can" helps non-observant guests and relatives. On Shabbat, Jewish law does not allow driving, photography or electronics, so this is a good place for a gentle explanation. Ask the Rabbi what to write and read [attending a Bar or Bat Mitzvah](/learn/attending-a-bar-bat-mitzvah) to see it from your guests' side.

## Common questions

### What should a Bar Mitzvah invitation say?
It should give the child's name and Hebrew name, the date, the place and time, what to expect, and how to respond. Begin with a short blessing line such as b'ezrat Hashem.

### How do I write the Hebrew name on the invitation?
Write the child's Hebrew name followed by *ben* (son of) or *bat* (daughter of) and the parents' Hebrew names. If you are unsure, ask the Rabbi.

### What does B"H or b'ezrat Hashem mean?
Chabad.org explains that B"H means "blessed is G-d" or "with G-d's help." Many people write it at the top of Jewish documents and invitations.

### Should we put the Hebrew date on the invitation?
Yes. It takes one line and adds meaning. For a Shabbat celebration, the Torah portion is also a good addition.

### How do I word a Shabbat invitation for guests who are not observant?
Explain gently what Shabbat involves, such as no phones, no photography and walking, and say that everyone is welcome. Ask the Rabbi for suggestions.

### Is it all right to put G-d's name on the invitation?
It is better not to spell it out. Write "G-d" or ה', since a printed invitation may be discarded. Ask the Rabbi.
