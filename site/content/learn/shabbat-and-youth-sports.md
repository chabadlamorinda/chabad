Youth sports often schedule games on Saturdays, which is Shabbat, when Jewish tradition asks us to rest from ordinary work and play. A parent in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda can handle this without guilt by talking to the coach early, looking for options, and deciding as a family what to do, with the Rabbi's advice.

## What Shabbat asks

Shabbat runs from before sundown on Friday until nightfall on Saturday, and is a day set apart for rest, family, prayer and joy. Traditional Jewish observance avoids ordinary weekday activities such as driving, handling money and playing organized sports, with details that depend on your level of observance. How strictly your family keeps Shabbat is personal, and so is how you decide about sports. Read [Shabbat for Beginners](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners). For a specific question about a game, a tournament or travel, ask the Rabbi.

The times change every week: see [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times). This week's Kabbalat Shabbat on Friday evening is at {kabbalat-shabbat}.

## No guilt, one step at a time

Many families start with small, achievable steps rather than all or nothing. It is not a failure to begin where you are.

1. **Talk to the coach before the season.** Ask for the schedule early and explain that Saturday is a day of rest for your family. Many coaches are understanding when they hear it in advance.
2. **Ask about options.** Is there a Sunday league, a team with fewer Saturday games, or a game time that ends before sundown on Friday or starts after Shabbat ends?
3. **Start with the holidays.** If Saturdays feel impossible right now, begin by not playing on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur. Read [Jewish Holidays and Lamorinda School Calendars](/learn/jewish-holidays-and-lamorinda-school-calendars).
4. **Keep Friday night, no matter what.** Even on a game weekend, a Shabbat dinner with candles, blessings and challah keeps Shabbat alive. See [Shabbat with Kids](/learn/shabbat-with-kids).
5. **Pick a few games and a few teams.** Decide what matters. A child can enjoy a Sunday league, school gym class or a backyard game.
6. **Ask the Rabbi.** Every family and every situation is different.

## True stories of young athletes

Chabad.org has reported on several young athletes who made these decisions. Elie Kligman, an 18-year-old high school baseball player from Las Vegas, was invited to an MLB-scouted showcase. He had never played on Shabbat, and said that once Shabbat begins, observing it is not up for debate. His high school coach made sure the team scheduled no games on Saturday, and the family said people were kind and helpful, though it also took planning and sacrifice, like leaving right after Shabbat to reach a Saturday-night game. Another Chabad.org story from Hawaii tells of nine-year-old Yossi Weinbaum, a soccer player who stepped off the field when a referee ordered him to remove his kippah. The league supported him, and he came back to the team. Both stories show that people often respect a child who is polite, clear and consistent.

## What a child learns from a parent's choice

When you decide to keep Shabbat, a child learns that some things matter more than winning, and that being Jewish is something to be proud of. If you decide that it is too hard right now, that is also a lesson in honesty and starting small. What children remember is the warmth of Friday night. Read [Jewish Pride](/learn/jewish-pride) and [Parents and Teenagers](/learn/parents-and-teenagers) for older kids.

## Sports and the kippah

Some boys wear a kippah while playing, with a clip or a headband. Check with the league's rules, and speak to the coach and referee in advance. For safety questions with helmets, ask the Rabbi and the coach.

## Common questions

### Can my child play soccer or baseball on Shabbat?
Traditional Jewish observance asks us to rest on Shabbat, and many observant families do not play games on that day. How your family decides is personal, so ask the Rabbi.

### What can I tell the coach about Saturday games?
Explain early, politely and clearly that Saturday is a day of rest and family for you, and ask what the options are. Many coaches are understanding when they know in advance.

### Are there youth sports leagues that avoid Shabbat?
Some communities and leagues offer Sunday or weekday play. Ask your league and school, and ask us if we can help you find options.

### Is it a sin if my child plays on Shabbat sometimes?
Judaism values every step. Decisions about your child are personal, so talk with the Rabbi without worry.

### Can my child wear a kippah while playing?
Many players wear a kippah with a clip. Check the league's rules in advance and talk to the coach.
