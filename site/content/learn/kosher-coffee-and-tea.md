Plain, unflavored coffee is the simplest case: Chabad.org says unflavored coffee from rest stops or convenience stores is generally fine. Flavored coffee, and coffee from a place that prepares non-kosher food, raises more questions, so look for kosher certification or ask the Rabbi.

## Plain coffee and tea

Plain unflavored coffee is the easy case. The questions begin with what is added, and with what the coffee is prepared on.

For tea, Chabad.org mentions using hot water with a tea bag or instant coffee when you are away from home (see below). A flavored tea or a blend with added ingredients is a different matter: check the package for a reliable kosher symbol (see [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols)) and ask the Rabbi if you are unsure.

## Flavored coffee and other additions

Flavorings, syrups, creamers and mixes are made of many ingredients, and some are not kosher or are made on shared equipment. That is why Chabad.org's guidance on hotel coffee makers speaks of pouches that are "pure unflavored coffee or certified." If a flavored drink or mix has no reliable symbol, it needs a question to the Rabbi. For more on ingredients, see [hidden kosher ingredients](/learn/hidden-kosher-ingredients).

Milk and creamers have their own questions. Chabad.org teaches that kosher milk should be produced under Jewish supervision, and some authorities accept government inspection where Jewish supervision is not available. Read more in [chalav Yisrael](/learn/chalav-yisrael) and ask the Rabbi what you should do.

## Coffee shops

Chabad.org says that coffee from a non-kosher cafe can be tricky, and it is hard to make a blanket statement. If you want to buy a drink in Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda or anywhere in the East Bay, you may ask the Rabbi about a particular place. Please do not rely on a chain's name or reputation: only the Rabbi or a kashrut agency can tell you.

If you want a safe option away from home, Chabad.org suggests hot water from a dedicated urn with instant coffee or a tea bag that you bring.

## Hotel room coffee makers

Chabad.org says a hotel room coffee maker is usable if all the coffee pouches are kosher, meaning pure unflavored coffee or certified. Avoid it if any pouch is not kosher, or if the maker is washed in a kitchen with non-kosher dishes, as in some upscale hotels. Also avoid coffee or hot water from hotel kitchens, restaurants, urns or pump bottles in non-kosher hotels unless you can confirm they are kosher. See [kosher when traveling](/learn/kosher-when-traveling).

## Common questions

### Is coffee kosher without a hechsher?
Chabad.org says plain, unflavored coffee from rest stops or convenience stores is generally fine. Flavored coffee and coffee mixes should carry a reliable kosher symbol; ask the Rabbi about a particular product.

### Is Starbucks coffee kosher?
Chabad.org says coffee from a non-kosher cafe can be tricky, and it is hard to make a blanket statement. Ask the Rabbi about a specific location.

### Is flavored coffee kosher?
Only if the flavoring is kosher. Look for a reliable symbol or ask the Rabbi.

### Is tea kosher?
Chabad.org mentions a plain tea bag with hot water from a dedicated urn as an option away from home. For flavored teas and blends, check the package for a reliable symbol and ask the Rabbi.

### Can I use a hotel room coffee maker?
Yes, if all the pouches are pure unflavored coffee or certified, and the maker was not washed in a kitchen with non-kosher dishes.

### Is the milk in my coffee kosher?
Milk should be produced under Jewish supervision, though some authorities accept government inspection. Ask the Rabbi what to do.
