A new home is a new beginning. Whether you just got married or just moved, the first weeks are the easiest time to set things up the Jewish way. Jewish tradition calls the home a "small sanctuary," and a few simple steps make it one.

## Mezuzahs on the doors

The first and most visible sign of a Jewish home is the [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah): a handwritten parchment with the Shema, placed on the right side of the doorway.

- **Every doorway** of rooms used for living needs one, not only the front door. Bathrooms do not get a mezuzah.
- **Kosher scrolls** are written by hand by a trained scribe. A printed scroll is not a mezuzah, no matter how nice the case.
- **Check them** twice in seven years, and whenever there is a reason to.
- **Celebrate.** Many people mark the occasion with a [Chanukat Habayit](/learn/chanukat-habayit), a housewarming with words of Torah.

## A kosher kitchen

A [kosher kitchen](/learn/kosher-kitchen) separates meat and dairy, with separate dishes, pots and utensils, and uses only kosher food. If you are starting fresh, it is much simpler to set it up from the beginning.

- **Kashering.** Some existing items, like many metal pots, ovens and sinks, can be made kosher; others cannot. The Rabbi can come and kasher what can be kashered.
- **Tevilat kelim.** New metal and glass dishes and utensils are immersed in a mikvah before first use. See [Tevilat Kelim](/learn/tevilat-kelim).
- **Label and color-code** meat and dairy so everyone in the house knows which is which.

## Shabbat candlesticks

A Jewish woman lights [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) every Friday before sunset. Keep the candlesticks in a place of honor, ready for each week. Many grooms give their bride candlesticks as a wedding gift.

## A pushka

A [pushka](/learn/pushka), a tzedakah box, turns a house into a home of giving. The Rebbe encouraged keeping one in the kitchen, and in children's rooms too. Drop in a coin before lighting candles and on weekdays.

## Jewish books

The Rebbe encouraged every family to make its home "a house full of books." Start with a [siddur](/learn/siddur), a Chumash, a book of [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) and a Haggadah, and add over time. See [The Jewish Home Library](/learn/jewish-home-library).

## A Shabbat table

Set aside what you need for a beautiful Shabbat: a Kiddush cup, a challah board and cover, a nice tablecloth, and enough chairs to welcome guests. See [Shabbat Meals](/learn/shabbat-meals) and [Hachnasat Orchim](/learn/hachnasat-orchim).

## Common questions

### Which doors need a mezuzah?
Most doorways of rooms used for living, including bedrooms, the kitchen and the garage entrance. Bathrooms do not. Ask the Rabbi about closets and unusual doors.

### Do I need to do everything at once?
No. Start with mezuzahs and Shabbat candles, and add one step at a time.

### We are renting. Do we still need mezuzahs?
Yes. Outside Israel, a renter puts up mezuzahs within thirty days of moving in. Ask the Rabbi about taking them down when you move out.
