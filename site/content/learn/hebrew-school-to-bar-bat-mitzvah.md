A Bar or Bat Mitzvah is the day a Jewish child becomes an adult in Jewish law, responsible for the mitzvahs: a boy at 13, a girl at 12. Years of Hebrew school make that day meaningful and relaxed. A child who already reads Hebrew, knows the prayers, understands the Torah portion and has lived the holidays arrives ready, and personal preparation can focus on what makes the day their own.

## What a Bar or Bat Mitzvah really is

Becoming Bar or Bat Mitzvah happens by itself on the Hebrew birthday; no ceremony is needed to make it happen. The Mishnah says, "At thirteen, for the mitzvahs" (Ethics of the Fathers 5:21). The celebration marks a beginning: from now on the child does the mitzvahs as their own. Hebrew school is about giving them the tools to do that with joy. See [Bar Mitzvah](/learn/bar-mitzvah) and [Bat Mitzvah](/learn/bat-mitzvah).

## How each part of Hebrew school prepares a child

**Hebrew reading.** With Aleph Champ, children learn to read Hebrew level by level. By Bar or Bat Mitzvah age, a child who has learned steadily can read blessings and prayers with confidence. See [Aleph Champ](/learn/aleph-champ-hebrew-reading).

**Prayer and the siddur.** Children who know the Shema, the blessings and how to find their way in a prayer book feel at home in synagogue, on their big day and after it.

**The weekly Torah portion.** Learning the parsha every week means that when a child studies their own [Bar Mitzvah portion](/learn/bar-mitzvah-parsha), it is a familiar world, not a foreign text.

**Shabbat, the holidays and mitzvahs.** A Bar or Bat Mitzvah is about living as a Jewish adult. Children who have celebrated the year from Rosh Hashanah to Shavuot, and practiced kindness and tzedakah, already know what that means.

## The Bar/Bat Mitzvah track (ages 12 to 13)

At Lamorinda Hebrew School, children ages 12 to 13 receive personal preparation for the big day, building on what they learned in earlier years. Girls can also join the **Bat Mitzvah Club**. For boys, the Bar Mitzvah brings the mitzvah of [tefillin and a first aliyah](/learn/bar-mitzvah-first-tefillin-and-aliyah). The preparation is personal, because every child is different.

## Starting late?

Not every child has years of Hebrew school behind them, and that is fine. Personal preparation can begin wherever a child is. See [preparing for a Bar or Bat Mitzvah without years of Hebrew school](/learn/bar-mitzvah-prep-without-hebrew-school) and [starting Hebrew school at any age](/learn/starting-hebrew-school-at-any-age).

## After the Bar or Bat Mitzvah

The big day is a beginning, not a graduation. Encourage your child to keep learning, keep coming to synagogue and keep doing the mitzvahs they prepared for. See [after the Bar Mitzvah](/learn/after-the-bar-mitzvah).

## Common questions

### How does Hebrew school prepare a child for a Bar or Bat Mitzvah?
It teaches Hebrew reading, prayer, the weekly Torah portion, the holidays and the mitzvahs, so that by 12 or 13 a child is ready, and personal preparation can focus on their own portion, blessings and words of Torah.

### When should Bar or Bat Mitzvah preparation begin?
Hebrew reading and prayer are best learned over years. Personal preparation usually begins about a year before. See the [Bar and Bat Mitzvah timeline](/learn/bar-bat-mitzvah-timeline).

### What is the Bat Mitzvah Club?
The girls' Bat Mitzvah Club is part of the Bar/Bat Mitzvah track at Lamorinda Hebrew School, for girls approaching their Bat Mitzvah. Call Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski at 925-384-7241 for details.

### Can my child have a Bar or Bat Mitzvah without Hebrew school?
Yes. A child becomes Bar or Bat Mitzvah on the Hebrew birthday regardless, and personal preparation can begin at any point. Call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242.

### Do we need to be members to celebrate a Bar or Bat Mitzvah at Chabad of Lamorinda?
No. Chabad of Lamorinda has no membership requirement. Call 925-384-7242 to talk about your child's Bar or Bat Mitzvah.
