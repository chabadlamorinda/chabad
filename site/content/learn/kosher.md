Kosher food is food that meets the requirements of Jewish law as set out in the Torah. It is not a style of cooking or a blessing a rabbi says over food: it is about which foods we eat, how animals are slaughtered, and keeping meat and dairy separate.

## What "kosher" means

Kosher (כָּשֵׁר, kasher) means "fit" or "proper." The laws of kosher, called kashrut, come from the Torah and were explained in detail by the Sages. They apply to everything we eat and drink.

## Why keep kosher?

The Torah gives these laws as a commandment, without listing a reason. Jewish tradition sees kosher as a way of bringing holiness into the most everyday act of eating. Chassidic teaching explains that food becomes part of us, giving us energy for living, so what we eat affects our soul as well as our body. Keeping kosher also connects Jewish families across the world and the generations.

## The basic rules

### Meat and poultry
- **Animals** must both chew their cud and have split hooves. Cows, sheep, goats and deer qualify. Pigs, rabbits and horses do not.
- **Birds** must be from traditionally accepted kosher species, such as chicken, turkey, duck and goose. Birds of prey are not kosher.
- **Shechitah:** the animal must be slaughtered by a trained, G-d-fearing shochet in the prescribed humane way.
- **Inspection and preparation:** the animal is checked for certain defects, and the blood is removed by salting and soaking. Some fats and the sciatic nerve are removed.

### Fish
Kosher fish must have both fins and scales, such as salmon, tuna, cod and tilapia. Shellfish (shrimp, lobster, crab, clams) and fish without scales like catfish and swordfish are not kosher.

### Meat and dairy
The Torah says three times, "Do not cook a kid in its mother's milk." From this we learn not to cook, eat or benefit from meat and dairy together. Kosher homes use separate dishes, and after eating meat, many wait six hours before eating dairy. Ask the Rabbi about your family custom.

### Pareve
Foods that are neither meat nor dairy, such as fruits, vegetables, grains, eggs and fish, are called pareve and can be eaten with either. (Fish is not eaten together with meat, though.)

### Other important rules
- **Insects** are not kosher, so produce like leafy greens is checked carefully.
- **Wine and grape juice** must be kosher-certified.
- **Processed foods** need reliable kosher supervision, because ingredients and equipment can be an issue. See [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols).

## How to start keeping kosher

You do not need to change everything overnight. Many people take it in steps:

1. Stop eating clearly non-kosher foods like pork and shellfish.
2. Avoid mixing meat and dairy in the same meal.
3. Buy only products with a reliable kosher symbol.
4. Buy kosher meat and poultry.
5. Set up a [kosher kitchen](/learn/kosher-kitchen) with separate meat and dairy dishes.

Every step is a mitzvah in its own right.

## Common questions

### Does a rabbi bless food to make it kosher?
No. Kosher food is kosher because of what it is and how it was produced. Rabbis and kosher agencies supervise production to make sure the rules are followed, but no blessing makes food kosher.

### Is kosher food healthier?
Kosher is a spiritual law, not a health diet. Some people choose kosher food for its careful supervision, but the reason Jews keep kosher is because it is a commandment from G-d.

### Are eggs and fish dairy?
No. Eggs and fish are pareve. Eggs must come from kosher birds and should be checked for blood spots. Fish and meat are not cooked or eaten together.

### Can I keep kosher when eating out?
Many people eat only in kosher-certified restaurants. Others start by ordering vegetarian or fish dishes elsewhere, but cooking equipment is still an issue there. Ask the Rabbi for guidance that fits your situation.
