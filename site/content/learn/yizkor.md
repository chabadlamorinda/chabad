Yizkor is a memorial prayer recited in synagogue four times a year, asking G-d to remember the souls of loved ones who have passed away. It is said on Yom Kippur, the last day of Passover, Shavuot, and Shemini Atzeret.

## What does Yizkor mean?

Yizkor means "May He remember." The prayer begins, "May G-d remember the soul of..." and continues with the person's Hebrew name and a pledge to give charity in their memory. It is one of the most heartfelt moments of the Jewish year, when the whole community pauses together to remember.

## When is Yizkor said?

Yizkor is recited four times each year, after the Torah reading on:

1. **Yom Kippur**
2. **Shemini Atzeret** (at the end of Sukkot)
3. **The last day of Passover**
4. **The second day of Shavuot**

## Why these days?

On holidays, our joy naturally reminds us of those we wish were with us. Jewish tradition teaches that the souls of the departed are also elevated on these holy days. Yizkor connects the two worlds, letting us share our holidays with those we love.

## Who says Yizkor?

Anyone who has lost a parent says Yizkor. Many also say it for a spouse, child, sibling or other relative, and there are special paragraphs for Jewish martyrs and for victims of the Holocaust.

There is a widespread custom that people whose parents are both living step out of the sanctuary during Yizkor. This is a custom of sensitivity and good wishes, not a requirement.

Customs differ on whether a mourner says Yizkor during the first year after a loss, so ask the Rabbi what to do.

## How to say Yizkor

1. **Find the Yizkor section** in your prayer book. It is often printed with an English translation.
2. **Say the paragraph** for each loved one, inserting their Hebrew name and their father's name (or as your community's custom directs).
3. **Pledge tzedakah.** The prayer includes a promise to give charity in their memory. Since money is not handled on holidays, you make the pledge and give after the holiday.
4. **Say El Malei Rachamim,** a memorial prayer that the leader or congregation recites.

## Lighting a memorial candle

It is customary to light a 24-hour memorial candle before the holiday begins. On a holiday, a flame may only be lit from an existing flame, so if you forget, ask the Rabbi how to light it properly.

## Why charity is part of Yizkor

A soul in heaven can no longer do mitzvot. When we give [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) in a loved one's name, we do a good deed on their behalf, bringing merit and elevation to their soul. This is the heart of Yizkor: remembering through action.

## Common questions

### Can I say Yizkor at home?

Yizkor is ideally said in synagogue with the community, but it can be said privately when that is not possible. The prayer itself does not require a minyan.

### What if I do not know my loved one's Hebrew name?

Say their name in English, or describe them as "my father" or "my mother." G-d knows exactly whom you mean. You can also ask us to help find the Hebrew name.

### Can I say Yizkor for someone who was not Jewish?

You may pray for anyone you love. The traditional Yizkor text is written for Jewish souls, but a heartfelt personal prayer is always welcome. Ask the Rabbi if you would like guidance.
