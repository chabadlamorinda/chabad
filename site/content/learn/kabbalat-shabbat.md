Kabbalat Shabbat means "welcoming Shabbat." It is the short, musical service on Friday at sunset, when the week ends and Shabbat arrives. The Sages pictured Shabbat as a queen and a bride, and Kabbalat Shabbat is how we go out to greet her: with songs, psalms and joy.

## Where it comes from

Already in the Talmud, great sages would put on their best clothes on Friday afternoon and say, "Come, let us go out to greet Shabbat the queen." About 450 years ago, the Kabbalists of Safed in the Land of Israel would walk out into the fields at sunset, singing, to welcome Shabbat. The service we say today grew out of their custom.

## What we say

- **Six psalms** (Psalms 95 to 99 and 29), one for each day of the week that has passed, beginning "Lechu neranena," "Come, let us sing to G-d."
- **Lecha Dodi**, "Come, my beloved, to greet the bride," the beloved song written in Safed by Rabbi Shlomo Alkabetz. Every congregation has its favorite tunes for it.
- **Psalm 92**, "A psalm, a song for the Shabbat day," and Psalm 93. With these words, Shabbat has begun.
- Then comes **Maariv**, the evening prayer, with the Shema and the Shabbat Amidah.

## Turning to the door

At the last verse of Lecha Dodi, "Bo'i kallah," "Come, O bride," everyone turns around to face the entrance of the synagogue and bows slightly, as if welcoming an honored guest coming through the door. It is one of the most moving moments of the week.

## What it feels like

You do not need to read Hebrew or know the tunes. Kabbalat Shabbat is about letting the week fall away: the emails, the traffic, the worries. Many people find that just sitting and listening to the singing is enough. Little by little, the melodies become your own.

## After the service

At home, Friday night continues with [Shalom Aleichem](/learn/shalom-aleichem), the blessings over the children, Kiddush over wine, challah, and a festive meal with songs. If you do not have a Shabbat table to go to, you are always welcome at ours: see [Shabbat at Chabad](/shabbat).

## Common questions

### How long is Kabbalat Shabbat?
The whole Friday night service, Kabbalat Shabbat and Maariv, usually takes about 30 to 45 minutes.

### Do I need to dress up?
Come as you are. Many people wear something a little nicer in honor of Shabbat, but everyone is welcome.

### Can I bring my children?
Yes. Children love the singing, and Friday night is a beautiful family time.

### What time is Kabbalat Shabbat in Lafayette?
