Some Jews choose to be buried in the Land of Israel because of the sages' teachings that those buried there will be the first to rise at the time of the resurrection, and that burial there atones for sins. It is arranged through a Jewish funeral home or burial society, and it should be discussed with a rabbi early.

This guide explains the reasons and the basic questions. It is not a substitute for guidance for your own case.

## Where the idea comes from

The idea goes back to the Torah. Jacob asked his son Joseph not to bury him in Egypt but with his fathers, and he was buried in the Cave of Machpelah in Hebron, where Abraham and Sarah are buried. Joseph, too, asked that his bones be carried up to the Land when the Jewish people left Egypt. Read more about the Land in [Israel and the Jewish people](/learn/israel-and-the-jewish-people).

The sages teach two things about burial in the Land:

1. **Resurrection.** Those buried in the Land of Israel will be the first to rise at the time of the resurrection.
2. **Atonement.** Burial in the Land of Israel atones for one's sins.

These teachings speak of a special merit of the Land. They take nothing away from a Jewish burial anywhere else. Most Jewish families bury their loved ones in a Jewish cemetery near home, and that is a full and proper Jewish burial.

## The soil of Israel

The Chevra Kadisha has a custom: they sprinkle earth from the Land of Israel into the grave when a burial takes place. See [the Chevra Kadisha and tahara](/learn/chevra-kadisha-and-tahara).

## Burial in Israel in practice

Some points about practical arrangements:

- **Plan ahead.** Buying a burial plot in advance is customary. If that was not done, the family should buy one before the burial.
- **Moving a body.** A body may be moved to another city for burial in Israel if the family wishes, or if the person asked for it.
- **Re-burial.** If someone was buried abroad, the family may later re-inter the body in Israel. For any move to a family plot, consult an expert rabbi.
- **Timing.** Jewish law calls for burial as soon as possible, and government requirements such as legal transport and paperwork are among the permitted reasons for delay. Read [Jewish funeral customs](/learn/jewish-funeral).

For the arrangements themselves, the transport, the paperwork and the cost, speak with a Jewish funeral home or burial society that handles burials in Israel. We cannot quote prices or recommend a provider here. Rabbi Labkowski can help you ask the right questions.

## Concerns, and the most important step

There is a passage in the Jerusalem Talmud that warns about bringing coffins into Israel, which the commentaries limit to people who made no effort to live in the Land during their lives and not to those who were prevented by circumstances. It also teaches that someone who lives in Israel and is buried there receives greater atonement. These are subtle questions, so ask the Rabbi about your own situation.

The most important step is to write your wishes down and tell your family. See [planning a Jewish funeral in advance](/learn/planning-a-jewish-funeral-in-advance) and the [halachic will](/learn/halachic-will).

## Common questions

### Why do Jews want to be buried in Israel?
The sages teach that those buried in the Land will be the first to rise at the resurrection and that burial there atones for sins. Jacob and Joseph, too, asked to be buried there.

### Can someone who lived in the Bay Area be buried in Israel?
Yes. A body may be moved to Israel if the family wishes or the person asked for it. Speak with a rabbi and a Jewish funeral home early.

### What is the soil of Israel in a Jewish burial?
The Chevra Kadisha sprinkles earth from the Land of Israel into the grave at a burial.

### Is burial in Israel required?
No. It is a special merit that some choose. Most Jewish families bury their loved ones in a Jewish cemetery near home, which is a full and proper Jewish burial. Ask the Rabbi about your own wishes.

### Should I buy a burial plot in advance?
Buying a plot in advance is customary.

### Can a body buried abroad be moved to Israel later?
The family may re-inter it in Israel, but any such move should be done in consultation with an expert rabbi.
