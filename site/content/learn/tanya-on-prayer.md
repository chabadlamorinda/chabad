The Tanya teaches that prayer begins before the first word: a person should stop, remember that G-d stands over him and knows his innermost thoughts, and pray with the awe of someone standing before a king. According to Chapter 41, this readiness, together with love, is what turns reciting words into real prayer.

Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi (the Alter Rebbe), who wrote the [Tanya](/learn/tanya), was not asking for a perfect feeling. He gives ordinary people a way to begin each day facing the right direction. For families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda who pray in a rush between the alarm clock and the school run, this is an encouraging idea.

## Start by remembering who you are speaking to

Chapter 41 on Chabad.org says that before a mitzvah, and before Torah study, a person should reflect that G-d "stands over him" and examines his innermost thoughts and feelings. The chapter also teaches that G-d's care is personal: "For my sake was the world created." Awe and closeness belong together.

Chapter 41 then applies this to the morning. Before prayer, a person recites the morning blessing that thanks G-d for the soul, acknowledging that the soul was given by Him and will one day be taken back, and offers it back to be united with G-d's oneness. The chapter says prayer itself should begin with that same readiness to give one's soul to G-d. See [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani) for the first words many people say on waking.

## Love and awe, like two wings

The same chapter compares serving G-d to a bird. Love is one wing and awe is the other, and without both the bird cannot fly. Love alone, the Alter Rebbe says, is not enough before a positive mitzvah. Awe is also needed, felt in the heart or at least held in the mind.

You do not have to be moved to tears. The chapter says a person who cannot reach deep awe through contemplation should still think about it in a simpler way, and sincere intention counts. See [kavanah in prayer](/learn/kavanah-in-prayer) for more on focus and intention.

## When prayer lifts you

Chapter 12 looks at what happens during prayer. It teaches that at the Shema and the Amidah the Divine soul holds "undisputed sovereignty" and the animal soul has no effect. The person focuses his mind on G-d's greatness, and this kindles love in the heart. (See the [Shema](/learn/shema) and the [Amidah](/learn/amidah).)

## Why the glow fades, and why that is normal

Chapter 12 is honest. After prayer the inspiration fades and the animal soul reawakens with worldly desires, "as though he had never prayed." The person himself has not changed. What remains, the chapter says, is the hidden natural love of G-d in the heart and the impression prayer left on the mind, which help him resist temptation later in the day.

So if your prayer felt flat, the Tanya does not say it was wasted. And if it felt wonderful and an hour later you were short-tempered, that is also described in the book. This ties to the [beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni), the ordinary person at the center of the Tanya, for whom the inner struggle continues.

The chapter closes with a saying of the Sixth Lubavitcher Rebbe: when a Jew studies Torah, he feels like a student before his teacher, and when he prays, he feels like a child before his father.

## A simple way to use this

These ideas come from the chapters above; how you apply them is up to you:

1. Pause for a moment before you pray and remember that you are speaking to G-d.
2. Thank Him for your soul with the morning blessing, or at least with Modeh Ani.
3. Pray at your own level. Sincere intention counts.
4. Do not be discouraged when the feeling fades. Take with you what remains.

For the order of the daily prayers, see [daily prayers](/learn/daily-prayers) and [prayer](/learn/prayer).

## Common questions

### What does the Tanya say about prayer?
The Tanya says prayer should begin with remembering that G-d stands over you and with a readiness to give your soul to Him, combining love and awe. It also says that during the Shema and Amidah the Divine soul holds sway, even though the effect fades afterward.

### What is Tanya Chapter 41 about?
Chapter 41 is about awe of G-d as the foundation of serving Him, and about preparing before prayer, Torah study and mitzvot by contemplating G-d's greatness. It compares love and awe to two wings of a bird.

### Why do I feel nothing when I pray?
Chapter 12 explains that the inspiration of prayer fades and that this is normal; it does not mean the prayer failed. The hidden love of G-d in the heart remains. Ask the Rabbi if you would like personal guidance.

### Do I need to feel awe to pray correctly?
No. Chapter 41 says that someone who cannot reach deep awe should still think about G-d's greatness at a simpler level, and sincere intention is what counts.

### What are the morning blessings in the Tanya?
Chapter 41 mentions the morning blessing that thanks G-d for the soul, given by Him and returned to Him, as the way to begin prayer. Many people say [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani) on waking.
