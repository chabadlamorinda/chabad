The Mishnah is the first written collection of the Oral Torah, the explanations and laws that G-d gave Moses at Sinai together with the Written Torah. It was compiled by Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi around the year 200 CE and is the foundation of the Talmud and all of Jewish law.

## The Oral Torah

The Written Torah is brief. It tells us to keep Shabbat, but not exactly what counts as work. It says to put on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin), but not what they look like. These details were given to Moses orally and passed down from teacher to student for generations.

The word Mishnah means "repetition" or "study," because it was learned by reviewing it again and again.

## Why it was written down

For over a thousand years, the Oral Torah was taught by heart. After the destruction of the Second Temple and years of persecution, Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi, known simply as "Rabbi," saw that the Jewish people were scattered and the chain of teaching was at risk. He gathered the teachings of the sages and organized them into a written, concise text so they would never be forgotten.

## The six orders

The Mishnah is divided into six orders (sedarim), together called Shas, an acronym of shisha sedarim:

1. **Zeraim (Seeds):** blessings, prayer and agricultural laws
2. **Moed (Festivals):** Shabbat and the holidays
3. **Nashim (Women):** marriage and divorce
4. **Nezikin (Damages):** civil and criminal law, and Pirkei Avot
5. **Kodashim (Holy Things):** the Temple service and kosher slaughter
6. **Taharot (Purities):** ritual purity and the [mikvah](/learn/mikvah)

Each order contains tractates (masechtot), 63 in total, which are divided into chapters and individual mishnayot.

## Mishnah and Talmud

Over the next three centuries, sages in Babylonia and the Land of Israel discussed and explained the Mishnah in depth. Their discussions, called the Gemara, together with the Mishnah, make up the [Talmud](/learn/talmud). Every page of Talmud begins with a mishnah and then unpacks it.

## Pirkei Avot

One tractate is especially well known: Pirkei Avot, "Ethics of the Fathers." It contains wise sayings about character, kindness and life, such as "Who is wise? One who learns from every person." It is customarily studied on Shabbat afternoons in the summer months.

## Learning Mishnah in memory of a loved one

The letters of the word Mishnah are the same as neshamah, "soul." For this reason it is a beloved custom to study Mishnah in memory of someone who has passed away, especially during the year of mourning and on the [yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit). Families often divide the tractates among friends to complete the entire Mishnah together.

## Common questions

### Is the Mishnah hard to learn?
The Mishnah is written in concise Hebrew, but with a good translation and commentary, anyone can begin. Many start with Pirkei Avot.

### What commentary should I use?
The classic commentary is by Rabbi Ovadiah of Bartenura, known as the Bartenura. Many English editions include a clear explanation based on it.

### How long does it take to learn the whole Mishnah?
At two mishnayot a day, the whole Mishnah takes about six years. Many join group study to finish together much faster.

### What is a siyum?
A siyum is a celebration held when a tractate or the whole Mishnah is completed. It includes a short teaching, a special Kaddish and often a festive meal.
