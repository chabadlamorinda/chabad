The Passover Seder is the festive meal held on the first night of Passover (and the second night outside Israel), where we retell the story of the Exodus from Egypt. It follows a set order of 15 steps, with matzah, four cups of wine, bitter herbs and the Haggadah.

## What is the Seder?

Seder means "order." The Torah commands, "And you shall tell your child on that day" (Exodus 13:8). The Seder turns that mitzvah into an experience. We do not just tell the story; we taste it, ask about it, and relive it as if we ourselves left Egypt.

The guidebook for the night is the Haggadah, which means "telling."

## The Seder plate

Chabad custom arranges the Seder plate following the Arizal, with three matzot underneath and six items on top:

- **Zeroa:** a roasted chicken neck or bone, recalling the Passover offering.
- **Beitzah:** a hard-boiled egg, recalling the festival offering.
- **Maror:** bitter herbs, usually romaine lettuce and horseradish.
- **Charoset:** a paste of apples, pears, nuts and wine, recalling the mortar the slaves used.
- **Karpas:** a vegetable such as onion or potato.
- **Chazeret:** more bitter herbs for the Korech sandwich.

## The 15 steps of the Seder

1. **Kadesh:** say Kiddush over the first cup of wine.
2. **Urchatz:** wash hands without a blessing.
3. **Karpas:** dip the vegetable in salt water and eat it.
4. **Yachatz:** break the middle matzah. The larger half becomes the afikoman.
5. **Maggid:** tell the story, starting with the Four Questions. Drink the second cup.
6. **Rachtzah:** wash hands with a blessing.
7. **Motzi:** say the blessing over bread on the matzot.
8. **Matzah:** say the special blessing and eat the [matzah](/learn/matzah).
9. **Maror:** eat bitter herbs dipped in charoset.
10. **Korech:** eat a sandwich of matzah and bitter herbs.
11. **Shulchan Orech:** enjoy the festive meal.
12. **Tzafun:** eat the afikoman.
13. **Barech:** say Grace after Meals over the third cup. Pour a cup for Elijah the Prophet.
14. **Hallel:** sing songs of praise over the fourth cup.
15. **Nirtzah:** conclude with "Next year in Jerusalem!"

## The four cups

We drink four cups of wine or grape juice, recalling four expressions of redemption G-d used in Exodus 6. Each cup should hold at least about 3.5 ounces, and we drink most of it while leaning to the left, like free people.

## The four questions

The youngest child asks, "Mah nishtanah?", "Why is this night different?" The questions are about the matzah, the bitter herbs, the dipping, and the leaning. The whole Haggadah is the answer. Children's curiosity is the heart of the Seder.

## Tips for hosting

- Prepare the Seder plate, salt water and wine before the holiday begins.
- Use handmade shmurah matzah for the main mitzvot if possible.
- Keep children engaged with questions, songs and small rewards.
- Do not rush: start after nightfall and take your time.

## Common questions

### How long does a Seder take?
It depends on your table. A focused Seder can take two to three hours, and many families go much longer with discussion and singing. The afikoman should ideally be eaten before midnight.

### Can I use grape juice instead of wine?
Yes. Grape juice may be used for the four cups by those who find wine difficult. It should be kosher for Passover.

### What if I have never led a Seder before?
Start with a clear Haggadah with English translation and instructions. Focus on the core mitzvot: four cups, matzah, maror and telling the story. If you need help, contact us.

### Why are there two Seders outside Israel?
Outside Israel, the first two days of Passover are both full holidays, a custom from when the calendar was set by witnesses in Jerusalem. So we hold a Seder on each of the first two nights.
