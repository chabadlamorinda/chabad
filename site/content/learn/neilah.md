Neilah (נעילה, "closing") is the final prayer service of Yom Kippur, said as the sun sets, when we ask to be inscribed and sealed for a good year. The Ark stays open throughout, we declare our faith together, and the service ends with one long blast of the shofar and the words "Next year in Jerusalem."

## What does Neilah mean?

The word means "closing the gate." Our sages picture the gates of heaven open to our prayers all through the Ten Days of Repentance, and in Neilah we pray with every last bit of strength before they close. For this reason it is often the most intense and emotional hour of the entire year.

Chassidic teaching adds a comforting thought: the Ark is open and G-d is near, as close as a parent to a child who is leaving. Even in the final minutes, nothing is too late.

## What happens in the Neilah service

Chabad.org describes the order of the service:

1. **Opening prayers** and the Amidah, with the Ark open for the entire service, a sign that heaven's gates are open to us.
2. **The cantor's repetition** of the Amidah.
3. **[Avinu Malkeinu](/learn/avinu-malkeinu)**, "Our Father, our King."
4. **A declaration of faith.** The *Shema* is said aloud together, the high point of the service. "Blessed be the name of the glory of His kingdom forever and ever" is said three times, and then "The L-rd, He is G-d" seven times, the words proclaimed by the people at Mount Carmel in the time of Elijah.
5. **The shofar.** One long, unbroken blast is sounded to close the service. See [the shofar](/learn/shofar).
6. **"Next year in Jerusalem."** The congregation sings these words together.

Another change in the language: in the Ten Days we ask to be "written" in the Book of Life, and in Neilah we ask to be "sealed." Ask the Rabbi if you would like to follow the words in your prayer book.

## A day that ends in joy

The shofar of Neilah also announces that the fast is over and the day has ended in forgiveness. People often leave with a lighter step: the closing of the gates is not a loss but a seal on what we achieved. After nightfall comes Havdalah, and then a festive meal; see [breaking the fast](/learn/break-fast-after-yom-kippur) and [Havdalah](/learn/havdalah).

## Common questions

### What is Neilah on Yom Kippur?
Neilah is the closing service of Yom Kippur, said at sunset, in which we make a last, intense plea to be sealed for a good year. It ends with a long shofar blast.

### Why is Neilah called "the closing"?
The word means "closing the gate." It describes the gates of heaven that stand open for our prayers and are about to close as the day ends.

### Why is the Ark open during Neilah?
The open Ark is a sign that the gates of heaven are open to our prayers. It stays open for the whole service.

### What is the shofar blown at the end of Neilah?
One long blast is sounded at the very end of the service. It marks the close of the day and, in Jewish tradition, the beginning of the joy of the break-fast.
