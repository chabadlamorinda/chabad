The best Jewish library for a child is a small shelf of books that matches their age and that they actually want to open: holiday stories, parsha books, Chassidic tales, a Hebrew primer and a children's prayer book. A child who grows up seeing Jewish books at home, and seeing parents read them, learns that Jewish learning belongs to the family.

## Why Jewish books matter

Children remember stories long after they forget lectures. Jewish tradition has always taught through story: the Torah is full of them, and Chassidic masters used tales to carry big ideas in simple words. A good picture book on Friday night, or a Chassidic story at bedtime, gives a child a warm picture of Jewish life that stays with them. This is part of *chinuch* (חִנּוּךְ), Jewish education at home; see [chinuch](/learn/chinuch).

For adults, our guide to [building a Jewish home library](/learn/jewish-home-library) covers the essential books for the whole family.

## Five kinds of books to look for

1. **Holiday books.** For each holiday, choose one or two picture books that explain the story and the customs. Take them out a few weeks before the holiday and read them together so that children arrive at Rosh Hashanah, Sukkot, Chanukah, Purim or Passover curious and ready.
2. **Parsha books.** Illustrated retellings of the weekly Torah portion help children follow what is read in synagogue and what is discussed at the Shabbat table. See [the parsha for kids](/learn/parsha-for-kids).
3. **Chassidic stories.** Short, true stories of the Rebbes and Chassidim teach kindness, faith and joy. Chabad's publishing house, Kehot, publishes collections of Chassidic stories for children, including a two-volume *Chassidic Stories for Children*. Browse our [stories page](/stories) too.
4. **Hebrew and Aleph-Bet books.** A colorful primer, a letter book and a simple reader. Use them with the plan in [teaching kids the Aleph-Bet](/learn/teaching-kids-the-aleph-bet).
5. **Prayer and blessing books.** A children's siddur with pictures and transliteration, and a book of blessings. They help children join in at home and at services.

## How to choose the right book

- **Age first.** Board books for toddlers, picture books for ages three to seven, and chapter books and story collections for older children. Preview a book before reading it to a child, to be sure it fits your family.
- **Warm and accurate.** Look for books from reliable Jewish publishers and recommended by a rabbi or teacher. If a book describes a custom or a halacha in a way you are unsure about, ask the Rabbi.
- **A joyful tone.** The goal is for a child to love being Jewish, so pick books that are positive and fun, not heavy with guilt or fear.
- **Lots of pictures.** For younger children, pictures carry the lesson.
- **Hebrew in context.** A few Hebrew words in a story, with a pronunciation guide, teach faster than a list.

## Making reading a habit

1. **A Shabbat basket.** Keep a basket of Jewish books near the table and let children choose one to read on Friday night or Shabbat afternoon (see [Shabbat with kids](/learn/shabbat-with-kids)).
2. **Bedtime story.** One story, then the Shema (see [the bedtime Shema](/learn/bedtime-shema)).
3. **Read before the holiday.** Take out the holiday book three weeks ahead.
4. **Ask a question.** "What would you have done?" turns a story into a conversation.
5. **Visit the library.** Public libraries have Jewish children's titles, so you can borrow before you buy.
6. **Let children see you read.** Read your own Jewish book on the couch.

## Common questions

### What are the best Jewish books for toddlers?
Sturdy board books about Shabbat, the holidays and the Aleph-Bet are a good start. Choose short, bright books that a toddler can hold. Ask the Rabbi if you want recommendations.

### How many Jewish books should a child have?
A small shelf is enough: a few holiday books, a parsha book, a Chassidic story collection, a Hebrew primer and a prayer book. Quality and regular reading matter more than quantity.

### Are Chassidic stories appropriate for young children?
Yes, many collections are written for children. Choose a simple, short one and read it together. Chabad publishes story books for children.

### Which book should I start with if we are new to Judaism?
Begin with a book about the holiday that is next on the calendar, or a simple book about Shabbat. See [this week](/this-week) for what is coming up.

### Should children read a Hebrew prayer book?
A children's siddur with transliteration and pictures is a good first step. It lets a child follow along and join in, and Hebrew reading grows from there.

### Where can I find books if I do not know what to buy?
Public libraries, Jewish bookstores and online Jewish publishers all have children's sections. You can also ask the Rabbi at 925-384-7242.
