The Talmud says that tzedakah, giving charity, is equal to all the other mitzvot combined (Bava Batra 9a). Chabad.org explains that this is because most mitzvot use only part of a person, while giving away part of your earnings gives something into which you put your whole self.

## What the Sages say

Chabad.org confirms that the saying is authentic and comes from the Talmud (Bava Batra 9a). It collects other sayings of the Sages that show how much weight they gave this mitzvah:

- **The Jerusalem Talmud** (Pe'ah 1:1) says that tzedakah and acts of kindness are equal to all the commandments of the Torah.
- **The Talmud** (Sukkah 49b) says that tzedakah is greater than all the sacrifices.
- **A Midrash** (Midrash Zutta on Song of Songs) teaches that the generation of the Flood and the people of Sodom might not have been destroyed had they given tzedakah.
- **Another Midrash** (Tanna d'Vei Eliyahu Zutta) says the world has stood on tzedakah since creation.

Chabad.org adds that the Sages taught that tzedakah brings atonement, protects from harsh decrees and hastens the redemption. See [Does charity avert a harsh decree?](/learn/tzedakah-averts-a-harsh-decree)

## A simple explanation: it takes your whole self

In an article on Chabad.org, Malkie Janowski explains why. Most mitzvot involve only part of us: the mouth for kosher food, the head for Torah study, the hands for Shabbat candles. Even sitting in a sukkah, which surrounds the whole body, does not engage a person's full talents and effort.

But most of our waking hours go to earning money for a comfortable life. When we set aside part of that money for charity, we give up something into which we invested our whole self, and we do it for G-d's sake. That is extraordinary, and each act of tzedakah does it. This is also why giving feels different from other mitzvot: it costs us something real. See [what giving does for the giver](/learn/what-giving-does-for-the-giver).

## The Tanya's answer

The Alter Rebbe, Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi, founder of Chabad, explains this in chapter 37 of the *Tanya* (Chabad's foundational book; see [Tanya](/learn/tanya)). Chabad.org presents it this way:

- The Sages call charity equal to all the mitzvot, and in the Jerusalem Talmud it is often called simply "the commandment," because it is the core of the mitzvot of action.
- In most mitzvot, only one faculty of the soul is involved, and only while the mitzvah is being done.
- Charity from earnings is different. The soul's whole energy was invested in the work that earned the money. When that money is given away, the whole soul's energy ascends to G-d.
- Even someone who does not earn his living by his own labor gives away money that could have sustained him, so he too is offering his life to G-d.

The chapter also notes that the Sages said charity hastens the redemption (Bava Batra 10a). The Tanya adds that Torah study works in a different way, and quotes the Sages that the essential thing is not study but deed (Avot 1:17).

## Common questions

### Why is tzedakah called equal to all the mitzvot?
The Talmud (Bava Batra 9a) says so. Chabad.org explains that most mitzvot engage only part of a person, while giving away part of one's earnings gives something into which one invested one's whole effort.

### Is tzedakah really more important than Shabbat or kashrut?
Chabad.org presents the saying as showing the unique weight of tzedakah. For how mitzvot relate to one another, ask the Rabbi at 925-384-7242.

### Why does the Jerusalem Talmud call tzedakah "the mitzvah"?
According to Chabad.org's presentation of the Tanya, because it is the core of the mitzvot of action and surpasses them.

### What does the Tanya say about tzedakah?
That charity from earnings lifts the whole energy of the soul to G-d, because the whole soul was invested in earning that money.

### How do I make tzedakah a regular part of my life?
Chabad.org recommends a charity box at home and at work, with coins put in at least once a day. Read [Tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).

### Does someone with little money have to give too?
Yes. Chabad.org notes that even a person who receives tzedakah should give some. Read [giving when money is tight](/learn/giving-when-money-is-tight).
