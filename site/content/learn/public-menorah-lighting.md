A public menorah lighting is a community event where a large Chanukah menorah is lit outdoors, with blessings and songs, so that everyone can see the light. It fulfills the idea of publicizing the miracle of Chanukah, and it is open to everyone.

## What is a public menorah lighting?

At the center is an oversized menorah, lit in honor of Chanukah. Chabad.org describes the typical event: short blessings before the lighting, songs afterward, one more flame added each night so that all eight burn on the last night, and the helper flame (shamash) is not counted. Many events include kids' crafts, Jewish music, a costumed Judah the Maccabee who poses for photos, and doughnuts and potato pancakes. Some are even paired with parades of cars topped with menorahs.

## Why do we light menorahs in public?

The sages established that Chanukah lights are kindled to publicize the miracle: the pure oil in the Temple that lasted eight days when there was only enough for one, after the Maccabees' victory over those who tried to force their culture and religion on the Jews. This is called *pirsumei nisa* (פרסומי ניסא), "publicizing the miracle." Chabad.org's Chanukah laws describe lighting a menorah at a public outdoor gathering as a maximum of *pirsumei nisa*, which may be done with a blessing.

## How public menorahs began

Chabad.org traces public menorahs to the Rebbe's call in the winter of 1973 for greater Chanukah awareness: at a gathering on 20 Kislev 5734 he spoke of the special strength of the Chanukah lights, because a lit menorah sends literal light into the street. Soon after, students handed out menorahs on street corners. Chabad.org gives more than one account of the first public lighting (one points to Independence Mall in Philadelphia in 1974), so details differ. Chabad.org also tells of a 1975 lighting in San Francisco's Union Square, arranged by Rabbi Chaim Drizin, and by 1979 a giant menorah was lit outside the White House. Today thousands are lit around the world. Read about [the Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe) and the [mitzvah tanks](/learn/mitzvah-tanks) that carried the same spirit onto the streets.

## What to expect if you come

Chabad.org says a public lighting is open to everyone, and you are encouraged to invite neighbors and friends. Children are welcome, you do not need to bring anything, and there is no required dress code, though men may want a kippah. Dress warmly for outdoor events. Photos and videos are generally welcome, and sharing them spreads the light. Some events give out a menorah kit if you do not have one at home.

If an electric menorah is used, or if the lighting takes place after the proper time, the blessings may be skipped. Ask the rabbi.

## Bring the light home

Chabad.org is clear that attending a public lighting does not replace lighting at home with the usual blessings. Light your own menorah each night; see [how to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah) and the [night-by-night guide](/learn/chanukah-night-by-night).

## Common questions

### What is a public menorah lighting?
It is a community event where a large menorah is lit outdoors with blessings and songs, open to everyone. Many include music, doughnuts and latkes.

### Why does Chabad light big menorahs in public?
To publicize the miracle of Chanukah, a mitzvah called pirsumei nisa. Chabad.org traces the public lightings to the Rebbe's 1973 call for greater Chanukah awareness.

### Can anyone attend a menorah lighting?
Yes. Chabad.org says it is open to all, and children are welcome. You do not need to bring anything.

### Does attending a public menorah lighting replace lighting at home?
No. Chabad.org says you should still light a menorah at home with the usual blessings.
