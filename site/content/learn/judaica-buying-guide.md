A Jewish home is built from mitzvahs, not from objects. Still, the right items make mitzvahs easier and more beautiful, and many become family heirlooms. Here are the essentials, and what matters when you buy them.

## A word before you shop

Judaism teaches hiddur mitzvah, making a mitzvah beautiful. That means choosing items with care and love, not necessarily spending a lot. A simple Kiddush cup used every Friday night is worth more than a silver one kept in a cabinet. Buy what you will use.

## For Shabbat

- **Candlesticks.** At least two, for the [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles). Many women add a candle for each child. Girls often light their own single candle. Choose candlesticks that are stable and easy to clean.
- **Kiddush cup.** It should hold at least a revi'it, roughly 3 to 5 ounces depending on the opinion (ask the Rabbi which to follow). See [Kiddush](/learn/kiddush).
- **Challah board, cover and knife.** Two challahs sit on a board under a cover during Kiddush. A cover can be simple or embroidered.
- **Havdalah set.** A braided candle with at least two wicks, a cup, and a spice box. See [Havdalah](/learn/havdalah).

## For the holidays

- **Chanukah menorah.** Its eight lights should be in a straight row, at the same height, with the shamash set apart. See [The Chanukah Menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah).
- **Seder plate and matzah cover** for Passover. See [The Seder Plate](/learn/seder-plate).
- **A shofar** is usually heard at the synagogue, but some families own one.

## Every day

- **Mezuzahs** on the doorways. The most important thing is a kosher handwritten scroll, not the case. See [Choosing a Mezuzah](/learn/choosing-a-mezuzah).
- **A tzedakah box** (pushka) in the kitchen, so the whole family can give a coin each weekday. See [Pushka](/learn/pushka).
- **Washing cup** for [netilat yadayim](/learn/netilat-yadayim), often with two handles, used in the morning and before bread.

## Books

- **A siddur** (prayer book), ideally with English or your own language next to the Hebrew. See [The Siddur](/learn/siddur).
- **A Chumash,** the Five Books of Moses, to follow the weekly Torah portion.
- **Books for children,** so that Jewish stories are part of bedtime.

See [A Jewish Home Library](/learn/jewish-home-library) for more ideas.

## Gifts for life events

- **New baby:** a pushka, or a [letter in a Torah](/learn/letter-in-a-torah).
- **Bar or bat mitzvah:** tefillin (for a boy, through the Rabbi), Shabbat candlesticks (for a girl), or a personal siddur.
- **Wedding:** candlesticks, a Kiddush cup or a challah board.
- **New home:** a kosher mezuzah for the front door.

More ideas are in [Jewish Gift Ideas](/learn/jewish-gift-ideas).

## Common questions

### Does it matter what the items are made of?
For most items, no. Silver, glass, wood or ceramic are all fine. What matters is that they work for the mitzvah, like a Kiddush cup big enough, or a menorah with lights in a row. Metal and glass items used for food, such as a Kiddush cup, usually need [immersion in a mikvah](/learn/tevilat-kelim) before first use; ask the Rabbi.

### Where should I buy Judaica?
Anywhere you like for candlesticks, cups and boards. For mezuzahs and tefillin, buy only from a reliable source or ask the Rabbi.

### We are just starting out. What should we buy first?
Shabbat candlesticks, a kosher mezuzah for the front door, and a tzedakah box. Add the rest as you go.
