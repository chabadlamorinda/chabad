On the first night of Chanukah you light one candle (plus the helper candle, the shamash), and every night you add one more until eight burn on the last night. Each night is a chance to add light, with the blessings, a song, a little gelt for the children and one new idea to talk about.

## The basic rules for every night

Here is what stays the same all eight nights, according to Chabad.org:

- **Add one light each night.** One on the first night, two on the second, and so on. The shamash ("servant") is the extra light used to kindle the others, so it is not counted.
- **Place the new light on the left.** The first night's candle is at the right. Each night you add a candle to the left of the others and light the newest one first, moving from left to right.
- **Say the blessings before lighting.** Three on the first night, two on every other night, then sing Haneirot Halalu. See [how to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah).
- **Let the lights burn about half an hour after nightfall,** and sit near them. The light is for looking at, not for using.

## Night by night: an idea for each candle

Chabad.org offers a thought for every night. Here are the themes, retold simply, with a small mitzvah to match.

1. **Night 1, Light.** The Torah opens with "Let there be light." Chanukah light is more than daylight; it is warmth and purpose. *Try:* switch off the room lights and just watch one flame together.
2. **Night 2, Two great lights.** The sun never changes, while the moon waxes and wanes. We are sometimes steady, sometimes not, and both can shine. *Try:* call a relative you have not spoken to in a while.
3. **Night 3, The lamp.** Our enemies tried to defile the pure oil of the Temple menorah, an attack on the Jewish soul itself. *Try:* look at the [oil and candles](/learn/menorah-oil-and-candles) guide and pick something special for your menorah.
4. **Night 4, Lighting the dark.** Shabbat candles shine indoors, but Chanukah candles are meant to shine into the darkness outside. *Try:* put the menorah in a window facing the street.
5. **Night 5, Increasing the light.** The candles grow every night, and we are asked to add a little in our own lives every day. *Try:* take on one new good deed and keep it.
6. **Night 6, Miracles for today.** The menorah is not only a reminder of ancient miracles. *Try:* tell your family about a small "miracle" in your life this year.
7. **Night 7, Spreading the light.** Our task is to bring light to others. *Try:* invite a neighbor or someone alone to see the candles.
8. **Night 8, The infinite light.** The Temple menorah lit the natural world; Chanukah points beyond it. *Try:* give tzedakah in the name of the whole family.

## Friday night and Saturday night

Because fire is not lit on Shabbat, the Friday night menorah is lit **before** the Shabbat candles, using enough oil or large candles to burn for the half hour; ordinary thin candles will not last. Chanukah candles are not moved or relit during Shabbat. Candle-lighting times for every Friday are on our [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times) page.

The Saturday night menorah is lit only after Shabbat ends, after Havdalah. Plan to light it a little later than on other nights. For the full picture see [Chanukah on Shabbat](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat).

## Common questions

### How many candles do you light on each night of Chanukah?
You light one candle on the first night and add one each night until there are eight, not counting the shamash. So the eighth night has eight candles plus the shamash.

### Which side do you start on?
The first candle goes on the right and each new one is added to the left. You light the newest candle first, then continue left to right.

### Do you light the menorah before or after the Shabbat candles?
On Friday afternoon you light the Chanukah menorah first, then the Shabbat candles. On Saturday night you light it after Shabbat ends.

### Can the menorah be electric?
Chabad.org says gas or electric lights do not fulfill the mitzvah; use oil with cotton wicks, or wax candles. An electric menorah can be used for decoration if a real flame is also lit.
