On the fifth of the Hebrew month of Tevet, **Hey Teves**, Chabad Chassidim around the world celebrate a day called **Didan Notzach**, "our side has won." It marks the day in 1987 when a United States federal court ruled that the great library of the Chabad Rebbes belongs to the Chabad community, and not to any one person. Today it is celebrated in a simple and beautiful way: by buying new Jewish books and learning from them.

## What happened

The sixth Chabad Rebbe, Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn, gathered a precious library of tens of thousands of books and manuscripts, much of it rescued from Europe. After his passing, the library stayed at Chabad headquarters at 770 Eastern Parkway in Brooklyn. In 1985 a family member took many of the books, claiming them as an inheritance. Agudas Chassidei Chabad, the central Chabad organization, went to court.

The Lubavitcher [Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe) explained that the question was not really about property. Rebbetzin Chaya Mushka, the Previous Rebbe's daughter, said it simply in her testimony: her father and his books belonged to the Chassidim. On 5 Tevet 5747, January 1987, the court agreed, and the books returned to the library. An appeal later confirmed the ruling.

## Why a court case became a holiday

The Rebbe taught that the victory was really a victory of the books themselves, of Torah study. The way to celebrate is to give the books what they "want": to be opened and learned. So on Hey Teves people:

- **Buy new Jewish books** (seforim), for themselves and for their children, and start learning from them that day.
- **Add to the home library**, so that a home is filled with Torah. See [building a Jewish home library](/learn/jewish-home-library).
- **Gather and farbreng**, sharing stories and a l'chaim.
- **Take good care of Jewish books**: putting them away respectfully, not leaving them open or on the floor.

## Common questions

### When is Hey Teves this year?
It is on the fifth day of Tevet, usually in late December or early January. See the [Jewish calendar](/calendar).

### Does the book have to be in Hebrew?
No. Any book of Torah counts: a prayer book, a Chumash with English or Russian translation, a book on the holidays, a children's Jewish book. The point is to learn from it.

### What is a good first book?
A [siddur](/learn/siddur), a Chumash with commentary, the [Tanya](/learn/tanya), or a good book on the weekly parsha are all wonderful starts. For a child, a picture book about a Jewish holiday or a mitzvah. Ask the Rabbi for a suggestion.
