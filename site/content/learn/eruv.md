An eruv is a symbolic boundary, often made of wires, poles and existing walls, that encloses an area so that carrying is permitted there on Shabbat. Without an eruv, Jewish law forbids carrying anything from a home into a public area, or within a public area.

## Why carrying is forbidden on Shabbat

Carrying from one domain to another is one of the [39 melachot](/learn/thirty-nine-melachot), the categories of work forbidden on Shabbat. The Torah treats a private area, like a home, and a public area, like a street, as separate domains. Moving objects between them, or carrying four cubits (about 6 feet) in a public area, is not allowed.

This includes keys, a phone, a tissue, a prayer book, or pushing a baby stroller.

## What an eruv does

The word eruv means "mixing" or "combining." An eruv combines many private and semi-public spaces into one shared private domain, by enclosing them with a continuous boundary.

An eruv is made of:

- **Existing walls and fences**, where they are high enough.
- **Doorway-like frames (tzurat hapetach)**, made of two poles and a wire or string across the top. Utility poles and wires are often used.
- **Natural boundaries**, like hills or bodies of water, in some cases.

There is also a symbolic food element, called eruv chatzerot, which combines the households into one community.

An eruv does not work in an area that is a true public domain according to Torah law, such as a very wide and busy thoroughfare. The rules are complex, and building an eruv requires an expert rabbi.

## How to know if the eruv is up

1. **Find out whether there is an eruv** in your area and its exact boundaries. Ask the Rabbi.
2. **Check each week.** Wires break and poles fall. Most eruvin have a weekly status update, usually by Friday afternoon.
3. **If it is down**, do not carry outside your home or private yard on that Shabbat.

## What you can do without an eruv

- Carry inside your own home, and in a fully enclosed private yard.
- Wear clothing, jewelry and a tallit, since these are worn, not carried.
- Some people use a special belt or tie clip with a key built in, worn as part of the clothing. Ask the Rabbi before relying on this.
- Leave things at the synagogue before Shabbat so you do not need to carry them.

## Why it matters for families

An eruv makes Shabbat much easier for families with young children. Parents can push strollers, carry babies, and bring food to friends. Many Jewish communities treat building an eruv as a major community project.

## Common questions

### Does an eruv allow other kinds of work?
No. An eruv only affects carrying. All other Shabbat laws remain the same.

### Can I carry my house key without an eruv?
Not in the usual way. Some people leave the door unlocked, use a combination lock, or wear a special key-belt. Ask the Rabbi about what works for you.

### Is there an eruv in Lamorinda?
Eruv coverage varies by neighborhood and can change. Call us before relying on any eruv here.

### Does an eruv apply on Yom Kippur?
Yes. Yom Kippur has the same carrying laws as Shabbat, so the eruv applies. On other holidays, carrying is generally permitted for a holiday need even without an eruv.
