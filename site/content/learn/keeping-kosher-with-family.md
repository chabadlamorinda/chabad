Many people start keeping kosher while their parents, in-laws, siblings or even spouse do not. It can feel lonely, and family meals can become tense. With patience and some planning, keeping kosher can actually bring families closer, and it never needs to cost you your relationships.

## Start with love, not lectures

Your family may worry that you are judging them or pulling away. Tell them the opposite is true: this is something meaningful to you, and you want to keep being part of every family dinner. Judaism teaches that [honoring parents](/learn/honoring-parents) is one of the Ten Commandments; keeping kosher is never a reason to be unkind. You are not asking them to change, only to help you be there.

## Eating at a family meal

- **Bring food you can eat**, enough to share. A kosher dish that everyone enjoys turns "your food" into "our food."
- **Use disposable plates and cutlery** for yourself, or bring your own.
- **Packaged kosher food** with a reliable symbol (see [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols)) is a simple, stress-free option.
- **Raw fruit and vegetables**, sealed drinks and many packaged snacks are often fine even in a non-kosher kitchen. Food cooked in a non-kosher kitchen or oven usually is not; ask the Rabbi about a specific case.
- **Order kosher food** for the whole family for a birthday or holiday meal. Many families come to love it.

## Holidays

For a Passover Seder, Rosh Hashanah or Thanksgiving, offer to host, bring a full kosher meal, or ask the Rabbi about kosher catering. A sealed, double-wrapped dish can often be heated in a non-kosher oven; ask how.

## When you host

A kosher home is a gift you can share. Invite your family for [Shabbat](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners): a beautiful meal, candles, challah and wine, with no pressure. Many relatives who were nervous about "keeping kosher" become your best guests.

## If your spouse does not keep kosher

This takes extra care and patience from both of you. Talk openly, go step by step, and keep [shalom bayit](/learn/shalom-bayit), peace at home, above all. Many couples find a path together. The Rabbi and the Rebbetzin are glad to talk with you, privately.

## Common questions

### Am I being disrespectful to my parents by not eating their food?
Not if you do it with warmth. Explain that it is about your own commitment, not about them, bring something delicious to share, and keep coming to every family meal.

### Can I eat a salad in a non-kosher restaurant or home?
It depends on how it was prepared and with what. Raw, washed vegetables are often fine, but dressings, cut surfaces and utensils can be issues. Ask the Rabbi for practical guidance.

### What about the family dog bowl, the dishwasher, the microwave?
Kitchen questions have practical answers. See [keeping a kosher kitchen](/learn/kosher-kitchen), or ask the Rabbi for your situation.
