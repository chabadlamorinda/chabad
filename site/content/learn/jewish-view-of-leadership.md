The Torah teaches that a leader is a servant: in Judaism, to lead is to serve. Rabbi Jonathan Sacks writes that Moses, the greatest leader in Jewish history, is also the most humble, and that the Torah calls him "the servant of the L-rd." Leadership is not a ladder to stand above others. It is a duty to the people and to G-d.

## Why we need leaders

Leadership is necessary: orchestras need conductors, teams need captains, armies need generals and nations need governments. The Book of Judges shows how chaotic society becomes without leadership. Judaism does not ask for no leaders. It asks what kind of leaders we need.

## Equal before G-d

The Torah stands in contrast to the ancient world of Mesopotamia and Egypt, whose ziggurats and pyramids pictured a king at the top, elites below and laborers at the base. This hierarchy was believed to be natural, so some people were born to rule and others to be ruled. Judaism disagrees. Every person is created in the image of G-d, so no one may rule another without consent. That is why Jewish leaders serve the people and G-d rather than standing above them.

## The lesson of Korach

In the Torah portion Korach, a man named Korach leads a revolt against Moses and Aaron. Korach's claims were partly true: the whole community is holy, and G-d dwells among them. His error was to say that Moses and Aaron set themselves above the congregation. The Torah's message, in Rabbi Sacks' reading, is that Korach projected his own ambition onto Moses and Aaron. He did not understand that in Judaism to lead is to serve. He was a demagogue seeking power for himself, not a democrat.

## The humblest leader

The Torah describes Moses as "very humble, more humble than anyone else." He is called "the servant of the L-rd" eighteen times in the Hebrew Bible, a title shared only by Joshua, who has it twice. See [Moses](/learn/moses) and [humility](/learn/humility).

## A king must stay humble

The king is the only person the Torah commands to be humble. He must keep a Torah scroll with him and not think himself better than his fellow Israelites. Maimonides teaches that although the king is greatly honored, he must be lowly of heart and gracious to all. He should protect even the humblest person's dignity, speak gently to the community and carry the nation's burdens like a nurse carries an infant. A leader who rules by fear, for personal glory instead of for Heaven, loses his share in the World to Come (Laws of Repentance 3:13). For another model of a leader, see [King David](/learn/king-david).

## When a leader makes a mistake

The Talmud (Berachot 27b-28a) tells that Rabban Gamliel, the head of the community, was removed from his post for acting high-handedly, until he acknowledged his fault and apologized. Elsewhere (Horayot 10a-b) he tells two sages he appointed to office that a position of leadership is a chance to serve, not an honor.

## Leading in your own life

Great leaders respect, honor and inspire others, and act from ideals rather than ambition. You may not run a nation, but if you lead a family, a team or a classroom, the same teaching can guide you.

## Common questions

### What does the Torah say about leadership?
Leadership is service. In a society where everyone has equal dignity before G-d, leaders serve the people and G-d.

### Why is Moses the model of a Jewish leader?
The Torah calls him "very humble, more humble than anyone else," and "the servant of the L-rd."

### What was wrong with Korach's complaint?
Korach said the whole community is holy, which is true, but he wanted power for himself and misread Moses as seeking to rule. In Judaism, to lead is to serve.

### What does Maimonides say about a king?
The king must be lowly of heart, gracious and gentle, protect the dignity of the humblest and carry the nation's burdens like a nurse with an infant.

### Can a leader admit mistakes?
Yes. The Talmud tells how Rabban Gamliel was removed from his post for acting high-handedly, until he acknowledged his fault and apologized.
