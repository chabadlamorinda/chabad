A Jewish headstone traditionally carries the deceased's Hebrew name, the Hebrew name of their father, and the Hebrew date and year of death, usually followed by the abbreviation תנצב"ה. It is made of stone or granite, with no carved human figure and no photograph, and the wording is checked with the Chevra Kadisha or a rabbi before it is ordered.

The stone is called a matzeivah (מצבה) or tzion (ציון).

## What the inscription includes

According to Jewish tradition, include:

1. **The Hebrew name** of the person who passed away.
2. **The Hebrew name of their father.** If you are not sure of the names, ask the Rabbi. See [Hebrew names](/learn/hebrew-names).
3. **The Hebrew date and year of death.**
4. **The abbreviation תנצב"ה**, which expresses the wish that the soul be "bound in the bond of life."

Some people also add the community where the person lived, or the tzaddik or sage they followed. Some add the letters פ"נ at the top, meaning "here is buried."

## Wording and language

- **Hebrew only.** Jewish tradition advises using only Hebrew.
- **Avoid extravagant praise.** Some believe the soul is held accountable for what is written, so keep the words simple and truthful.
- **Check before ordering.** Review the text with the Chevra Kadisha or a competent rabbi before it goes to the stonecutter. If you are near Lafayette, you can call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 and ask about it.

## What not to put on the stone

Jewish law is clear that carving or engraving the figure of a human being on a tombstone, and mounting photographs, is forbidden. The stone should not become a place for pictures of people.

## Material and appearance

Use stone or granite. Keep it similar to neighboring stones, so no family feels pressured to buy something more elaborate.

The stone goes at the head of the grave, and the plot is outlined with a low frame. Planting grass or flowers on the grave is discouraged.

## When is the stone set?

The timing differs among communities: the day after shiva, at the end of sheloshim (thirty days), or after twelve months. Follow your community's custom.

The unveiling is traditionally held within the first year after death, between the end of shiva and the yahrzeit. The rabbi should be called six or seven weeks ahead to set a date. See [unveiling](/learn/unveiling).

## Common questions

### What do you write on a Jewish headstone?
Write the Hebrew name, the Hebrew name of the father, and the Hebrew date and year of death, with תנצב"ה. Some add the community or the sage they followed.

### What does תנצב"ה mean?
It stands for "May his (or her) soul be bound in the bond of life." It is part of the standard inscription.

### Can I put a photograph or carving of the person on a Jewish headstone?
No. Carving or engraving a human figure and mounting photographs are forbidden.

### Can the inscription be in English?
Jewish tradition advises using only Hebrew. If you wish to add anything else, ask the Rabbi.

### When should a Jewish headstone be put up?
It depends on custom: the day after shiva, after thirty days, or after twelve months. Ask the Rabbi what is customary for your family. The unveiling is usually held within the first year.

### Who should check the Hebrew on a headstone?
The Chevra Kadisha or a competent rabbi, before the stone is ordered.
