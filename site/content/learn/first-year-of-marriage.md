The first year of marriage has a special name in Jewish tradition: shana rishona, "the first year." The Torah treats it as a protected year of joy, a time to build the foundation of a lifelong partnership.

## The Torah's year of joy

The Torah says: "When a man takes a new wife, he shall not go out with the army... he shall be free for his home for one year, and he shall bring joy to his wife whom he has taken" (Deuteronomy 24:5). In ancient Israel, a new husband was exempt from military service for a whole year.

The message is timeless. The first year is not a time to be distracted by everything else. It is a time to put the marriage first, to learn each other, and to make each other happy.

## Sheva Brachot: the first week

The celebration does not end at the wedding. For seven days, family and friends host festive meals for the couple, and when there is a minyan and a new guest, the seven wedding blessings are said again after the meal. See [Sheva Brachot](/learn/sheva-brachot). It is a week that surrounds the couple with love, and a reminder that a Jewish marriage is part of a whole community.

## Building habits that last

What you start in the first year tends to stay. Some good habits:

- **Shabbat together.** Light [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles), make Kiddush, and sit down to a real meal without phones. Decide together how you want your Shabbat to look. See [Shabbat Meals](/learn/shabbat-meals).
- **Family purity.** The monthly rhythm of [family purity](/learn/family-purity) brings renewal and closeness. Keep learning and keep asking questions.
- **Learning together.** Even ten minutes a week on the [weekly parsha](/learn/weekly-parsha) gives you something to talk about besides chores.
- **Tzedakah.** Put a [pushka](/learn/pushka) in the kitchen and give together.
- **A Jewish home.** Mezuzahs, books and a kosher kitchen. See [Setting Up a Jewish Home](/learn/setting-up-a-jewish-home).

## Talking well

Two people from two families bring two sets of expectations. Disagreements are normal. What matters is how you handle them:

- **Speak gently,** and never in front of others.
- **Listen first.** Try to understand before trying to be understood.
- **Say thank you** often, for small things.
- **Do not keep score.** Marriage is not fifty-fifty; each gives fully.

See [Shalom Bayit](/learn/shalom-bayit) and [Marriage Arguments: Jewish Wisdom](/learn/marriage-arguments-jewish-wisdom).

## In-laws and family

The Torah says a man "leaves his father and mother and cleaves to his wife." A new couple remains close to and honors their parents, but they now make their own decisions together. Be generous with both families, divide holidays fairly, and never complain about your spouse to your parents. See [In-Laws](/learn/in-laws).

## Common questions

### Is the first year supposed to be easy?
Not always. Adjusting to life together takes work. The Torah's point is that the first year deserves special attention, so problems can be solved while they are small.

### Should we avoid traveling apart in the first year?
Many couples try to spend as much time together as possible in shana rishona, in the spirit of the Torah's verse. Every situation is different; ask the Rabbi if a question comes up.

### We did not grow up observant. Where do we start?
Start with one thing you can do together, like Friday night candles and dinner. Build from there, step by step.
