On Shabbat afternoon you can nap, take a calm walk, learn Torah, play a simple game, visit friends, pray the afternoon service and enjoy a light third meal. Jewish tradition describes this as time to rest, study and spend with family, a stretch of the day that is not about doing but about being.

For families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, the long quiet hours after lunch can feel strange at first, with phones put away. Here are ideas to fill them with calm and joy.

## Rest first

The afternoon follows the morning service and the Shabbat lunch. Jewish tradition lists a nap as a classic Shabbat pleasure, along with study. Resting on Shabbat is not laziness; it is part of the day, a *menuchah* (מְנוּחָה), rest of body and soul. Let yourself sleep, read or sit.

## Learn something

The tradition suggests Torah study, or attending a nearby class, as a natural way to spend the afternoon. Some ideas:

- Read the week's Torah portion with your children; see [the parsha](/parsha) and [weekly parsha](/learn/weekly-parsha).
- Read a Jewish book, stories or a short [dvar Torah](/learn/dvar-torah-at-the-shabbat-table).
- Between Passover and Rosh Hashanah, jewish tradition says to study a chapter of *Pirkei Avot* (Ethics of the Fathers) after the afternoon prayer. Ask the Rabbi about the custom.
- Read a story from [Jewish stories](/stories) aloud.

## Go outside, calmly

The tradition encourages walking within walking distance of home, to synagogue, to friends or to a park. Remember not to carry outside an eruv, and to keep a calm pace. Our guide to [Shabbat walks in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda](/learn/shabbat-walks-in-lamorinda) covers the rules and a few ideas, so we do not repeat them here; ask the Rabbi if you are unsure about your route.

## Games and play for children

The tradition suggests offering non-electronic games or toys so young children are happy at the table, and the same idea works all afternoon. Good choices:

- Books and playing with dolls or stuffed animals.
- Storytelling, a family sing-along, and guessing games.
- Running around the yard, within reason, or a calm game outside.

Some activities are not for Shabbat, such as writing, drawing and playing a musical instrument, which jewish tradition lists among the things avoided. For particular games (puzzles, board games, building sets, ball games), the answers depend on the details, so ask the Rabbi. More ideas are in [Shabbat with kids](/learn/shabbat-with-kids).

## Visit friends and invite guests

Shabbat afternoon is a perfect time to walk to a friend's home. Jewish tradition lists visiting friends among the ways to spend the day. You can also invite people for the third meal or for a casual cup of tea ready before Shabbat. See [hachnasat orchim](/learn/hachnasat-orchim) for the mitzvah of welcoming guests.

## Mincha and the third meal

The tradition describes the afternoon prayer, Mincha, followed by a light third meal between Mincha and Maariv, the evening prayer. Quiet songs and a Torah thought suit this meal as Shabbat winds down. See [Seudah Shlishit](/learn/seudah-shlishit) and [Havdalah](/learn/havdalah), which ends Shabbat. Check [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times) for when Shabbat ends.

## Common questions

### What can you do on Shabbat afternoon?
Nap, read, study Torah, take a calm walk, play simple games, visit friends and eat a light third meal. Jewish tradition lists all of these.

### What are good Shabbat games for kids?
Books, storytelling and singing are common. Writing and drawing are avoided, so ask the Rabbi about any specific game.

### Can children play outside on Shabbat?
Yes, calmly. Simple play like tag is fine, but carrying outside an eruv is not. See [Shabbat walks](/learn/shabbat-walks-in-lamorinda).

### Is it okay to take a nap on Shabbat?
Yes. The tradition lists a nap among the pleasures of Shabbat afternoon.

### Can I visit friends on Shabbat?
Yes, if you can walk there and are within the Shabbat boundary. The tradition lists visiting friends among the ways to spend Shabbat. See [Techum Shabbat](/learn/techum-shabbat).
