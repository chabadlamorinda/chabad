Grandparents are the keepers of Chanukah memories. Whether your grandchildren sit next to you at the menorah or live far away, you can pass on the tradition by lighting, singing, telling stories, giving gelt and showing them what Chanukah meant in your own life.

## Why grandparents matter on Chanukah

Chanukah is a holiday of passing a flame from one generation to the next. The shamash, the helper candle, lights each of the others and loses nothing, and that is a picture of grandparents: you give your light and it grows brighter. A Chanukah companion for the eight nights offers a thought, a story and an activity for each night, which is exactly the kind of thing a grandparent can do with a child.

If you are near, you can simply be present. If you are far, the next sections can help.

## When your grandchildren are with you

1. **Let them help.** A small child can hold the shamash with your hand over theirs, place a candle or choose the song.
2. **Tell your story.** Where you lit a menorah as a child, which songs you sang, what your own grandmother cooked. Children treasure these. Short and true is better than long.
3. **Give a little gelt,** and a few coins for the tzedakah box. See [Chanukah gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt).
4. **Make latkes together.** See [Chanukah foods](/learn/chanukah-foods).
5. **Play dreidel.** Even a small child can spin. See [dreidel](/learn/dreidel).
6. **Bless each grandchild.** Put a hand on their head and give a blessing, as many do on Friday night. See [blessing your children](/learn/blessing-your-children).
7. **Keep it safe.** With grandchildren in the house, place the menorah on a fireproof surface, out of reach, and stay nearby. See [menorah fire safety](/learn/menorah-fire-safety).

## When your grandchildren live far away

Distance cannot take away the connection. A few ideas, with one important point about the mitzvah itself.

- **Each family lights its own menorah.** Every household lights at the right time where it lives, in its own home. A video call is a lovely way to share the moment, but it does not replace lighting the menorah yourself. If you have questions about how to coordinate, or about Friday night and Saturday night, ask the Rabbi. On Shabbat, calls are not made, so arrange your visits in advance.
- **Call before or after the lighting.** Sing a song together, show the menorah and hear about their day.
- **Send a "Chanukah box."** A small menorah, candles, a dreidel, gelt and a note with a story or blessing.
- **Mail a card or a recorded story.** A short video of you telling the Chanukah story becomes a keepsake.
- **Send a gift of tzedakah.** Give in their name and tell them where it went.
- **Read a story over the phone** each night, from [Chanukah bedtime stories](/learn/chanukah-bedtime-stories).

For more on staying close from a distance, see [grandparenting from afar](/learn/grandparenting-from-afar) and [Jewish grandparents](/learn/jewish-grandparents).

## If you cannot light a flame yourself

Some grandparents live alone, in a care facility or have trouble lighting a match. Sturdy tea lights on a fireproof plate are a simpler option. If a flame is not possible, ask the Rabbi. For help for seniors and families, see [help for seniors and families](/help/help-for-seniors-and-families). More ideas for older adults are in [Jewish seniors in Lamorinda](/learn/jewish-seniors-in-lamorinda).

## Common questions

### How can grandparents celebrate Chanukah with grandchildren?
Light together, tell your own Chanukah stories, make latkes, play dreidel and give gelt and a blessing. Even a short, warm time is remembered.

### Can we light the menorah together over a video call?
Each household should light its own menorah in its own home. A video call is lovely for songs and stories, but it does not replace lighting. Ask the Rabbi about your situation, especially on Friday night and Saturday night.

### What should grandparents give for Chanukah?
Gelt is the traditional gift. Many grandparents add a small gift, a book or time together, and a coin for tzedakah. See [Chanukah gifts](/learn/chanukah-gifts).

### How can I stay close if my grandchildren live far away?
Call around the lighting, send a Chanukah box, mail a card or a recorded story and tell them about the tzedakah you gave in their name. See [grandparenting from afar](/learn/grandparenting-from-afar).

### How do I pass on Jewish tradition to my grandchildren?
Share your own stories and customs, do the mitzvot together and show your joy. The example matters more than the lecture.
