Eishet Chayil, "A Woman of Valor," is a 22-verse poem from the end of the Book of Proverbs that is sung at home on Friday night before Kiddush. It praises the wise, kind and capable woman who builds a Jewish home, and it is often sung by a husband and children in honor of the woman of the house.

## Where it comes from

Eishet Chayil is Proverbs 31:10 to 31, written by King Solomon. Its 22 verses follow the order of the Hebrew alphabet, from alef to tav, one letter per verse. This alphabetical form suggests a complete praise, "from A to Z."

The Midrash teaches that Abraham first spoke these words in praise of Sarah. Over time, the custom spread, through the Kabbalists of Safed, of singing it every Friday night after [Shalom Aleichem](/learn/shalom-aleichem).

## What it says

The poem opens: **"Eishet chayil mi yimtza, v'rachok mipninim michrah."** "A woman of valor, who can find? Her worth is far beyond pearls."

It then describes a woman who:

- Is trusted fully by her husband.
- Works hard and wisely, managing her home and her business.
- Opens her hand to the poor and needy.
- Speaks with wisdom, and "the teaching of kindness is on her tongue."
- Is clothed in strength and dignity, and faces the future with confidence.
- Is praised by her children and husband.

It closes with the famous lines: "Grace is false and beauty is vain; a woman who fears G-d, she shall be praised."

## Deeper meanings

The Sages and Kabbalists read Eishet Chayil on several levels at once:

- **The Jewish woman.** First and simplest, it honors the wife and mother who builds the home. See [Jewish women](/learn/jewish-women).
- **Shabbat.** Shabbat is called a bride and a queen, and the poem can be read as praise of Shabbat itself.
- **The Torah.** Some read it as praise of the Torah, which is "more precious than pearls."
- **The Shechinah.** Kabbalists read it as a song to the Divine Presence.
- **The soul.** Chassidic teaching sees in it the soul, which works hard in this world and earns its reward.

## When and how to sing it

1. Return home Friday night and gather at the table.
2. Sing [Shalom Aleichem](/learn/shalom-aleichem).
3. Sing Eishet Chayil. Many sing it standing.
4. Make [Kiddush](/learn/kiddush) and begin the [Shabbat meal](/learn/shabbat-meals).

There are many tunes, some lively and some slow. Some families go around the table, each person reading a verse.

## Common questions

### Who sings Eishet Chayil?
Traditionally the husband sings it, often joined by the children, as a tribute to his wife. Anyone may sing it, and in many homes the whole family sings together.

### What if I am single or there is no woman at the table?
It is still sung. Because of its deeper meanings about Shabbat, the Torah and the soul, Eishet Chayil is said every Friday night by everyone, not only by married men.

### Is Eishet Chayil only for Friday night?
It is sung on Friday night, and it is also often read at a woman's funeral as a tribute to her life. Some brides and couples choose verses from it for their wedding.

### Why does it praise hard work and business?
Judaism sees everyday work, done honestly and with kindness, as holy. The woman of valor shows that wisdom, faith and practical skill go together.
