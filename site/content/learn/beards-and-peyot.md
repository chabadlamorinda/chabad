Some Jewish men grow beards and peyot (פֵּאוֹת, sidelocks) because of a verse in the Torah: "You shall not round off the corners of your head, and you shall not destroy the edge of your beard" (Leviticus 19:27). Peyot means "corners" or "edges," and refers to the hair near the ears.

Many men observe this commandment in different ways, and no one is judged by how he looks. Every Jew is welcome.

## What the Torah says

According to Chabad.org, the Talmud understands "the corners of your head" as the hair between the ears and the temples. This prohibition applies to men only. The Torah does not give a reason. Maimonides explains that shaving in this way was the practice of ancient idol-worshippers, and the Torah wished to separate Jews from it. Others say it is a *chok*, a commandment beyond human understanding.

## The razor rule in plain words

The Talmud (Makkot 20a) reads the verse's words "round off" and "destroy" as referring to a razor, the only tool at the time that removed hair completely. Many people therefore say that using a razor on the beard and the edges of the head is the main prohibition, while trimming with scissors is treated differently. Maimonides and the Sefer Hachinuch consider cutting the beard without shaving it forbidden but not punishable.

What about electric shavers? This is a matter of discussion among rabbis, and practice differs. If you have a personal question, ask the Rabbi.

## The mystical meaning of the beard

Chabad.org explains that in Kabbalah the beard has special meaning. It describes the beard's "thirteen locks" as representing G-d's Thirteen Attributes of Mercy, so growing it connects a person to divine compassion. Another Chabad.org article explains that hair grows from the head downward, linking the mind to action. A beard represents the bridge between knowing what is right and living it.

Eastern European Jews generally wore full beards until the mid-nineteenth century. When the Enlightenment spread, many Jews stopped, but Chassidim were less influenced and kept them, partly because of the Kabbalistic meaning.

## Peyot: short and long

Not every community wears peyot the same way. Chabad.org notes:

- **Chabad:** many, including Chabad, have the custom of trimming the peyot, so they are shorter and often tucked behind the ears.
- **Other Chassidic groups:** some never cut them, and some curl or wrap them.

Chabad's custom follows the Arizal (Rabbi Yitzchak Luria), who trimmed his peyot with scissors so that the hairs of the peyot and beard would not mix, because they correspond to different spiritual qualities. The Code of Jewish Law allows trimming with scissors, with care not to cut too close to the skin.

## The upsherin

The custom of leaving a boy's hair uncut until he is three, then cutting it with the peyot left, is the [upsherin](/learn/upsherin) (Yiddish for "to shear off"). It is a joyful celebration marking the start of the child's Torah education.

## A respectful word

A beard and sidecurls are only one way of expressing Jewish identity. Judaism teaches that every Jew matters regardless of appearance, and no one is "more Jewish" for how he looks. Other signs of Jewish life include the [kippah](/learn/kippah) and [tzitzit](/learn/tzitzit); read about [Jewish symbols](/learn/jewish-symbols) as well.

If you see a bearded man in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda, say hello. Most are happy to answer a question.

## Common questions

### Why do some Jewish men have beards?
They follow the verse in Leviticus 19:27 about not destroying the edge of the beard, and many also hold the Kabbalistic idea that the beard connects us to G-d's mercy. Chabad.org explains both.

### What are peyot and why do some Jewish men wear them?
Peyot are the sidelocks near the ears. The Torah says not to round off the corners of the head, and the Talmud identifies these corners as the hair between the ears and temples. This applies to men.

### Do all Jewish men have to grow a beard?
No. Practice varies among Jews, and no one is judged for his appearance. Many men follow the razor rule in different ways. Ask the Rabbi about your own custom.

### Can Jewish men shave?
According to the Talmud, a razor is the main concern. Opinions differ about electric shavers and trimming, so please ask the Rabbi.

### Do Chabad men have long sidecurls?
Chabad.org says that many, including Chabad, have the custom of trimming the peyot, following the Arizal. Other Chassidic groups do not cut theirs.

### What is an upsherin?
It is a boy's first haircut at age three, leaving the peyot, as a celebration of the start of his Jewish education. See the guide on the upsherin.
