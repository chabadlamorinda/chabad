Torah study gives a child far more than facts. It shapes character, builds a proud Jewish identity, sharpens the mind, gives a child confidence to ask big questions, and gives them a place where they belong. Jewish tradition has always put children's learning first, and Chabad teaches that every child, from every kind of home, deserves that gift.

## Why Torah learning starts with children

The Talmud says that as soon as a child can speak, a parent teaches the verse *"Torah tzivah lanu Moshe, morashah kehillat Yaakov"*, "The Torah that Moses commanded us is the inheritance of the congregation of Jacob" (Deuteronomy 33:4). The very first words a Jewish child learns say that the Torah is an *inheritance*: it already belongs to them, whatever their background.

The Talmud also teaches that the world exists in the merit of the breath of children learning Torah (Shabbat 119b). The sages say that schoolchildren's learning is not interrupted, not even to build the Holy Temple. That is how precious a child's learning is. See [chinuch, Jewish education](/learn/chinuch) and [the twelve Torah verses](/learn/twelve-torah-verses) that the Rebbe asked every child to know.

## Seven things Torah study gives a child

**1. Good character.** Torah is not only stories; it is a way of living. Children learn from Abraham's kindness to guests, from Moses' humility and from Joseph's forgiveness. Values become real people they can picture. See [Jewish values kids learn in Hebrew school](/learn/jewish-values-kids-learn-in-hebrew-school).

**2. A proud Jewish identity.** A child who knows the stories, the holidays and the Hebrew letters does not need to guess what being Jewish means. In Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, where many children are the only Jewish child in class, that confidence matters.

**3. Confidence to ask questions.** Jewish learning loves questions. The Passover Seder is built around a child's questions, and the sages say "the bashful cannot learn" (Ethics of the Fathers 2:5). A child who learns that questions are welcome grows into a confident thinker.

**4. A sharp mind.** Reading a new alphabet, following a story across generations and discussing "what would you do?" all exercise the mind. Torah learning has trained Jewish minds for thousands of years.

**5. A sense of meaning.** Children ask why they are here and whether what they do matters. Torah answers: every person was created for a purpose, and every good deed makes the world a little more holy.

**6. Belonging.** Learning together with other Jewish children, and celebrating Shabbat and the holidays together, gives a child a community that feels like family.

**7. A gift for life.** King Solomon wrote, "Educate a child according to his way; even when he grows old he will not depart from it" (Proverbs 22:6). What a child learns with joy stays with them as an adult.

## Joy makes it last

Children remember what they enjoy. That is why Torah for children should be fun, exciting and hands-on: songs, games, crafts, stories and celebrations, not only worksheets. When a child bakes for Shabbat, builds a model of the Holy Temple or wins a Hebrew reading level, the learning becomes part of them. See [hands-on Jewish learning for kids](/learn/hands-on-jewish-learning-for-kids).

## How the Rebbe saw Jewish education

The Lubavitcher Rebbe made Jewish education for every child one of his central campaigns. He taught that every Jewish child is precious, that no child should be left without Jewish learning, and that a little Torah given with warmth can change a whole life. That conviction is behind Chabad schools, camps and Hebrew schools around the world, including here in Lafayette. See [the Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe).

## Common questions

### What are the benefits of Torah study for children?
Torah study builds good character, a proud Jewish identity, confidence to ask questions, a sharp mind, a sense of meaning and a community to belong to. Learned with joy, it stays with a child for life.

### At what age should a child begin learning Torah?
Jewish tradition says as soon as a child can speak, beginning with the verse "Torah tzivah lanu Moshe" and the Shema. Lamorinda Hebrew School welcomes children from age 3.

### My child is not interested in religion. Will Torah study still help?
Yes. Children connect to stories, songs, crafts and friends long before they think about "religion". When learning is fun and hands-on, interest follows.

### Is once a week enough?
A weekly class gives structure, friends and a teacher. A few minutes at home during the week, like a blessing or a question about the parsha, make it stick. See [Jewish learning at home with kids](/learn/jewish-learning-at-home-with-kids).
