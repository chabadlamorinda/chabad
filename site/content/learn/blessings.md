A bracha (blessing) is a short prayer Jews say before and after eating, before doing a mitzvah, and at special moments in life. Saying a blessing turns an ordinary act, like eating an apple, into a moment of connection with G-d.

## What a bracha is

Bracha (בְּרָכָה, plural brachot) means "blessing." The Talmud teaches that everything in the world belongs to G-d, and saying a blessing is like asking permission before we enjoy it. Chassidic teaching adds that the word bracha is related to "drawing down": each blessing draws G-dly energy into the food and into us. King David instituted saying 100 blessings each day, and many people make that a daily goal.

## How a blessing begins

Most blessings start with the same six words:

**Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam...**
"Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe..."

Then comes the ending for the specific food or mitzvah. Those listening answer "Amen," meaning "I agree, it is true."

## The six blessings before eating

1. **Bread** (made from wheat, barley, rye, oats or spelt): ...hamotzi lechem min ha'aretz. "...who brings forth bread from the earth." Wash hands first. See [challah](/learn/challah).
2. **Grain foods** like cake, cookies, crackers, pasta and cereal from the five grains: ...borei minei m'zonot. "...who creates various kinds of nourishment."
3. **Wine and grape juice:** ...borei p'ri hagafen. "...who creates the fruit of the vine."
4. **Fruit from trees** like apples, oranges, grapes, olives and nuts: ...borei p'ri ha'etz. "...who creates the fruit of the tree."
5. **Food that grows from the ground** like vegetables, potatoes, bananas and strawberries: ...borei p'ri ha'adamah. "...who creates the fruit of the earth."
6. **Everything else**, including meat, fish, eggs, dairy, drinks and candy: ...shehakol nih'yeh bidvaro. "...by whose word all things came to be."

Some foods are tricky, such as mixtures, rice, and fruits that are processed. When in doubt, ask the Rabbi. Shehakol, when said after the fact, covers almost everything.

## The blessings after eating

- **Birkat Hamazon** (Grace After Meals): after eating at least an olive-sized piece of bread (roughly one ounce). It is said from a siddur or bentcher.
- **Al Hamichyah** (a shorter three-in-one blessing): after grain foods (mezonot), wine or grape juice, or the special fruits of the Land of Israel: grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives and dates.
- **Borei Nefashot:** after all other foods and drinks.

An after-blessing is said when you ate at least an olive-sized amount (about one ounce) or drank about a revi'it (roughly 3 ounces or more), within a short time. Ask the Rabbi for exact amounts.

## Shehecheyanu: the blessing for new things

This blessing thanks G-d for bringing us to a special moment. It is said on eating a new seasonal fruit for the first time that year, wearing a significant new garment, on the first night of most holidays, and other joyous milestones.

**Transliteration:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, shehecheyanu v'kiy'manu v'higiyanu lazman hazeh.

**Translation:** Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has granted us life, sustained us, and brought us to this time.

## Common questions

### What blessing do I say on coffee or water?
Shehakol. When drinking water only to quench your thirst, you say Shehakol. If you drink just to swallow a pill, no blessing is said.

### What if I already started eating and forgot the blessing?
If food is still left, say the blessing and then continue eating. If you have finished, say the after-blessing if you ate enough.

### Do I need to say a blessing in Hebrew?
Blessings may be said in any language you understand, as long as you include G-d's name and kingship. Hebrew is ideal, and the blessings are short and easy to learn.

### Which blessing comes first if I have several foods?
There is an order of priority, generally: hamotzi, mezonot, hagafen, ha'etz, ha'adamah, shehakol. If you eat bread at a meal, the hamotzi usually covers everything eaten as part of the meal. Ask the Rabbi for the details.
