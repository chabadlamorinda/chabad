The twelve tribes of Israel descend from the twelve sons of Jacob: Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun, Dan, Naphtali, Gad, Asher, Joseph and Benjamin. Today almost every Jew is a member of the Jewish people, and a Jewish family often knows only whether it is Kohen, Levi or Yisrael.

## The twelve sons and their mothers

[Jacob](/learn/jacob-our-father) had four wives in the Torah's account, and the sons came from them:

- **Leah:** Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar and Zebulun (and a daughter, Dinah).
- **Rachel:** Joseph and Benjamin.
- **Bilhah, Rachel's maidservant:** Dan and Naphtali.
- **Zilpah, Leah's maidservant:** Gad and Asher.

Joseph's two sons, Ephraim and Menashe, each became a tribe, which is how the count stays at twelve even though Levi received no land share.

## The blessings of Jacob

Before he died, Jacob blessed each son, and the Torah says each received the blessing fitting for him (Genesis 49). Chabad teaching, following Rashi, explains that every tribe received all the blessings, with its own special quality as the strongest. Judah was given the strength of a lion, Benjamin the hunting power of a wolf, and Naphtali the swiftness of a deer. Some tribes excelled in business or charity, others in Torah study. The Jewish people needs all of them, [like a family](/learn/israel-and-the-jewish-people) with different gifts.

## Levi and the Kohanim

The tribe of Levi was set apart to serve in the Tabernacle and later the [Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple). Aaron's family, from within Levi, are the Kohanim, the priests. Even today a Kohen blesses the congregation with the [priestly blessing](/learn/birkat-kohanim), a Levi has honors such as being called second to the Torah, and the rest are Yisraelim. See [Kohen, Levi, Yisrael](/learn/kohen-levi-yisrael).

## Why we are called "Jews"

The word Jew, Yehudi (יְהוּדִי), comes from Judah (Yehuda). After the kingdom split and the ten northern tribes were exiled, the people who remained were known as Yehudim, which became the name of the whole nation. But the Jewish people did not come only from Judah and Benjamin and half of Menashe. Chabad.org explains that a tenth of the ten tribes stayed in the Land, and others, gathered by the prophet Jeremiah, were brought back and joined the Jewish nation.

## What about the ten lost tribes?

Some of the exiled tribes are considered lost, mixed among the nations. The Talmud (Sanhedrin 110b) records a disagreement: Rabbi Akiva said they will not return, Rabbi Eliezer said G-d will bring them out of their darkness, and Rabbi Shimon said it depends whether they repent. Ezekiel prophesied that Ephraim and Judah will again be one nation, and those who follow Rabbi Eliezer understand this to happen with Moshiach. Be careful of claims that a modern group is a "lost tribe"; for such questions, ask the Rabbi.

## How do I know my tribe?

Most families do not have a record of their tribe. The tradition we do have is Kohen, Levi or Yisrael, usually passed from father to child. If you do not know, ask your family and then ask the Rabbi. Families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda are always welcome to ask.

## Common questions

### What are the names of the 12 tribes of Israel?
They are Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun, Dan, Naphtali, Gad, Asher, Joseph (Ephraim and Menashe) and Benjamin.

### What tribe am I from?
Most Jews know only whether they are Kohen, Levi or Yisrael; the exact tribe is generally not known. Ask the Rabbi about your family tradition.

### Why are Jews called Jews?
The word comes from Judah (Yehuda), whose tribe's name came to describe the whole Jewish people.

### Are Jews descended only from the tribe of Judah?
No. Chabad.org explains that remnants of the other tribes also joined the Jewish people.

### What happened to the ten lost tribes?
They were exiled, and some were gathered back by Jeremiah while others are considered lost. Tradition speaks of their return in the time of Moshiach.

### Why is Levi not counted among the land-owning tribes?
Levi served in the Sanctuary and was supported by the others, while Joseph's two sons each became a tribe, keeping the number of land-owning tribes at twelve.
