The candles are lit, the room is dim, and for a moment everyone is quiet. Then someone starts to sing. Chanukah has its own songs, and they are part of the mitzvah: the half hour while the candles burn is meant for sitting together, singing and telling the story. Here are the songs you will hear in almost every Jewish home.

## Haneirot Halalu: "These lights"

Right after the blessings, as the candles are lit, we say or sing *Haneirot Halalu*, "These lights." The words are very old; they appear in the tractate Soferim, from the age of the Talmud. In Chabad custom it is said every night, beginning after the first candle is lit, while the rest are kindled.

The paragraph says in plain words why we are here: "We light these lights for the miracles, the wonders, the salvations and the battles You performed for our ancestors in those days, at this time, through Your holy priests." Then comes the heart of it: "These lights are holy, and we are not permitted to make use of them, only to look at them, in order to thank and praise Your great Name."

That is the whole law of the Chanukah lights in one sentence. We do not read by them or light another candle from them. We look at them, and we say thank you. See [how to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah) for the blessings themselves.

## Maoz Tzur: "Rock of my salvation"

*Maoz Tzur* is the song most Jews know by heart. It was written in Europe about 800 years ago; the author signed his name, Mordechai, in the first letters of its verses. The first verse is a prayer: "Mighty Rock of my salvation, it is fitting to praise You; restore my house of prayer, and there we will bring a thanksgiving offering." The verses then walk through Jewish history, one rescue at a time: Egypt, Babylon, Haman's plot in Persia, and finally the Greeks, "who defiled all the oils," until "from the one remaining flask a miracle was made for the roses," a poetic name for the Jewish people. The last verse asks G-d to bring the final redemption.

Many families sing only the first verse; some sing all six. The English "Rock of Ages" is a loose adaptation, not a translation. Haneirot Halalu comes first, right after the lighting, and Maoz Tzur is beloved everywhere; you are welcome to sing it. During Chanukah the prayers also add *Al Hanissim*, "For the miracles," a short retelling of the story that many sing at the table too; see [Al Hanissim and Hallel](/learn/al-hanissim-and-hallel).

## The children's songs

- **Sevivon Sov Sov Sov**, "Spin, dreidel, spin," a Hebrew song whose refrain is the dreidel's letters: "a great miracle happened there." See [the dreidel](/learn/dreidel).
- **I Have a Little Dreidel**, the English classic, with a Yiddish original.
- **Oh Chanukah, Oh Chanukah** (Yiddish *Oy Khanike*), which packs latkes, dreidels and the candles into one happy verse.
- **Mi Yemalel**, "Who can retell the mighty deeds of Israel?" with its answer: "In every generation a hero arises to save the people."

Chassidic families also sing *niggunim*, wordless melodies, by the candles; see [the niggun](/learn/niggun). You will find more songs and games at [the kids' corner](/kids).

## When the songs are sung on Shabbat

On Friday afternoon the menorah is lit before the Shabbat candles, so the singing moves into the Shabbat meal, where Chanukah songs join the [zemirot](/learn/zemirot). On Saturday night the menorah is lit after Shabbat ends; many families make Havdalah first and then light and sing. See [Chanukah on Shabbat](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat), and ask the Rabbi about the order in your home.

## Common questions

### Do I have to sing, or is it enough to light?
Lighting with the blessings is the mitzvah. The songs are a custom, but a precious one: they are how children learn the story and why the candles feel like a celebration and not a chore. Even one verse, hummed badly, counts.

### What if I do not know the Hebrew?
Read the English, or sing the melody without words. Haneirot Halalu and Maoz Tzur appear in every siddur with translation, and the children's songs are easy to find. Nobody at a Chabad menorah lighting is checking your pronunciation.

### Is there a special song for the eighth night?
No fixed one, but the eighth night, when all the lights burn together, is when families tend to sing the most. See [the last day of Chanukah](/learn/zot-chanukah).
