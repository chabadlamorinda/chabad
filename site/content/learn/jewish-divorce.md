A Jewish divorce is completed with a get, a document written according to Jewish law and given by the husband to the wife in the presence of a Beth Din, a rabbinical court. A civil divorce alone does not end a Jewish marriage, so a get is needed for either person to remarry according to Jewish law.

## Before divorce: help is available

Judaism treasures marriage and [shalom bayit](/learn/shalom-bayit), peace in the home. The Talmud says that when a couple divorces, "even the altar sheds tears." If your marriage is struggling, reaching out early for support can make a real difference. For a confidential conversation, visit [/help/shalom-bayit](/help/shalom-bayit).

At the same time, Judaism recognizes that sometimes a marriage cannot continue. Divorce is permitted, and the Torah gives a dignified way to end a marriage. Nobody going through it should feel judged.

## What a get is

The Torah describes a husband writing "a scroll of severance" and placing it in his wife's hand (Deuteronomy 24:1). This document is the get.

- It is **handwritten by a trained scribe** (sofer) specifically for this couple.
- It follows a precise, ancient text with the names of the husband and wife.
- It is **signed by two witnesses.**
- The entire process is supervised by a **Beth Din** of experienced rabbis.

## Why a get matters

Without a get, the couple remains married in the eyes of Jewish law, even after a civil divorce. If the woman later remarries without a get, there can be serious consequences for the new marriage and for children born from it. A get protects both people and allows each to move forward with a clean start.

## How the process works

1. **Contact a rabbi** who can refer you to a recognized Beth Din.
2. **The Beth Din meets** with the husband and wife, usually separately, to confirm details such as names and that the get is given freely.
3. **The scribe writes the get** in the presence of the Beth Din, and the witnesses sign.
4. **The husband gives the get** to the wife, or through an appointed agent if they cannot be in the same place.
5. **The Beth Din keeps the get** on file and gives each person a document confirming the divorce.

The process usually takes a few hours. The get must be given willingly, and the Beth Din handles it with discretion and respect.

## After the get

A divorced woman waits about three months before remarrying, according to Jewish law. A divorced man may remarry sooner. Divorce is a painful loss, and it is normal to need time to heal. Community, friends and a listening rabbi can help.

## Common questions

### Do I need a get if we only married civilly?
It depends. A civil-only marriage between two Jews may or may not require a get. Ask the Rabbi, who can look into your situation.

### What if my spouse refuses to give or accept a get?
This is a painful situation. Batei Din and rabbis work to help, and there are steps that can be taken. Please reach out to the Rabbi for guidance.

### Do we both have to be in the room?
Not necessarily. A get can be delivered through an agent appointed by the Beth Din, which is often helpful when spouses live far apart.

### Can a divorced person remarry in a Jewish wedding?
Yes, once a valid get has been given. There are a few special rules, such as for a Kohen, so speak with the Rabbi when planning a [Jewish wedding](/learn/jewish-wedding).
