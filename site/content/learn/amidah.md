The Amidah is the central prayer of every Jewish service, said silently while standing, facing Jerusalem. It is also called Shemoneh Esrei, "eighteen," for its original eighteen blessings, and in the Talmud simply Tefillah, "the prayer."

## What the Amidah is

Amidah means "standing." We stand before G-d like servants before a king, feet together, focused and still. The Sages compare it to a private audience with the King of kings.

The prayer was composed by the Men of the Great Assembly about 2,400 years ago. Later, in Yavneh, the Sages added a nineteenth blessing, but the name Shemoneh Esrei stuck.

## The three parts

The weekday Amidah has three sections:

1. **Praise (3 blessings).** We praise G-d as the G-d of our ancestors, the One who gives life, and the Holy One.
2. **Requests (13 blessings).** We ask for wisdom, repentance, forgiveness, redemption, healing, livelihood, the ingathering of exiles, justice, the rebuilding of Jerusalem, the coming of [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach) and that G-d hear our prayers.
3. **Thanks (3 blessings).** We ask G-d to accept our service, thank Him for His constant kindness, and ask for peace.

On Shabbat and holidays the middle requests are replaced by one blessing about the holiness of the day, so the Amidah has seven blessings.

## When it is said

- **Shacharit,** the morning prayer
- **Mincha,** the afternoon prayer
- **Maariv,** the evening prayer
- **Musaf,** an extra Amidah on Shabbat, holidays and Rosh Chodesh
- **Neilah,** a fifth Amidah at the close of Yom Kippur

## How to say the Amidah

1. **Face Jerusalem.** In the United States, this means facing east.
2. **Take three steps back, then three steps forward,** as if approaching the King.
3. **Stand with feet together,** like the angels.
4. **Say the words quietly,** loud enough for you to hear but not those near you. The Sages learned this from Chana, whose lips moved but her voice was not heard.
5. **Bow** at the beginning and end of the first blessing and at the beginning and end of the blessing of thanks.
6. **Do not interrupt** the Amidah to talk.
7. **At the end,** take three steps back, bow left, right and forward, and then stand in place briefly.

## The repetition

When there is a [minyan](/learn/minyan), the chazan repeats the Amidah aloud. During the repetition, the congregation says Kedushah, a passage echoing the angels' praise: "Holy, holy, holy."

## Common questions

### Can I add my own requests?
Yes. You can add personal requests in the relevant blessing, for example praying for someone sick in the blessing of healing, or in Shema Koleinu. Many also add personal words at the end, before stepping back.

### What if I do not know Hebrew?
You may pray the Amidah in any language you understand. Using a [siddur](/learn/siddur) with translation helps you follow along.

### Why do people sway during prayer?
Swaying, or shuckling, helps many people concentrate. The soul is compared to a flame, always reaching upward. It is a custom, not a requirement.

### What do I do if I am walking past someone praying the Amidah?
Avoid walking directly in front of someone saying the Amidah, within about four cubits (six feet or so). Wait or walk around if you can.
