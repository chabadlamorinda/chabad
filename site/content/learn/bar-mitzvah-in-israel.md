Many families celebrate a bar mitzvah at the Western Wall in Jerusalem, usually with a weekday Torah reading on a Monday or Thursday morning, followed by a family trip. Plan about a year ahead, book the Wall in advance, and keep preparing with your Rabbi at home.

## Why a Monday or Thursday?

Torah is read publicly on Monday and Thursday mornings, and on Rosh Chodesh, the first day of the Hebrew month. Chabad.org teaches that a bar mitzvah boy should try to have his first aliyah (being called to the Torah) on a Monday or Thursday morning, or at Minchah on Shabbat. The Western Wall Heritage Foundation, which runs the plaza, holds bar mitzvah ceremonies on the days when the Torah is read. Learn more in [Torah reading](/learn/torah-reading).

## Step by step

1. **Choose the date.** Your child's Hebrew birthday decides the bar or bat mitzvah date; see [the bar and bat mitzvah timeline](/learn/bar-bat-mitzvah-timeline). Then find the nearest Monday, Thursday or Rosh Chodesh that works for the family. Ask the Rabbi if anything is unclear.
2. **Register with the Wall.** The Western Wall Heritage Foundation asks families to register in advance. Check its official website for current steps.
3. **Start preparing at home.** Learn the blessings and the Torah reading. See [Bar mitzvah parsha](/learn/bar-mitzvah-parsha) and [Hebrew reading for adults](/learn/hebrew-reading-for-adults) for parents who want to learn too.
4. **Begin tefillin.** Chabad custom is for a boy to begin putting on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) about two months before his bar mitzvah, first without the blessing and then with it. A Chabad tefillin booth is staffed in daytime hours at the entrance to the men's section of the Wall.
5. **Plan the trip.** Read [Your first trip to Israel](/learn/first-trip-to-israel).

## A bat mitzvah in Israel

A girl becomes bat mitzvah at 12. Many families mark it in Israel with a family trip, prayer at the Wall, visits to holy places and a festive meal or a gathering with her friends and family. Customs for how a girl is called or what she reads differ between communities, so ask the Rabbi about what is right for your family. See [Bat mitzvah](/learn/bat-mitzvah).

## Still celebrate at home

A bar or bat mitzvah is a day for the whole community, and many families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda hold a celebration at home, before or after the trip, so that grandparents and friends who cannot travel can share the simcha. Read about [bar and bat mitzvah in Lamorinda](/learn/bar-bat-mitzvah-in-lamorinda).

## What to bring and ask

- Check what to wear and what the Wall's officials require.
- Bring a tallit and, for a boy, his tefillin.
- Ask the Rabbi for help with any halachic question.

## Common questions

### Can you have a bar mitzvah at the Western Wall?
Yes. The Western Wall Heritage Foundation holds bar mitzvah ceremonies on days when the Torah is read, and families register in advance.

### Which days can you have a bar mitzvah at the Western Wall?
Mondays, Thursdays and Rosh Chodesh, when the Torah is read at weekday morning services.

### How early should I plan a bar mitzvah in Israel?
About a year ahead, so there is time to book the Wall, plan travel and prepare your child.

### Can a girl have a bat mitzvah in Israel?
Yes, families mark it in many ways. Customs differ, so ask the Rabbi what suits your family.

### When does a boy start wearing tefillin?
Chabad custom is to begin about two months before his bar mitzvah, first without the blessing and then with it.

### Should we still celebrate at home?
Many families do. Call Chabad of Lamorinda at 925-384-7242 to plan preparation and a celebration.
