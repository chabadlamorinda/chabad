In Jewish thought, the number seven stands for the natural world, and the number eight stands for what is beyond nature. This is a teaching of the Lubavitcher Rebbe, and it explains why Chanukah has eight days and eight lights, when the Menorah of the Temple had only seven.

## Seven is nature, eight is beyond

The world was created in seven days, and the week repeats in a cycle of seven, which is why seven stands for the natural order. Eight is one step beyond it. Chabad.org teaches that this is why the Midrash connects the final redemption with the number eight, and why, according to the Rebbe, the harp played in the Temple had seven strings while the harp of the future will have eight.

You can see the same idea in our guide to [the Temple menorah and the Chanukah menorah](/learn/temple-menorah-and-chanukah-menorah). In the Temple, G-dliness was open, and seven lights were enough. Today there is more spiritual darkness, and so the Chanukah lights, eight of them, reach beyond nature to light up the world.

## Chanukah: a miracle beyond nature

Chanukah celebrates two miracles: a small group defeated a great army, and one flask of oil burned for eight days. Both are beyond nature. Rabbi Yehuda Shurpin writes on Chabad.org that the Jews could have used impure oil. The miracle of the pure oil was therefore not a necessity but a gift, a sign of G-d's love that is above reason and above nature. It is also one of the answers listed to why the holiday is eight days. Our guide on [why Chanukah is eight days](/learn/why-is-chanukah-eight-days) lists other answers, and [Which Chanukah miracle?](/learn/which-chanukah-miracle) explains the miracles.

## Brit milah on the eighth day

A baby boy is circumcised on the eighth day of his life. This too is a mitzvah that goes beyond nature: it is a covenant that perfects the body as nature made it. Chabad.org notes that the Greeks forbade circumcision, and that one explanation of the eight days of Chanukah ties them to it. Read [brit milah](/learn/brit-milah) to learn more about this covenant.

## Shemini Atzeret, the eighth day

The festival of Shemini Atzeret, "the eighth day of assembly," comes right after the seven days of Sukkot. On Chabad.org, Rabbi Yitzi Hurwitz connects the eight days of Chanukah to Sukkot and Shemini Atzeret. The Torah places the commandment of the Menorah right after the festival of Sukkot, including Shemini Atzeret, and the Sages saw this as a hint. During the first seven days of Sukkot we are surrounded by G-dly energy, but cannot fully take it in; on the eighth day we connect to a deeper bond with G-d, called the *yechidah*, and are able to absorb it. He describes this as a light that makes even the darkness shine. See [Shemini Atzeret](/learn/shemini-atzeret).

## Even in the dreidel

The four letters of the dreidel, nun, gimmel, hei and shin (נ, ג, ה, ש), stand for "A great miracle happened there." According to a Chabad.org teaching, their numerical value is 358, which is also the value of the word Mashiach (משיח). The teaching links this with the hope for the final redemption. For more on how letters carry numbers, see [gematria](/learn/gematria).

## What does this mean for us?

The Rebbe taught that Chanukah prepares us for the messianic era. Each night we add a light, and the Rebbe taught that this shows steady growth "from strength to strength." For the practical side, see [lessons of Chanukah](/learn/lessons-of-chanukah).

## Common questions

### Why is the number eight important in Judaism?
Chabad.org explains that seven stands for the natural order and eight for what is above nature. This is why Chanukah has eight lights, brit milah is on the eighth day and Shemini Atzeret is the eighth day.

### Why does the Chanukah menorah have eight branches when the Temple menorah had seven?
The Rebbe taught that seven is complete within nature, and eight reaches beyond it. Today's spiritual darkness needs a light beyond nature.

### What is the connection between Chanukah and brit milah?
Brit milah is on the eighth day, and the Greeks forbade it. One answer on Chabad.org ties the eight days of Chanukah to it.

### What is Shemini Atzeret and how does it relate to Chanukah?
It is the festival right after Sukkot, called "the eighth." Chabad.org notes that the Torah places the Menorah commandment right after Sukkot, including Shemini Atzeret.

### What does 358 have to do with Chanukah?
The letters of the dreidel add up to 358, the same as the word Mashiach, according to a Chabad.org teaching.
