A Jewish girl becomes a bat mitzvah on her 12th Hebrew birthday. From that day she is responsible for the mitzvot, and her choices carry real weight. The celebration is a chance to mark that milestone with something she will remember for years: not just a party, but a project and a mitzvah that are her own. See our guide to the [bat mitzvah](/learn/bat-mitzvah).

## Mitzvah projects

A mitzvah project lets a girl feel the joy of giving. Choose something that fits her interests:

- **Challah baking for others.** Bake [challah](/learn/challah) and bring it to new parents, mourners, seniors or someone who is sick. Learning to [separate challah](/learn/hafrashat-challah) adds a special mitzvah traditionally entrusted to Jewish women.
- **Visiting seniors.** Visit a grandparent's friends or a senior residence with songs, cards or a Shabbat treat. See [Bikur Cholim](/learn/bikur-cholim).
- **A tzedakah campaign.** Raise money for a cause she cares about, or give a portion of her gifts to [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).
- **Learning project.** Study the women of the Torah, such as Sarah, Rivkah, Rachel, Leah, Miriam, Devorah and Esther, and share what she learned.
- **Kindness challenge.** One act of [chesed](/learn/chesed) every day for a month, kept in a journal.

## Learning about women of the Torah

Many girls find it inspiring to choose one heroine and learn her story in depth. What did she do? What was hard for her? What can a girl learn from her today? This also makes a beautiful theme for her speech.

## A speech

Many girls share a short d'var Torah at their celebration. A simple outline: one idea from the Torah portion or from a woman of the Torah, what it means to her now that she is 12, and a heartfelt thank you to her parents and family. Five minutes is plenty.

## Lighting Shabbat candles

Lighting [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) is a mitzvah given especially to Jewish women and girls. In Chabad, girls light from a young age. If she has not yet started, her bat mitzvah is a wonderful time to begin, with her own candlesticks as a gift she will keep for life.

## The celebration

Celebrations can be large or simple: a Shabbat meal, a party with friends, or a gathering of women and girls. Some families add an activity everyone can do together, such as a challah bake. What matters most is the meaning behind it.

## Common questions

### At what age is a bat mitzvah?
Twelve, on her Hebrew birthday. She becomes a bat mitzvah on that day whether or not there is a celebration.

### Does a bat mitzvah girl need a mitzvah project?
It is not required, but it is a wonderful way to begin her Jewish adult life with giving. Many girls say it was the best part.

### Can we celebrate if we are not observant?
Yes. Every Jewish girl is welcome to celebrate her bat mitzvah, whatever her family's background.
