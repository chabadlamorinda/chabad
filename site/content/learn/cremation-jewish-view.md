Jewish tradition calls for burying the body in the earth and does not practice cremation. This is one of the oldest Jewish customs, rooted in the Torah and in a deep respect for the human body as the home of the soul.

This guide explains the traditional view and the reasons behind it. If your family is facing this question, or has already made a choice, please know that the Rabbi is here for you without judgment.

## What the Torah teaches

The Torah says, "You shall surely bury him" (Deuteronomy 21:23). From Abraham burying Sarah in the Cave of Machpelah to today, Jews have laid their loved ones to rest in the earth. G-d told Adam, "For dust you are, and to dust you shall return" (Genesis 3:19). Burial lets the body return to the earth naturally, in its own time.

## Why burial matters

- **The body is holy.** Judaism teaches that the body was the vessel for the soul, the means through which a person did mitzvot, loved and lived. It deserves dignity even after death.
- **It belongs to G-d.** Jewish thought sees our bodies as entrusted to us by G-d. We care for them in life and return them respectfully in death.
- **Faith in the future.** Belief in the resurrection of the dead in the time of Moshiach is a core Jewish teaching. Burial expresses this hope. Read more in our guide to the [afterlife](/learn/afterlife).
- **The soul and the body.** Tradition teaches that the [soul](/learn/the-soul) remains connected to the body after death and is comforted by a gentle, natural return to the earth.

Many Jewish families also feel the weight of history. After the Holocaust, many feel that choosing burial is a way of honoring those who were denied it.

## Why some families consider cremation

People think about cremation for many understandable reasons: cost, simplicity, family members far away, or simply not knowing the Jewish view. These concerns are real, and the Rabbi is glad to help with each one.

- **Cost.** A simple, traditional Jewish funeral uses a plain wooden casket and is often less costly than people expect. If cost is a concern, please call. We will do everything we can to help.
- **Distance.** Families spread across the country can still arrange a burial and gather for shiva, in person or together in spirit.

## If a loved one was already cremated

Your loved one is still part of the Jewish people, and their soul is still loved. You can still say [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish), mark their yahrzeit, say Yizkor, give tzedakah and study Torah in their memory. These bring great merit to the soul.

There are questions about which mourning customs apply and what to do with the ashes. Every situation is different, and the Rabbi will gently guide you.

## Common questions

### Is cremation forbidden in Jewish law?
Yes. Traditional Jewish law requires burial and does not permit cremation. The reason is respect for the body and the soul, not a judgment of any person or family.

### Can ashes be buried in a Jewish cemetery?
Policies differ from cemetery to cemetery, and there are halachic questions involved. Please speak with the Rabbi, who can help you explore the options.

### My parent asked to be cremated. What should I do?
This is a painful position, and you are not alone. Speak with the Rabbi before anything is decided. He can help you think it through with care for your parent, your family and Jewish tradition.
