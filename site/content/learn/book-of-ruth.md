The Book of Ruth (מְגִלַּת רוּת) is a short book of the Bible about a Moabite woman, Ruth, who chose to join the Jewish people out of loyalty to her mother-in-law Naomi, and became the great-grandmother of King David. It is read on Shavuot, the festival of the giving of the Torah, because Ruth's sincere acceptance of the Torah mirrors what the whole Jewish people did at Sinai.

## The story in short

A famine struck the Land of Israel, and a man named Elimelech moved with his wife Naomi and his two sons to the land of Moab. There the sons married two Moabite women, Orpah and Ruth. After Elimelech and both sons died, Naomi was left a widow with two widowed daughters-in-law.

When the famine ended, Naomi decided to return home to Bethlehem and urged both young women to stay with their own families. Orpah went back. Ruth insisted on going with Naomi. Her words became famous: your people will be my people, and your G-d my G-d. According to the Sages, Ruth also accepted the whole Torah, including its difficult parts.

## Gleaning and kindness

Back in Bethlehem, Ruth went to gather the stalks and sheaves that Torah law requires harvesters to leave for the poor, in order to feed Naomi. The owner of the field, Boaz, a relative of Naomi and a leader of the Jewish people, noticed Ruth's modesty and diligence. He invited her to glean in his field and told his workers to leave extra grain for her.

## Boaz, Ruth and the redemption of the family

Naomi told Ruth to approach Boaz, and Ruth asked him to marry her and to redeem the family land, which was his duty as a relative. Boaz agreed, but explained that another relative had a prior right. When that relative declined, Boaz and Ruth married. Their son Oved, whom Naomi helped raise, was the father of Yishai (Jesse), who was the father of King David. See [King David](/learn/king-david).

## Why we read it on Shavuot

Chabad.org gives three reasons.

1. **King David.** Shavuot is the day King David was born and passed away, and Ruth was his great-grandmother.
2. **The harvest.** The story takes place at harvest time, and Shavuot is also called the festival of the harvest.
3. **Choosing the Torah.** Ruth's sincere conversion parallels the holiday's theme. On Shavuot the whole Jewish people accepted the Torah, so in a sense every Jew is a convert.

It is read on the first night, often as part of the all-night learning ([Shavuot night learning](/learn/shavuot-night-learning)), or in the morning service on the second day. For the holiday as a whole, see [Shavuot for beginners](/learn/shavuot-for-beginners) and [the month of Sivan](/learn/sivan).

## What Ruth teaches us

- **Loyalty.** Ruth stayed with Naomi when she had nothing to gain. Real kindness does not look for a reward.
- **Sincere conversion.** Many women who convert take the name Ruth. Her story is the model for those who join the Jewish people today, and for Jewish courts, which make sure that a candidate understands the commitments of Jewish life. If you are curious, read [converting to Judaism](/learn/converting-to-judaism) and talk privately to the Rabbi.
- **The power of women.** The book could have been called the Book of Boaz, but bears Ruth's name. The Rebbe taught that this shows the central role of Jewish women, who shape future generations.
- **Hope.** Tradition says the future redemption will come through one of Ruth's descendants.

## Common questions

### What is the Book of Ruth about?
It is the story of Ruth, a Moabite woman who stayed loyal to her mother-in-law Naomi, joined the Jewish people, married Boaz, and became the great-grandmother of King David.

### Why do we read the Book of Ruth on Shavuot?
Because Shavuot is King David's birthday and yahrtzeit, because the story is set at harvest time, and because Ruth's acceptance of the Torah mirrors the Jewish people's at Sinai.

### Who was Ruth?
Ruth was a Moabite woman who married into Naomi's family. According to tradition, she was linked to Moabite royalty, and she is considered the model of a sincere convert.

### What does "your people shall be my people" mean?
These are Ruth's words to Naomi, declaring that she would join the Jewish people and serve the G-d of Israel. The Sages taught that she accepted the whole Torah.

### When is the Book of Ruth read?
It is read on the first night of Shavuot, as part of all-night learning, or in the morning service on the second day. Ask the Rabbi about your community's custom.
