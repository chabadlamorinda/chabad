Every Chanukah includes at least one Shabbat, and some years Chanukah begins on a Friday night. On Shabbat itself we do not light fire, so the Friday and Saturday night lightings need a little planning. Here is how it works.

## Friday: menorah first, then Shabbat candles

On Friday afternoon, the menorah is lit **before** the Shabbat candles, because once Shabbat begins we no longer light.

1. **Use bigger candles or more oil.** On a normal night the lights must burn at least half an hour after nightfall. On Friday we light earlier, so the lights must last until at least half an hour after nightfall. Small colored Chanukah candles usually burn out too soon. Use larger candles, or fill oil cups generously.
2. **Light a little before the Shabbat candles.** Say the usual blessings and light the menorah, then light the Shabbat candles at candle lighting time, 18 minutes before sunset. Do not wait until the last minute: the menorah must be lit before Shabbat begins.
3. **Leave the menorah where it is.** Once Shabbat begins, the menorah is not moved until after Shabbat.

Many families give the children their Chanukah gelt on Friday before candle lighting, a double portion, since money is not handled on Shabbat.

## Shabbat day

There is no lighting on Shabbat day. We add **Al HaNissim**, the Chanukah thanksgiving prayer, in the Amidah and in Grace after Meals, and the full Hallel is said in the morning service, as on every day of Chanukah. See our guide to [Al HaNissim and Hallel](/learn/al-hanissim-and-hallel).

## Saturday night: Havdalah, then the menorah

When Shabbat ends, at home we first make **Havdalah**, the blessing that separates Shabbat from the week, and then light the Chanukah menorah. In the synagogue the order is reversed: the menorah is lit first and Havdalah follows. Wait until Shabbat has ended (see the time on our Shabbat page) before lighting anything.

## When the eighth candle is on Friday

In years when the last night of Chanukah falls on Friday, the same rules apply: all eight lights are lit before the Shabbat candles, with enough oil or long enough candles to burn into the night.

## Common questions

### Why light the menorah before the Shabbat candles and not after?
Lighting the Shabbat candles brings in Shabbat for the woman who lights them, and after that she does not light fire. Lighting the menorah first lets both mitzvahs be done.

### What if my Chanukah candles will not last long enough on Friday?
Use Shabbat candles or tea lights in the menorah instead, or use olive oil cups filled well. The lights only need to stand in a row at the same height, except the shamash.

### Can I light the menorah on Saturday afternoon before Shabbat ends?
No. Wait until Shabbat is over, then make Havdalah and light the menorah.

### Do I need a separate Havdalah candle on Saturday night of Chanukah?
Yes. The Havdalah candle is a separate mitzvah. Use the Havdalah candle for Havdalah and then light the menorah.
