The Arizal (האריז"ל), Rabbi Yitzchak (Isaac) Luria, was a great teacher of Kabbalah who lived in Tzfat (Safed) in the Land of Israel in the 1500s. Although he lived there only about two years and passed away at 38, his teachings changed Jewish spiritual life and still shape how many Jews pray and welcome Shabbat.

## Who was the Arizal?

According to Chabad.org, he lived from 1534 to 1572 (some sources differ by a few years). He is known as the Ari, "the lion," and as the Arizal, an acronym for his title and for "may his memory be a blessing." His family was from Germany and had settled in Jerusalem, where he was born. His father, Shlomo, descended from Rashi, and died when the boy was very young.

He was raised in Egypt by his wealthy uncle, Rabbi Mordechai Francis, a scholar from a Sephardic background. This is why the Ari is described as having an Ashkenazi father and a Sephardic upbringing. He studied with great Torah teachers in Egypt, including the Radbaz, Rabbi David ibn Zimra. Documents found in the Cairo Geniza show that he earned his living in business, trading in spices and wheat, rather than relying on a stipend.

## Years in solitude

Chabad.org says the young Ari spent years alone by the Nile, studying the [Zohar](/learn/kabbalah) and other Kabbalistic texts, coming home to his family only for Shabbat. There he developed a complete picture of the world and of the role of the Torah and its commandments in it.

## Two years in Tzfat

Around 1570 he brought his family to the Land of Israel and settled in [Tzfat](/learn/tzfat), a center of Kabbalah at the time. He first studied with Rabbi Moshe Cordovero (the Ramak), and after the Ramak's passing, the students chose the Ari as their teacher. His closest student, Rabbi Chaim Vital, recorded his teachings. The Ari wrote little himself: mostly Aramaic hymns for the three Shabbat meals. Everything else we know of his Torah comes through Rabbi Chaim Vital and other students. Though the Ari taught for only about two years, his teachings from that time are vast.

## Teachings that changed Jewish life

- **Hidden sparks.** Drawing on the Zohar, the Ari taught that sparks of Divine light are hidden within everything in the world, and that we raise them up by using the world for good: a meal eaten for the sake of Shabbat, a coin given to charity.
- **Tzimtzum.** He explained creation as G-d "contracting" His presence to make room for a world.
- **Meaning in every mitzvah.** Every prayer, mitzvah and line of Torah contains layers of meaning, so that observance becomes alive.

## Customs we keep because of him

The Jews of Tzfat already greeted Shabbat with a special service. Rabbi Shlomo Alkabetz, who lived in Tzfat at the same time, composed [Lecha Dodi](/learn/lecha-dodi). Chabad.org tells that each week the Ari and his students went out into the fields at sunset and recited Psalms facing the setting sun. This is the root of our [Kabbalat Shabbat](/learn/kabbalat-shabbat) service. The Ari also made Shabbat a center of spiritual devotion, with festive meals and songs, and he championed the Melaveh Malkah meal that escorts Shabbat out. The Nusach HoAri prayer text is used by some communities to this day. Ask the Rabbi about any particular custom.

He is also credited with identifying the graves of many Talmudic sages in the Galilee, sites that had been lost over time.

## The Ari's passing and legacy

The Arizal passed away on the fifth of Av, in 1572, at the age of 38. Chabad.org says that his revival of Kabbalah is said to have inspired the Chassidic movement. [Chassidus](/learn/chassidus), which Chabad follows, draws on these inner teachings of the Torah and brings them into everyday life. For the Kabbalah itself, see our guide to [Kabbalah](/learn/kabbalah).

## Common questions

### Who was the Arizal?
The Arizal, Rabbi Isaac Luria (1534 to 1572), was a leading teacher of Kabbalah who lived in Tzfat. His teachings were recorded by Rabbi Chaim Vital.

### What does Arizal mean?
Arizal (האריז"ל) is an acronym of his title and of the phrase "may his memory be a blessing." He is also called the Ari, "the lion."

### Why is the Arizal connected to Tzfat?
He lived in Tzfat for only about two years from around 1570, but his teachings from that time are vast, and his name is closely tied to the city.

### What did the Arizal teach about the world?
He taught that sparks of Divine light are hidden in all things and that we raise them through good use, and he explained creation through G-d's "contraction" (tzimtzum).

### Which Shabbat customs come from the Arizal?
He and his students went out at sunset on Friday to receive Shabbat, and he made Shabbat meals and songs central. Chabad.org also credits him with championing Melaveh Malkah.
