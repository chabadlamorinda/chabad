Retirement often brings mixed feelings: relief at leaving work, and a question about what comes next. Judaism has a clear answer. You may retire from a job, but there is no retiring from growing, learning and giving. In many ways these can be the richest years of life.

## No retirement from life

The Torah describes Abraham in his old age as "coming with his days," and the commentaries explain that he brought every one of his days with him, each one filled with meaning. Judaism sees age as a blessing of wisdom and experience, not a time to fade.

The Rebbe, Rabbi Menachem M. Schneerson, spoke strongly about this. He encouraged older people not to think of retirement as the end of productive life, but as an opportunity: now there is time for what was hard to fit in before, especially learning Torah and helping others. He urged that seniors be valued for their wisdom and given ways to contribute.

## Learning

- **A daily habit.** Even a few minutes of [daily Torah study](/learn/daily-torah-study) a day adds up.
- **A class.** Many people discover they love learning once they have time. See [Torah classes in Lamorinda](/learn/torah-classes-in-lamorinda).
- **A study partner.** Learning with a friend, in person or by phone, keeps the mind sharp and the friendship strong.
- **Hebrew.** It is never too late. See [Hebrew Reading for Adults](/learn/hebrew-reading-for-adults).

## Giving

Years of experience are a gift to share:

- **Volunteer** with people who need help: visiting the sick, mentoring, driving. See [Volunteering in Lamorinda](/learn/volunteering-in-lamorinda).
- **Share skills** from your career with a community organization.
- **Give [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah)** wisely and consider an [ethical will](/learn/ethical-will) to pass on your values.

## Grandparenting

Grandparents carry the family story. Tell your grandchildren where the family came from, light Shabbat candles or make Kiddush with them, and give them a sense of belonging. See [Jewish Grandparents](/learn/jewish-grandparents).

## Health

Judaism teaches that our bodies are on loan from G-d and must be cared for. Stay active, see your doctor, eat well and keep up friendships. Loneliness affects health; if this is a struggle, see [Feeling Lonely](/learn/feeling-lonely) and reach out.

## Common questions

### Is it wrong to rest after retiring?
Not at all. Rest is good, and Shabbat teaches its value. The point is to keep a sense of purpose alongside the rest.

### I never learned much about Judaism. Is it too late?
Never. Rabbi Akiva began learning at 40 and became one of the greatest sages. Every question is welcome, at any level.

### How can I find meaningful volunteer work?
Think about what you love and what you are good at, then ask. The Rabbi can suggest ways to help people in the community.
