Rabbi Akiva was a Jewish sage of the first and second centuries who began studying Torah at age 40 and became one of the greatest teachers in Jewish history. His story teaches that no one is too old or too far behind to begin, and that love for every fellow Jew is the heart of the Torah.

## Who was Rabbi Akiva?

Akiva (עֲקִיבָא) was a shepherd, unlearned and poor, working for Kalba Savua, one of the richest men in Jerusalem. Today Rabbi Akiva is quoted on nearly every page of the [Talmud](/learn/talmud), and most of the teachings in the Talmud and Midrash are built on what he taught.

## Starting at forty

According to the tradition Chabad.org tells, Akiva saw water dripping on a stone and noticed that the soft water had worn a hole in the hard rock. He reasoned: if water can carve stone, then words of Torah can penetrate my mind. He sat down to learn the Hebrew alphabet, together with his young son.

The lesson is simple and personal. Many families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda say, "I never had a Jewish education." Rabbi Akiva began from the very first letter, and so can you. See [adult education](/programs/adult-education).

## Rachel, who believed in him

Rachel was the daughter of Kalba Savua. She saw Akiva's potential and agreed to marry him on one condition: that he study Torah. Her father disowned her, and the couple lived in great poverty, in a barn. Rachel urged her husband to go away and learn, and he spent 24 years studying under Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua.

Rabbi Akiva never forgot what he owed her. He told his own students: "What is mine and what is yours is hers." Behind every great Torah scholar stands someone who made it possible.

## 24,000 students and the days of the Omer

Rabbi Akiva taught more students than any other sage: 12,000 pairs of disciples, which is how the number 24,000 is often explained. The Talmud tells that they all died within a short time, "because they did not treat each other with respect."

Jews remember them every year in the weeks between Passover and Shavuot, the days of [counting the Omer](/learn/counting-the-omer), which is why these weeks have customs of mourning. [Lag BaOmer](/learn/lag-baomer) is a day of joy within this season. After the tragedy, Rabbi Akiva started over, teaching five new students in the south, and through them the Torah was revived.

## "Love your fellow as yourself"

Rabbi Akiva taught that "You shall love your fellow as yourself" (Leviticus 19:18) is a great principle of the Torah. He also taught that a person should not insult or curse someone just because he was insulted or cursed. The story of his students shows why this was so important to him: respect between people is the foundation of learning. Read more about [love of a fellow Jew](/learn/ahavat-yisrael).

## Hope, courage and the Shema

After the destruction of Jerusalem and the Temple, when others saw only doom, Rabbi Akiva saw hope and a brighter future. When the Romans outlawed the teaching of Torah, he kept teaching in public. He compared the Jewish people to fish: just as fish cannot live out of water, Jews cannot live without Torah.

He was arrested and died as a martyr, reciting the Shema while the Romans tore his flesh with iron combs. His death is remembered in the Elegy of the Ten Martyrs, said on Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av. Learn the [Shema](/learn/shema) he said with his last breath.

## Common questions

### Who was Rabbi Akiva?
Rabbi Akiva was a first-century Jewish sage who began learning Torah at age 40 and became one of the greatest teachers of the Talmud. Most teachings in the Talmud and Midrash rest on his teachings.

### How did Rabbi Akiva start learning Torah?
He saw water wear down a hard stone and concluded that Torah could penetrate his mind too. He began with the alphabet, studying beside his young son.

### Who was Rachel, the wife of Rabbi Akiva?
Rachel was the daughter of the wealthy Kalba Savua. She married the poor shepherd Akiva on condition that he study Torah, and she encouraged him to study for 24 years.

### Why did Rabbi Akiva's 24,000 students die?
The Talmud says they died because they did not treat each other with respect. Jews mourn them each year between Passover and Shavuot.

### What did Rabbi Akiva say about loving your fellow?
He taught that "You shall love your fellow as yourself" is a great principle of the Torah.

### How did Rabbi Akiva die?
He was arrested by the Romans for teaching Torah and died reciting the Shema.
