A legacy gift is a gift in a will, trust or beneficiary form that helps Jewish life continue after the donor is gone. Chabad.org has told the stories of ordinary people who did this, and each says the same thing: they wanted the Judaism that touched them to be there for the next generation.

## Susan Moss: "They offered me a home I never had"

Chabad.org (Yehuda Mayteles, 2020) tells of Susan Moss of Long Island, then 76. She grew up in Queens in a family that could not afford synagogue membership, so she knew little of Judaism. As a child she followed friends to Hebrew school and listened from the stoop through the open door.

She taught elementary school in New York City for 35 years and lived modestly. When her mother died she left her a small inheritance, which Susan invested and saw grow. About six years before the article, she saw an ad for Torah classes at a nearby Chabad House and went. She felt she had found what she had been looking for. She credits Rabbi Shalom Ber Cohen of Village Chabad in Stony Brook with answering her questions and checking on her when she is ill.

In her will she directed that money to promote Jewish continuity. She said, "I am certainly not going to take it with me." And of Chabad, "They offered me a home that I never had."

## Myrna Horton: for the children

Another Chabad.org article (2018) introduces Myrna Horton of Chabad of Tustin, California, 84. She left a portion of her estate to Chabad of Tustin so that children could learn about their religion, saying she felt good knowing "some of my estate will be used for children's education." She had met the rabbi at a car wash years earlier, and, hesitant to return to synagogue after many years, became a regular at Shabbat services, where she "loved the camaraderie."

She told Chabad emissaries not to be afraid to ask supporters about legacy gifts: giving, she said, is "a wonderful blessing." The article reports that she died unexpectedly a week after the interview.

## Dr. Marvin and Marsha Frank: "an extension of our house"

The same article tells of a couple from West Orange, New Jersey. Dr. Marvin Frank and Marsha arranged a bequest to Chabad of West Orange to sustain Jewish life beyond their lifetimes. They had noticed a large menorah outside a building, walked in, and found the Chabad center. They became close to the rabbi's family and helped build a new center. Marsha called the shul "an extension of our house" and said that leaving a legacy costs nothing during one's lifetime.

## What the stories have in common

- **Ordinary people.** A teacher, a woman of 84, a doctor and his wife.
- **A personal connection.** Each was welcomed by a rabbi and a community.
- **A wish for continuity.** Jewish education for children and a Jewish future.

Chabad.org reports that donors pledged more than $80 million in future gifts in the first five years of the Chabad Tomorrow planned-giving program, and thousands of donors worldwide have made legacy gifts to local Chabad centers. (Figures as reported by Chabad.org in 2020.)

## What a legacy gift is, and how it works

Chabad.org's planned-giving page lists three ways: gifts in a will or trust (a dollar amount, an asset or a percentage), retirement plan gifts (naming a charity as beneficiary of an IRA or 401(k)), and beneficiary designations on life insurance or a donor-advised fund. See [Leaving a Jewish Legacy](/learn/leaving-a-jewish-legacy-gift). This guide is not legal or tax advice; please ask your attorney and tax advisor.

## Common questions

### What is a legacy gift to a synagogue or Chabad?
It is a gift arranged now, usually in a will, trust or beneficiary form, that takes effect later to support Jewish life.

### Does a legacy gift cost anything now?
Chabad.org notes that a bequest costs nothing during your lifetime. Your attorney can explain the details.

### Can I change my mind later?
Chabad.org notes that a bequest in a will can be changed if your circumstances change. Ask your attorney.

### Does a donor-advised fund grant count as maaser?
Yes, a grant from your donor-advised fund to Chabad of Lamorinda counts as maaser. Call 925-384-7242 to arrange it.

### Can I include Chabad of Lamorinda in my estate plans?
Yes. Call the Rabbi or Rebbetzin and see [/legacy](/legacy).

### Should I tell the Rabbi if I do?
Chabad.org's planned-giving page invites donors to let the organization know, so it can thank them in their lifetime. The Rabbi would be honored to hear and to thank you.
