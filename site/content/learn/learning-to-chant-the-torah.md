Trope (also called cantillation or ta'amim) is the set of musical notes that tell a reader how to chant each word of the Torah. A boy who wants to read at his Bar Mitzvah learns his portion word by word with its melody, usually over several months, with a teacher and a recording to practice with. Reading is a beautiful option, but it is not required.

## What trope is

The Torah scroll is written without vowels, punctuation or musical marks. The reader learns them separately. The marks in a printed Chumash (a book of the Five Books of Moses) are called ta'amim (טְעָמִים), from the word *ta'am*, "taste" or "flavor." According to Jewish tradition, they guide pronunciation, emphasis and the structure of each sentence, and different communities sing them in different melodies, though the principles are the same. So there is no single "wrong" tune: ask your teacher which tradition your family will follow.

## Do we need to read at all?

No. Our [Bar Mitzvah guide](/learn/bar-mitzvah) is clear that the heart of the day is taking on the mitzvot and being called to the Torah for an aliyah. A boy can read all of his portion, part of it, the [haftarah](/learn/haftarah), or only say the blessings over the Torah when he is called up. Many families find that a shorter reading, well learned, gives more joy than a long reading rushed. Ask the Rabbi what is right for your son. See also [the Torah reading](/learn/torah-reading) to understand what the congregation is doing when the scroll is open.

## A simple practice plan

1. **Start with the Hebrew letters and vowels.** If your child is still hesitant, begin with [Bar Mitzvah prep without years of Hebrew school](/learn/bar-mitzvah-prep-without-hebrew-school).
2. **Find the portion.** See [your child's Bar Mitzvah Torah portion](/learn/bar-mitzvah-parsha), then divide it into small pieces of a few verses.
3. **Listen first.** Jewish tradition offers audio recordings of the Torah reading with trope, so that your son can follow the text as he hears it.
4. **Practice ten to fifteen minutes a day**, rather than an hour once a week. Short and regular beats long and rare.
5. **Learn the blessings** before and after the reading. They are the one part every boy says.
6. **Rehearse standing up**, with a pointer (a *yad*), in a room with family listening.
7. **Do a dress rehearsal** at the synagogue with the Rabbi, if you can.

## Helping at home

You do not have to read Hebrew to help. Sit with your child, listen to the recording together, praise effort and be patient with mistakes. Fear is the biggest obstacle, and the best remedy is a calm routine. If your child learns differently, see [a Bar or Bat Mitzvah with special needs](/learn/special-needs-bar-bat-mitzvah).

## What about girls?

A Bat Mitzvah in Chabad practice is marked outside the synagogue service, with learning, a talk and a celebration rather than a public Torah reading. Girls can still learn to read Hebrew well and study the Torah portion of their Bat Mitzvah week in depth. Read [Bat Mitzvah](/learn/bat-mitzvah) for more ideas, and ask the Rebbetzin or the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### What is Torah trope?
Trope, or ta'amim, are the musical notes that show how to chant each word of the Torah. They also mark where sentences pause and which words are stressed.

### How long does it take to learn a Torah portion?
It depends on the child, the length and the time spent. Many families start several months ahead and practice a little each day. The Rabbi can help you choose a portion size that fits.

### Does a boy have to read from the Torah at his Bar Mitzvah?
No. The essential parts are taking on the mitzvot and receiving an aliyah. Reading is optional and many boys read only a few verses.

### Can my child learn trope if he does not read Hebrew well yet?
Yes, often with extra time. Start with the letters and vowels and add the melody step by step. Ask the Rabbi for a plan.

### Where can we find recordings to practice with?
Jewish tradition has Torah and haftarah reading recordings with trope, and Rabbi Labkowski can point you to the right ones for your portion.

### Can a girl read from the Torah at her Bat Mitzvah?
In Chabad practice the Bat Mitzvah is celebrated with learning, a talk and mitzvot, not a public reading. Ask the Rabbi or Rebbetzin about what suits your daughter.
