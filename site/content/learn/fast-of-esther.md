Purim is the noisiest, happiest day of the Jewish year. The day before it is one of the quietest. Taanit Esther, the Fast of Esther, is a daytime fast that leads straight into the Megillah reading, and understanding it makes Purim itself deeper.

## Where the fast comes from

When Haman's decree to destroy the Jews was sealed, Queen Esther prepared to go before King Achashverosh uninvited, a crime punishable by death. She asked Mordechai: "Go, gather all the Jews, and fast for me; do not eat or drink for three days." Then she went in, and the story turned.

Months later, on the 13th of Adar, the day Haman had chosen for the massacre, the Jews of Persia gathered to defend themselves. Jewish tradition teaches that they fasted and prayed that day as well, as Jews have always done when going out to battle, and G-d gave them victory. The next day, the 14th, became Purim.

Our fast on the 13th of Adar remembers both: Esther's fast of courage and the people's fast on the day of battle. It is a reminder that the Purim miracle did not come from Esther's beauty or Mordechai's cleverness, but from a nation that turned to G-d together.

## Who fasts, and when

The Fast of Esther is one of the [minor fast days](/learn/minor-fast-days): it begins at dawn, not the night before, and ends at nightfall, when the Megillah is read. Only eating and drinking are forbidden; washing, work and leather shoes are all fine.

Healthy adults fast. Because this fast is a custom of remembrance rather than a day of mourning, the sages were lenient with it. Pregnant and nursing women do not fast, nor does anyone who is ill or weak, nor children before Bar or Bat Mitzvah. If you take medication or are unsure about your health, ask your doctor and then the Rabbi. Nobody should enter Purim unwell.

**When it moves.** A fast is never held on Shabbat, and this one is not pushed later either, because it would land on Purim. So when the 13th of Adar falls on Shabbat, the Fast of Esther moves back to the Thursday before. Check [this year's Purim page](/holidays/purim) for the dates.

## The half-shekel

In the time of the Holy Temple every Jew gave a half-shekel coin each year in the month of Adar, so that the communal offerings came from everyone equally, rich and poor alike.

Today we remember this on the afternoon of the Fast of Esther, before the Mincha prayer: each person gives three half-dollar coins to [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah). The custom is for men from Bar Mitzvah and up, and many families give for every member, including children and expected babies. Ask the Rabbi about your own family.

## From fasting to feasting

The Fast of Esther is the doorway to Purim. In the afternoon the Mincha service includes the special fast-day Torah reading and the prayer Aneinu, "Answer us." As the sun sets, people arrive at the synagogue in costume, children with noisemakers, and the fast ends with the reading of the [Megillah](/learn/megillah).

Chassidic teaching sees the order as the point. On Purim we celebrate that G-d is present even when His name does not appear, as it does not once in the whole Book of Esther. The fast is the moment of turning to Him in the hidden place; the feast is the joy of discovering He was there all along. One leads to the other. A good Purim, with its [mishloach manot](/learn/mishloach-manot), gifts to the poor and the [Purim feast](/learn/purim-seudah), begins with a quiet day of prayer.

## Common questions

### Do children fast on the Fast of Esther?
No. Children before Bar or Bat Mitzvah do not fast, and because this is a lenient fast, even older children who feel weak may eat. Teach them the story instead.

### Can I brush my teeth or shower during the fast?
Yes. Only eating and drinking are forbidden on the Fast of Esther. Brushing teeth is permitted as long as you do not swallow water; showering, work and driving are all fine.

### Do I have to give the half-shekel in silver or half-dollar coins?
The custom is three coins of the local half-unit, which in America is three half-dollars. Most synagogues provide them; you exchange your donation for the coins and give the coins. Any amount of tzedakah given with this in mind is good.
