In the Torah, Adam and Eve are the first man and woman, created by G-d and placed in the Garden of Eden. In Jewish teaching the story is about free choice, responsibility and the ability to return to G-d, and it teaches that every human being is a whole world.

## The story in brief (Genesis 1 to 3)

G-d forms Adam (אָדָם) from the dust of the earth (adamah) and breathes a soul into him. G-d places him in the Garden of Eden and later creates Chavah (Eve) as his partner. In the garden there are two special trees. Chabad.org describes the Tree of Life, which would grant eternal life, and the Tree of Knowledge of good and evil. The Tree of Knowledge stands in the middle of the garden, and G-d gives Adam one prohibition: eat from any tree, but not from that one. The serpent persuades Eve, she eats and gives to Adam, and they are sent out of the garden.

## What the fruit was

The Torah does not name the fruit, and "apple" is not in the text. The Sages offer different views, including wheat, grape and fig. Chabad.org explains that the Tree of Knowledge would introduce an evil inclination and an awareness of immorality, a world in which good and bad are mixed together. The details are debated, so ask the Rabbi if you want to study the sources.

## Lessons of the story

- **Every person is a whole world.** The Mishnah teaches that Adam was created alone so that each person can say, "The world was created for me." Whoever saves one life is considered to have saved a whole world. See [what is a mensch](/learn/being-a-mensch).
- **Free choice.** A person is given a command and the ability to choose. See [free will](/learn/free-will).
- **Responsibility, not inherited guilt.** In Jewish teaching, each person is accountable for his or her own deeds, and every soul comes into the world pure. A mistake can be repaired.
- **Return is always possible.** The path back to G-d is [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah), which means returning.
- **Created in G-d's image.** Every human being has a divine soul and infinite worth.

## Adam, Eve and science

Many people ask how the story fits with science. Chabad's approach is that the Torah is not a science textbook but G-d's teaching for life. For this question, see [Torah and science](/learn/torah-and-science) and ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### Who were Adam and Eve in the Torah?
They were the first man and woman, created by G-d and placed in the Garden of Eden.

### What was the forbidden fruit in Judaism?
The Torah does not say. The Sages suggest wheat, grape or fig, and not an apple.

### What does the Tree of Knowledge mean in Judaism?
Chabad.org explains that it would introduce an evil inclination and an awareness of immorality. It represents the choice between good and evil.

### Does Judaism believe in original sin?
No. In Jewish teaching, each person is responsible for his or her own deeds, and every soul is born pure.

### Why does the Torah say Adam was created alone?
The Mishnah teaches that it shows each person is a whole world and every human being is equally valuable.
