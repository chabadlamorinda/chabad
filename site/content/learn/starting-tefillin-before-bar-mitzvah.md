In Chabad custom, a boy begins putting on tefillin about two months before his Bar Mitzvah, first without the blessing and then, a few weeks later, with it. This gives him time to learn it calmly, so that by his 13th Hebrew birthday it already feels like his own.

## Why start early

Tefillin (תְּפִלִּין) are the black leather boxes with handwritten Torah passages that a Jewish man wears on weekday mornings. A boy becomes responsible for the mitzvah on his Bar Mitzvah date, but doing it for the first time on that very morning is a lot to ask. According to Chabad.org, the practice is to begin about two months earlier. If Chol HaMoed (the middle days of a festival) falls in that stretch, when tefillin are not worn, the start can be moved a little earlier. Ask the Rabbi to set your son's start date. See [the Hebrew date of the Bar Mitzvah](/learn/bar-mitzvah-hebrew-birthday-date) to count back.

## The two stages, as Chabad.org describes them

1. **First, without a blessing.** He lays the tefillin, says the Shema and learns the order, so he gets used to the feel and the straps.
2. **A few weeks later, with the blessing.** Once he can put them on properly, he adds the blessing. The blessing is said only when he can lay them correctly.

At the beginning, a father or teacher helps him and checks the placement. After that, he should still be watched for a while. The Rabbi can sit with you and your son together.

## Practice at home

- **Choose a fixed time**, such as before school, with the same simple routine each morning.
- **Learn the order:** arm first, then head, and removing them in reverse. See the step-by-step instructions in [Tefillin](/learn/tefillin).
- **Say the Shema** with them on. Even a short prayer makes it real.
- **Rehearse without the tefillin first** if he is nervous: a practice with a strap or a scarf takes the fear away.
- **Do it together.** A father who also puts on tefillin shows his son what it means. If you have not yet done it yourself, the Rabbi would be glad to help you start too.

## His own pair

A boy usually receives his own pair for the Bar Mitzvah. Tefillin must be handwritten and checked, and a pair that is cheap or of unknown origin may not be kosher. Chabad.org advises buying only from someone who can vouch for the scribe and the parchments. Chabad custom is also to wear two kinds of tefillin, named for Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam, starting around the same two months. This is a detail to ask about before you buy. Read [Buying tefillin](/learn/buying-tefillin), and have any pair checked; see [checking mezuzahs and tefillin](/learn/checking-mezuzahs-and-tefillin).

## What is not said

Chabad.org notes that the Shehecheyanu blessing is not said when a boy first puts on tefillin or on the day of his Bar Mitzvah, because his joy is mainly in the mitzvot he will do from now on. The words of the tefillin blessing are in [Tefillin](/learn/tefillin).

## Common questions

### When does a boy start tefillin before his Bar Mitzvah?
In Chabad custom, about two months before the Bar Mitzvah, first without the blessing and a few weeks later with it. Ask the Rabbi for your son's exact date.

### Does a boy have to wait until he is 13?
The obligation begins at 13, and the practice period before it is for learning. Practicing at the start is the custom, so the first day is not on the Bar Mitzvah itself.

### Do we need to buy tefillin right away?
You need a kosher pair by the time he starts, or a borrowed kosher pair for practice. Ask the Rabbi before ordering, because good tefillin can take time to arrive.

### Do girls put on tefillin?
Tefillin are a mitzvah for men and boys in Chabad custom. Girls take on mitzvot such as Shabbat candles. See [Girls lighting Shabbat candles](/learn/girls-lighting-shabbat-candles).

### What if my son is nervous or has special needs?
Go slowly and make a calm routine. Call the Rabbi, who will shape the practice around your son.
