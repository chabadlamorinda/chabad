Yes, a Jewish parent can pray for a child at any time, in any language, in their own words. Chabad.org calls the moment of Shabbat candle lighting, when the eyes are covered after the blessing, an especially auspicious time for private prayer for whatever your heart desires, and for most parents, that means their children.

## You do not need special words

Prayer in Judaism is not only the prayer book. The Chabad.org article on prayer recommends praying in Hebrew or in a language you understand, and stresses concentration (*kavanah*, כוונה) over quantity. A parent's quiet "please, G-d, watch over my daughter today" is real prayer. See [talking to G-d in your own words](/learn/talking-to-g-d-in-your-own-words) and [praying alone at home](/learn/praying-alone-at-home).

## The candle-lighting moment

The best-known time for a mother's personal prayer is Friday afternoon. The steps, from Chabad.org:

1. Put coins in a charity box.
2. Light the candles at least 18 minutes before sunset. Check [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times).
3. Draw your hands toward yourself and cover your eyes.
4. Say the blessing.
5. Keep your eyes covered a moment longer and pray from your heart.
6. Uncover your eyes and greet your family with "Shabbat Shalom."

The Chabad.org article does not give a special text for children here, only the invitation to pray for whatever you wish. If you would like a particular Hebrew prayer, ask the Rabbi or Rebbetzin; we will not guess at wording. Read more in [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).

## Hannah: the model of a parent's prayer

Hannah (*Chanah*, חנה) was childless for years. She went to the Tabernacle in Shiloh and prayed from the depths of her heart, so quietly that only her lips moved. The high priest Eli thought she was drunk, but she told him that she had "poured out" her soul before G-d. He apologized and blessed her, and she left with renewed trust. Her story is read on Rosh Hashanah, and Chabad.org notes the lessons: heartfelt prayer matters, honesty with G-d is welcome, and trust can strengthen us. Read [Hannah and her prayer](/learn/hannah-and-her-prayer).

## Other ways to pray for a child

- **Say Psalms.** Tehillim are a treasury of ready-made prayers. See [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) and the [prayer list page](/tehillim).
- **Bless your children on Friday night.** The Shabbat blessing is its own beautiful custom: [blessing your children](/learn/blessing-your-children).
- **Pray when it is hard.** There is no wrong time. Parents of teens or of a sick child can pray the same simple words every day.

## Common questions

### Can I pray for my children in English?
Yes. Chabad.org recommends praying in Hebrew or in a language you understand.

### When is the best time to pray for my children?
Shabbat candle lighting, after the blessing with your eyes covered, is an especially auspicious time for private prayer. You can also pray at any hour.

### Do I need a prayer book?
No, for personal prayer in your own words. A prayer book helps for the standard services.

### What should a father do?
Fathers can pray in their own words as well, in the morning or at any time, and bless their children on Friday night. See [being a Jewish father](/learn/being-a-jewish-father).

### Is it all right to be angry or sad in prayer?
Yes. Hannah poured out her pain honestly, and Chabad.org highlights this as a lesson about heartfelt prayer.
