**If you are thinking about ending your life, or are worried about someone, please call or text 988 now** (the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline in the United States), or call 911. You are not alone, and your life is precious. You can also call the Rabbi or Rebbetzin.

If you have lost someone you love to suicide, we are deeply sorry. This guide is for you and your family.

## Judaism's compassion

Some people have heard that Judaism treats suicide harshly, and they worry that their loved one will be denied a Jewish burial or that the family may not mourn. Please be comforted.

Jewish law has always been very careful in judging a person who took their own life, and very slow to say that someone acted with a clear and free mind. Today rabbis understand that suicide almost always comes from illness and overwhelming pain, like depression, which a person does not choose. For this reason, the person is generally buried in a Jewish cemetery and mourned in the normal way, with a funeral, [shiva](/learn/shiva) and Kaddish. Every situation is a little different, so please speak with the Rabbi, who will guide the family gently.

A person is not defined by the last moment of their life. Their soul is a part of G-d, and the good they did remains.

## Comfort for the family

- **It is not your fault.** Families often ask, "What did I miss?" or "What if I had called?" These feelings are natural, but suicide comes from an illness, not from anything a loved one failed to do.
- **Do not carry it alone.** Grief after suicide can be especially heavy and confusing. A grief counselor or a support group for suicide loss survivors can help a great deal.
- **Speak of your loved one.** Remember their life, not only their death. It is right to share their memory, their kindness and their name.
- **Children need honest, gentle words** suited to their age. See [Talking to Kids About Death](/learn/talking-to-kids-about-death).

## Kaddish and remembering

[Kaddish](/learn/kaddish) is said for a person who died by suicide, just as for anyone. The yahrzeit is kept each year, a candle is lit, and good deeds and tzedakah in the person's memory elevate their soul. If you cannot say Kaddish yourself, the Rabbi can arrange for it to be said. See [The Mourning Journey](/learn/mourning-journey).

## For friends and community

- **Come to the shiva** and simply be present. See [Comforting Mourners](/learn/comforting-mourners).
- **Do not ask how it happened,** and never judge or blame.
- **Keep calling** in the weeks and months after, when the visitors have gone.

## Common questions

### Will my loved one be buried in a Jewish cemetery?
In nearly all cases today, yes. Speak with the Rabbi, who will help arrange a proper Jewish funeral and burial.

### Do we sit shiva and say Kaddish?
Generally yes, in the normal way. The Rabbi will guide you through each step.

### Is my loved one's soul at peace?
We trust in G-d's infinite compassion. He alone knows the pain a person carried. Our prayers, Kaddish and good deeds bring their soul comfort and elevation.
