The simplest and best thing to write in a Chanukah card is "Happy Chanukah," which Chabad.org says works in all settings and with all Jews. For a Hebrew touch, write *Chanukah sameach* (חנוכה שמח), and for a Yiddish one, *Freilichen Chanukah*; then add a warm line about light.

A card is a small way to spread the light of the holiday. Below are sample messages you can use as they are or make your own.

## The traditional greetings

- **Happy Chanukah** or **Happy Hanukkah.** Both spellings are correct, so write whichever you like. See [Hanukkah or Chanukah?](/learn/hanukkah-or-chanukah-spelling).
- **Chanukah sameach (חנוכה שמח).** The Hebrew greeting, meaning "happy Chanukah."
- **Freilichen Chanukah.** The Yiddish greeting for the same wish.

Chabad.org's glossary lists these last two as the traditional holiday greetings. For more occasions, see [holiday greetings](/learn/holiday-greetings).

## Themes that fit the holiday

Chabad.org's Chanukah e-cards show what the holiday inspires, with titles such as "Light over Darkness," "A Time of Miracles," "A Warm Glow," "A Special Circle of Light," "Festival of Light," "Live Miraculously" and "Thanks for the Warm Feelings." Even the lighter ones are about warmth and food, such as the cards that say "I Didn't Forget..." with latkes or doughnuts. These themes are a good starting point: light, miracles, warmth, gratitude and a sweet treat.

## Sample messages

**For family:**
- "Wishing you eight nights of light, warmth and joy. Happy Chanukah!"
- "May the lights of the menorah fill your home with joy. We love you. Chanukah sameach!"
- "Thinking of you as we light the menorah tonight. Freilichen Chanukah!"

**For friends and neighbors:**
- "Happy Chanukah! May your home glow with the warmth of friends and family."
- "Sending you light and sweetness this Chanukah. So glad you are our neighbor."

**For someone who lives far away:**
- "We lit a candle for you tonight. Wishing you a Chanukah full of light. Chanukah sameach!"

**For coworkers and clients:**
- "Wishing you and your family a Happy Chanukah."
- "Warm wishes for a bright and joyful Chanukah."
- "Thank you for a wonderful year. Wishing you light and warmth."

For colleagues who are not Jewish, a simple warm line about light and joy is enough. Keep it short, friendly and sincere. For ideas on sharing the holiday at work, read [Chanukah for friends and coworkers](/learn/chanukah-for-friends-and-coworkers).

## Adding a gift or a mitzvah

It is a longstanding custom to give children money (gelt) on Chanukah, and Chabad.org says cash is the traditional choice. See [Chanukah gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt) and [Chanukah gifts](/learn/chanukah-gifts). Chabad.org also notes that it is customary to increase charity during Chanukah, so a card can come with a gift to tzedakah.

## Common questions

### What do you write in a Chanukah card?
Write "Happy Chanukah" or "Chanukah sameach," then add a short wish about light, warmth and joy. A personal line about the person makes it special.

### How do you say Happy Chanukah in Hebrew?
Say *Chanukah sameach* (חנוכה שמח). It is the traditional Hebrew greeting.

### How do you say Happy Chanukah in Yiddish?
Say *Freilichen Chanukah*, a traditional Yiddish greeting.

### Is it Hanukkah or Chanukah on the card?
Both are correct. Chanukah is preferred by Ashkenazim, and Hanukkah is more common among Sephardim and non-Jews. Choose either.

### What should I write to a coworker?
Keep it simple and warm: "Wishing you a Happy Chanukah," or a line about light and joy. That works in all settings.
