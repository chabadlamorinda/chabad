The Book of Exodus, called Shemot (שמות, "Names") in Hebrew, is the second of the Five Books of Moses. It tells how the Jewish people, enslaved in Egypt, are redeemed, receive the Torah at Mount Sinai, and build the Mishkan, the portable Sanctuary, so that G-d can dwell among them.

## Why is it called Shemot, "Names"?

The book opens by listing the names of Jacob's sons, though we already met them in [Genesis](/learn/book-of-genesis). Chabad.org shares traditional reasons: the Jews in Egypt kept their Jewish names and did not give in completely to the culture around them; G-d counts His people by name, as He counts the stars; and a name expresses a person's inner essence. The book's message is that even in a long, hard exile, a Jew's inner identity can be revealed and awakened.

## The 11 portions, in order

1. **Shemot:** a new king arises who "did not know Joseph," and the Israelites are enslaved. Moses is born, raised in Pharaoh's palace, and at the burning bush is sent to redeem his people.
2. **Va'eira:** G-d promises redemption in four expressions, and the first plagues begin.
3. **Bo:** the last plagues, the first Passover offering, and the Exodus.
4. **Beshalach:** the sea splits, the people sing the Song at the Sea, and they receive manna in the desert. See [Shabbat Shirah](/learn/shabbat-shirah).
5. **Yitro:** Moses' father-in-law Jethro joins the Jews, and the Ten Commandments are given at Sinai. See [the Ten Commandments](/learn/ten-commandments).
6. **Mishpatim:** laws of fairness, kindness and community life, and the people say "we will do and we will hear."
7. **Terumah:** G-d asks for contributions to build the Mishkan.
8. **Tetzaveh:** the priestly garments and the service.
9. **Ki Tisa:** the census, the golden calf, Moses' prayers, and the second tablets.
10. **Vayakhel:** the people gather and give generously to build the Mishkan.
11. **Pekudei:** the accounting of the work, and the Divine presence fills the Mishkan.

The book moves from the "iron furnace" of Egypt to a people who build a home for G-d.

## Big ideas from Exodus

- **Freedom has a purpose.** The Exodus is not only escape; it leads to Sinai. We are freed to serve and to make the world a dwelling for G-d.
- **Every person matters.** The Mishkan was built from the gifts of everyone, each from the heart. See [Moses](/learn/moses).
- **Remembering.** We recall the Exodus every day in prayer and every year at the [Passover Seder](/learn/passover-seder). See [the Exodus story](/learn/exodus-story).

## How Exodus connects to Passover

We read Exodus in the winter, and the reading leads up to Passover. The story of Bo and Beshalach is the story we tell at the Seder. Reading the book in the weeks before the holiday is a good way to prepare, and for families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda it is a natural time to start planning. See [Passover for beginners](/learn/passover-for-beginners).

## Common questions

### What is the Book of Exodus about?
Exodus tells of the slavery in Egypt, the ten plagues, the splitting of the sea, the giving of the Torah at Sinai, the sin of the golden calf, and the building of the Mishkan.

### Why is the book of Exodus called Shemot?
Shemot means "Names." The book starts with the names of Jacob's sons who came to Egypt, which teaches that each Jew has a name and an identity that Egypt could not erase.

### How many Torah portions are in Exodus?
There are eleven, from Shemot to Pekudei.

### Who wrote Exodus?
In Jewish tradition Moses wrote the whole Torah as G-d dictated it. See [the Torah](/learn/torah).

### When do we read Exodus in synagogue?
We begin it after Genesis ends, in winter, and read it through the weeks leading to Passover. Check [this week's parsha](/parsha) for the current portion.
