An unveiling is a short ceremony at the cemetery when a headstone is dedicated to a loved one, usually within the first year after their passing. Family and friends gather, recite Psalms, share memories, and remove a cloth covering the stone.

## Why do we set a headstone?

Setting a headstone, called a matzeivah in Hebrew, is an ancient Jewish custom. The Torah tells how Jacob placed a pillar on the grave of his wife Rachel (Genesis 35:20). A headstone honors the person, marks the grave so it is not forgotten, and gives family a place to visit and pray.

The unveiling ceremony itself is a more recent custom, but it has become a meaningful way to gather the family once more.

## When is the unveiling held?

Customs vary:

- Many families set the stone after the shloshim (30 days) or at around 11 or 12 months.
- Many hold the unveiling close to the first [yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit).
- There is no requirement to wait a full year. In many communities the stone is set as soon as it is ready.

Unveilings are generally not held on Shabbat, holidays, Rosh Chodesh or certain other days. Ask the Rabbi to help choose a date.

## What to write on the headstone

A traditional Jewish headstone usually includes:

- **The Hebrew letters פ"נ** at the top, meaning "here lies."
- **The person's Hebrew name** and their father's Hebrew name. See our guide to [Hebrew names](/learn/hebrew-names).
- **The Hebrew date of death,** and often the English dates as well.
- **The English name.**
- **The letters תנצב"ה** at the bottom, an abbreviation for "May his or her soul be bound up in the bond of eternal life."

A short line describing the person, such as "beloved mother and grandmother," is common. Keep the design dignified and modest. We are glad to help check the Hebrew text before it is engraved, since mistakes are hard to fix.

## The unveiling ceremony

The ceremony is simple and usually takes 15 to 30 minutes:

1. **Psalms.** Family and friends recite selected [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) (Psalms).
2. **Words of remembrance.** A Rabbi or family members share thoughts about the person.
3. **The unveiling.** A cloth covering the stone is removed.
4. **El Malei Rachamim.** A memorial prayer for the soul.
5. **Kaddish.** If a minyan of ten Jewish men is present, mourners recite [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish).

## Placing a stone

Before leaving, many people place a small pebble on the headstone. This custom shows that someone visited and that the person's memory endures, solid and lasting like a stone.

## Common questions

### Is an unveiling required?

The headstone is the important custom. A formal ceremony is not required, but many families find it brings comfort and closure.

### Can we hold an unveiling without a Rabbi?

Yes. Family members can recite Psalms and share memories. A Rabbi can help guide the ceremony and ensure the prayers are said properly, so we encourage you to reach out.

### Who should attend?

Close family and friends usually attend. Unlike a funeral, an unveiling is typically small and invitations are personal.

### What should I wear?

Respectful, modest clothing. Men usually wear a kippah.
