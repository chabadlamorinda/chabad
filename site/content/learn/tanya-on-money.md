According to the Tanya, money is not dirty and not the goal: it is your life-energy, earned through your effort, and when you give it to tzedakah you lift that energy to G-d. The same Tanya teaches that a person who truly trusts G-d knows his livelihood comes from Him, which is the spiritual answer to worry.

Many families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda feel pulled between these: wanting security and wanting to do good. The Tanya holds both.

## Money is life-energy

Chapter 37 of the Tanya explains why tzedakah is special. The Sages say charity is "equal to all the other mitzvot together." The Tanya's reason is that the money comes from one's labor, so the full energy of the animal soul goes into earning it. Giving it to charity lifts all that energy to G-d.

Even someone who does not work for a living, the chapter says, gives "the life of his soul" to G-d, since the money could have sustained his life. In other words, when you give, you are not only parting with dollars, you are giving a piece of your effort and your life. The chapter adds that because charity elevates so much of the soul, it hastens the redemption.

This is why the Tanya does not treat earning money as something unspiritual. Honest earnings carry real effort. See [Tanya at Work](/learn/tanya-at-work).

## Honesty comes first

Chapter 37 speaks of tzedakah given from money that was not obtained through theft, and it teaches that a mitzvah done with something forbidden cannot be raised to holiness. That fits what many learn at home: how you earn matters as much as what you do with it. See [business ethics](/learn/business-ethics).

## Trust, not anxiety

Epistle 11 of the Iggeret HaKodesh asks the reader not to crave physical life, children or sustenance for their own sake, but only insofar as they help one serve G-d. It teaches that a person who truly trusts G-d knows that his needs and affairs, in every detail, come from G-d. The epistle says that anxiety comes from self-love: from wanting "the life of the flesh" because one thinks that is his good. It cites the teaching "gam zu letovah," "this too is for the good," and that "in the light of the King's countenance, there is life."

The epistle gives no practical advice on earning, budgeting or debt. It speaks to the heart, not to the bank account. If money worry is heavy, the Tanya's teaching is spiritual support, not financial or medical advice, and it is right to seek practical help and to talk to the Rabbi. See [money worries](/learn/money-worries).

## Giving and success: the Rabbi's teaching

The Torah and G-d promise success for all giving of tzedakah. Maaser, giving a tenth of your income, is unique because the promise is stated clearly and we are invited to test it: the Talmud (Taanit 9a) teaches, "Give a tenth in order that you will become wealthy," and in the prophet Malachi (3:10) G-d says, "Test Me on this." The Rebbe taught that we may test G-d in this way with all tzedakah, not only with maaser. A legacy gift and a donor-advised fund grant also count as maaser.

This is not meant to pressure anyone, and no one who is struggling has done anything wrong. If your situation is hard, call the Rabbi. See [maaser](/learn/maaser) and [does tzedakah bring wealth](/learn/does-tzedakah-bring-wealth), or [giving when money is tight](/learn/giving-when-money-is-tight).

## Common questions

### What does the Tanya say about money?
Chapter 37 says the money you earn carries the energy of your own labor, so giving it to tzedakah lifts that energy to G-d. Epistle 11 says a person who truly trusts G-d knows his livelihood comes from G-d.

### Is wanting money wrong in Judaism?
The Tanya does not call earning wrong. Chapter 37 treats honest earnings as carrying real effort, and Epistle 11 asks us to want sustenance for the sake of serving G-d. Ask the Rabbi about your own situation.

### How should I stop worrying about money, according to Chabad?
Epistle 11 teaches trust in G-d as the spiritual answer. It does not offer financial advice, so seek practical help too, and talk to the Rabbi if the worry is heavy.

### Is giving tzedakah a promise of success?
Yes. The Torah and G-d promise success for all giving. Maaser is unique because the promise is stated clearly and we are invited to test it: G-d says "Test Me" about the tithe (Malachi 3:10), and the Talmud teaches "give a tenth in order that you will become wealthy" (Taanit 9a). For personal situations, call the Rabbi.

### Does tzedakah count if I earn little?
Chapter 37 says even someone who does not work for a living gives the life of his soul when he gives. Ask the Rabbi what is right for you; see [giving when money is tight](/learn/giving-when-money-is-tight).
