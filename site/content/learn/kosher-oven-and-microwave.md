The ideal in a kosher kitchen is to use each oven for only one category of food: meat, dairy or pareve. Meat and dairy should never be baked in the same oven at the same time, even in separate pans. A microwave is usually used only for sealed or covered food, and whether it can be made kosher after non-kosher use is a question for the Rabbi.

## The ideal: one oven, one category

Jewish law keeps meat and milk apart (see [meat and milk in Jewish law](/learn/meat-and-milk)). An oven is hot and enclosed, so the taste and steam of one food can reach another. The ideal, therefore, is to use each oven for meat, dairy or pareve only. If you have one oven, a separate toaster oven or portable broiler can serve the other category.

Never bake or broil meat and dairy in the same oven at the same time, even in separate bakeware. And dairy should not be baked in a meat oven, or meat in a dairy oven, without the guidance of a rabbi.

## One oven for both: only with the Rabbi

If you have just one oven, as many homes in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda do, using one oven for both meat and dairy, at different times, requires consulting a qualified rabbi. Do not decide this alone. Call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 and describe your oven and how you cook.

## Covering food

Covering is how an oven can stay "pareve." Here is how it works:

- Cover meat or dairy food tightly on all sides, including the bottom.
- Put foil under the pan, and change it when you switch between meat and dairy.
- Open the covered pan only after taking it out of the oven.

Pareve food (such as vegetables, or a kugel with no meat or dairy) may be baked in a meat or dairy oven and then served with the other category only if the oven, racks and broiler are thoroughly clean and 24 hours have passed since the oven last held meat (or dairy). If the oven is fully clean, the 24-hour wait is not needed. Foil under the bakeware helps keep the racks clean.

## Self-cleaning ovens

The self-clean cycle both cleans and koshers an oven in one step. This includes convection ovens that have a self-clean setting. Follow the manufacturer's instructions. A "steam clean" feature on newer ovens does not reach a high enough temperature to count. Ovens without self-clean are the hardest to kasher, and you should ask the Rabbi to guide you. For more, see [how to make your kitchen kosher](/learn/kosher-kitchen).

## The microwave

A microwave is best used for sealed or well-wrapped food. A non-kosher microwave or oven may be used if the food is sealed, and it is best to wrap it twice so one layer protects the food if the other bursts.

Can a microwave be kashered? Many authorities do not permit it. It cannot be done if the microwave has browning or convection features, or if you cannot confirm it has gone 24 hours unused. If there is a pressing need, most kashrut organizations allow it only under strict steps: clean thoroughly, treat the vents, wait 24 hours unused, wrap the glass turntable, and steam a cup of water until steam covers all the inside surfaces. Ask the Rabbi first.

## Passover

Passover has its own rules for ovens and microwaves. See [kosher for Passover](/learn/kosher-for-passover) and ask the Rabbi well before the holiday.

## Common questions

### Can I use one oven for both meat and dairy?
Ideally, no: each oven is used for only one category. Using one oven at different times for both needs the guidance of a qualified rabbi, and meat and dairy should never be in it at the same time.

### Is a self-cleaning oven kosher?
The self-clean cycle cleans and koshers an oven, including a convection oven with that cycle. A steam-clean setting is not enough. Ask the Rabbi about your specific oven.

### Can I use the same microwave for meat and dairy?
There is no simple rule for this; consult a qualified rabbi about microwaves. Ask Rabbi Labkowski what fits your kitchen.

### Can a microwave be made kosher?
Many authorities do not allow it, and it cannot be done for microwaves with browning or convection features. If there is a real need, ask the Rabbi about the steps.

### Do I need a separate toaster oven?
A toaster oven or portable broiler can serve the other category when you have one main oven, and portable broilers should be used for only one category because they cannot be properly koshered.

### Can I eat food from a non-kosher kitchen's oven or microwave?
Sealed, double-wrapped food can be heated in a non-kosher microwave or oven. Unwrapped food is a different question. Ask the Rabbi.
