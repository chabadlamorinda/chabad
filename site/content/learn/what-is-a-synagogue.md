A synagogue is a Jewish house of prayer, study and community. Jews gather there to pray together, read the Torah, learn and celebrate, and many people simply call it *shul*, the Yiddish word for "school."

## Three names, three purposes

The synagogue has several names, and each one tells you something about it:

- **Beit Knesset** ("house of gathering"): the Hebrew name. It is where the community comes together.
- **Beit Tefillah** ("house of prayer"): where Jews pray, especially with a [minyan](/learn/minyan) of ten.
- **Shul** ("school"): a reminder that the synagogue is also a place of Torah learning.

The word *synagogue* comes from Greek and also means "gathering place." Some communities call their synagogue a temple, but traditionally the word Temple refers to the [Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple) in Jerusalem.

## A short history

After the Holy Temple was destroyed, the synagogue became the center of Jewish life wherever Jews lived. The Talmud calls it a *mikdash me'at*, a "small sanctuary." Prayers replaced the Temple offerings, and the community gathered around the Torah instead of the altar.

## What you will see inside

- **The Aron Kodesh (Holy Ark):** a cabinet, usually on the wall facing Jerusalem, that holds the Torah scrolls.
- **The Torah scrolls:** handwritten on parchment, dressed in a cover and often crowned with silver.
- **The Ner Tamid (eternal light):** a light that always burns above the Ark, recalling the Menorah in the Temple.
- **The Bimah:** a raised table where the Torah is read.
- **Siddurim:** prayer books. Ask for one with English if you need it. See our guide to the [siddur](/learn/siddur).
- **The Mechitzah:** in traditional synagogues, a partition between the men's and women's sections, so everyone can focus on prayer.

## What happens at a synagogue

- **Daily prayers:** morning (Shacharit), afternoon (Mincha) and evening (Maariv).
- **Shabbat and holidays:** the busiest times, with the weekly [Torah reading](/learn/torah-reading), a short talk and often a Kiddush meal afterward.
- **Life events:** bar and bat mitzvahs, baby namings, aufrufs before a wedding, and saying Kaddish for a loved one.
- **Learning and community:** classes, children's programs and celebrations.

## Visiting a synagogue

You do not need to know anything to walk in. Dress respectfully; men cover their heads with a [kippah](/learn/kippah), which is provided. On Shabbat, phones are put away. Sit anywhere that is free, follow along as best you can, and do not worry about making mistakes. Our guide to [going to synagogue for the first time](/learn/first-time-at-synagogue) explains the service step by step.

## Common questions

### Do I need to be a member to go to a synagogue?
Many synagogues have membership, but at Chabad no membership is required. Every Jew is welcome at every service and holiday, whatever their background.

### What is the difference between a synagogue and a temple?
Both words are used for local Jewish houses of prayer. Traditionally, "the Temple" means the Holy Temple in Jerusalem, and a local house of prayer is called a synagogue or shul.

### Can a non-Jew visit a synagogue?
Yes. Non-Jewish friends and family members are welcome to visit and observe. Some parts of the service, such as being called to the Torah, are for Jewish participants.

### Can I pray at home instead of at a synagogue?
Yes, prayer at home is real prayer, and G-d hears every heart. Praying with a minyan adds special prayers, like Kaddish and the Torah reading, and the strength of a community.
