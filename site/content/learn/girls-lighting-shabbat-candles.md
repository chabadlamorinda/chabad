Yes. In Chabad custom, a Jewish girl lights her own Shabbat candle from about age three, once she can understand Shabbat and say the blessing, and she lights one candle until she marries. In 1974 the Lubavitcher Rebbe encouraged all Jewish girls to take up this mitzvah, with their parents' consent.

## Where the custom comes from

According to Jewish tradition, girls once lit candles as a matter of course, but the custom nearly disappeared after World War I. In 1974 the Rebbe, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson, asked Jewish girls to light Shabbat and holiday candles again. He said it should be with their parents' agreement, from about three years old, and that parents should buy a special candlestick for the girl. The idea is simple: a small child adds her own light to the world, and the home becomes brighter and calmer for it. For the full instructions every household can use, see [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).

## How many candles?

- **A girl lights one candle** until she is married.
- **After marriage,** a woman lights two, and many add one for each child.
- **If no woman** past Bat Mitzvah age is home, the man of the house lights.

## How a girl lights, step by step

1. **Pick a time.** Candle lighting is 18 minutes before sunset on Friday; see the [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times) for the week, and never light after sunset.
2. **Give tzedakah first.** A coin in the charity box makes it a ritual of giving. See [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).
3. **Her own candlestick.** She lights her own candle, in her own holder, near the table where the family will eat.
4. **She lights first.** Jewish tradition says the girl lights before her mother, so that her mother can help if needed.
5. **Cover the eyes** and say the blessing, then uncover and look at the light.
6. **Wish everyone "Shabbat Shalom."** Hugs and a family blessing are welcome. See [blessing your children](/learn/blessing-your-children).

Once lit, the candles are not moved until Shabbat ends.

## The blessing

**Hebrew:** בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו, וְצִוָּנוּ לְהַדְלִיק נֵר שֶׁל שַׁבָּת קֹדֶשׁ

**Transliteration:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu l'hadlik ner shel Shabbat kodesh.

**Translation:** Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us to light the candle of the holy Shabbat.

A very young child can say it a few words at a time after her mother. The blessing changes before holidays; ask the Rabbi.

## Making it a family habit

- **Practice the blessing** at bedtime in the days before.
- **Let her choose the candlestick** with you. A grandparent's gift of a candlestick is a lovely way to mark the start.
- **Safety:** a grown-up stays close. Place the candles away from curtains and little hands.
- **Keep it joyful.** A sweet song or a treat after lighting makes a good memory.
- **Start late if you have not started.** The tradition says a girl who has not begun can start on the Shabbat closest to her Bat Mitzvah. See [Bat Mitzvah](/learn/bat-mitzvah).

## Why it matters

Jewish tradition sees the home as a small sanctuary, and Shabbat candles as its light. A girl who lights is not only learning a ritual: she is told that she can change the atmosphere of her family. Chinuch, the education of children, begins with doing, not only with explaining; see [chinuch](/learn/chinuch).

## Common questions

### Should girls light Shabbat candles?
Yes. In Chabad custom, girls light one candle from about age three, a practice the Rebbe encouraged in 1974.

### How old should a girl be to light?
About three, once she understands Shabbat and can say the blessing. If she has not started, she can start at any age.

### How many candles does a girl light?
One, until she marries. After marriage she lights two, and many add one for each child.

### Does a girl light before her mother?
Yes. Jewish tradition says she lights first, so her mother can help her if needed.

### Does a girl need her own candlestick?
It is encouraged. A special candlestick, perhaps a gift, makes the mitzvah her own.

### What if we are away from home on Friday?
Light where you will eat Shabbat dinner. Ask the Rabbi for practical advice about hotels or places that limit open flames.
