A grant from a donor-advised fund counts as maaser (the tithe, usually a tenth of income given to tzedakah), as Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski has confirmed. To arrange a grant or a gift of stock, call Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski at 925-384-7242 or Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski at 925-384-7241. Chabad.org's guides to maaser do not address donor-advised funds or gifts of stock directly. What the guides do teach is a clear set of rules about intention, timing and who may receive maaser, and those rules show which questions to ask.

## What maaser is

Maaser (מַעֲשֵׂר) means "a tenth." Chabad.org explains that maaser kesafim, giving a tenth of income, is the common practice, and that giving up to a fifth (chomesh) is praiseworthy. It is a structured system, unlike tzedakah in general, which has no fixed percentage. See [Maaser](/learn/maaser).

Most opinions, Chabad.org notes, hold that maaser is not biblically mandated, so it may often be used for other mitzvahs and not only for the poor. Poor people are still the primary recipients, and poor relatives come first.

## What counts as income

According to Chabad.org's maaser guide, income includes salary, gifts, inheritance and interest. It does not include money you must repay, such as a loan. Investment gains count only once they are realized, for example when an asset is sold and the profit is received. Ideally, maaser is set aside when income is received, but it can also be calculated at any point during the year.

This matters for stock and other investments: the guide treats the gain as income when it is actually realized. How that works for a particular gift of stock, with a particular history, is a question for your tax advisor, and whether it counts as maaser is a question for the Rabbi.

## The questions about modern giving

**Does a donor-advised fund (DAF) count?** Yes. Rabbi Labkowski has confirmed that a donor-advised fund grant counts as maaser. Chabad.org's maaser guides do not discuss DAFs, but a grant recommended from your fund to Chabad of Lamorinda counts toward your tenth. Chabad of Lamorinda accepts donor-advised fund grants; call the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin to arrange one.

**Does giving stock count?** Chabad.org's guide mentions only that investment gains count as income once realized. Chabad of Lamorinda accepts gifts of stock. Please ask the Rabbi how it counts as maaser, and your tax advisor about taxes.

**Can I use maaser to pay a pledge?** Chabad.org says maaser can cover a pledge only if you intended to use maaser when you made the pledge. A pledge made without that intent must come from regular funds.

**Can I use maaser for something I am already required to do?** Chabad.org gives the example of Purim's required gifts to the poor (matanot la'evyonim), which must come from regular funds, and says that using maaser for your own Torah learning is problematic. Best practice, according to the guide, is to use your own money for mitzvahs when you can and reserve maaser for charity.

## A simple way to prepare

1. **Decide your intention first.** Is this gift maaser, or from other funds?
2. **Write down the details,** such as the date, the amount and whether it is a pledge.
3. **Keep records** for your tax advisor. Tax questions belong with your tax advisor and your attorney, not in this guide.
4. **Ask your Rabbi** before the gift, not after.

## Common questions

### Can maaser go to a donor-advised fund?
Yes. A donor-advised fund grant counts as maaser, as Rabbi Labkowski has confirmed, and Chabad of Lamorinda accepts these grants. To arrange one, contact Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski (925-384-7242) or Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski (925-384-7241).

### Does a gift of stock count as tzedakah?
Chabad of Lamorinda accepts gifts of stock; call 925-384-7242 to arrange one. How the value of a gift of stock is counted for maaser is a question for the Rabbi. Ask your tax advisor about taxes.

### Do I have to give maaser if I am struggling?
No. Chabad.org says basic needs come first, and you are not obligated to give maaser if you cannot cover them. The Rebbe encouraged giving a partial amount when needed. Ask the Rabbi. See [Giving Tzedakah When Money Is Tight](/learn/giving-when-money-is-tight).

### Is maaser calculated on gross or net income?
Chabad.org says mandatory withholdings such as income tax and Social Security can be deducted, but personal living expenses are not. Ask the Rabbi for your situation.

### Can I give at the end of the year?
Many people do. See [Year-End Tzedakah](/learn/year-end-tzedakah).

### Who can receive maaser?
Poor people are the primary recipients, and Chabad.org also lists communal institutions such as a synagogue. Ask the Rabbi about your cause.
