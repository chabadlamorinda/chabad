Rabbi Meir Baal Haness ("Master of Miracles") was a great sage of the Talmud. The custom is that when you lose something or face a hard moment, you give charity in his merit and call out three times, "G-d of Meir, answer me!"

## Who was Rabbi Meir?

Rabbi Meir lived in the Talmudic era and was described as the most distinguished scholar of his generation. His colleagues often did not follow his rulings because they could not fully grasp his reasoning. Some commentaries say that in the Messianic age halacha will follow his opinions. He asked to be buried standing, which hints that he keeps praying for others. Read more about the [Talmud](/learn/talmud).

## The custom

When someone faces a difficult situation and seeks help, they give charity and pledge the money in Rabbi Meir's merit. Then they say "G-d of Meir, answer me!" three times. The custom appears in *Keter Shem Tov*, attributed to the Baal Shem Tov. The Lubavitcher Rebbe often encouraged giving to this charity. Chabad.org notes that the original sources do not include the word "Rabbi" in the phrase.

## Where the words come from

The Talmud (Avodah Zarah 18a-b) tells how Rabbi Meir disguised himself to rescue a woman from a terrible fate. When wild dogs attacked him, he cried out "G-d of Meir, answer me" and was saved. The guard who helped him later said the same words when he faced punishment and was spared.

## When something is lost

Many Jews say the prayer when looking for a lost item. The practice, as Chabad.org presents it:

1. Say "G-d of Meir, answer me!" three times.
2. Pledge charity in the merit of Rabbi Meir, naming what you lost, and ask to find it.
3. Give the money to charity.
4. And, with a smile, keep searching. We must do our part.

If you find something that belongs to someone else, see [Returning lost items](/learn/returning-lost-items).

## Charity for the Land of Israel

"Tzedakah of Rabbi Meir Baal Haness" usually means funds for the poor of the Land of Israel. Chabad.org notes a different view that such money may go to other causes, if no specific charity was named when the pledge was made, so ask the Rabbi if in doubt. Colel Chabad, founded in 1788 by Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, is the oldest continuously operating charity for the poor in Israel.

## Why charity in his name?

The Talmud teaches that Jews are always called G-d's children. Rabbi Meir held this view regardless of conduct. Giving charity in his merit reflects that bond. And Rabbi Meir considered the minority outcome too, a source of hope for people in dire situations.

## Common questions

### Who is Rabbi Meir Baal Haness?
He was a Talmudic sage whose name means "Master of Miracles." Jews give charity in his merit when they need help.

### What do you say to find a lost item?
Say "G-d of Meir, answer me!" three times, pledge charity in Rabbi Meir's merit and keep looking.

### Do I have to say "Rabbi" in the prayer?
Chabad.org notes that the original sources do not include the word "Rabbi," and some suggest omitting it.

### Where does the charity go?
By common practice it goes to the poor of the Land of Israel, such as through Colel Chabad. Ask the Rabbi if you have questions.

### Is the prayer a replacement for searching?
No. We say it and also search. We do our part and trust G-d.

It is a Jewish custom for anyone seeking help. Call 925-384-7242 for personal questions.
