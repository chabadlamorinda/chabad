A niggun is a Jewish melody, usually sung without words, that expresses what words cannot: longing for G-d, joy, or a quiet stirring of the soul. Niggunim are at the heart of Chassidic life, sung at the Shabbat table, before prayer and at Chassidic gatherings called farbrengens.

## What a niggun is

Niggun is Hebrew for "melody." In Chassidic tradition, it usually means a tune sung with simple syllables like "ai, yai, yai" or "bim bam," or with a few words of a verse repeated many times. Many niggunim have been passed down for generations, and some are attributed to the great Chassidic Rebbes.

A well-known Chassidic saying puts it this way: words are the pen of the heart, and song is the pen of the soul. A niggun reaches the part of us that lies deeper than thought or speech.

## Why niggunim matter

- **They open the heart.** A niggun before prayer helps us leave daily worries behind and stand before G-d with feeling.
- **They lift the spirits.** The Baal Shem Tov taught that joy is essential in serving G-d, and a lively niggun can turn a heavy mood into a happy one.
- **They unite people.** When a room sings one tune together, differences fade. No one needs to know Hebrew or read music to join in.
- **They carry teaching.** Chassidic masters explain that certain niggunim mirror the soul's journey, from distance and longing to closeness and joy.

## Kinds of niggunim

1. **Dveikus niggunim:** slow, deep melodies of longing and attachment to G-d, often sung before prayer or at a farbrengen.
2. **Simcha niggunim:** fast, joyful tunes for dancing, weddings and holidays.
3. **Marches:** strong, uplifting tunes that express confidence and faith.
4. **Niggunim with words:** melodies set to verses of Torah or prayer, such as songs for the Shabbat table.

## Famous Chabad niggunim

- **The Alter Rebbe's Niggun,** a deep, contemplative melody associated with the founder of Chabad, Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi.
- **The Daled Bavos (Four Stages),** also attributed to the Alter Rebbe, whose four parts reflect four levels of the soul's ascent. It is sung with great respect, often at weddings and special occasions.
- **Niggunim taught by the Rebbe,** many of which the Rebbe introduced at farbrengens, often on Simchat Torah, and which are now sung around the world.

## How to sing a niggun, step by step

1. **Listen first.** Hear the tune a few times before joining.
2. **Start slowly.** Many niggunim begin quietly and build.
3. **Close your eyes** if it helps you focus inward.
4. **Repeat.** A niggun is meant to be sung again and again, each time a little deeper.
5. **Let the tune move you.** Do not worry about your voice. The niggun is about the soul, not performance.

## Common questions

### Do I need a good voice to sing niggunim?
Not at all. Niggunim are sung by everyone, in groups, with all kinds of voices. Sincerity matters far more than talent.

### Why are niggunim often sung without words?
Words limit what we can express. A wordless tune can carry feelings too deep for language, and anyone can sing it, whatever language they speak.

### When are niggunim sung?
At Shabbat and holiday meals, before and during prayer, at farbrengens, at weddings, and any time a person wants to lift their spirit. Some Chassidim hum a niggun while studying or working.

### Can a niggun help with prayer or meditation?
Yes. Chassidim often sing a slow niggun before prayer to prepare the heart. It pairs naturally with [Jewish meditation](/learn/jewish-meditation).
