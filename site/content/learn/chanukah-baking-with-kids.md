Chanukah baking with kids can be as easy as cutting dreidel cookies, tucking a piece of gelt into the dough, or decorating doughnuts with glaze and sprinkles. It is a way to bring the holiday's story into your kitchen, with adults handling anything hot.

## Ideas to start with

Chanukah dessert ideas include peanut butter gelt cookies with a chocolate-gelt center, a surprise cake with coins hidden inside, chocolate thumbprint cookies, honey and spice cookies, butter cookie dreidels, chocolate mint coins, gelt fudge, and colorful "paint-splatter" cookies cut with a dreidel cutter. Its kids' recipe page also has jelly doughnuts and a guide to decorating doughnuts. Those pages have the full amounts and steps, so open them for the recipes.

## Five easy projects

1. **Dreidel cookies.** Roll out dough, cut with a dreidel cookie cutter, and let the children decorate with icing. The four Hebrew letters on the dreidel stand for "a great miracle happened there" (see [dreidel](/learn/dreidel)).
2. **Gelt cookies.** Press a piece of chocolate gelt into the center of a baked cookie, a popular idea. Use pareve or dairy gelt to match the dough ([kosher gelt and candy](/learn/kosher-chanukah-candy-and-gelt)).
3. **Doughnut decorating.** Buy or bake plain doughnuts and set out bowls of glaze and sprinkles. Many families decorate doughnuts this way. This is also what children can do at the Chanukah celebration (see below).
4. **Paint-splatter cookies.** Colorful icing flicked on cookies, a favorite with children. Cover the table first.
5. **Gelt-in-a-cake.** A cake that hides chocolate coins inside, so the children find the "gelt" when they cut it.

Let the children measure, stir, press and sprinkle. An adult does all frying, oven work and hot oil. For frying safety, see [frying safely on Chanukah](/learn/frying-safely-on-chanukah).

## Kosher notes for baking

- **Look for a kosher symbol** on flour, chocolate, sprinkles, food coloring and jam ([kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols)).
- **Pareve or dairy?** Butter cookies are dairy, so serve them after a dairy meal. Oil-based cookies are pareve and can follow a meat meal ([pareve](/learn/pareve)).
- **Check the eggs** for blood spots before baking ([kosher eggs](/learn/kosher-eggs)).
- **Hidden ingredients** like gelatin or some glazes may not be kosher ([hidden kosher ingredients](/learn/hidden-kosher-ingredients)).
- **Dedicated pans.** If you keep kosher, use pans and cutters that were not used for non-kosher food.

## Allergies and safety

Peanut butter, nuts and eggs are common allergens, so ask before sharing treats with a class, a playdate or a party. Label what is in each treat. Keep small children away from the oven and the stove.

## Common questions

### What are easy Chanukah baking projects for kids?
Dreidel-shaped cookies, gelt cookies, decorated doughnuts and paint-splatter cookies. All have simple steps, and adults handle anything hot.

### Is Chanukah gelt kosher?
It is kosher only if the package has a reliable kosher symbol. See [kosher gelt and candy](/learn/kosher-chanukah-candy-and-gelt).

### Can kids help make sufganiyot?
Children can fill, glaze and decorate them, but an adult should do the frying. See [sufganiyot](/learn/sufganiyot).

### Should Chanukah cookies be pareve or dairy?
Either is fine. Pareve cookies can follow meat or dairy meals, while butter cookies are dairy. Plan around your meal.

### Where can my child decorate a doughnut in Lafayette?
At the Grand Chanukah Menorah Lighting on December 6 at 4:00 PM at Lafayette Plaza Park, which has a Decorate Your Own Donut.

### How do I keep Chanukah baking kosher?
Use ingredients with a kosher symbol, keep meat and dairy tools separate, and check eggs. Ask the Rabbi if something is unclear.
