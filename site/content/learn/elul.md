Elul is the last month of the Jewish year, the 30 days leading up to Rosh Hashanah. It is a time of reflection, [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah) and drawing closer to G-d, so we enter the new year prepared.

## What is Elul?

Elul usually falls in August or September. In Jewish history, it is the month when Moses went up Mount Sinai for the third time, for 40 days, to receive the second tablets after the sin of the golden calf. Those 40 days, from the first of Elul to Yom Kippur, were days of G-d's special favor and forgiveness.

The name Elul is often read as an acronym of the verse "Ani l'dodi v'dodi li," "I am my Beloved's and my Beloved is mine" (Song of Songs 6:3). It describes the love between G-d and the Jewish people.

## The King in the field

The founder of Chabad, Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, gave a famous parable. Usually, a king lives in his palace, and anyone who wants to see him must go through guards and protocol. But once a year, the king travels out to the fields. Anyone can come and greet him, and he receives everyone warmly.

During Elul, G-d is like the King in the field. He is close and approachable, wherever we are. It is the easiest time of year to reconnect.

## Elul customs

### Blowing the shofar

The [shofar](/learn/shofar) is blown each weekday morning after prayers, from the beginning of Elul until the day before Rosh Hashanah. It is a wake-up call to prepare. It is not blown on the day before Rosh Hashanah, to separate the custom from the Rosh Hashanah mitzvah.

### Psalm 27

From the first of Elul through Hoshana Rabbah, Psalm 27, "L'David Hashem ori," "G-d is my light," is said in morning and afternoon prayers.

### Three extra chapters of Tehillim

Chabad custom, from the Baal Shem Tov, is to say three extra chapters of [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) each day of Elul, from chapter 1 on, so that all 150 are completed by Yom Kippur.

### Checking mezuzahs and tefillin

Many have their [mezuzahs](/learn/mezuzah) and [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) checked by a scribe in Elul, to make sure they are kosher for the new year.

### Extra tzedakah and Torah study

Elul is a time to add in giving, learning and kindness.

### Selichot

Ashkenazi Jews begin saying [Selichot](/learn/selichot), prayers for forgiveness, in the last days of Elul.

### Wishing a good year

From the beginning of Elul, people wish each other "ketivah vachatimah tovah," "May you be written and sealed for a good year," and sign letters that way.

## How to make the most of Elul

1. **Take stock.** Set aside a few minutes each day to reflect on the past year.
2. **Choose one thing to improve.** Small, real steps matter more than big promises.
3. **Make amends.** Reach out to anyone you may have hurt, and ask forgiveness.
4. **Add one mitzvah.** Light Shabbat candles, put on tefillin, give tzedakah, or learn a little Torah daily.

## Common questions

### When does Elul start?
Elul begins on Rosh Chodesh Elul, which is two days, the 30th of Av and the 1st of Elul. You can find the exact dates with our [Hebrew date converter](/tools/hebrew-date).

### Is Elul a sad time?
Not at all. While it is serious, it is filled with closeness to G-d. The King is in the field and welcomes everyone.

### What if I have not been observant before?
That is exactly what Elul is for. Every Jew can come close to G-d right now, from wherever they are. Contact us if you would like to talk or learn together.

### Why is the shofar not blown on Shabbat in Elul?
Blowing the shofar is not done on Shabbat at any time of the year, including Elul.
