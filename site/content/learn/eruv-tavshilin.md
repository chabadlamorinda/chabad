An eruv tavshilin is a simple ritual done the day before a Jewish holiday that falls on Thursday or Friday, which allows you to cook on the holiday for Shabbat. You set aside a cooked food and a piece of bread or matzah before the holiday, say a blessing and a short declaration, and then save them for Shabbat.

## Why it is needed

On a Jewish festival (Yom Tov), cooking is allowed for that day's meals, but not for another day. When a holiday is on Friday, we need to cook for Shabbat, which follows right after. The Sages created the eruv tavshilin, "the mixing of cooked foods," so that the preparation for Shabbat begins before the holiday. The cooking on the holiday then continues something already started.

It also reminds us to keep Shabbat special and not let it be forgotten in the excitement of the holiday.

## When you need one

You make an eruv tavshilin on the day before the holiday whenever a festival day falls on Friday, for example:

- Rosh Hashanah on Thursday and Friday.
- The first or last days of Sukkot, Passover or Shavuot on Thursday and Friday, or on Friday alone.

## How to make an eruv tavshilin, step by step

1. **On the day before the holiday,** before sunset, take a plate.
2. **Put a cooked food on it,** such as a hard-boiled egg, a piece of fish or meat. Many use a hard-boiled egg.
3. **Add a baked food,** like a challah roll or a piece of matzah, at least the size of an egg.
4. **Hold the plate and say the blessing:**

**Hebrew:** בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו, וְצִוָּנוּ עַל מִצְוַת עֵרוּב

**Transliteration:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu al mitzvat eruv.

**Translation:** Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us concerning the mitzvah of eruv.

5. **Say the declaration,** printed in the siddur in Aramaic. You may say it in English: "Through this eruv, it shall be permitted for us to bake, cook, keep food warm, light candles, and do everything necessary on the holiday for Shabbat."
6. **Put the food aside** where no one will eat it by mistake.
7. **Eat it on Shabbat.** Many use the bread as one of the two loaves at a Shabbat meal and eat the cooked food at that meal.

## Important details

- **One per household** is enough. The person who makes it can include everyone in the home.
- **Keep the food until Shabbat preparations are done.** If it is eaten or lost before then, ask the Rabbi.
- **Finish cooking early.** Shabbat cooking should be done with time to spare, so the food could be eaten on the holiday itself.
- **Candles:** on a Friday holiday, light Shabbat candles before sunset from an existing flame.

## Common questions

### What if I forgot to make an eruv tavshilin?
Call the Rabbi right away. Many rabbis make an eruv tavshilin on behalf of everyone in their community who forgot. This can be relied on once, not every year.

### Can I make the eruv tavshilin on the holiday itself?
No. It must be made before the holiday begins, on the afternoon before.

### Does every family need its own eruv tavshilin?
Each household should make one. Guests staying with you for the holiday are included in yours.

### Can I use matzah instead of challah?
Yes. On Passover, matzah is used. At other times, any bread or matzah works.
