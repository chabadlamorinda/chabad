The teenage years can be the hardest years of parenting. Doors slam, phones never leave their hands, and the child who used to tell you everything now tells you nothing. Jewish tradition has walked with parents through this for thousands of years.

## "Educate a child according to his way"

King Solomon wrote: "Educate a child according to his way" (Proverbs 22:6). Every child is different. What works with one teen fails with another. The goal is not to make them copies of us, but to help each one become their best self.

## Love and limits

The Talmud teaches: "The left hand pushes away, and the right hand draws close." Firmness is sometimes needed, but love must always be stronger.

- **Spend time without an agenda:** a drive, a meal out, a walk. Teens often talk when they don't have to look at you.
- **Few rules, clearly explained,** and kept consistently. Explain the why.
- **Choose your battles.** Hair and clothes may not be worth a war. Safety and honesty are.
- **Never shame,** especially not in front of others.
- **Apologize when you are wrong.** It teaches more than any lecture.

## Screens and phones

Agree on rules together: phones out of bedrooms at night, no phones at the Shabbat table, and parents keeping the same rules. Shabbat itself is a weekly break from screens that many teens come to love.

## Jewish identity

Teens ask hard questions about G-d, Judaism and Israel. Welcome the questions: they mean your teen cares. Give them Jewish experiences of their own: a teen program, a trip, a mitzvah project, a role at the Seder or a Shabbat meal with their friends. See [Jewish teens in Lamorinda](/learn/jewish-teens-in-lamorinda).

## When to get help

If your teen seems withdrawn, hopeless, is using drugs or alcohol, or there is talk of self-harm, get professional help right away (in a crisis, call or text 988). For the everyday struggles, a conversation with the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin can help you see things differently and find the next step.

## Common questions

### My teen refuses to come to synagogue. Should I force them?
Forcing usually backfires. Keep the door open, keep Shabbat warm and welcoming at home, and find a Jewish activity they enjoy. Talk with the Rabbi about your teen.

### How do I talk to my teen about dating and relationships?
Early, often and calmly, with your values explained. See our guide to [Jewish dating](/learn/jewish-dating).

### My spouse and I disagree on how strict to be. What now?
Agree in private and present a united front. Children feel secure when parents are together.
