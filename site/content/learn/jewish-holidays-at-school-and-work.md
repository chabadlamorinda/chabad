Jewish holidays follow the Hebrew calendar, so they fall on different weekdays every year, often in the middle of the school or work week. With a little planning, you can keep the holidays fully and still keep teachers and employers happy.

## Which days matter most

The holidays when work, school, driving and writing are not done (like Shabbat) are:

- **Rosh Hashanah**, two days, in September or early October.
- **Yom Kippur**, one day, ten days later.
- **Sukkot**, the first two days, and **Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah**, two more days, a week later.
- **Passover**, the first two days and the last two days, in March or April.
- **Shavuot**, two days, in May or June.

Each begins at sundown the evening before. Other days, like Chanukah and Purim, are not days off from work, though Purim has mitzvahs to fit into the day. Every year's dates are on our [Jewish holidays](/holidays) page.

## At school

California law lets students be excused from school for the observance of a holiday or ceremony of their religion, with a note from the parent. Absences for religious holidays are excused, and students may make up work and tests.

Tips:
- **Send the dates in September** for the whole school year, so teachers can plan tests around them.
- **Talk to coaches and club leaders** about games and performances on holidays.
- **Colleges** usually have a religious accommodation policy: email professors in the first week of the semester.

## At work

Employers in California must reasonably accommodate an employee's religious observance unless it causes real hardship, and most employers are glad to work it out. Common solutions: using vacation or personal days, swapping shifts, or making up hours.

Tips:
- **Give notice early**, with all the dates for the year.
- **Plan coverage** for your work and offer to make up time.
- **Remember Fridays in winter**, when Shabbat begins early. See [Shabbat in the winter](/learn/shabbat-in-winter).

## A sample note

"Dear Ms. Cohen, our family observes the Jewish holidays. Our son will be absent on [dates] for Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur and Sukkot. We would be grateful if tests and assignments due on those days could be made up. Thank you for your understanding."

## Common questions

### Will my child be marked absent?
The absence is excused when you send a note for a religious holiday. It may still appear on the record as an excused absence. Ask the school how it records it.

### Can my employer refuse?
An employer may refuse only if the accommodation would cause undue hardship. Most cases are solved by using personal days or swapping shifts. For a real dispute, speak with an employment lawyer.

### Do we need to take off Chanukah?
No. Chanukah is not a day off from work or school. Light the menorah at home each night.
