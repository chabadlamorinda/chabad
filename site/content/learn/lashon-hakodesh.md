Lashon HaKodesh (לְשׁוֹן הַקֹּדֶשׁ) means "the Holy Tongue," the traditional name for the Hebrew of the Torah and prayer. Jewish tradition teaches that it is the language through which G-d created the world, and that is why it is holy.

## What "Holy Tongue" means

Chabad.org notes that Jews traditionally call Hebrew Lashon HaKodesh. Two classic explanations are given. Maimonides said the language earned the name because it has no words for lewd acts or private parts of the body. Nachmanides said it is the language G-d used to convey His will to the prophets.

## The language of creation

The Torah describes G-d creating the world through ten utterances. Chabad.org explains the Kabbalistic teaching that these were spoken in Hebrew, and that G-d continually renews creation, so that each thing receives its life through its Hebrew name. In the Chassidic teaching of the Tanya, the letters are like building blocks, and words are channels of spiritual life. See [Tanya](/learn/tanya) and [gematria](/learn/gematria).

This helps explain why a Hebrew name matters. See [Hebrew names](/learn/hebrew-names).

## Torah Hebrew and modern Hebrew

Today's Hebrew, **Ivrit** (עִבְרִית), was revived as an everyday spoken language by Zionist thinkers in the late 19th century, after more than a thousand years in which it was not commonly spoken. Chabad.org notes that modern Hebrew differs from Biblical Hebrew in syntax and pronunciation, and some maintain it is not the same as Lashon HaKodesh. Still, the written alphabet is the same, and an Israeli child can read much of the Torah. For example, the word for "yes," *ken*, is modern; the Bible has no plain word for "yes."

## Why we pray in Hebrew, and does G-d hear other languages?

Most Jewish prayers are in Hebrew, which is why children learn to read before they understand. Many Jews also say personal prayers in the language they think in, and G-d hears every language. Whether a given prayer may be said in another language is a question for the Rabbi. See [why pray](/learn/why-pray) and [talking to G-d in your own words](/learn/talking-to-g-d-in-your-own-words).

## Learning to read

The Hebrew alphabet has 22 letters, all consonants. In prayer books vowel dots (*nekudot*) show how to read. In a Torah scroll there are none. Many adults learn to read in a few weeks. See [the Hebrew alphabet](/learn/hebrew-alphabet) and [Hebrew reading for adults](/learn/hebrew-reading-for-adults).

## Common questions

### What does Lashon HaKodesh mean?
It means "the Holy Tongue," the traditional name for Hebrew as used in the Torah and prayer.

### Why is Hebrew called the holy language?
Tradition holds that G-d created the world through Hebrew speech and used it to speak to the prophets. Maimonides adds that it has no words for lewd acts.

### Is modern Hebrew the same as Biblical Hebrew?
They share an alphabet and root words, but differ in syntax and pronunciation. Some say modern Hebrew is not the same as Lashon HaKodesh.

### Can I pray in English?
Yes, you can speak to G-d in any language. For the set prayers, ask the Rabbi.

### Do I need to know Hebrew to be Jewish?
No. Every Jew belongs, and you can learn at any age.
