Yes, Jewish men go to the mikvah too: it is a Jewish custom, not a requirement for everyone, to immerse in a mikvah (מִקְוֶה), a pool of natural water, on the day before Yom Kippur, before Shabbat and holidays, and, for many Chassidim, every day before prayer. It is a way to renew and prepare spiritually. Rabbi Labkowski in Lafayette can tell you where and when.

## What a mikvah is

A mikvah is a pool built according to Jewish law, filled with natural water, in which a person immerses completely. Many people think of it as a women's mitzvah, tied to [family purity](/learn/family-purity), and it is, but men have their own traditions. Our general guide explains [what a mikvah is](/learn/mikvah). Women in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda: see [mikvah near Lafayette](/learn/mikvah-near-lafayette).

## Before Yom Kippur

Chabad.org describes it as a widely accepted custom for men to immerse in a mikvah on the day before Yom Kippur, to honor the sanctity of the day and to repent and be purified. Today it is among the most widely kept mikvah customs of the year, and many men who go at no other time go then. The custom is not to say a blessing on this immersion. See [the day before Yom Kippur](/learn/erev-yom-kippur) and [Yom Kippur for beginners](/learn/yom-kippur-for-beginners).

## Every day: the Chassidic custom

Many Chassidic Jews immerse every day before morning prayer. There are two reasons. First, Ezra the Scribe decreed that a man who had become impure should not pray or study Torah before immersing; most authorities say this decree is no longer in force, but many still immerse before prayer, which is described as making prayer "more accepted." Second, even when pure, a man is compared to a priest preparing to serve in the Temple, so it is fitting to immerse before standing before G-d.

## Before Shabbat and holidays

Some also immerse before Shabbat and the holidays, or on days when the Torah is read, to add holiness to the day. Many men do this on Friday afternoon, before [Shabbat](/learn/shabbat). It is a good example of a custom you can take on slowly.

## What it means

Chabad teaching connects the Hebrew word for immersion, tevilah (טבילה), to bittul (ביטול), self-nullification. When you go under the water, you let go of your ego and come up with humility. Chassidic teaching also says that immersion can revive a dull mind and prepare it for prayer and [tefillin](/learn/tefillin). The Chassidic masters also say it strengthens faith and understanding.

## Common questions

### Do men go to the mikvah?
Yes. Jewish men immerse by custom, especially before Yom Kippur, and many do so before Shabbat, holidays and daily prayer.

### Why do men immerse before Yom Kippur?
To honor the sanctity of the day and to repent and be purified. It is a widely accepted custom for men, and every man is encouraged to do it.

### Is it required for a man to go to the mikvah?
Most of these are customs, not requirements. For a personal question, ask the Rabbi.

### Do you say a blessing when men immerse before Yom Kippur?
The custom is not to say a blessing for this immersion.

### Why do some Chassidic men immerse every day?
To prepare for prayer, as a priest prepared for Temple service, and to bring humility and fresh inspiration to prayer.
