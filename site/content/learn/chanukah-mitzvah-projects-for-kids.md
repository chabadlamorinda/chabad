The best Chanukah mitzvah project for a child is one they can do with their own hands: put part of their gelt in a tzedakah box, make a card for someone who is alone, or bring a menorah to a friend. Chanukah is all about light, and the more we share it, the brighter the holiday becomes.

## Start with the gelt

[Chanukah gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt) is the traditional way children learn about giving. Jewish tradition explains that a part of the gelt is meant to go to tzedakah, charity, and suggests about ten percent, with the rest used for good, wholesome things. Chanukah is also a time when many people increase their giving.

1. **Count the gelt together.** Let your child see how much they received.
2. **Decide a share.** A tenth is the classic amount; a child can choose to give more.
3. **Choose where it goes.** A child feels more connected when they pick the cause: helping people who are sick, lonely or in need.
4. **Put it in the pushka.** A small box at home helps. See [pushka](/learn/pushka) and [children, money and tzedakah](/learn/children-money-and-tzedakah).
5. **Give it together.** Drop the gift in the box at the end of the night, or give through [Chabad of Lamorinda's giving page](/donate?fund=holidays#give).

A child who gives part of what they have learns that the money is a tool for kindness. In a talk to the Chabad community, the Rebbe asked everyone to increase their tzedakah during Chanukah, and he spoke about the importance of educating children, starting with our own.

## Ideas for kids of every age

- **Make cards for seniors.** Children can draw menorahs and write a few words. Write "Happy Chanukah" and sign. Cards can go to a neighbor, a grandparent or someone in a care facility.
- **Share a menorah and treats.** The tradition suggests bringing menorahs, treats and handmade cards to people in hospitals and nursing homes who may not have family nearby. A family can keep a "menorah kit" ready for the unexpected. Check first with the facility and with the Rabbi.
- **Give a menorah to a friend.** A simple menorah, candles and a note about how to light them is a gift that can start a new tradition.
- **Invite someone who is alone.** A neighbor or classmate could join you for the lighting and a latke. Hospitality is a mitzvah.
- **Call or video a grandparent.** A phone call with a song is a real kindness.
- **Collect food or warm clothes.** Ask your child's school or your neighbors, and bring the items to a local organization.
- **Sponsor a kindness.** Your family can sponsor visits and help for those in need through the [Chesed Fund](/donate?fund=chesed#give).

## How to guide your child

1. **Talk about why.** The light of the menorah is not for us to use; it is to be seen and shared. That is a good picture for kindness.
2. **Let your child choose.** Children care more about what they pick.
3. **Make it a habit, not a one-time event.** A little each night is better than one big gesture.
4. **Thank them.** Notice the effort, not only the result.

For more ways to teach children to give, see [volunteering in Lamorinda](/learn/volunteering-in-lamorinda) and [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).

## Common questions

### What is a good mitzvah project for kids on Chanukah?
Giving part of their gelt to tzedakah, making cards for seniors and sharing a menorah with a friend are all easy and meaningful.

### How much of the gelt should a child give to tzedakah?
Tradition suggests about ten percent. A child may give more if they wish.

### Why do we give gelt on Chanukah?
Several reasons are given, including rewarding children for learning Torah and teaching them about charity. See [Chanukah gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt).

### Can children visit a nursing home on Chanukah?
Often yes, with a parent and after checking with the facility. Bring a menorah, treats and cards. Ask the Rabbi if you are unsure.
