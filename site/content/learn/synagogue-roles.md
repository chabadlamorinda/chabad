In a synagogue, the rabbi teaches and guides the community, the gabbai (גבאי) organizes the service and gives out honors, the chazzan (חזן) leads the prayers, and the baal koreh (בעל קורא) reads the Torah. Most of these jobs are done by volunteers from the congregation, and a first-time visitor can usually be given a small honor if they wish.

## The rabbi: teacher and guide

Chabad.org describes the rabbi as the congregation's spiritual guide, who often gives sermons and classes. The rebbetzin, the rabbi's wife, also has a leadership role in the community. A rabbi is not a priest and does not stand between you and G-d. He is a teacher who answers questions about Jewish law and life, and he is the one to ask about case-by-case questions. See [What Is a Rabbi?](/learn/what-is-a-rabbi).

## The gabbai: the one who runs the service

The word gabbai is often translated as "warden." Chabad.org says the gabbai helps organize and run the synagogue. In practice, in many synagogues he:

- Decides who is honored with each part of the Torah reading and calls them up by name.
- Makes sure the service has what it needs: books, a Torah, a reader.
- Gives people the honors, and announces page numbers or customs when needed.

If you are not sure where to sit or what to do, he is a good person to ask. Gabbai duties differ from one community to another, so ask the Rabbi about how it works in a given place.

## The chazzan or shliach tzibbur: the prayer leader

The chazzan (also called the *shliach tzibbur*, שליח ציבור, "the community's messenger") leads the prayers aloud. Chabad.org says that in most Orthodox communities, the chazzan is a volunteer from the congregation, though some communities hire a professional cantor. He reads the opening and closing lines of prayers aloud, and some prayers are reserved for the chazzan when there is a minyan of ten: Barchu, Kaddish and the repetition of the Amidah. Under Jewish law, a man past bar mitzvah age leads, and the congregation answers "Amen." Read [Leading the Prayers](/learn/leading-the-prayers) for more, and [minyan](/learn/minyan).

## The baal koreh: the Torah reader

The baal koreh (בעל קורא, "master of reading") reads the weekly portion from the Torah scroll, which has no vowels or punctuation, in a special melody. It takes careful preparation. He reads while the person honored with an aliyah stands beside him and says a blessing before and after. Learn more in [Torah reading](/learn/torah-reading) and the [weekly portion](/parsha).

## Other honors you may see

- **Aliyah** (עליה, "going up"): being called up to the Torah for a blessing.
- **Hagbah** (הגבהה): lifting the open Torah so the congregation can see the text.
- **Gelilah** (גלילה): dressing and rolling the Torah scroll closed.
- **Opening the Ark**, and carrying the Torah around the room.
- **Kiddush sponsor:** a family that sponsors the Kiddush after services in honor of a simcha or in memory of a loved one.

## If you are given an honor

If a gabbai offers you an honor, you may accept or politely decline. Nobody expects a newcomer to know everything. Tell him or the Rabbi that it is your first time, and he will tell you where to stand and what to say. Chabad.org suggests that a shy newcomer simply tell the rabbi or a congregant it is their first visit. For a Hebrew name and the blessings, ask for a transliterated card. ## Common questions

### What is a gabbai in a synagogue?
A gabbai is the person who organizes the service and gives out the honors, often translated as "warden." The role differs from one synagogue to another.

### What does a chazzan do?
The chazzan leads the congregation in prayer, reading key parts aloud and representing the community. In most Orthodox synagogues he is a volunteer from the congregation.

### What is a baal koreh?
The baal koreh is the person who reads the Torah aloud from the scroll, following its traditional melody.

### Who leads services in a synagogue?
The chazzan leads the prayers, while the gabbai organizes, and the rabbi teaches and guides. Different people may lead different parts of the service.

### Can a first-timer be given an honor?
Yes, often. Tell the gabbai or Rabbi that it is your first visit, and they will guide you through it.

### Is a rabbi the same as a priest?
No. A rabbi is a teacher and legal guide, not an intermediary between you and G-d.
