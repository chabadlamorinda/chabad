Tevilat kelim is the mitzvah of immersing new metal and glass food utensils in a mikvah or natural body of water before using them. It applies to dishes, pots and cutlery made or owned by a non-Jew and bought for use with food.

## What it is and why we do it

The Torah describes the Jewish people immersing utensils taken from Midian before using them (Numbers 31:23). From this the Sages learned that utensils coming from non-Jewish ownership into Jewish use are immersed in a [mikvah](/learn/mikvah).

Tevilah is not about cleaning. A brand-new pot is already clean. It is a spiritual transition, bringing the utensil into the holiness of a Jewish kitchen. Just as a person immerses in a mikvah to mark a new level of holiness, so does a dish.

## Which items need immersion

**Immerse with a blessing:**
- Metal: stainless steel, aluminum, cast iron, copper, silver
- Glass, including Pyrex and glass bakeware

**Immerse without a blessing (common custom):**
- Glazed porcelain and ceramics
- Some coated items such as enamel pots

**No immersion needed:**
- Wood, plain unglazed earthenware, paper, and disposable items
- Plastic, by most opinions (some immerse it without a blessing)

Only items that touch food directly need tevilah. A can opener or a storage container for unwrapped food may be different questions. Ask the Rabbi about specific items, especially appliances like toasters and kettles.

## How to immerse, step by step

1. **Remove labels and glue.** Anything stuck to the item blocks the water. Soak off stickers and residue completely.
2. **Go to a kelim mikvah,** a mikvah built for utensils, or a natural body of water such as the ocean or a lake. Not every river or stream qualifies, so ask.
3. **Hold the item loosely,** or wet your hand first, so the water reaches every surface.
4. **Say the blessing** (below) before the first immersion.
5. **Immerse the item completely,** all at once, so it is fully underwater for a moment.
6. **Take it out.** It is ready to use.

If you immerse several items, say one blessing for all of them.

## The blessing

**Hebrew:** בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו, וְצִוָּנוּ עַל טְבִילַת כֵּלִים

**Transliteration:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu al tevilat keilim.

**Translation:** Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us concerning the immersion of vessels.

For a single item, many say "al tevilat keli."

## Common questions

### Can I use a new pot before it is immersed?
No. Utensils that need tevilah should not be used for food until they are immersed. If you are stuck, ask the Rabbi; there are solutions for unusual situations.

### Do dishes from a Jewish-owned store need immersion?
It depends on who made and owned the item. If a Jewish company manufactured it and it was only ever owned by Jews, immersion may not be needed. Most items are made by non-Jewish companies, so they do need it.

### Do I immerse an electric kettle or toaster?
Appliances can usually be immersed and left to dry well for several days before use. Because of the details involved, ask the Rabbi about each appliance.

### Do I need to immerse dishes again after moving?
No. Once a utensil is immersed, it does not need to be immersed again, even if you move or store it.
