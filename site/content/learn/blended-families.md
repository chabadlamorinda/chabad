Many families today are blended: a parent remarries, and children, stepparents and step-siblings learn to become one home. It can be a great blessing, and it can be hard. Jewish tradition has real wisdom for it, starting with one simple idea: a home is built on peace, patience and kindness, one day at a time.

## Honoring a stepparent

The Talmud teaches that the mitzvah of honoring parents includes honoring a father's wife and a mother's husband. Jewish law asks children to treat a stepparent with respect, especially while their own parent is alive, out of honor for that parent too. Respect does not have to mean instant closeness; it means courtesy, kindness and not speaking badly. Love often grows from there.

## For the stepparent

- **Go slowly.** Children need time. Let warmth grow before discipline; the child's own parent usually leads on rules at first.
- **Never compete with the other parent.** A child should never feel they must choose. Speak respectfully of the other parent, always.
- **Be a steady presence.** Show up for school events, Shabbat dinner, the small moments. Trust is built in ordinary days.

## Bringing two families together

- **Shabbat is a gift.** A weekly Shabbat dinner, with candles, Kiddush, challah and everyone at the table, gives a new family a rhythm and a sense of "us." See [Shabbat](/learn/shabbat).
- **Make new traditions** while honoring old ones: each child's favorite holiday food, a new family custom at Chanukah.
- **Fairness matters.** Children watch closely how gifts, attention and rules are shared.

## Holidays and simchas

Holidays, Bar and Bat Mitzvahs and weddings can bring up hard feelings about who stands where. Plan ahead, talk openly, and keep the child at the center: it is their day. The Rabbi is glad to help think through a Bar or Bat Mitzvah or a wedding with a blended family, so everyone feels included.

## Peace in the home

The Sages call peace in the home, [shalom bayit](/learn/shalom-bayit), so precious that G-d Himself bends the truth to protect it. In a blended family, peace is built with patience, gentle words, a willingness to give in on small things, and lots of forgiveness.

## Common questions

### Do I have to call my stepparent "Mom" or "Dad"?
No. What matters is respect and kindness. Many families find a name that feels right for everyone.

### My stepchild is not Jewish, or only one side keeps traditions. What do we do?
Every family's situation is different. Please speak privately with the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin; they will listen and help with warmth.

### Can my stepchild say Kaddish for me, or I for a stepparent?
There are customs about saying Kaddish for a stepparent. Ask the Rabbi about your situation.
