Every child deserves to feel safe and respected. When a child is bullied, in person or online, it can hurt deeply. Judaism takes the dignity of every person, and the power of words, very seriously.

## Jewish values

- **Every person is created in G-d's image.** Humiliating someone is compared in the Talmud to spilling blood.
- **Words matter.** The Torah forbids hurting people with words (ona'at devarim) and spreading gossip (see [lashon hara](/learn/lashon-hara)).
- **Don't stand idly by** (Leviticus 19:16): bystanders who stay silent share the harm; those who speak up do a mitzvah.

## If your child is being bullied

1. **Listen calmly** and thank them for telling you. Believe them.
2. **Tell them it is not their fault.**
3. **Write down what happened,** with dates and screenshots for online bullying.
4. **Contact the school in writing** and ask what steps will be taken. Follow up.
5. **Build their confidence:** a friend group, an activity they're good at, a Jewish program where they feel at home.
6. **Watch their mood.** If they seem withdrawn or hopeless, get professional help (in a crisis, 988).

If the bullying is about being Jewish, see [facing antisemitism](/learn/facing-antisemitism).

## If your child is the one bullying

Stay calm, take it seriously, and find out why. Help them make it right with a sincere apology and changed behavior. That is [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah) in action, and a lesson they will carry for life.

## Raising kind kids

Praise kindness more than grades. Talk at the Shabbat table about doing a kindness this week. Encourage them to sit with the child who is alone.

## Common questions

### Should I contact the other child's parents?
Usually it is better to go through the school first. If you do talk to the parents, stay calm and stick to facts.

### What about online bullying?
Save evidence, block and report the account, involve the school, and limit apps where it happens.

### Can my child talk to the Rabbi?
Yes. A trusted adult outside the family can help a child feel stronger. Call 925-384-7242.

### How do I help my child stand up for others?
Practice simple words together ("Stop. That's not okay.") and praise them when they act.
