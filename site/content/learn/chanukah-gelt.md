Chanukah gelt is money given to children, and often to teachers and others, during Chanukah. Gelt is Yiddish for "money," and the custom is centuries old, long before chocolate coins existed.

## Why we give gelt

There are several reasons for this beloved custom:

- **Education.** The word Chanukah is related to chinuch, "education." The Greeks tried to stop Torah learning, and the Maccabees restored it. Giving children money rewards and encourages their Jewish learning.
- **Celebrating Jewish freedom.** After their victory, the Maccabees eventually ruled an independent Jewish state that minted its own coins. Coins became a symbol of that freedom.
- **Tzedakah.** Chanukah gelt gives children the chance to give charity from their own money, and in the past it helped poorer families and Torah teachers during the winter.
- **Joy.** Chanukah is a time of light and celebration, and children feel it most when they receive something.

## When gelt is given

Chabad custom, recorded in the book of Chabad customs, is to give Chanukah gelt on the fourth or fifth night. The Rebbe encouraged giving gelt every night of Chanukah, and on Friday giving a double portion for Shabbat, when money is not handled.

Many families give gelt right after lighting the menorah, while the candles are burning.

## How to give gelt, step by step

1. **Light the menorah** together and sing.
2. **Give each child coins** or bills. The amount matters less than the moment.
3. **Set aside some for tzedakah.** Encourage children to put part of their gelt in a [pushka](/learn/pushka), a charity box.
4. **Let them decide** how to use or save the rest.
5. **Play dreidel** with chocolate coins, nuts or pennies. See our [dreidel](/learn/dreidel) guide.

## Gelt and Jewish values

Chanukah gelt teaches that money is a tool. We receive it as a gift from G-d, and we use it to do good. When children give some of their own gelt to tzedakah, they learn early that giving is part of having.

Some families also connect gelt to learning: a coin for each Jewish fact a child learned, each blessing they said, or each Chanukah story they can retell.

## Chocolate gelt

Gold-wrapped chocolate coins came much later, but they are now part of the fun. Many families give both real money and chocolate. If you buy chocolate gelt, look for a reliable kosher symbol. See our [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols) guide.

## Common questions

### How much Chanukah gelt should I give?
There is no set amount. Give what is comfortable and meaningful for your family. Some give a little each night, others a larger gift once.

### Is Chanukah gelt the same as Chanukah gifts?
Gelt is the traditional custom. Presents became common later. Many families keep the focus on gelt, light and family time rather than big gifts.

### Do adults get gelt too?
Traditionally, gelt was given to children and to teachers. Many grandparents give it to grown grandchildren too, and it is always a nice gesture to support Torah teachers at Chanukah time.

### Can I give gelt on Shabbat?
No. Money is not handled on Shabbat, which is why the Rebbe suggested giving a double portion on Friday before candle lighting.
