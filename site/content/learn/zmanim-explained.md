Zmanim (זְמַנִּים, "times") are the times of day set by Jewish law for prayers and mitzvahs, such as the latest time to say the morning Shema, midday, sunset and nightfall. They are worked out from the sun's position in the sky, so they change every day and from place to place, and you can see them for Lafayette on [our Shabbat times page](/shabbat-times).

## Why the times are tied to the sun

Many mitzvahs belong to a particular part of the day or night. The morning Shema, the afternoon Mincha prayer, the evening Shema and the end of Shabbat all depend on whether it is day or night and how far along the day is. Chabad.org explains that its zmanim are astronomical: some are fixed by how far the sun's center sits below the horizon, and others are counted in proportional hours.

We never type the times by hand on this site; the calendar computes them for Lafayette and the nearby towns. Use this guide to understand what each name means, and look up the actual times in [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times) or the [Jewish calendar](/learn/jewish-calendar).

## A short glossary of the times

Here are the main zmanim, in the order they happen through the day, with definitions based on Chabad.org:

- **Alot Hashachar (עלות השחר), dawn:** the moment the first light appears on the eastern horizon.
- **Misheyakir (מִשֶּׁיַּכִּיר):** when there is enough light to recognize an acquaintance from a short distance. It is ideally the earliest time to put on a tallit and tefillin and to say the morning Shema.
- **Netz or Hanetz Hachamah (הנץ החמה), sunrise:** when the top edge of the sun becomes visible. It is ideally the earliest time for the morning Amidah.
- **Sof Zman Shema (סוף זמן קריאת שמע), latest time for the morning Shema:** the end of the third proportional hour of the day.
- **Sof Zman Tefilah (סוף זמן תפילה), latest time for the morning prayer:** the end of the fourth proportional hour. If missed, Shacharit can be said until midday.
- **Chatzot (חצות), midday:** the midpoint between sunrise and sunset.
- **Mincha Gedola:** half an hour after midday, the earliest time for the afternoon prayer.
- **Mincha Ketana:** two and a half proportional hours before the day ends.
- **Plag Hamincha:** one and a quarter proportional hours before the day ends; it is the earliest time to light Shabbat candles.
- **Shkiah (שקיעה), sunset:** when the top edge of the sun disappears. Daytime mitzvahs should be done by then.
- **Tzeit Hakochavim (צאת הכוכבים), nightfall:** when three medium stars appear. This is the start of night for the evening Shema.

Shabbat and holidays end at a separate, slightly later time, when a smaller cluster of three stars is visible. Look for it in [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times).

## The halachic hour: why times shift

A halachic hour, called a shaah zmanit (שעה זמנית, "proportional hour"), is not 60 minutes. The daylight period from sunrise to sunset is divided into twelve equal parts, and each part is one hour. So in summer, when days are long, the hour is longer than 60 minutes; in winter it is shorter. Chabad.org gives an example: with sunrise at 5:00 am and sunset at 7:30 pm, there are 14.5 hours of daylight, and each halachic hour is 72.5 minutes, so the third hour ends at 8:37:30 am. (Another view counts from dawn to nightfall; this is why a "latest Shema" may differ between calendars.)

This is also why the "latest Shema" time changes every day, so check it each morning. It is a good thing to know when you read about [Shabbat in winter](/learn/shabbat-in-winter), when Shabbat begins early.

## When does the Jewish day begin?

The Jewish day starts at nightfall, not at midnight or sunrise, which is why Shabbat and holidays begin on the evening before. This comes from the Torah's description of creation: "And it was evening, and it was morning." So the evening [Shema](/learn/shema) and Maariv belong to the start of the new day. See [Mincha and Maariv](/learn/mincha-and-maariv) and [daily prayers](/learn/daily-prayers) for how the day's prayers fit these times.

## Add a safety margin

Chabad.org advises adding or subtracting at least two minutes to every published time, and not relying on the times until the last minute. Temperature, air pressure, clocks, differences within a city and rounding all matter. At very high latitudes, or for family purity and a bris near sunset, ask a rabbi.

## Common questions

### What are zmanim?
Zmanim are the halachic (Jewish law) times of day for mitzvahs, based on the sun, such as sunrise, latest Shema, midday, sunset and nightfall.

### What is the latest time to say the morning Shema?
It is the end of the third proportional hour of the day. Check the exact time for Lafayette on our Shabbat times page, and if you miss it, ask the Rabbi what to do.

### What is a halachic hour?
It is one twelfth of the daylight period, so it is longer in summer and shorter in winter.

### Why does the Jewish day start at night?
Because the Torah says of each day of creation, "And it was evening, and it was morning."

### Why add a few minutes to the times?
Because of temperature, air pressure, clocks and rounding. Chabad.org suggests at least two minutes of margin.
