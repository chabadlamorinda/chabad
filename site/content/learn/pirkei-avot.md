Pirkei Avot, "Chapters of the Fathers," usually called Ethics of the Fathers, is a small book of big wisdom. It collects the favorite sayings of the great Sages of the Mishnah about how to live: how to treat people, how to learn, how to work, and how to stay humble. Many of its lines have become everyday Jewish sayings.

## What is it?

Pirkei Avot is a tractate of the [Mishnah](/learn/mishnah), the first written collection of the Oral Torah, compiled by Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi about 1,800 years ago. Unlike the rest of the Mishnah, which deals with laws, Avot is almost all about character and values. It has five chapters, plus a sixth chapter about the greatness of Torah study that was added for reading on Shabbat.

It opens with the chain of tradition: "Moses received the Torah from Sinai and passed it to Joshua, Joshua to the Elders, the Elders to the Prophets..." The message: these teachings are not just good advice. They are part of the Torah given at Sinai. Our Sages teach that one who wants to be truly pious should study and live by the words of Avot.

## When is it studied?

It is customary to study one chapter of Pirkei Avot every Shabbat afternoon, after Mincha, in the weeks between Passover and Shavuot. In Chabad and many other communities, the cycle continues through the whole summer until the Shabbat before Rosh Hashanah, going through the six chapters several times.

Before each chapter we say: "All Israel has a share in the World to Come." After it we say: "G-d wanted to bring merit to Israel, so He gave them much Torah and many mitzvot."

## A handful of famous teachings

**"The world stands on three things: on Torah, on avodah (serving G-d), and on acts of kindness."** (Shimon the Righteous, 1:2)
A good life needs all three: learning, a relationship with G-d through prayer, and caring for others.

**"If I am not for myself, who will be for me? And if I am only for myself, what am I? And if not now, when?"** (Hillel, 1:14)
Take responsibility for your own growth, never forget others, and don't wait.

**"Do not judge your fellow until you have reached his place."** (Hillel, 2:4)
You never fully know what someone else is going through.

**"It is not up to you to finish the work, but you are not free to stop doing it."** (Rabbi Tarfon, 2:16)
Do your part, even if the job is bigger than you.

**"Who is wise? One who learns from every person. Who is strong? One who conquers his impulses. Who is rich? One who is happy with his portion. Who is honored? One who honors others."** (Ben Zoma, 4:1)
Real wisdom, strength, wealth and honor are inside us.

**"Run to do even a small mitzvah... for one mitzvah brings another."** (Ben Azzai, 4:2)
Every good deed builds momentum.

## Common questions

### Do I need to know Hebrew to study Pirkei Avot?
No. It is available in English with commentary, and it is one of the best places for a beginner to start learning Torah.

### How long is each chapter?
Each chapter takes about 10 to 20 minutes to read, longer with discussion. It is a lovely thing to read at the Shabbat table with family or guests.

### Why study it in the summer?
The original custom was between Passover and Shavuot, to refine our character as we prepare to receive the Torah. Many communities extended it through the summer, when long Shabbat afternoons leave time to learn, and it helps prepare for the new year.

### Is there a commentary you recommend?
The classic commentaries include Rashi and the Rambam. Collections of the Rebbe's insights on Pirkei Avot have been published in English. Ask the Rabbi for a copy that suits you.
