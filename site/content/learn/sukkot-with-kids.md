Sukkot is a week of eating outdoors in a little hut, waving branches and fruit, and ending with dancing. It is called "the time of our joy," and children feel it right away.

## Why we live in a sukkah

"When the Jewish people left Egypt, they walked through the desert for forty years, and G-d protected them with clouds all around, like a big hug. To remember, we build a sukkah, a hut with a roof of branches, and we eat in it for the whole holiday."

## Building the sukkah together

A sukkah can be a simple kit in the backyard or on a deck. It needs at least three walls and a roof of schach: branches, bamboo or palm leaves, things that grew from the ground, laid loosely enough to see a few stars at night. Children can hand up the bamboo, hold the poles steady and spread the branches. Build it soon after Yom Kippur, so the first mitzvah of the new year is a happy one. See our [sukkah](/learn/sukkah) guide for the details.

## Decorations and crafts kids can make

Many families hang children's drawings, paper chains and fruit in the sukkah. In Chabad the custom is to keep the sukkah itself plain, so the mitzvah is the beauty, and to put the children's creativity into the table and the meal. Either way, kids can make:

- Placemats with pictures of the Four Kinds or of the sukkah.
- Name cards for guests, and a welcome sign for the [Ushpizin](/learn/ushpizin), the holy guests Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Moses, Aaron, Joseph and David.
- A Sukkot guest book where every visitor signs.
- A centerpiece of real fruit for the table.

## Eating in the sukkah

All week we eat our meals in the sukkah. When eating bread or cake, we say the blessing "Leishev basukkah," thanking G-d for the mitzvah of sitting in the sukkah. Invite friends and neighbors: a sukkah is made for guests. Bring sweaters and a blanket for the cooler evenings.

## The lulav and etrog

Every day of Sukkot except Shabbat, we take the Four Kinds together: the etrog (a citron), the lulav (a palm branch), three hadassim (myrtle) and two aravot (willow). We say a blessing and wave them to the front, right, back, left, up and down, because G-d is everywhere. Girls and boys both take part.

Each kind is like a different kind of Jew. The etrog has taste and smell, like someone who learns Torah and does good deeds. The lulav's date has taste but no smell, the myrtle has smell but no taste, and the willow has neither. We hold them all together, because every Jew counts and we are one people. See [the Four Kinds](/learn/four-kinds).

## Simchat Torah: dancing with flags

Sukkot ends with Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah, when we finish reading the Torah and start again. Everyone dances around the Torah scrolls in seven circles called [hakafot](/learn/hakafot). Children wave Simchat Torah flags, ride on shoulders and sing as loud as they like. Make or decorate a flag at home beforehand.

## Common questions

### Do children have to eat every meal in the sukkah?
Children are trained in the mitzvah step by step, and their parents decide what is comfortable. For rain, cold or a child who cannot sit long, ask the Rabbi.

### Can kids hold the lulav and etrog themselves?
Yes. Show them how to hold it gently, since a damaged etrog can become unfit for the blessing, and help them say the blessing word by word.

### Do we need our own sukkah?
It is a beautiful mitzvah to have one at home, but you can also eat in a friend's sukkah. If you need a sukkah or a set of the Four Kinds, call the Rabbi.
