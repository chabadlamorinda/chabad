The last of the 613 mitzvot is to write a Torah scroll. The Lubavitcher Rebbe found a way for every Jew to share in it: by owning a letter in a Torah scroll written for Jewish unity.

## The Rebbe's campaign

In 1981 the [Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe) called for Torah scrolls to be written in which every Jewish child could have a letter of their own. The first of these children's Torahs was written in Jerusalem, in the Old City, and more have been written since as more children joined. Adults can take part too, in Torah scrolls written for all the Jewish people.

It became one of the Rebbe's best-known [mitzvah campaigns](/learn/ten-mitzvah-campaigns), and Jewish children all over the world have letters.

## Why it matters

- **Unity.** A Torah scroll is kosher only if every letter is there. If even one letter is missing, the whole scroll is not complete. In the same way, every Jew is needed, and the Jewish people are whole only together.
- **Sharing in the 613th mitzvah.** Owning a letter is a way to take part in [writing a Torah](/learn/hachnasat-sefer-torah).
- **A gift for a child.** The Rebbe spoke of the special merit and protection this brings to Jewish children. Many parents get a letter for a new baby, and many children get one for a birthday.
- **Connection.** A child who knows "I have a letter in the Torah" feels that the Torah belongs to them.

## How to get a letter

Letters are registered through the international registry for these Torah scrolls, typically for a small sum so that every family can take part. You give the person's Hebrew name and their mother's Hebrew name. If you do not know a Hebrew name, the Rabbi can help you choose one; see [Hebrew Names](/learn/hebrew-names).

The easiest way is to call Rabbi Labkowski, who can help you sign up a child, the whole family, or a loved one.

## Ideas for giving a letter

- For a new baby, at the [baby naming](/learn/baby-naming) or [brit](/learn/brit-milah).
- For a birthday, a first day of school or an [upsherin](/learn/upsherin).
- For a bar or bat mitzvah.
- For grandchildren, nieces and nephews.
- For every child in a class or family together.

## Common questions

### Who can get a letter?
Every Jew, child or adult. The children's Torah scrolls are for Jewish children; adults can have a letter in the Torahs written for all Jews.

### Does it cost a lot?
No. It is kept to a small sum on purpose, so that no child is left out. Ask the Rabbi if cost is a concern.

### Do I get a certificate?
Many families receive a certificate for the child's letter. Ask the Rabbi how it works when you sign up.

### My child is not yet named in Hebrew. Can we still do it?
Yes. Call the Rabbi, and he will help you with a Hebrew name and the sign-up.
