Samson (Shimshon, שִׁמְשׁוֹן) was a judge of Israel, famous for his miraculous strength, who began to save the Jewish people from the Philistines. He was a Nazirite from birth, and the Bible says his power came from G-d and his commitment, not from muscle alone.

## An angel announces a baby

Samson was from the tribe of Dan. His parents, Manoach and his wife, whom the Talmud names Tzlalponis, had no children for many years. An angel appeared to the mother and told her she would have a son who would begin to save Israel from the Philistines. From the womb he would be a Nazirite, dedicated to G-d: no wine or grape products, and no razor on his hair. She herself was told to avoid wine and anything impure during the pregnancy.

Chabad.org teaches from the Lubavitcher Rebbe that the parents were remarkable people: Manoach prayed that the angel return and was grateful, and his wife was a woman of prayer and clarity. The lesson drawn is that what a mother takes in during pregnancy matters, which is why Jewish women are careful about kosher food during that time.

## What is a Nazirite?

A Nazirite (nazir, נָזִיר) is a person who takes a vow to be extra holy: to avoid wine and grape products, not to cut his hair, and to stay away from the dead. The Rambam describes three types: someone who takes a vow for a set time (usually thirty days), someone for life, and someone, like Samson, who is a Nazirite from the womb.

The Rebbe asks a question: how could a vow that the parents and an angel started become binding? The answer given on Chabad.org is that it became binding when Samson, at age thirteen, chose to keep it himself. Our own choice to continue what our parents began carries more weight than the original promise. That is a wonderful message for any family raising children in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda: the day your child makes a Jewish practice his or her own is the day it truly begins.

## Samson's feats

According to the Bible and Chabad.org's account:

1. As a young man, Samson killed a lion with his bare hands.
2. At his wedding feast in Timnah he posed a riddle, from the lion and the honey that bees had made in its carcass, to thirty Philistine guests.
3. He caught three hundred foxes, tied torches to them and sent them into the Philistines' wheat fields.
4. In Gaza he lifted the city gates, bars and all, and carried them away.
5. When the men of Judah, afraid of the Philistines, bound him and handed him over, he snapped the ropes and defeated a thousand men with a donkey's jawbone.

The sources explain that Samson did not lead an army, since that could bring more attacks on the Jews. Instead he took on the Philistines himself. After the jawbone episode, the Jewish people recognized him as chosen by G-d and appointed him a judge. The story is also in the Book of Judges, after [Joshua](/learn/joshua) and [Deborah](/learn/deborah).

## Delilah and the secret of his strength

The Philistine leaders offered Delilah a reward to find out where Samson's strength came from. Three times Samson gave her a false answer (seven fresh bowstrings, new ropes, weaving his hair), and each time he broke free. At last, after she pressed him, he told her the truth: he was a Nazirite, and if his hair were shaved, his strength would leave him.

That is the point of the story. The hair was not magic. It was a sign of his dedication to G-d, and when he gave up that connection, the strength left him. The Philistines captured him, put out his eyes and took him to Gaza in chains.

## Samson's last prayer

At a great Philistine feast Samson was brought out to entertain. Blind and bound, he prayed to G-d for strength one last time, so that the Philistines would know that G-d alone is G-d. His strength returned, and he pushed apart the pillars that supported the building, and it collapsed. He died with them, and his family brought him home for burial.

The Bible says he led Israel for twenty years. Chabad.org notes that sources differ: another tradition counts forty years, twenty during his life and twenty after, when his influence kept the Philistines away. Another Chabad.org article counts twenty-two years, so we do not give an exact number here.

## Common questions

### Who was Samson in the Bible?
Samson (Shimshon) was a judge of Israel from the tribe of Dan, a Nazirite from birth, with miraculous strength, who began to save the Jewish people from the Philistines.

### Why was Samson's hair his strength?
His uncut hair was the sign of his Nazirite vow to G-d. His strength came from G-d, and it left him when that connection was broken.

### What is a Nazirite?
A Nazirite is a person who vows to avoid wine and grape products, not to cut his hair and not to come near the dead, in order to be especially holy.

### Who was Delilah?
Delilah was a woman whom the Philistine leaders paid to learn the secret of Samson's strength. She pressed him until he told her about his vow.

### How did Samson die?
Samson prayed for strength one last time and pushed apart the pillars of the building where the Philistines were gathered, and it collapsed on them and on him.

### How long did Samson lead Israel?
The Bible says twenty years. Jewish sources discuss other counts, so for details ask the Rabbi.
