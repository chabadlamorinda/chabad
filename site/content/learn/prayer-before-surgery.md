Surgery, a biopsy, a scan you are worried about: it is natural to feel anxious. Judaism gives us simple, meaningful things to do before a medical procedure, so we go in with faith, with the prayers of others behind us, and with trust that G-d works through the hands of the doctors.

## Going to the doctor is a mitzvah

The Torah says "and he shall surely heal," which the Sages explain as permission, and even a duty, for doctors to heal and for us to seek the best care. Taking care of your health is part of serving G-d. Prayer does not replace the doctor; it goes together with good medicine.

## Five things to do before

1. **Ask for a blessing.** Let the Rabbi know, and he will give you a blessing and pray for you. Give your Hebrew name and your mother's Hebrew name (for example, "Sarah bat Leah"), the way names are used in prayers for healing.
2. **A Mi Sheberach.** The prayer for healing is said for you at the Torah reading. See [the Mi Sheberach](/learn/mi-sheberach).
3. **Tehillim.** Saying Psalms is the classic Jewish prayer in time of need. Chapter 20 ("May G-d answer you on the day of distress") and chapter 121 ("I lift my eyes to the mountains, from where will my help come?") are often said before surgery. Friends and family can divide the book of Tehillim between them. See [Tehillim for every occasion](/tehillim).
4. **Tzedakah.** Give charity before the procedure, even a few coins into a [pushka](/learn/pushka). The Sages teach that tzedakah brings blessing and protection.
5. **Check your mezuzahs and tefillin.** Many people have their mezuzahs checked by a scribe at a time like this, as a way of adding protection to the home. See [the mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah).

## A short prayer in your own words

You can pray in any language. One simple prayer before surgery:

> Master of the world, I place myself in Your hands. Please guide the hands of the doctors and nurses, give them wisdom and care, and send me a complete and speedy recovery, so that I may serve You in health and joy. Amen.

Many also say the [Shema](/learn/shema) before going under anesthesia, and put a small book of Tehillim in their hospital bag.

## On the day

- If you can, say the morning blessings and put on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) (for men) before you leave home.
- Bring something small and Jewish with you: a Tehillim, a Shabbat candle for Friday, a card with your Hebrew name.
- Afterward, when you recover, there is a custom to say the blessing of thanks, **Birkat HaGomel**, at the Torah reading. Ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### Can surgery be scheduled on Shabbat?
Urgent and emergency care is always done on Shabbat: saving life comes first. For planned surgery, people try to schedule it earlier in the week so that recovery does not fall on Shabbat or a holiday. Ask the Rabbi about your case. See also [Shabbat in the hospital](/learn/shabbat-in-the-hospital).

### What if I don't know my Hebrew name?
Call the Rabbi. We can help you find it, or give you one. Your English name with your mother's name works too.

### Can someone else pray for me if I can't?
Yes. Family, friends and the whole community can say Tehillim and a Mi Sheberach for you. That is part of the strength of a community.

### Is it OK to be afraid?
Of course. Faith does not mean having no fear. It means knowing you are not alone. Call the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin any time you want to talk.
