Ushpizin are seven holy "guests" who, according to the Zohar, visit every sukkah during the seven days of Sukkot: Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Moses, Aaron, Joseph and King David. Each day one of them leads, and the others come along.

## What the word means

Ushpizin is Aramaic for "guests." The Zohar teaches that when a Jew sits in the sukkah, the shade of faith, the souls of these great leaders of our people come to join. The sukkah becomes a place where heaven and earth meet.

## The guest of each day

The order follows the Kabbalistic order of the seven emotional attributes (middot), with each guest embodying one:

1. **Abraham:** kindness (chesed)
2. **Isaac:** strength and discipline (gevurah)
3. **Jacob:** harmony and truth (tiferet)
4. **Moses:** victory and endurance (netzach)
5. **Aaron:** splendor and humility (hod)
6. **Joseph:** foundation and connection (yesod)
7. **David:** kingship (malchut)

Each day is a chance to think about the quality of that day's guest and bring a little more of it into your life.

## The Chassidic Ushpizin

In Chabad, we also speak of the "Chassidic Ushpizin," the great Chassidic leaders who visit along with the biblical guests:

1. The Baal Shem Tov
2. The Maggid of Mezritch
3. Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi, the Alter Rebbe
4. Rabbi DovBer, the Mitteler Rebbe
5. Rabbi Menachem Mendel, the Tzemach Tzedek
6. Rabbi Shmuel, the Rebbe Maharash
7. Rabbi Sholom DovBer, the Rebbe Rashab

The Rebbe would often speak about the Ushpizin of the day at the holiday gatherings, drawing a lesson from each one. Learn more about these leaders in our guide to the [Chabad Rebbes](/learn/chabad-rebbes).

## How to welcome the Ushpizin

Many prayer books include an Aramaic invitation to the guests, recited on entering the sukkah. Chabad custom is not to recite this printed text. Instead, the Ushpizin are welcomed by talking about them at the meal and by living up to their example.

Ways to bring the Ushpizin into your sukkah:

- **Invite real guests.** The Zohar teaches that the share of the Ushpizin belongs to the poor. A sukkah without guests is missing something. See our guide to [hachnasat orchim](/learn/hachnasat-orchim).
- **Share a story** at each meal about that day's guest.
- **Give tzedakah** before the holiday or on the intermediate days.
- **Let kids decorate** with pictures or name cards of the seven guests.

## Common questions

### Are the Ushpizin really there?
The Zohar describes their presence as a spiritual reality, not something we see. Their presence is felt in the holiness and joy of the sukkah, especially when we live by their values.

### Do the Ushpizin come on Shemini Atzeret?
No. The Ushpizin are linked to the seven days of Sukkot. Outside Israel, many still eat in the sukkah on Shemini Atzeret, but the cycle of seven guests is complete. Read about the end of the holiday in our [Hoshana Rabbah](/learn/hoshana-rabbah) guide.

### Why is Joseph after Aaron and not with the patriarchs?
The order follows the Kabbalistic attributes, not the order of history. Joseph represents yesod, the attribute that connects, which comes sixth.
