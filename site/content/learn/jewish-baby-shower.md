In many places a baby shower before the birth is a given. Many traditional Jewish families do things differently: they wait until after the baby is born to celebrate, give gifts and set up the nursery. Here is why, and some warm ways to celebrate and help instead.

## Why many families wait

There is no law against a baby shower, but there is a long-standing custom to avoid celebrating a baby before he or she arrives. The reasons people give include:

- **Humility and trust in G-d.** A birth is in G-d's hands. Waiting expresses that we do not take any blessing for granted.
- **Avoiding [ayin hara](/learn/ayin-hara)** (the "evil eye"): not drawing too much attention to a blessing before it is complete.
- **Sensitivity.** If, G-d forbid, something goes wrong, the family is not left with a room full of baby things.

Customs vary widely. Some families do not even buy baby items before the birth; others buy them and keep them at the store or with a relative; others are comfortable with a small gathering. If you are planning something for a friend, ask her what she prefers.

## Warm alternatives before the birth

- **A blessing gathering.** In some communities women gather before a birth to say [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) and blessings for the mother. This is sometimes called a "Brucha." It focuses on prayer and good wishes rather than gifts.
- **Practical help.** Offer to plan meals for after the birth, arrange rides or help with older children.
- **Mitzvot for the mother.** Some women take on an extra mitzvah or give [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) in the merit of an easy birth.

## After the baby is born

This is the time to celebrate. Helpful things to bring or do:

- **Meals** for the family for the first weeks.
- **Gifts** for the baby, and something for the mother too.
- **Help with siblings,** errands or laundry.
- **A short visit,** if the family welcomes it. New parents are tired; keep it brief.

For a boy, the next celebration is the [brit milah](/learn/brit-milah) on the eighth day. For a girl, the father names her at the Torah. See [Baby Naming](/learn/baby-naming).

## Shir Hamaalot cards

A Chabad custom is to hang cards with Psalm 121, "Shir Lamaalot" (often called Shir Hamaalot), and other verses in the birthing room and later near the baby, as a blessing and protection for mother and child. Many families pack them in the hospital bag. The Rabbi or Rebbetzin can tell you where to get a set.

## Common questions

### Is it forbidden to have a baby shower?
No. It is a custom, not a law, and customs vary. Many traditional families prefer to wait; if you are unsure, ask the Rabbi.

### Can I buy things for the baby before the birth?
Many families buy only what they need right away, or order items and have them delivered after the birth. Do what feels right for your family.

### What is a good gift for a new Jewish baby?
Practical baby items are always welcome. Meaningful gifts include a tzedakah box, a children's book of blessings, or a gift for the mother.
