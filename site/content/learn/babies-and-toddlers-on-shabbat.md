Caring for a baby on Shabbat is a mitzvah, and the rules are practical: prepare bottles and food before Shabbat, use a stroller only where there is an eruv, and put the baby's health first. A sick child comes before Shabbat rules, and you should never wait to call a doctor.

What follows is general guidance from Chabad.org's article on baby and toddler care on Shabbat. For your specific questions, call the Rabbi.

## Strollers and the eruv

With a kosher eruv, one may carry a baby or push a stroller. Without an eruv, carrying and pushing are not permitted. See [the eruv](/learn/eruv). Do not open or close a stroller's hood or fly net, but if it was attached before Shabbat, it can be moved. Before Shabbat, check whether your neighborhood is inside an eruv by asking the Rabbi.

## Bottles and formula

- Prepare as much as you can before Shabbat: have hot water ready (on a covered hotplate, or in a thermos) and open the cartons and jars.
- Sterilizing bottles by boiling is not allowed on Shabbat, so have them ready before candle lighting.
- An electric bottle warmer is not allowed. There are permitted ways to warm a bottle, such as standing it in a bowl of hot water that was kept hot from before Shabbat, with the Rabbi's guidance.
- When mixing powder (formula or baby cereal) with liquid, there are rules about the order and stirring, so ask the Rabbi how to do it.

## Diapers and wipes

Disposable diapers: opening the tape for the first time on Shabbat is a concern, so many prepare by opening and closing the tapes before Shabbat. Ask the Rabbi what you should do. Soiled disposables can be thrown out at once. Wipes, creams and cloth diapers each have their own rules, so ask.

## When the baby is sick

If a baby or toddler has a fever or you worry that something is wrong, call the doctor and do what is needed, even on Shabbat. Pikuach nefesh, saving life, overrides Shabbat ([pikuach nefesh](/learn/pikuach-nefesh)). Then call the Rabbi, who will help with the details afterward.

## Making Shabbat calm with little ones

- Light candles earlier and keep dinner simple. See [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).
- Have a Shabbat-only toy box and a favorite book.
- Do not insist on a long meal. A few minutes of Kiddush, challah and a song is a beginning.
- Naps are part of Shabbat. Rest is the point.
- Bless your children on Friday night ([blessing your children](/learn/blessing-your-children)).

## Common questions

### Can I use a stroller on Shabbat?
Yes, if there is an eruv in your area; without one, it is not permitted. Ask the Rabbi whether your neighborhood is covered.

### How do I prepare a bottle on Shabbat?
Get everything ready before Shabbat, and use only permitted ways to warm it. Ask the Rabbi for the details of formula and powders.

### Can I use disposable diapers on Shabbat?
Yes, but there are details about opening the tape and about wipes. Ask the Rabbi what to do beforehand.

### What if my baby is sick on Shabbat?
Call the doctor and do what is needed. Health comes before Shabbat rules.

### Is Shabbat possible with a newborn?
Yes. Start small with a candle, Kiddush and challah, and add more as you can.
