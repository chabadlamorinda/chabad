A Jewish wedding is a ceremony under a chuppah (canopy) in which a bride and groom are joined through a ring, a marriage contract called a ketubah, and seven blessings. It is considered one of the holiest days of a person's life, when two halves of one soul are reunited.

## The meaning of a Jewish wedding

Jewish mysticism teaches that before birth, a husband and wife are one soul that is divided into two. Marriage brings them back together. That is why the wedding day is compared to a personal Yom Kippur: many brides and grooms fast until the ceremony and pray for a fresh start.

## Before the wedding

- **Aufruf:** on the Shabbat before the wedding, the groom is called up to the Torah and the community celebrates.
- **Mikvah:** the bride immerses in a [mikvah](/learn/mikvah) shortly before the wedding, a deeply spiritual preparation.
- **Not seeing each other:** many couples do not see each other for the week before the wedding, which makes the day even more special.

## The ceremony, step by step

1. **Kabbalat panim:** the bride and groom greet guests in separate rooms. The groom sits at a table called the tish, surrounded by friends.
2. **Signing the ketubah:** at the groom's tish, two kosher witnesses sign the marriage contract, which lists the groom's obligations to his wife.
3. **Badeken (veiling):** the groom covers the bride's face with a veil, recalling how Rebecca veiled herself when she met Isaac.
4. **The chuppah:** the couple stands under a canopy, ideally outdoors under the open sky, symbolizing the home they will build. The bride may circle the groom seven times.
5. **Betrothal blessings:** blessings are said over a cup of wine.
6. **The ring:** the groom places a plain ring on the bride's finger and says, "Behold, you are consecrated to me with this ring according to the law of Moses and Israel." This is the legal heart of the marriage.
7. **Reading the ketubah:** the contract is read aloud.
8. **Sheva brachot (seven blessings):** seven blessings are recited over a second cup of wine, often by honored guests.
9. **Breaking the glass:** the groom breaks a glass to remember the destruction of the Temple in Jerusalem, even at our happiest moments. Everyone shouts "Mazel tov!"
10. **Yichud:** the couple spends a few private minutes together.

## After the wedding

The celebration continues with dancing and a festive meal. For the next seven days, festive meals called sheva brachot are held in the couple's honor.

## Common questions

### What is a ketubah?

A ketubah is the Jewish marriage contract, written in Aramaic. It guarantees the wife's rights and the husband's responsibilities. The bride keeps it, and it should be stored safely.

### Why is the ring plain?

The ring should be a simple band with no stones, so its value is clear and the commitment is pure and unbroken, like a circle with no end.

### Do both bride and groom need to be Jewish?

A Jewish wedding according to Jewish law is between two Jews. If you have questions about your situation, please speak privately with the Rabbi. We are here to help with kindness.

### Can we have a Jewish wedding if we are already civilly married?

Yes. Many couples who married civilly later choose to have a chuppah. It is a beautiful step at any stage. Ask the Rabbi about the details.

### Who can officiate?

A Rabbi who knows the laws of marriage should guide the ceremony to ensure it is valid, especially the ring, the witnesses and the ketubah.
