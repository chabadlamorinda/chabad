You can keep kosher almost anywhere with a little planning: pack basics, order kosher airline meals, buy certified products at local stores, and connect with the Jewish community at your destination. Many travelers find that keeping kosher on the road becomes part of the adventure.

## Plan before you go

- **Find the local Jewish community.** Chabad centers around the world welcome travelers, and many can help you find kosher food or even join a Shabbat meal.
- **Look for kosher restaurants and stores** at your destination, and check that their supervision is reliable.
- **Book a room with a kitchenette** or at least a fridge if you can.
- **Plan Shabbat** ahead. Know where you will be, and arrange food before Friday afternoon.

## What to pack

- Sealed, certified snacks, crackers, granola bars and nuts
- Ready-to-eat kosher meals that do not need refrigeration
- Disposable plates, cutlery and cups
- Heavy-duty aluminum foil and oven bags
- A small knife and cutting board, used only for your food
- Grape juice and small challah rolls or matzah for Shabbat

## On the plane

Most airlines offer sealed kosher meals if you request them in advance, usually at least 24 to 48 hours before your flight. The meal should arrive with its original seals intact. If a seal is broken, do not eat it. Bring your own food as a backup in case the meal does not arrive.

## Shopping locally

Many ordinary supermarkets carry products with familiar kosher symbols. Fresh fruits and raw vegetables are kosher, but some need washing and checking (see [checking for insects](/learn/checking-for-insects)). Plain bottled water, many breads with certification, and packaged goods can carry you a long way. Learn the symbols in our guide to [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols). Be aware that symbols abroad may be unfamiliar, so ask locally which ones are reliable.

## Heating food in a non-kosher kitchen

A non-kosher oven or microwave can be used if your food is fully sealed:

1. **Double wrap** the food in two layers of foil or two sealed oven bags.
2. **Heat** it in the oven or microwave without opening the wrapping.
3. **Unwrap** and eat on your own disposable dishes.

Do not use hotel pots, pans or dishes for your food. For anything beyond this, ask the Rabbi.

## Prayers for the journey

Before traveling out of your city, it is customary to say Tefilat Haderech, the Traveler's Prayer, asking G-d for a safe journey. Many also give a coin to [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) before leaving, since the Sages teach that those on a mitzvah mission are protected.

## Common questions

### Can I eat at a vegetarian or vegan restaurant?
Unfortunately, no. Without supervision there are many concerns, including cooking equipment, wine and grape products, insects in produce and bread baked without kosher rules. A vegetarian label does not make food kosher.

### Is coffee from a coffee shop kosher?
Plain black coffee is generally acceptable, but flavored drinks and syrups raise questions. Ask the Rabbi about your usual order.

### What if I cannot find kosher food at all?
Pack more than you think you need, and rely on fruits, vegetables and certified packaged goods. A local Chabad center can usually help.

### How do I keep Shabbat in a hotel?
Plan ahead for food, check with the hotel about lighting candles safely, and avoid electronic key cards on Shabbat. Ask for a regular key if possible, and ask the Rabbi about details.
