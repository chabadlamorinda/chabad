When you are stressed about an exam, Jewish wisdom suggests two steps: do your honest best to prepare, and then trust G-d with the result. Chabad teaching holds that effort and trust go together, and that your worth is never decided by one grade.

## Effort and trust go together

A text on trust in G-d, presented on Chabad.org, teaches that trust does not mean being passive. A person still has to use the natural means that seem best, and then rely on G-d for the outcome. This is called trust, bitachon (בִּטָּחוֹן), together with effort, hishtadlut (הִשְׁתַּדְּלוּת). For a student it means: study, ask for help, get ready as well as you can, and then let go of the worry that you control everything. Read [bitachon](/learn/bitachon) and [anxiety and worry](/learn/anxiety-and-worry).

## You are more than a grade

The Lubavitcher Rebbe answered a student who was worried about the future by saying it was too early for anxiety, since circumstances change and worry now accomplishes nothing. He said that devoted study is the best preparation for life. That letter was written to a yeshivah student, but the message is relevant to every student: focus on the task in front of you. A test measures what you studied on one day. It does not measure your soul. For more, see [success and failure](/learn/success-and-failure).

## A prayer in your own words

Many people find calm in saying a few words to G-d before a test, such as asking for help to think clearly and to remember what they studied. If you want to say something, say it simply and sincerely, in your own words. You might also say the Book of [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim), the Psalms, which many people turn to when they need comfort. If you would like to know whether there is a custom or a particular psalm for exams, ask Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242. Read also [prayer](/learn/prayer).

## Practical ways to ease the pressure

These are general suggestions, not Jewish law:

- Break studying into small pieces and take breaks.
- Rest and eat properly. The body needs care.
- Talk to a teacher, parent, friend or counselor if you are overwhelmed.
- Remember that one result does not decide your future.

Shabbat can be a gift for a stressed student: a day with no studying, screens or work. See [Shabbat for beginners](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners). For a student who feels they cannot take a break, ask the Rabbi to help work it out.

## When stress becomes more than stress

Some stress is normal, but if you feel hopeless, cannot sleep for days, or have thoughts of harming yourself, please talk to a trusted adult or a doctor right away. In the United States you can call or text 988, the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, at any hour. Jewish tradition values life above all. Read [mental health: a Jewish view](/learn/mental-health-jewish-view). The Rabbi and Rebbetzin are also available to talk, at 925-384-7242.

## Common questions

### Is there a Jewish prayer for before an exam?
You can pray in your own words, asking for clarity and calm, or say Tehillim. Ask the Rabbi if there is a particular custom for you.

### Is it wrong to worry about exams?
Caring about your work is good. But worry that takes over does not help. Do your best and trust G-d with the outcome.

### How can I trust G-d and still study hard?
Chabad.org teaches that the two go together. You make the effort, and G-d brings the result. Read [bitachon](/learn/bitachon).

### My teenager is overwhelmed by school pressure. What can I do?
Listen first, and remind your child that their value is not one grade. See [parents and teenagers](/learn/parents-and-teenagers), and talk to the school counselor or your doctor if needed. The Rabbi is also glad to talk, at 925-384-7242.

### Should I study on Shabbat?
Please ask the Rabbi about studying and exams on Shabbat and holidays, since the rules depend on the situation.
