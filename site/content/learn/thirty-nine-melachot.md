The 39 melachot are the categories of creative work that the Torah forbids on Shabbat. They come from the work done to build the Mishkan, the portable Sanctuary in the desert, and they include activities like cooking, writing, lighting a fire and carrying in a public area.

## What does "work" mean on Shabbat?

Many people think Shabbat work means effort. But in Jewish law, melachah means creative work: changing or creating something in the physical world. Moving heavy furniture inside your home is not a melachah, while flipping a light switch or writing a word can be.

On Shabbat, we step back from creating and controlling the world, and acknowledge that G-d is the Creator. We stop being masters of the world for a day, and instead enjoy it.

## Where the 39 come from

The Torah places the command to rest on Shabbat right next to the instructions for building the Mishkan (Exodus 31 and 35). The Sages learned from this that the types of work needed to build the Mishkan are the types forbidden on Shabbat. The Mishnah (Shabbat 7:2) lists them: 39 in all.

## The 39 categories

**Growing and preparing food**

1. Plowing
2. Sowing
3. Reaping
4. Gathering
5. Threshing
6. Winnowing
7. Selecting
8. Grinding
9. Sifting
10. Kneading
11. Baking (cooking)

**Making clothing**

12. Shearing
13. Whitening (laundering)
14. Combing raw fibers
15. Dyeing
16. Spinning
17. Warping (setting up the loom)
18. Making loops (heddles)
19. Weaving
20. Separating threads
21. Tying a knot
22. Untying a knot
23. Sewing
24. Tearing

**Making leather and writing**

25. Trapping
26. Slaughtering
27. Skinning
28. Tanning
29. Smoothing
30. Marking
31. Cutting to shape
32. Writing
33. Erasing

**Building**

34. Building
35. Demolishing
36. Extinguishing a fire
37. Kindling a fire
38. The final hammer blow (completing an object)
39. Carrying from one domain to another

## How they apply today

Each category includes related activities. Some examples:

- **Cooking** includes baking, frying and boiling. Food is prepared before Shabbat and kept warm in permitted ways.
- **Kindling** includes turning on lights and, according to most authorities, using electrical devices.
- **Writing** includes typing and texting.
- **Carrying** in a public area is forbidden unless there is an [eruv](/learn/eruv).
- **Selecting** has detailed rules about picking out what you want from a mixture.

The Sages also added protective rules, such as not handling certain objects (muktzeh), like money or tools.

## Saving a life comes first

All Shabbat laws are set aside to save a life or prevent serious harm. If someone may be in danger, call for help immediately, drive to the hospital or do whatever is needed.

## Common questions

### Do I need to know all 39 to keep Shabbat?
No. Start with the basics: lighting [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles), making Kiddush, and avoiding the most common melachot like cooking, writing, driving and using phones. Learn more one step at a time.

### Why is driving forbidden on Shabbat?
A car engine works by burning fuel, which involves kindling a fire, and driving also usually involves carrying and other melachot.

### Can I use a timer on Shabbat?
Yes. Timers set before Shabbat to turn lights or appliances on and off are widely used and permitted. Ask the Rabbi about specific devices.

### Can a non-Jew do work for me on Shabbat?
Asking a non-Jew to do a melachah for you on Shabbat is generally not permitted, with some exceptions, such as for a sick person or extreme cold. Ask the Rabbi.
