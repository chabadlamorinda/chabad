Musaf (מוסף) means "additional": it is the extra Amidah prayer Jews say on Shabbat, festivals and Rosh Chodesh, after the morning service. It recalls the additional offerings that were brought in the Holy Temple on those special days and takes their place today.

## What Musaf means

Every day has three prayers: morning, afternoon and evening. Chabad.org explains that Musaf is a fourth service, added on days that are special. The daily prayers correspond to the daily Temple offerings, and Musaf corresponds to the extra offerings of Shabbat, the festivals and Rosh Chodesh. Since the Temple no longer stands, our prayer takes the place of those offerings and marks the day as set apart.

There is a teaching in this too: something added is greater than the base it is added to. Musaf is said after the regular service, reaching a higher level of connection.

## When Musaf is said

- **Shabbat**, after the Torah reading and before the end of the morning
- **Festivals** such as Passover, Shavuot and Sukkot
- **Rosh Chodesh**, the beginning of the Jewish month
- **Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur**, when Musaf is the longest

It is said after the morning prayers and before the afternoon prayers. Ideally it is said before about the seventh hour of the day, and in practice it follows right after the morning service. On weekdays that are not special, there is no Musaf. See [Shabbat day](/learn/shabbat-day) for the full flow of the morning.

## What happens during Musaf

Musaf is its own [Amidah](/learn/amidah), with its own middle blessings that are different from the other prayers of the day. Each one describes that day's additional offering. With a minyan, the Amidah is repeated aloud by the prayer leader.

The Kedushah (the part where the congregation declares G-d's holiness) is included. In Ashkenazic communities it begins "Kadosh," while Sefardic and Chassidic communities, including Chabad, begin "Keter." On Shabbat and holidays, the first verse of the Shema is part of the Kedushah in Musaf, a moving moment, when the whole room says "Hear, O Israel" together. Learn more in [the Kedushah](/learn/kedushah) and in the [Shema](/learn/shema).

Musaf is usually fairly short, except on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, when it is one of the highlights of the day.

## Tefillin on Rosh Chodesh

Tefillin are worn on Rosh Chodesh morning but taken off for Musaf. Chabad.org gives two reasons. First, the Kedushah of Musaf begins with "Keter," which means "crown," and wearing a crown (tefillin on the head) during it is not appropriate. Second, tefillin are a "sign," as are Shabbat and festivals. The Rosh Chodesh Musaf mentions the day's additional offering, which is also a sign, and a second sign is unnecessary. This holds even for those who do not say "Keter." Read about [Rosh Chodesh](/learn/rosh-chodesh).

## Common questions

### What is Musaf?
Musaf is the "additional" prayer said on Shabbat, festivals and Rosh Chodesh. It stands in place of the extra offerings brought in the Temple on those days.

### When is Musaf said on Shabbat?
It is said after the morning service and the Torah reading, and before the afternoon prayers. It follows right after the morning prayers.

### Why is there a Musaf?
The daily prayers correspond to the daily Temple offerings. On special days there were additional offerings, and Musaf takes their place.

### Why do people take off tefillin for Musaf on Rosh Chodesh?
Chabad.org says tefillin are removed because the Kedushah begins with "Keter" (crown), and because tefillin are a sign like the Musaf offering. Ask the Rabbi if you have questions.

### Is there Musaf every day?
No. There is Musaf only on Shabbat, festivals and Rosh Chodesh. Ordinary weekdays have the three regular prayers.

### Do I have to stay for Musaf as a first-time visitor?
You are welcome to stay for as much as you like. Come, listen, and ask the Rabbi anything.
