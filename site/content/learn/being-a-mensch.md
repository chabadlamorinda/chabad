A mensch (מענטש, also spelled mentsch) is Yiddish for a decent, considerate, trustworthy person. Literally the word means "a human being," and when a Jewish parent tells a child "be a mensch," they mean: be good at heart, whatever else you achieve.

## What the word really means

Chabad.org notes that mensch basically means "person" or "human," and that the word is hard to define exactly. It is similar to "refined" or "well-mannered," yet a mensch is a mensch. One Chabad.org article describes a mensch as someone who respects the needs and wishes of others, especially those in his or her care.

The idea is older than the word. The Mishnah in Pirkei Avot says, "In a place where there are no people, strive to be a person" (2:5). Being a mensch is a choice made again and again, often when no one is watching.

## Manners come first

The Sages teach that derech eretz (דרך ארץ, "the way of the land," meaning decency and good manners) comes before Torah. A person can study a great deal and still be unkind; Judaism says that is not the goal. Torah is meant to make us better people.

This matters in a family. A child who learns to say "thank you" and "I'm sorry" is learning Torah, in a way.

## Seven everyday habits of a mensch

1. **Greet people warmly.** Say hello first, to everyone, including the cashier.
2. **Keep your word.** A promise is a promise, even when it costs you.
3. **Say thank you.** Gratitude is at the heart of Jewish life; see [blessings](/learn/blessings).
4. **Protect other people's dignity.** Never embarrass someone in public; see [Pirkei Avot](/learn/pirkei-avot) and the guide on [kind speech](/learn/lashon-hara).
5. **Help without being asked.** Notice who needs a hand. See [chesed](/learn/chesed).
6. **Be honest in business and at home.**
7. **Apologize and forgive.** A mensch owns mistakes.

## A short self-check

For teens: Did I treat someone kindly today who could do nothing for me? Did I speak about anyone behind their back?
For parents: Do my children see me being a mensch at home, in the car and on the phone?
For everyone: Whom do I need to call, thank or forgive?

## Common questions

### What does mensch mean?
Mensch is a Yiddish word meaning a decent, honest, caring person. Literally it means "a human being."

### What is the Jewish idea behind being a mensch?
Judaism teaches that derech eretz, basic decency, comes before Torah, and that Torah should make us kinder people.

### How do you be a mensch?
Greet people, keep your word, say thank you, protect others' dignity, help without being asked and apologize when wrong.

### Is being a mensch more important than being religious?
In Jewish teaching they belong together: mitzvot between people and G-d, and between people and people, are both essential. A person who is "religious" but unkind is missing the point.

### Can a child be taught to be a mensch?
Yes, mostly by example. Praise kindness, practice thank-yous and let children help others.
