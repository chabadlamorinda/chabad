Being Jewish as a teen means you are part of a people more than 3,000 years old, with a soul that is fully Jewish whether or not you feel religious. High school is a great time to figure out what that means for you, ask hard questions and find your own way to connect.

## What makes you Jewish

Jewish identity is not a grade you earn or a feeling you have on some days. Every Jew has a G-dly soul, and that connection is permanent. You do not need to know Hebrew, keep every mitzvah or have had a Bar or Bat Mitzvah to belong. Read more in [who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew).

## Asking real questions

Teens often wonder: Do I believe in G-d? Why do mitzvahs matter? What does Judaism say about friends, dating, social media, the future? Judaism has always welcomed questions; the Talmud is basically a book of arguments. Bring your questions to the Rabbi, or read our guides on [belief in G-d](/learn/belief-in-g-d) and [why mitzvahs matter](/learn/why-mitzvot).

## Simple ways to live it, step by step

You do not have to do everything at once. Pick one:

1. **Say the Shema** before bed, or [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani) when you wake up.
2. **Guys:** put on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) on a weekday morning. **Girls:** light [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) on Friday before sunset.
3. **Give tzedakah** from your own money, even a little.
4. **Do one Shabbat thing:** a Friday night dinner with family, or a phone-free hour.
5. **Learn one thing a week,** like the weekly [parsha](/parsha).
6. **Wear something Jewish** if you like, such as a Star of David or chai.

## Facing antisemitism

Unfortunately, some teens face antisemitic comments at school, online or in sports. Some practical advice:

- **You are not alone.** Tell a parent, a trusted teacher or the Rabbi.
- **Report it.** Schools have policies, and harassment should be taken seriously.
- **Be proud, not defensive.** Knowing your history and values gives you confidence. See [Jewish pride](/learn/jewish-pride) and [Israel and the Jewish people](/learn/israel-and-the-jewish-people).
- **Answer with light.** The Rebbe taught that the response to darkness is to add more light: one more mitzvah, one more act of kindness.

## Jewish friends and community

It helps to have Jewish friends who get it. Teen events, volunteering and holiday celebrations are a good way to meet them. After high school, many teens connect with Chabad on campus; read our [guide for college students](/learn/jewish-college-students).

## For parents

Teens are more likely to keep what they choose for themselves. Invite rather than pressure, keep the conversation open, and let them see Judaism as joyful in your own home. See [Jewish parenting](/learn/jewish-parenting).

## Common questions

### Am I Jewish if I do not feel religious?
Yes. If your mother is Jewish, or you converted, you are fully Jewish. Feelings come and go, but your Jewish soul is always there. Every mitzvah you do is real.

### I did not have a Bar or Bat Mitzvah. Is it too late?
No. You became Bar or Bat Mitzvah automatically at 13 or 12. A celebration or first aliyah can happen at any age. Call us and we will help you plan one.

### What should I do if someone says something antisemitic at school?
Do not keep it to yourself. Tell a parent, a teacher or the Rabbi, and report it to the school. Stay proud and safe.
