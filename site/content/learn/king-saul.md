King Saul (Shaul, שָׁאוּל) was the first king of the Jewish people. He began as a modest young man from the tribe of Benjamin who went out to find his father's lost donkeys, was anointed king by the prophet Samuel, and ended his life in battle at Mount Gilboa.

## The young man who went looking for donkeys

The Book of Samuel tells that Saul's father lost his donkeys, and Saul went to search for them. When he could not find them, his servant suggested asking the prophet Samuel. Samuel, who had been told by G-d to expect him, anointed Saul as king.

Chabad.org emphasizes how humble Saul was. When Samuel spoke of the kingship, Saul answered that he was from the smallest of the tribes and from the least of its families. Afterward, he told his uncle about the donkeys but kept the news of the kingship to himself. The sources describe him as a man of "outstanding modesty and humility," and of natural nobility.

## A great beginning

Saul led Israel in battle against its enemies, and the people accepted him. Chabad.org's summary says that he began "brilliantly." Read about [Samuel the prophet](/learn/samuel-the-prophet), who guided him, and about [the kingdoms of Israel and Judah](/learn/kingdoms-of-israel-and-judah) that followed.

## Why did Saul lose the kingdom?

The Sages and the sources on Chabad.org point to several moments:

1. **Impatience.** Before a battle with the Philistines, Saul was told to wait for Samuel to offer the sacrifices. When the soldiers grew restless, he did not wait. He was told that his kingdom would not last.
2. **Amalek.** Saul's first mission was to wipe out Amalek completely. He spared King Agag and the best of the animals. When Samuel confronted him, Saul first explained himself. When he finally said he had sinned, it was too late. The Talmud warns against being "overly righteous" like Saul, who acted as if he was more merciful than G-d. For this nation and its meaning, see [Parshat Zachor](/learn/parshat-zachor).
3. **Nob.** Saul listened to slander against the priests of the city of Nob and ordered them killed. Only one survived. Chabad.org says this sin sealed Saul's fate.

The sources also say that Saul's humility, a wonderful quality, could turn into a weakness: he was afraid to stand firm against the will of the people. Humility means knowing your worth and using it for G-d, not fearing others. See [humility](/learn/humility).

## Saul and David

G-d told Samuel to anoint a new king: [David](/learn/king-david), a young shepherd from Bethlehem. David came to serve Saul, and Saul remained king for years, but he no longer had G-d's favor. Chabad.org describes Saul as jealous of David and hunting him, even though David stayed loyal. David refused to harm Saul, who was still the anointed king. Read about [jealousy and envy](/learn/jealousy-and-envy).

The Chassidic teachers contrast the two. When rebuked by the prophet Natan, David admitted his sin and repented, and so the royal line continued from him. Saul gave excuses.

## The death of Saul

Facing the Philistines at Mount Gilboa, Saul had, according to the sources, tried to get guidance but could not. Chabad.org's account says the Israelite army was routed. Saul's sons, including Jonathan, were killed, and Saul, badly wounded, fell on his sword. The next day, the Philistines hung his body on the wall of Beth-Shan. The men of Jabesh-Gilead risked their lives to take the bodies down and bury them, and the people fasted for seven days.

David's lament for Saul and Jonathan ("How the mighty have fallen") is one of the most moving passages in the Bible. David mourned his rival as a king.

## Lessons for today

- **Humility is a gift, with a danger.** Be humble, and also be decisive when doing what G-d asks.
- **Do the whole mitzvah.** Partial obedience is not a small thing.
- **Own your mistakes.** Real teshuvah says "I was wrong" at once.

## Common questions

### Who was King Saul?
King Saul was the first king of Israel, a man of the tribe of Benjamin anointed by the prophet Samuel. He began humbly and led the people in battle, but lost the kingdom after serious failures.

### Why did Saul lose the kingdom?
The sources point to his failure to wait for Samuel, his not carrying out the command to destroy Amalek completely, and his killing of the priests of Nob.

### Why was Saul looking for donkeys?
His father's donkeys had been lost, and Saul went to find them. This led him to the prophet Samuel, who anointed him king.

### Was Saul jealous of David?
Chabad.org's summary describes Saul as jealous of David and hounding him. David did not take revenge and honored Saul as king.

### How did King Saul die?
Saul died in battle with the Philistines at Mount Gilboa. His sons, including Jonathan, were killed in the same battle.

### What can we learn from King Saul?
We learn that humility is a great quality, that we must carry out G-d's commands fully, and that we should take responsibility for our mistakes.
