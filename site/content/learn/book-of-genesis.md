The Book of Genesis, called Bereishit (בראשית, "In the beginning") in Hebrew, is the first of the Five Books of Moses. It tells the story of the world from Creation to the death of Joseph, and it narrows from all humanity to one family: Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and their children, the roots of the Jewish people.

## What Genesis is, in a few facts

Genesis is the first book of the Torah, and it has the most weekly portions of the five books: twelve. Chabad.org notes that it covers some 2,300 years. Its Hebrew name comes from the first word of the Torah. The English name "Genesis" means "origin." Over half of the book is about our Patriarchs and Matriarchs, and about a quarter is the story of Joseph and his brothers, which leads straight into the [Book of Exodus](/learn/book-of-exodus).

Every year the reading begins again on [Simchat Torah](/learn/simchat-torah-for-beginners), and we read the whole Torah, one portion each week. See [the weekly Torah portion](/learn/weekly-parsha) and [what happens at the Torah reading](/learn/torah-reading).

## Part one: the world before our people

**Bereishit.** G-d creates the world in six days and rests on the seventh, the first Shabbat. Adam and Eve are created, placed in the Garden of Eden, and then make the first mistake. Their son Cain kills his brother Abel. Lesson: every person is created in G-d's image, and we are each responsible for our brother.

**Noach.** The world becomes corrupt, and Noah, a righteous man, builds an ark. After the Flood, people build the Tower of Babel and are scattered. Lesson: one righteous person can save a world. Read more in [Noah and the Flood](/learn/noah-and-the-flood).

## Part two: the Patriarchs and Matriarchs

**Lech Lecha and Vayeira.** Abraham answers G-d's call to leave his home and go to the land G-d will show him. He and Sarah welcome guests, and after many tests, Isaac is born. G-d asks Abraham to offer Isaac, and in the end stops him. Lesson: kindness and faith. See [Abraham and Sarah](/learn/abraham-and-sarah).

**Chayei Sarah and Toldot.** Sarah passes away and Abraham buries her in Hebron. Isaac marries Rebecca. Their twins, Esau and Jacob, grow up very different, and Jacob receives the blessing. Lesson: building a Jewish home takes prayer. See [Isaac](/learn/isaac-our-father).

**Vayeitzei and Vayishlach.** Jacob flees to his uncle Laban, marries Leah and Rachel, and has twelve sons. He returns home and makes peace with Esau. Lesson: faith through hard years of work. See [Jacob](/learn/jacob-our-father) and [the Matriarchs](/learn/the-matriarchs).

## Part three: the brothers

**Vayeishev, Mikeitz and Vayigash.** Joseph, Jacob's favorite, is sold by his jealous brothers. He rises in Egypt, interprets Pharaoh's dreams, and prepares the land for famine. When his brothers come to buy food, he tests them, and then reveals himself with the words "I am Joseph." Lesson: forgiveness, and that G-d guides events for good. See [Joseph and his brothers](/learn/joseph-and-his-brothers).

**Vayechi.** Jacob spends his last years in Egypt, blesses his sons and grandsons, and asks to be buried in Hebron. The family stays in Egypt, setting the stage for slavery and the Exodus. Lesson: blessing the next generation. See [the twelve tribes](/learn/twelve-tribes) and [Ephraim and Menashe](/learn/ephraim-and-menashe).

## Common questions

### What is the Book of Genesis about?
Genesis is the Torah's first book, telling the story of Creation, the first humans, Noah, and the lives of Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and Joseph. It explains where the world and the Jewish people come from.

### How many Torah portions are in Genesis?
There are twelve: Bereishit, Noach, Lech Lecha, Vayeira, Chayei Sarah, Toldot, Vayeitzei, Vayishlach, Vayeishev, Mikeitz, Vayigash and Vayechi.

### Why is Genesis called Bereishit?
Jewish books are named for their first important word, and the book opens with the word "Bereishit," "In the beginning." "Genesis" is the English word meaning "origin."

### When do Jews read the Book of Genesis?
We begin it on Simchat Torah, in the fall, and read it over the following weeks, ending with Vayechi in winter. Then we start Exodus. Check [this week's parsha](/parsha) for the current portion.

### Who wrote Genesis?
In Jewish tradition, G-d gave the whole Torah to Moses, who wrote it down. See [the Torah](/learn/torah) for more.
