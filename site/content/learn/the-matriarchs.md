The four matriarchs (imahot, אמהות) are Sarah, Rebecca, Rachel and Leah, the wives of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob and the mothers of the Jewish people. Their stories fill the Book of Genesis, and the Talmud calls them the spiritual base on which the nation was built.

## Sarah: faith and a home open to all

Sarah was Abraham's wife. Her faith, and the miraculous birth of Isaac when she was very old, assured that the covenant with G-d would continue. She shaped Isaac's upbringing and decided that Ishmael should not remain in the household, a decision G-d told Abraham to accept. Rashi, the classic commentator, teaches that while Sarah lived, her Shabbat candles stayed lit from week to week, a blessing in her tent. This is one reason Jewish women light [Shabbat candles](/learn/abraham-and-sarah) to this day.

## Rebecca: kindness at the well

Rebecca married Isaac. When Abraham's servant came to find a wife for Isaac and asked her for a drink, she offered water to him and also to all his camels. That simple, generous act showed she was the right one. Rebecca later saw which of her sons would carry on the covenant, and she made sure Jacob received his father's blessing. Her gift is chesed, kindness, with wisdom.

## Rachel: tears and sacrifice for her children

Rachel was Jacob's beloved wife and for many years had no children. She had two sons, Joseph and Benjamin, and died giving birth to Benjamin. She was buried on the road near Bethlehem, not with Jacob in Hebron. Her tomb, Kever Rachel, is a place where Jews pray. Rachel has become the image of a mother who cries for her children and gives herself for them, and a Jewish tradition says her prayers for her children are heard.

## Leah: prayer and gratitude

Leah was Jacob's first wife and was not loved as much as Rachel, so she lived with disappointment. Still she bore six of the twelve tribes of Israel, and among them Levi, the priests and Levites, and Judah, whom David and the kings came from. When Judah was born, she said, "This time I will thank G-d." Leah teaches us to find gratitude even in a hard story.

## Why four?

The Talmud (Berachot 16b) speaks of three patriarchs and four matriarchs. Jacob also had children with Bilhah and Zilpah, so why not six? The answer is that the title depends on spiritual role more than on bloodline: the four great women who formed the nation's spiritual base. The patriarchs and matriarchs, except Rachel, are buried together in the Cave of the Patriarchs in Hebron.

## How women carry on their legacy

Each mother gave a gift that is still passed on: Sarah's faith and Shabbat candles, Rebecca's kindness, Rachel's love for her children, Leah's gratitude. Jewish women today pass these on by lighting candles, giving [blessings to children](/learn/blessing-your-children), opening their homes and teaching the next generation. ## Common questions

### Who are the four matriarchs of Judaism?
The four matriarchs are Sarah, Rebecca, Rachel and Leah, the wives of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob and the mothers of the Jewish people.

### Why are there only four matriarchs?
The Talmud counts four because they were the women who formed the spiritual base of the nation, even though Jacob's other wives, Bilhah and Zilpah, also had children.

### Where are the matriarchs buried?
Sarah, Rebecca and Leah are buried in the Cave of the Patriarchs in Hebron. Rachel was buried on the road near Bethlehem.

### What is Kever Rachel?
Kever Rachel is Rachel's tomb near Bethlehem, a place where Jewish people have long come to pray, especially for children and healing.

### What is the Jewish custom of blessing daughters?
On Friday night many parents bless daughters with "May G-d make you like Sarah, Rebecca, Rachel and Leah." See [blessing your children](/learn/blessing-your-children).
