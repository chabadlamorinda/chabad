The Purim story is told in the Book of Esther (the Megillah): in ancient Persia, the wicked Haman plotted to destroy the Jews, but Queen Esther and her cousin Mordechai, with courage and a hidden Divine hand, turned the decree around and saved the Jewish people. We celebrate every year on Purim (פּוּרִים) by hearing the Megillah, giving gifts and charity, and holding a festive meal.

## The story, chapter by chapter

**The feast and Queen Vashti.** The Jews lived in exile under King Ahasuerus (Achashverosh), who ruled 127 provinces from his capital, Shushan. At a great royal feast he summoned his queen, Vashti, to appear before his guests. She refused, and she was removed.

**Esther becomes queen.** A new queen was sought. Esther (also called Hadassah), an orphan raised by her cousin Mordechai, was brought to the palace. On Mordechai's instruction she did not reveal that she was Jewish. The king chose her as queen. Mordechai, sitting at the palace gate, uncovered a plot against the king and told Esther, who told the king in Mordechai's name. The deed was written in the royal chronicles.

**Haman's rise and decree.** The king promoted Haman, an arrogant minister, and ordered everyone to bow to him. Mordechai would not. Furious, Haman decided to destroy not just Mordechai but all the Jews. He cast a lot (a *pur*, hence the name Purim) to choose a date, and the king signed a decree that on the thirteenth of Adar the Jews would be destroyed.

**Mordechai and Esther.** Mordechai put on sackcloth and cried out. He asked Esther to go to the king. Esther knew that going to him uninvited could mean death. Mordechai answered: "Who knows whether it was for a time like this that you came to royalty?" Esther asked all the Jews of Shushan to fast for three days, and then said, "If I perish, I perish." See [the Fast of Esther](/learn/fast-of-esther).

**The banquets.** The king welcomed Esther and agreed to come to a banquet with Haman. Haman, delighted, went home and built a very tall gallows for Mordechai.

**The turn.** That night the king could not sleep, and his chronicles were read to him: they told of Mordechai's service, which had never been rewarded. In the morning the king asked Haman how to honor a man he wished to honor. Haman, assuming it was himself, described a royal parade, and then was told to do all of it for Mordechai. At the second banquet Esther revealed that she was Jewish and that Haman had plotted to destroy her people. Haman was hanged on the gallows he had made.

**The victory.** A royal decree cannot be undone, so a second decree allowed the Jews to defend themselves. On the thirteenth of Adar they were saved, and the day became a day of joy, with feasting, gifts to friends and charity to the poor. Mordechai and Esther wrote the account down and established the holiday for all generations.

## The hidden miracle

Notice what is missing: G-d's name does not appear anywhere in the Megillah. The sages saw in the name Esther a hint to the Hebrew *hester*, "hiding": G-d appeared to be hidden, yet He was directing every event: the queen who was removed, the orphan who became queen, the sleepless night. Purim teaches that coincidences are sometimes a Divine hand we cannot yet see.

## What Esther asks of us

Esther was comfortable in the palace and could have stayed silent. She chose to speak up for her people. Chabad teaching stresses that every Jew, in every generation, has a moment like this: a place and time where only they can help. Purim's mitzvahs ([the four mitzvahs of Purim](/learn/the-four-mitzvahs-of-purim)) are about unity: sending food to friends, giving to the poor, being together.

## Common questions

### What is the story of Purim?
The Purim story is the Book of Esther: Haman, a Persian minister, plotted to destroy the Jews, but Queen Esther and Mordechai saved them, and we celebrate the rescue every year. The full account is read from the Megillah.

### Who was Queen Esther?
Esther was a Jewish orphan raised by her cousin Mordechai who became queen of Persia, kept her identity hidden until the moment she needed to reveal it, and risked her life to save the Jewish people.

### Who was Haman?
Haman was the chief minister of King Ahasuerus, who plotted to annihilate the Jews because Mordechai would not bow to him. Our tradition calls him a descendant of Amalek, the nation that attacked Israel after the Exodus.

### Why isn't G-d mentioned in the Book of Esther?
The Megillah tells of a time when G-d's presence was hidden. The sages saw this as the lesson of Purim: G-d's hand directs events even when it is not obvious.

### Why do we call it Purim?
Purim means "lots," because Haman cast a lot (a *pur*) to choose the day for his plan. The lot that seemed random led to the Jews' victory.
