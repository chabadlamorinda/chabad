Aliyah means "going up." It is the word used for a Jew moving to live in the Land of Israel. (It is also the word for being called up to the Torah; for that, see [Getting an Aliyah](/learn/aliyah).) For thousands of years Jews prayed three times a day to return to Zion, and today many make that journey.

## The mitzvah of living in the Land

The Land of Israel is the land G-d promised to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob and gave to the Jewish people. The Torah calls it "a land that the eyes of the L-rd your G-d are always upon" (Deuteronomy 11:12). Many great authorities, including the Ramban (Nachmanides), count living in the Land of Israel as a mitzvah, and the Sages speak at length of its special holiness. There are also mitzvahs that can only be kept there, such as those connected to its produce.

Moving is a big decision that touches family, work, health and more. Whether and when to move are personal questions, and it can help to talk them through with the Rabbi. See also [Israel and the Jewish People](/learn/israel-and-the-jewish-people).

## The Law of Return

Israel's Law of Return gives every Jew the right to come to Israel as an immigrant (oleh) and become a citizen. It also includes certain family members, such as children and grandchildren of Jews and their spouses. The details are set by Israeli law and can change, so check the current rules with the official aliyah organizations.

## The practical path

- **Nefesh B'Nefesh** works with the Jewish Agency and the Israeli government to help Jews from North America make aliyah. It guides you through the application, paperwork and planning, and offers help after you arrive.
- **The Jewish Agency** handles aliyah from other countries.
- **Plan ahead:** housing, schools for children, health insurance, Hebrew study (ulpan) and work. Financial benefits for new immigrants exist, but they change over time, so check current details with the official organizations.

## Proof of Jewish status

As part of the application, you will need to show that you are Jewish, or eligible under the Law of Return. Documents often include a letter from a rabbi who knows you, along with family records such as a parents' [ketubah](/learn/ketubah) or other papers. See [Who Is a Jew?](/learn/who-is-a-jew).

Rabbi Labkowski can help with such a letter for members of our community, and can help you find documents if your family's records are incomplete. It is best to start early.

## Keeping your community ties

- **Find a Chabad House** near your new home. There are Chabad centers all over Israel, and the Rabbi can help connect you.
- **Come back to visit.** We would love to see you.

## Common questions

### Is aliyah only for religious Jews?
No. Jews of every background make aliyah. Every Jew is welcome in the Land of Israel.

### What do I need from a rabbi?
Usually a letter confirming that you are Jewish, based on what the rabbi knows of you and your family. Call the Rabbi early so there is time to gather what is needed.

### My family has no records. What can I do?
Many families are in this situation, especially families from the former Soviet Union. The Rabbi can help you work out what documents may be accepted. See [Jewish Genealogy](/learn/jewish-genealogy).
