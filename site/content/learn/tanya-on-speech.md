In the Tanya, speech is one of the three "garments" of the soul, along with thought and action: the way the soul steps out into the world and becomes real. That is why Judaism treats words as powerful, since the soul is present in them, and why a word of Torah, prayer or kindness can connect us to G-d.

This guide explains what the Tanya says about speech and how it can shape an ordinary day in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda.

## What is a "garment of the soul"?

Chapter 4 of the [Tanya](/learn/tanya) says that besides its inner powers, the G-dly soul has three garments through which it expresses itself: thought, speech and action. When we use the garments for Torah and mitzvot, the soul is "dressed" in them and its inner life becomes visible. See also the guide on the [three garments of the soul](/learn/three-garments-of-the-soul).

Each garment has its field:

- **Action:** doing a mitzvah, such as putting on tefillin or wearing tzitzit.
- **Speech:** speaking words of Torah, including the laws that explain how to do the mitzvot.
- **Thought:** understanding Torah as deeply as the mind can.

## Why words of Torah are so special

The chapter explains that Torah is the wisdom of G-d. So when we speak its words, even when no action is involved, we are joined to Him. The Alter Rebbe, Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi, adds the wonder of it: G-d's infinite wisdom took the form of the language and laws of the Torah, so ordinary human speech can hold it.

Chapter 35 continues the thought. Studying Torah with thought and speech draws the light of the Shechinah, G-d's presence, upon the G-dly soul.

## "In your mouth and in your heart"

Chapter 35 builds on the verse "For the matter is very near to you, in your mouth and in your heart, that you may do it" (Deuteronomy 30:14). The "mouth" is speech, the "heart" is thought and feeling, and the verse ends with "that you may do it," which points to action. In other words, what we say and feel should lead to what we do.

The "very near" part is encouraging. As Chassidic teaching explains, the mind can move the heart, so a person does not have to wait for a feeling to arrive. It is within reach to speak the words and let the heart follow.

The Tanya comes back to this verse in other chapters too. Chapter 17 explains why it is true: the mind is under a person's control even when the heart is not, so love of G-d in the mind is enough to guide the mouth and the hands. Chapter 41 uses it to teach that serving G-d with love and awe is within simple reach of every Jew.

## What this means for everyday speech

The chapters we opened speak mainly of speech for Torah and prayer. But the idea carries over to other words. If speech is a garment of the soul, then each time we speak we show something of who we are. In Chapter 30, in a discussion of humility, the Tanya quotes the advice Rabbi Shimon's father gave him: "Keep away from slander." Our tradition has detailed laws about harmful speech; see [lashon hara](/learn/lashon-hara) and [hurtful words](/learn/hurtful-words).

A few questions you can ask yourself before speaking:

1. **Is it true and kind?** If not, can it wait?
2. **Does it build or break?** Ask the question before the answer.
3. **Could this word be a blessing?** See [blessings](/learn/blessings): saying a short blessing aloud turns an ordinary moment into one of connection.

These questions are a way to apply the Tanya's idea, not quotations from it.

## Common questions

### What does Tanya say about speech?
Tanya calls speech one of the three garments of the soul, with thought and action. It says speech used for Torah study connects the soul to G-d.

### What are the three garments of the soul?
Thought, speech and action. They are how the G-dly soul expresses itself using the Torah and mitzvot.

### What does "in your mouth and in your heart" mean?
It is from Deuteronomy 30:14. The Tanya (Chapter 35) reads it as pointing to speech, thought and feeling, all leading to doing the mitzvah.

### Does Tanya say anything about gossip?
The chapters we studied for this guide do not focus on gossip, but quote the advice to keep away from slander. See [lashon hara](/learn/lashon-hara) for the laws.

### Does it matter if I don't feel anything when I pray or learn?
Chassidic thought says the heart's feelings follow the mind, and that what counts is that they lead to action. Ask the Rabbi about your own situation.
