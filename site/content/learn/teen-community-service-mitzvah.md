Community service is a mitzvah in Jewish tradition: it combines *tzedakah* (צְדָקָה), giving that is a matter of justice, with *chesed* (חֶסֶד), acts of kindness. For a Jewish teen, volunteering is a chance to put Jewish values into action by helping neighbors, seniors and people in need, and many teens find that it also builds confidence and friendships.

## Why service is a Jewish idea

Chabad.org explains that tzedakah is not "charity" in the casual sense: the word comes from *tzedek*, justice. Everything we have is a gift from G-d, and sharing it is simply the right thing to do. The Sages set a tenth of one's earnings as the proper amount of giving, and those who can afford it should aim for a fifth. Our guide to [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) tells more.

Chesed goes beyond money: it is giving of ourselves, our time and our attention. Visiting the lonely, helping a neighbor and welcoming a stranger are all chesed. See [chesed](/learn/chesed) and, for visiting the sick, [bikur cholim](/learn/bikur-cholim).

For teens, service also fits a bigger theme: you are not too young to make a difference. Even a teen's smile and a few hours of help can brighten someone's day.

## Maimonides' ladder: giving well

Maimonides, as Chabad.org describes, taught eight levels of giving. The highest is helping a person become self-sufficient, for example by a loan or work that preserves their dignity. The lowest is giving unwillingly. For a teen, this is a helpful way to think about service: give cheerfully, give respectfully and, when you can, help someone stand on their own.

## Volunteer ideas for Jewish teens

Here are ideas that fit a teen's schedule. Always check with a parent first and with the organization.

1. **Visit seniors.** A friendly visit or a phone call can mean a great deal to an older neighbor who lives alone. Chabad of Lamorinda has a page about [help for seniors and families](/help/help-for-seniors-and-families).
2. **Help with holiday packages.** Deliveries of food, challah or holiday items to people who cannot get out are a classic teen mitzvah. Call 925-384-7242 to hear what is planned.
3. **Help at community events.** Setting up, welcoming guests, helping with games for younger children and cleaning up are all valuable. See [events](/events).
4. **Teach and help younger kids.** Teens can help with children's programs or tutor a younger child in reading.
5. **Organize a drive.** Collect food, coats, books or school supplies in your school or neighborhood.
6. **Bake for others.** Challah or cookies for a neighbor or for someone who is ill, with a kind note.
7. **Join a local cause.** Schools, libraries, food pantries and environmental groups in your town often welcome teens. Our guide to [volunteering in Lamorinda](/learn/volunteering-in-lamorinda) has more.
8. **A Bat or Bar Mitzvah project.** If you are preparing for a milestone, see [Bat Mitzvah projects](/learn/bat-mitzvah-projects) for ideas that can continue after the big day.

## How to start

1. **Pick something you care about.** Seniors, animals, children, the environment, hunger.
2. **Talk to your parents.** Make a plan for time and transportation.
3. **Reach out.** Call or sign up. At Chabad of Lamorinda, volunteers can fill out the [volunteer form](/forms/volunteer).
4. **Start small.** One hour a month is better than a big promise you cannot keep.
5. **Be consistent and kind.** Show up on time and treat people with respect and dignity.
6. **Reflect.** Ask yourself what you learned and who you helped.

## A note on school service hours

Many schools ask students for community service hours. Whether a particular activity counts, and whether a signature is needed, is up to your school and the organization. Ask your school counselor first, and then ask the organization whether it can provide the verification you need. We cannot promise this for every activity, so call 925-384-7242 to ask.

## Common questions

### Is community service a mitzvah?
Yes. Helping others is a central Jewish value, expressed through tzedakah and chesed. Chabad teaches that giving of ourselves and our resources is a responsibility, not just a favor.

### How can a Jewish teen volunteer near Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda?
Start with seniors, community events and local drives. Ask the Rabbi at 925-384-7242 or sign up on the volunteer form to hear what is available.

### Do teens need to give a tenth of their money to tzedakah?
Tradition sets a tenth for earned income. For teens with small allowances or jobs, many families start with a small, regular amount. Ask the Rabbi about what is right for your teen.

### Can volunteering at a Jewish organization count for school service hours?
It depends on the school and the activity. Ask your school what it requires, and then ask the organization. Chabad of Lamorinda cannot promise it for every activity.

### What is the difference between tzedakah and chesed?
Tzedakah is giving, usually money or resources, as a duty of justice. Chesed is acts of kindness with our time and effort. Both are mitzvot, and they work best together.

### I am shy. What is an easy first step?
Write a card or bake challah for a neighbor, or help set up at an event. Small acts count and get easier.
