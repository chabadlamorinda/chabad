Angels appear throughout the Torah, from the visitors who came to Abraham's tent to the ladder in Jacob's dream. The Hebrew word for angel, **malach**, simply means "messenger." Angels are spiritual beings created by G-d to carry out His will.

## What angels are

- **Messengers.** Each angel has a task. Rashi explains that one angel does not carry out two missions.
- **Spiritual, without a body.** When angels appear as people in the Torah, it is so they can be seen.
- **Without free choice.** Angels do exactly what they were made to do. People can choose, and so we can grow. Chassidus teaches that a Jewish soul serving G-d by choice reaches higher than an angel.
- **Never prayed to.** Maimonides lists among the principles of faith that we pray only to G-d, not to angels or any other being.

## Michael, Gabriel, Raphael and Uriel

When three "men" visited Abraham after his brit milah (Genesis 18), Rashi explains that they were angels: **Michael** came to tell Sarah she would have a son, **Raphael** came to heal Abraham, and **Gabriel** came to overturn Sodom.

In the [bedtime Shema](/learn/bedtime-shema) we say: "On my right is Michael, on my left Gabriel, before me Uriel, behind me Raphael, and above my head the Presence of G-d." Many parents say these words with their children every night.

## Angels in our prayers

In the Kedushah of the morning prayers we repeat the words the angels say in Isaiah's vision: "Holy, holy, holy is the L-rd of Hosts; the whole world is full of His glory." The morning blessings before the [Shema](/learn/shema) describe the angels praising G-d. Each day we join their song.

## Angels made by mitzvahs

"One who does one mitzvah acquires for himself one advocate" (Ethics of the Fathers 4:11). The commentators explain that every mitzvah creates a good angel that speaks up for us. A kind word, a coin of tzedakah, a candle lit for Shabbat: each one makes something lasting. See [Pirkei Avot](/learn/pirkei-avot).

## Shalom Aleichem on Friday night

The Talmud (Shabbat 119b) teaches that two angels escort every person home from synagogue on Friday night. If they find the candles lit and the table set, the good angel says, "May it be like this next Shabbat too," and the other angel must answer "Amen."

That is why, before Kiddush, we sing [Shalom Aleichem](/learn/shalom-aleichem): "Peace be with you, ministering angels." We welcome them, ask them to bless us, and wish them well as they go.

## Common questions

### Does everyone have a guardian angel?
Jewish sources speak of angels that accompany and protect a person, such as the verse "He will command His angels for you, to guard you in all your ways" (Psalms 91:11). The ultimate protector is always G-d.

### Can I pray to an angel?
No. We pray only to G-d. We may ask G-d to send His blessing through His messengers, as in Shalom Aleichem.

### Do angels have wings?
Isaiah describes the seraphim with six wings. These are prophetic images of a spiritual reality, not a description of a physical body.

### How can I bring angels into my home?
Do mitzvahs. Light [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles), say the bedtime Shema with your children, and fill your home with kindness.
