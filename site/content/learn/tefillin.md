Tefillin are two small black leather boxes containing handwritten Torah passages, worn on the arm and head by Jewish men during weekday morning prayers. Putting them on binds our mind, heart and actions to G-d, and it is one of the most important mitzvot in the Torah.

## What tefillin are

Tefillin (תְּפִלִּין), sometimes called phylacteries in English, come from the Torah's command in the [Shema](/learn/shema): "Bind them as a sign upon your arm, and let them be a symbol between your eyes" (Deuteronomy 6:8).

There are two parts:

- **Shel yad (arm tefillin):** one box with a single parchment containing four Torah passages, worn on the upper arm facing the heart.
- **Shel rosh (head tefillin):** a box with four compartments holding the same four passages, worn on the head above the forehead.

The passages include the Shema and the Exodus from Egypt. They are handwritten by a trained scribe, and the boxes and black straps are made of leather from a kosher animal.

## Why we wear tefillin

The arm tefillin rests opposite the heart, and the head tefillin sits over the mind. Together they express our commitment to direct our feelings, our thoughts and our actions toward G-d and goodness. In 1967, just before the Six-Day War, the Rebbe launched a worldwide campaign encouraging every Jewish man to put on tefillin, teaching that each pair of tefillin brings blessing and protection.

## Who and when

- **Jewish men and boys** from bar mitzvah age (13) wear tefillin. In Chabad custom, boys start practicing about two months before their bar mitzvah.
- **Weekday mornings**, during morning prayers. They may be put on any time during daylight.
- **Not on Shabbat or major Jewish holidays**, since these days are themselves a "sign" between G-d and the Jewish people.

## How to put on tefillin, step by step

These directions are for a right-handed person. A left-handed person wears the arm tefillin on the right arm.

1. **Roll up your left sleeve** and stand.
2. **Place the arm tefillin** on your upper left arm, on the muscle, with the box tilted slightly toward your heart.
3. **Say the blessing** (below).
4. **Tighten the strap** to secure the box, and wrap the strap seven times around your forearm.
5. **Put on the head tefillin.** Place the box above your forehead at the hairline, centered between your eyes. The knot sits at the back of your head, and the two straps hang down in front.
6. **Finish the hand:** wrap the remaining arm strap three times around your middle finger, then around your hand.
7. **Say the Shema** and, ideally, other morning prayers.
8. **Remove them** in reverse order: undo the finger wraps, take off the head tefillin, then the arm tefillin.

Never be shy to ask for help. It quickly becomes easy.

## The blessing

**Hebrew:** בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו, וְצִוָּנוּ לְהָנִיחַ תְּפִלִּין

**Transliteration:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu l'haniach tefillin.

**Translation:** Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us to put on tefillin.

In Chabad custom, this one blessing covers both the arm and head tefillin. Many other communities add a second blessing before the head tefillin.

## Common questions

### Do I need to be religious to put on tefillin?
No. Every Jewish man has the mitzvah of tefillin, whatever his background. Putting them on even once is a great mitzvah.

### What is the minimum I should say while wearing tefillin?
At least the first paragraph of the Shema. If you have more time, say the full morning prayers. See our guide to [prayer](/learn/prayer).

### How often should tefillin be checked?
Tefillin can wear out or be damaged without you noticing. It is good practice to have them checked by a qualified scribe periodically, and if the boxes get damaged or the straps crack, check right away.

### Can I put on tefillin in the afternoon?
Yes. Tefillin may be worn any time during the day until sunset. Morning prayers are best, but if you missed them, put them on later.
