Hannah (Chanah, חַנָּה) was the wife of Elkanah and the mother of the prophet Samuel. Her heartfelt, silent prayer for a child at the Tabernacle in Shiloh is the model for how Jews pray the [Amidah](/learn/amidah): quietly, with moving lips, pouring out the heart to G-d. Her story is read on Rosh Hashanah.

## A woman who longed for a child

Hannah was childless for many years, while Elkanah's other wife, Peninah, had children. Peninah would taunt her. Our Sages say Peninah meant well, hoping to drive Hannah to pray, but the pain she caused was still wrong. Elkanah loved Hannah and tried to comfort her, but her heart was broken. ## The prayer at Shiloh

Hannah went to the Tabernacle and prayed, weeping. She promised that if she were given a son, she would dedicate him to G-d's service. Her prayer was silent: her lips moved but her voice was not heard. Eli the High Priest saw her and thought she was drunk and rebuked her. She answered that she was a woman with a heavy heart, pouring out her soul before G-d. Eli then blessed her, saying that G-d should grant her request.

## How Hannah shapes our prayer

Chabad.org notes that this is the source for the way we recite the Amidah: in a whisper, with the lips moving. It shows two truths. First, prayer is a conversation: you can speak openly and honestly to G-d in your own words, as in [talking to G-d in your own words](/learn/talking-to-g-d-in-your-own-words). Second, prayer can come from the quiet of the heart.

## The birth of Samuel

Hannah conceived and named her son Samuel (Shmuel). When he was weaned she brought him to Shiloh to be raised by Eli, as she promised. Samuel grew into a prophet and leader of Israel. Hannah later had two more sons and two daughters. She offered a song of thanks (I Samuel 2:1-10), considered prophetic, and our Sages count her among the seven prophetesses, like [Miriam](/learn/miriam).

## Hannah on Rosh Hashanah

The haftarah, the reading from the Prophets, on the first morning of Rosh Hashanah is Hannah's story. Our tradition says that Sarah and Hannah were both remembered by G-d on this day, which is called the Day of Remembrance. It is a day to turn to G-d with a full heart: see [Rosh Hashanah for beginners](/learn/rosh-hashanah-for-beginners).

## Praying like Hannah

1. **Find a quiet moment.** No need for fancy words.
2. **Say what is truly in your heart,** to G-d, in any language.
3. **Make a promise** to do a good deed in return.
4. **Do not be discouraged** by people who misjudge you. Eli first misunderstood her and then blessed her.

## Common questions

### Who was Hannah in the Bible?
Hannah was the wife of Elkanah and the mother of the prophet Samuel, famous for her prayer for a child.

### Why is Hannah the model for prayer?
Her silent, heartfelt prayer is the source for reciting the Amidah quietly, with the lips moving.

### Why do we read about Hannah on Rosh Hashanah?
Her story is the haftarah of Rosh Hashanah morning, because tradition says she was remembered by G-d on that day.

### Is there a Jewish prayer for a baby?
Jews pray from the heart, as Hannah did, and also recite Psalms (Tehillim) and other prayers; ask the Rabbi what fits your situation.

### What did Hannah promise?
She promised to dedicate her son to the service of G-d, and she kept her word after Samuel was weaned.
