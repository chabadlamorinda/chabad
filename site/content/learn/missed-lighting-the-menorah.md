If you forgot to light the Chanukah menorah, light it as soon as you remember, as long as someone in the household is still awake, and say the blessings. If everyone is asleep or the night has passed, call the Rabbi, because the details depend on the situation.

## First: do not panic

Missing the usual time is common, especially in a busy week. The sources are clear on the main point: the best time is shortly after sunset, but the mitzvah can still be done later if the family is awake and can see the lights. Start with [when to light](/learn/when-to-light-the-menorah) if you are not sure about the ideal time.

## Coming home late

The lights are best lit when the family is together, because the idea of the mitzvah is to publicize the miracle at home. Here are two options for a family member who comes home very late:

1. **Be present at an earlier lighting**, such as at a friend's home or the synagogue, and see the menorah there.
2. **Light your own menorah on arrival**, having decided not to rely on the home lighting, with the blessings.

Which one fits depends on your situation, so ask the Rabbi if you are unsure. If you know in advance you will be late, it is often best to wait for each other.

## Lighting late at night

The laws of Chanukah teach that if you did not light at the right time, you may light later in the night with a blessing as long as someone in the household is awake. If everyone is asleep and you cannot wake a few people, ask the Rabbi what to do; there is a view that you light without the blessing so the miracle is still shown, and views differ on the exact point after midnight.

## Missing a whole night

A night that has fully passed cannot be made up the next night. You do not light double. The next evening, you simply light the correct number for that night. Do not skip the remaining nights: each night is its own mitzvah. This is the kind of question where it is best to speak to the Rabbi about your specific case.

## Eating while you wait

If you are waiting to light, do not begin a bread meal, and avoid more than a small amount of grain snacks until you have lit. If you need a proper meal, ask someone to remind you.

## Common questions

### What should I do if I forgot to light the menorah?
Light it as soon as you remember, with blessings, if someone in the household is awake. If not, ask the Rabbi.

### Can I light the Chanukah candles late at night?
Yes, while others in the household are awake, you can light with the blessings. After that the details depend, so call the Rabbi.

### Can I make up a missed night of Chanukah?
No. You continue the next night with that night's number of lights. Do not light extra.

### What if I come home after everyone lit?
You can light your own menorah, having decided not to rely on the earlier lighting, or see a lighting elsewhere that night. Ask the Rabbi which fits you.

### What if I am traveling?
Light where you will be sleeping that night. See [Chanukah away from home](/learn/chanukah-away-from-home).

### Does it matter if the lights are lit before or after the stars appear?
Chabad custom is shortly after sunset; other communities light at nightfall. If you are late either way, you can still light later that night while the household is awake.
