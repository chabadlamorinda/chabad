Chanukah is the Jewish eight-day Festival of Lights, which remembers a miracle in the Temple in Jerusalem more than 2,000 years ago, when one day's worth of pure oil burned for eight days. Each night Jewish families light one more candle on a menorah, until eight burn on the last night.

## Chanukah in two minutes

Long ago, the Syrian Greeks tried to force their culture and religion on the Jewish people. The Maccabees fought back and won. When they reached the Temple in Jerusalem they found only enough pure olive oil for one day, yet it burned for eight. The Sages established Chanukah so that the miracle would be remembered and publicized.

Today Jewish families light a menorah, a special eight-branched candelabrum (plus a helper candle), each evening of the holiday. They sing, play dreidel, give a little gelt, and eat foods cooked in oil such as potato pancakes ([latkes](/learn/latkes)) and jelly doughnuts ([sufganiyot](/learn/sufganiyot)). For the full story, read the [Chanukah story](/learn/chanukah-story).

## How to wish someone a happy Chanukah

The most common greeting is simply "Happy Chanukah" (or "Happy Hanukkah"), and in Hebrew, "Chanukah sameach," which means a joyous Chanukah. Chabad.org notes that both spellings are correct: Chanukah is the way many Ashkenazi Jews spell it, while Hanukkah is common among Sephardic Jews and those transliterating from modern Hebrew. Nobody will mind either way. A friendly "Happy Chanukah!" to a Jewish neighbor or coworker is a warm gesture. See [holiday greetings](/learn/holiday-greetings) for more.

## Kind ideas for gifts and treats

Giving a gift is never required, and Chabad.org explains that gifts are not a mitzvah of Chanukah; the long-standing custom is gelt, a little money, mostly for children. If you would like to do something kind:

- **A kosher-certified treat,** such as a sealed box of chocolates or cookies with a kosher symbol. See [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols). Asking your friend what works for them is always welcome.
- **A card** with a warm note about the holiday.
- **A gift of time,** such as asking about their plans or inviting them for coffee.

Teachers can also be thanked with a note or a small treat. If you are unsure, just ask.

## Including a Jewish colleague or student

Chanukah is not a major day off work, so many Jewish people go to work or school and light the menorah in the evening. A few ways to help:

1. Ask if they would like to share what Chanukah means to them, and let them choose how much to share.
2. At a staff or class event, include foods that are kosher or make sure there are options, and ask them.
3. Be flexible with evening schedules on the eight nights, when families light the menorah at home. Read [Chanukah at work and college](/learn/chanukah-at-work-and-college).
4. If you are a teacher, our guide [presenting a Jewish holiday in class](/learn/presenting-a-jewish-holiday-in-class) can help.

## Common questions

### What is Hanukkah in simple words?
Hanukkah is the Jewish eight-day Festival of Lights. It remembers the Maccabees' victory and the miracle of one day's oil burning for eight days in the Temple.

### How do you say Happy Hanukkah?
Say "Happy Chanukah" or "Happy Hanukkah," or in Hebrew, "Chanukah sameach." Both spellings are correct.

### Is it okay to say Happy Hanukkah to a Jewish coworker?
Yes. It is a friendly, welcome greeting. If you do not know whether someone celebrates, you can simply ask.

### What is a thoughtful Hanukkah gift for a Jewish friend or coworker?
A kosher-certified treat or a kind card is a safe, warm choice. Gifts are not required, since the traditional custom is gelt for children.

### Can anyone come to a menorah lighting in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda?
Public lightings are open to everyone, according to Chabad.org. Chabad of Lamorinda's [Grand Chanukah Menorah Lighting](/events/chanukah-grand-menorah-lighting-2026) is December 6 at 4:00 PM.
