A mitzvah is a commandment from G-d, one of the 613 in the Torah, and also a way to connect with G-d through a real action. Many people use the word for any good deed, which is a lovely thing, but the Jewish idea is deeper: a mitzvah is an act that joins you, the material world and the Creator.

## Commandment or connection?

The Hebrew word *mitzvah* comes from *tzav*, "command." That is the plain meaning. Chassidic teaching also connects it with the Aramaic *tzavta*, "attachment" or "companionship." A mitzvah binds the one who does it to the One who commanded it: when you do what G-d asked, you are in His company. As Chassidic teaching puts it, the reward of a mitzvah is the mitzvah itself, because it is the connection.

A commandment is not only a rule. It is also a gift: a way to turn everyday things (a candle, a coin, a piece of bread) into something holy.

## Why 613?

Jewish tradition counts **613 mitzvot** in the Torah: 248 "do" and 365 "don't." The Talmud teaches that this is the number given at Sinai. Tradition links the 248 with the parts of the body and the 365 with the days of the year, a hint that a mitzvah is for the whole person and for every day. Some mitzvot apply only in the Land of Israel or in Temple times, and some apply only to certain people, so no one is meant to do all 613. See [the 613 mitzvot](/learn/613-mitzvot).

## The Rebbe's idea: one more mitzvah

The Lubavitcher Rebbe taught that every Jew can always do one more mitzvah. He encouraged people not to compare themselves with others, and not to wait until they "know enough," but to add one mitzvah in a way that is real. He asked people to focus on ten simple ones that anyone can do. Learn about them in [The Rebbe's ten mitzvah campaigns](/learn/ten-mitzvah-campaigns), and about [the Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe).

## Why mitzvot matter

- **They turn belief into life.** Faith becomes something you do.
- **They give structure to a day.** Blessings, Shabbat and tzedakah shape time.
- **They bring good into the world.** Each one is real, even if no one sees it.

For more on the reasons, read [why mitzvot](/learn/why-mitzvot).

## How to start small

Do not try everything at once. Choose one:

1. **Light Shabbat candles** on Friday night. See [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).
2. **Give tzedakah** each day, even a coin. See [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).
3. **Put up a mezuzah** on your door. See [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah).
4. **Say the Shema** at night.

Keep it for a month, and let it become part of you. Then add another. If you are not sure what fits you, ask the Rabbi. And remember: no mitzvah is too small, and no one is "too far."

## Common questions

### What is a mitzvah?
A mitzvah is a commandment from G-d in the Torah. People also use the word for any good deed.

### What does mitzvah mean in Judaism?
It means "commandment," and Chassidic teaching also hears in it the word for attachment: a mitzvah connects us with G-d.

### How many mitzvot are there?
613: 248 things to do and 365 things not to do. No one does all of them, because many depend on place, time or who you are.

### Is any good deed a mitzvah?
In everyday speech, yes. In Jewish law, a mitzvah is something the Torah commands, and many kind acts, such as giving tzedakah, are also mitzvot.

### What is the difference between a mitzvah and a custom?
A mitzvah comes from the Torah or the Sages. A custom (minhag) is a tradition that communities adopt. Both are part of Jewish life.

### Which mitzvah should I do first?
Choose one that speaks to you, such as Shabbat candles, tefillin or a mezuzah. The Rebbe encouraged every Jew to do one more mitzvah.
