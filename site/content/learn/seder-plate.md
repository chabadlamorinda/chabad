The seder plate (ke'arah) holds the symbolic foods used at the Passover seder: the shank bone, egg, bitter herbs, charoset, vegetable and a second bitter herb. Together with three matzahs, it tells the story of slavery and freedom right on the table.

## The items and their meaning

- **Zeroa (shank bone):** recalls the Passover offering brought in the Holy Temple. Chabad custom uses a roasted chicken neck with most of the meat removed. It is not eaten at the seder.
- **Beitzah (egg):** a hard-boiled egg, recalling the festival offering (chagigah) in the Temple. It is also a sign of mourning for the Temple.
- **Maror (bitter herbs):** recalls the bitterness of slavery. Chabad uses grated horseradish and romaine lettuce together.
- **Charoset:** a paste of apples, pears, nuts and wine, recalling the mortar the Jews used to make bricks in Egypt.
- **Karpas (vegetable):** dipped in salt water at the start of the seder. Chabad custom uses raw onion or boiled potato.
- **Chazeret (second bitter herb):** used for the "Korech" sandwich of matzah and maror. Chabad uses horseradish and romaine here too.

Salt water is placed near the plate, not on it.

## The three matzahs

Under or beside the plate are three whole [shmurah matzahs](/learn/matzah), covered with a cloth. In Chabad custom they are placed one above the other, separated by cloths, on a plate or tray, with the seder items on top of the cover. The three matzahs are called Kohen, Levi and Yisrael.

## How to arrange the plate, step by step

The Chabad arrangement follows the Arizal, the great Kabbalist, and forms two triangles:

1. **Top right: zeroa** (shank bone).
2. **Top left: beitzah** (egg).
3. **Center, below them: maror.**
4. **Below the maror, on the right: charoset.**
5. **Below the maror, on the left: karpas.**
6. **Bottom center: chazeret.**

"Right" and "left" are from the point of view of the person leading the seder. Many Haggadahs include a diagram you can follow.

## Preparing the plate

- Roast the zeroa and boil the egg before the holiday.
- Prepare plenty of maror and charoset, since everyone at the table needs a portion. Horseradish is grated before the holiday begins, and lettuce should be washed and carefully checked for insects. See our guide to [checking for insects](/learn/checking-for-insects).
- Each head of household may have their own plate. Guests can share from the leader's plate.

## Using the plate during the seder

The plate is not just decoration. During the seder you will:

1. Dip the karpas in salt water.
2. Break the middle matzah for the [afikoman](/learn/afikoman).
3. Point to the matzah and maror while explaining them.
4. Eat matzah, then maror dipped in charoset, then the Korech sandwich.

## Common questions

### Do I need a special seder plate?
No. Any large plate works as long as the items are arranged correctly. A beautiful plate honors the mitzvah, but it is not required.

### Can I use a lamb shank bone?
Many communities do. Chabad and others prefer a chicken neck so that it does not look like a real Passover offering, which can only be brought in the Temple.

### What do I do with the items after the seder?
The egg can be eaten at the meal, and many eat it dipped in salt water. The zeroa is not eaten at the seder but may be eaten on the next day of the holiday.

### Can a vegetarian have a seder plate?
Ask the Rabbi. Some use a roasted beet in place of the bone. The main mitzvot, matzah, maror and the four cups, do not require meat.
