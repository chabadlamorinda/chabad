Judaism teaches that death is not the end. The soul is eternal: it leaves the body, returns to G-d, and continues to exist and grow in a spiritual world. In the future, Jewish tradition teaches, the righteous will rise again in the resurrection of the dead.

## The soul lives on

The [soul](/learn/the-soul) is "a part of G-d above," and like its Source it cannot die. When a person passes away, the body returns to the earth and the soul returns to G-d. King Solomon wrote, "The dust returns to the earth as it was, and the spirit returns to G-d who gave it" (Ecclesiastes 12:7).

## Gan Eden

After death, the soul enters *Gan Eden*, the Garden of Eden, a spiritual realm of closeness to G-d. There it enjoys the reward of the Torah study and mitzvot it did during life. Jewish teaching describes many levels of Gan Eden, and the soul keeps rising higher over time.

## A time of cleansing

Tradition teaches that before entering Gan Eden, many souls go through a process of cleansing, sometimes called *Gehinnom*. This is not eternal punishment. It is more like a spiritual washing that removes the effects of mistakes, so the soul can fully enjoy G-d's closeness. For most people it lasts no more than twelve months.

This is why [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish) for a parent is said for only eleven months: to show that we see our parent as a good person who does not need the full time.

## The World to Come and the resurrection

Jewish tradition speaks of *Olam Haba*, the World to Come. In its fullest sense, this refers to the time after [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach) comes, when the dead will be brought back to life. Souls will return to bodies, and the world will be filled with the knowledge of G-d.

Belief in the resurrection of the dead is one of Maimonides' Thirteen Principles of Faith and is mentioned three times a day in the Amidah prayer. It is also one reason Jewish tradition calls for burial.

The Mishnah teaches, "All Israel has a share in the World to Come." Jewish tradition also teaches that righteous people of all nations have a share.

## This world matters most

Even with all this, Judaism focuses on life here and now. The Mishnah says, "One hour of teshuvah and good deeds in this world is better than all the life of the World to Come." Only in this world can we do mitzvot and make the world a better place. That is the soul's real mission.

## How the living can help a soul

Our good deeds on behalf of someone who has passed away truly help their soul rise. Traditional ways include:

- **Saying Kaddish** during mourning and on the [yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit).
- **Giving tzedakah** in their memory.
- **Learning Torah,** especially Mishnah, in their name.
- **Doing mitzvot** and acts of kindness in their honor.
- **Saying Yizkor** on holidays.

## Common questions

### Do Jews believe in heaven and hell?
Judaism speaks of Gan Eden, a spiritual paradise, and a temporary process of cleansing. It does not teach eternal damnation for ordinary people.

### Will I see my loved ones again?
Jewish tradition teaches that souls are reunited, and that in the time of the resurrection, families will be together again.

### Do Jews believe in reincarnation?
Jewish mystical sources, including the Kabbalah of the Arizal, teach that some souls return to this world to complete a mission or repair something. This is called *gilgul*.

### Can souls know what happens to their families?
Tradition teaches that souls are aware of their loved ones and take joy in their good deeds, especially those done in their memory.
