The Yissachar-Zevulun partnership is an arrangement in which one person devotes himself to Torah study while another supports him, and the two share in the merit of the learning. The idea comes from two of Jacob's sons, Yissachar (יִשָּׂשכָר), the scholar, and Zevulun (זְבוּלֻן), the merchant, and it teaches that someone who makes Torah study possible becomes a true partner in it.

## Where the partnership comes from

At the end of his life, Moses blessed each tribe. In the blessing of Zevulun and Yissachar (Deuteronomy 33:18) Zevulun is mentioned first, even though Yissachar was the older brother. Rashi, quoting the Midrash (Genesis Rabbah 99:9), explains that the two tribes entered a partnership. Zevulun lived by the sea, traded by ship, and supplied the food that Yissachar needed so that Yissachar could devote himself to Torah. Because Yissachar's Torah came through Zevulun's support, Zevulun is named first. This is known as the Torah-business partnership.

See [The Twelve Tribes](/learn/twelve-tribes) for more about each tribe.

## A partner in the learning

The Rema, an important authority on Jewish law, rules that supporting someone who studies Torah is considered as if the supporter had studied himself. A person can agree with a friend that the friend will study while he provides the livelihood, and they will share the reward. This works because it is the supporter's help that lets the scholar study instead of working.

Rabbi Moshe Feinstein pointed out that this is unusual. Normally, when you help someone do a mitzvah, they receive the full reward and you receive a separate reward. In a Yissachar-Zevulun partnership, the supporter shares the same reward as the scholar. The Talmud says that such an agreement should be made in advance, and Rabbi Feinstein gave further conditions, which not all authorities accept. If you are thinking of a formal partnership of this kind, ask the Rabbi.

## What this means for ordinary families

Few of us will agree to support a full-time scholar. But the idea behind the partnership is for everyone: Torah learning does not happen by itself. Someone provides a classroom, a teacher, books and a place to gather, and those who help provide them become partners in what is learned.

When you support Torah classes, a teacher, or a program of Jewish learning, you are doing a modern version of what Zevulun did. See [Daily Torah Study](/learn/daily-torah-study) for how Torah learning can be part of every day, and [Why Giving to Jewish Education Matters](/learn/why-give-to-jewish-education).

According to Rabbi Feinstein, the support given in a formal Yissachar-Zevulun partnership is not deducted from maaser because it is a partnership, not charity. This is a detail for a personal conversation with the Rabbi. For ordinary giving, see [Maaser](/learn/maaser).

## Common questions

### What is the Yissachar-Zevulun partnership?
It is an agreement where one person studies Torah while another supports him, and they share the merit. It is based on Rashi's explanation of Moses' blessing to the tribes of Zevulun and Yissachar.

### Do I get the reward of Torah study if I support someone who learns?
The Rema rules that it is considered as if the supporter had studied himself, when there is such a partnership. The details are complex, so ask the Rabbi.

### Why is Zevulun mentioned before Yissachar?
Rashi explains that Yissachar's Torah came through Zevulun's support, so Zevulun is named first even though Yissachar was older.

### Does it work for other mitzvot, like tefillin?
No. The partnership applies to Torah study and cannot be made for other mitzvot.

### Can I support Torah learning even if I am not a business owner?
Yes. Any gift that makes learning possible makes you a partner in it, whatever the size. Call 925-384-7242 to hear how.

### Is a Yissachar-Zevulun gift the same as maaser?
Rabbi Feinstein's view is that a formal partnership is not part of maaser. Ordinary gifts to Torah learning can be maaser. Ask the Rabbi for your case.
