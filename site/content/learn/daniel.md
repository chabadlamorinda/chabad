Daniel was a Jewish sage and leader exiled to Babylon who refused to give up his Jewish practice: he kept kosher, prayed three times a day facing Jerusalem, and was thrown into the lions' den for it, where G-d saved him. His story is in the Book of Daniel, and it is a story of courage for anyone keeping Judaism in a world that pushes back.

Here is the story as Chabad.org tells it, and what it can teach teens and families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda.

## Who was Daniel?

Chabad.org tells that Daniel, together with three companions, Chananiah, Mishael and Azariah, was taken to Babylon by King Nebuchadnezzar after the destruction of the First Temple. In Babylon he was called Belteshazzar. Chabad.org adds that he was a member of the Men of the Great Assembly, the sages who gathered at the start of the Second Temple era. His book, the Book of Daniel, is part of Ketuvim (Writings) in the [Tanach](/learn/tanach).

Chabad.org notes that scholars debate whether to count Daniel as a prophet. He did, however, have visions about future kingdoms and the time of the Messiah.

## Daniel keeps kosher

The young captives were selected to serve in the king's court and offered the royal delicacies and wine. Daniel and his friends refused the non-kosher food and ate legumes (seeds) and drank water. Chabad.org says they looked healthier than those who ate the royal food.

They also faced pressure to change: the king even gave them idol-based names. The lesson is that they did not hide who they were. See [kosher](/learn/kosher).

## Dreams and the writing on the wall

Daniel became known for interpreting dreams. When Nebuchadnezzar had a dream he could not remember, Daniel and his friends prayed and the dream was revealed. Chabad.org says Daniel described a succession of empires that would rise and fall. The king wanted to worship Daniel, but Daniel refused and made him governor and head of the wise men.

Years later, King Belshazzar held a feast and drank from the gold and silver vessels taken from the Temple in Jerusalem. A hand appeared and wrote on the wall: *Mene, Mene, Tekel, Upharsin*. No one could read it. Daniel was called and explained that the king's reign had been measured and would end. That very night Belshazzar was killed. (In Chabad.org's telling, it was assassination.)

## The lions' den

Under King Darius, jealous officials convinced the king to ban prayer to anyone but the king for thirty days, on pain of the lions' den. Daniel went to his upper room, where the windows faced Jerusalem, and prayed three times a day as he always did.

His enemies reported him. Darius did not want to harm Daniel, but the law could not be changed. As Daniel was lowered in, the king said, "May your G-d, whom you worship continually, rescue you!" At dawn Daniel was unharmed. He said an angel had closed the lions' mouths. Darius then had the accusers thrown into the den.

Daniel's three daily prayers are one root of our own three daily prayers. See [daily prayers](/learn/daily-prayers).

## What teens and families can learn

1. **Be yourself openly.** Daniel did not hide his Judaism.
2. **Small habits are big.** Praying at the same time every day was what saved him from forgetting who he was.
3. **Face Jerusalem.** Having something that points your life in a direction is a source of strength.
4. **Courage can be quiet.** Daniel did not fight. He simply stayed faithful.
5. **You are not alone.** G-d is with us in every "den." See [Jewish identity for teens](/learn/jewish-identity-for-teens).

Daniel is also linked, in some traditions, to the Purim story, though identifications vary. Ask the Rabbi if you would like to learn more.

## Common questions

### Who was Daniel in the Bible?
Daniel was a Jewish sage exiled to Babylon who rose to leadership and stayed faithful to Judaism. The Book of Daniel records his story and visions.

### Why was Daniel thrown into the lions' den?
He kept praying three times a day despite a law forbidding prayer to anyone but the king. He was saved, and his accusers were thrown in instead.

### Why did Daniel pray facing Jerusalem?
Jews pray toward Jerusalem, the site of the Holy Temple. Daniel prayed in a room with windows facing Jerusalem.

### Did Daniel keep kosher?
Yes. He and his three friends refused the king's non-kosher food and ate legumes and water instead.

### Was Daniel a prophet?
He had prophetic visions, but scholars debate whether he is counted among the prophets. His book is in the Writings. See [what is a prophet](/learn/what-is-a-prophet).
