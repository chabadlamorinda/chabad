Techiyat hameitim (תחיית המתים), the resurrection of the dead, is the Jewish belief that in the time of Moshiach the dead will be brought back to life, with soul and body reunited. It is the thirteenth of the thirteen principles of Jewish faith, and we mention it in our prayers every day.

## One of the thirteen principles of faith

Maimonides listed thirteen basic principles of Jewish belief, and the last is that the dead will live again. Chabad.org calls it Techiyat HaMeitim, the "vivification of the dead," one of the cardinal principles or "foundations" of Judaism. You can read all thirteen in [the thirteen principles of faith](/learn/thirteen-principles-of-faith).

## Where we say it every day

In the Amidah, the central prayer said three times a day, the second blessing praises G-d "who revives the dead" (mechayeh hameitim). We say it not as a figure of speech, but as a statement of trust: the life that G-d gives is stronger than death. See [the Amidah](/learn/amidah).

## Why body and soul are reunited

Why would the body come back? Chabad.org explains: so that both can experience the results of their actions. The soul does not act alone. It is the body that does the deed, and the soul gives it direction. Chabad.org tells a parable of a king with two guards in his orchard, one blind and one lame. Together they could steal fruit, one carrying the other, so both were held to account. In the same way, G-d does not deprive any creature of what it is due. Read more in [the soul](/learn/the-soul) and [the afterlife](/learn/afterlife).

## When it will happen: the time of Moshiach

Chabad.org teaches that the dead will be revived in the Era of Moshiach. That era is the same natural world, but without human corruption and evil, a time of continued growth. In that era the revived will live a perfect but still physical life, which will eventually end. Only the World to Come is fully beyond nature. Read [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach).

Jewish thinkers have different views on the details. Maimonides taught that the World to Come is purely spiritual. Nachmanides taught that resurrection leads to an eternal life with souls enclothed in bodies, and Chabad.org says Chassidic and Kabbalistic teaching agrees with him. Questions about this are very welcome. Ask the Rabbi.

## Ezekiel and the dry bones

The prophet Ezekiel (chapter 37) describes G-d showing him a valley of dry bones, which come together and live again. The portion is read as the haftarah on Shabbat Chol Hamoed Pesach, during Passover. Chabad.org cites the Tur, quoting Rav Hai Gaon, that the resurrection of the dead will take place in the month of Nissan, which is why it is read then. The Talmud (Sanhedrin 92b) asks whether this was a real event or a parable, and different Sages answer differently. In either case, it is a vision of hope for a people who felt lost. See [Passover for beginners](/learn/passover-for-beginners).

## Living with this belief

This belief does not take away from grief. It speaks of hope after pain. Many people find it a comfort when a loved one has died, as in the words of consolation for mourners. If you are mourning, please speak to the Rabbi. For practical help, see [a Jewish funeral](/learn/jewish-funeral).

## Common questions

### Do Jews believe in the resurrection of the dead?
Yes. It is the thirteenth of Maimonides' thirteen principles of Jewish faith, and Chabad.org calls it a foundation of Judaism.

### Where is the resurrection of the dead mentioned in Jewish prayer?
In the second blessing of the Amidah, which says G-d "revives the dead," said three times each day.

### When will the dead be resurrected?
Chabad.org teaches that it will take place in the Era of Moshiach. We do not know the date, so we hope and prepare.

### What does Ezekiel's vision of the dry bones mean?
It is a prophecy of renewal. The Sages read it as a sign of the resurrection and the haftarah is read during Passover. The Talmud discusses whether it was literal or a parable.

### Will the body and soul be together again?
Yes. Chabad.org teaches that body and soul are reunited so both can experience the results of what they did together.

### Is this different from the World to Come?
Chabad.org says the Era of Moshiach is a natural world with revived people, while the World to Come is beyond nature. Opinions differ on the details, so ask the Rabbi.
