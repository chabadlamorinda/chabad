Many people are surprised to learn that reincarnation is part of Jewish tradition. In Hebrew it is called **gilgul neshamot**, the "rolling" or cycling of souls. It is taught mainly in [Kabbalah](/learn/kabbalah) and Chassidus, and its message is less about past lives than about the purpose of this one.

## Where it comes from

- **The Zohar**, the central book of Kabbalah, speaks about souls returning to this world.
- **The Arizal**, Rabbi Isaac Luria of Safed (16th century), taught about gilgul in detail. His student Rabbi Chaim Vital recorded these teachings in *Shaar HaGilgulim*, "The Gate of Reincarnations."
- **Not every scholar agreed.** Some medieval thinkers, such as Rabbi Saadia Gaon, rejected the idea. Over time, through the influence of Kabbalah, it became widely accepted, and it is part of Chassidic teaching.

You can even find it in the prayer book. In the [bedtime Shema](/learn/bedtime-shema) we forgive anyone who hurt us, "whether in this gilgul or in another gilgul."

## Why a soul returns

Kabbalah teaches that every [soul](/learn/the-soul) comes into the world with a mission: mitzvahs to do, character to refine, and a part of the world to make holier. This is called the soul's **tikkun**, its repair or completion.

If a soul did not finish its task, it may come back to complete what is missing. Often it returns not to do everything over again, but for a few particular things. The Arizal also taught that a soul can return in a gentler way, joining another person for a time to help them, which is called **ibbur**.

## Not a reason for fatalism

It can be tempting to think, "If I'm here to fix something from a past life, my life is already decided." Judaism teaches the opposite:

- **[Free will](/learn/free-will) is real.** Every choice you make is yours.
- **You cannot know your past lives,** and you do not need to. The Torah tells you what to do: the mitzvahs in front of you today.
- **No one is "stuck."** A hard situation may be part of your mission, but that makes it an opportunity, not a sentence.
- **Every mitzvah counts.** The [Baal Shem Tov](/learn/baal-shem-tov) taught, and the Rebbe often repeated, that a soul may come down to this world for seventy or eighty years just to do a favor for another person.

## Common questions

### Do all Jews believe in reincarnation?
It is widely accepted, especially in Chassidic and Kabbalistic teaching, though some earlier scholars disagreed. It is not one of the core principles of faith that everyone must hold.

### Can I find out who I was in a past life?
Jewish tradition does not encourage trying. Great Kabbalists like the Arizal were known for this insight, but for the rest of us the focus is the mission in front of us now.

### Does reincarnation replace the World to Come or the Resurrection?
No. Judaism teaches a [World to Come](/learn/afterlife) and the resurrection of the dead in the days of [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach). Gilgul is part of the soul's journey, not a replacement for them.

### Does this mean my hardships are deserved?
Not at all, and we should never say that about anyone else. Ask the Rabbi if this question weighs on you; see also [why bad things happen](/learn/why-bad-things-happen).
