Yes, a child with little or no Hebrew school can have a meaningful Bar or Bat Mitzvah. A child becomes a Bar or Bat Mitzvah on the Hebrew birthday whether or not the preparation was long, and the essentials, taking on mitzvot, learning the blessings and sharing a few words of Torah, can be learned in a matter of months, at the child's own pace.

## First, take a breath

Many families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda come to this late: the date is near, and the child has not read Hebrew before. That is common, and it is not too late. Jewish tradition presents the day as the beginning of an active Jewish life, not a test of years of study. The Bar or Bat Mitzvah itself happens automatically; preparation is about making it meaningful. See [Bar Mitzvah](/learn/bar-mitzvah) and [Bat Mitzvah](/learn/bat-mitzvah).

## What matters most

- **The mitzvot the child takes on.** For a boy, [tefillin](/learn/tefillin); for a girl, Shabbat candles, challah and a personal mitzvah. See [girls lighting Shabbat candles](/learn/girls-lighting-shabbat-candles).
- **The blessings** for the aliyah, if a boy is called to the Torah. Short, learnable and the one thing every boy says. See [aliyah](/learn/aliyah).
- **Understanding.** Why we keep mitzvot and what it means to be part of the Jewish people.
- **A few words of Torah.** A short talk in English, with a Hebrew phrase, is perfect. See [the Bar Mitzvah speech](/learn/bar-mitzvah-speech).

A full Torah reading or haftarah is wonderful but optional. See [learning to chant the Torah](/learn/learning-to-chant-the-torah).

## A calm plan, step by step

1. **Find the Hebrew date** and count the weeks left. See [finding the Hebrew date](/learn/bar-mitzvah-hebrew-birthday-date).
2. **Call the Rabbi** and tell him honestly where your child is. At Chabad of Lamorinda, every child learns at their own pace with the Rabbi and Rebbetzin, and the Rabbi can shape the plan around your time. Call 925-384-7242.
3. **Start with the letters and vowels.** Many children can learn to sound out Hebrew in a few weeks with short daily practice. See [the Hebrew alphabet](/learn/hebrew-alphabet) and [Hebrew lessons in Lamorinda](/learn/hebrew-lessons-in-lamorinda).
4. **Choose one or two blessings** to learn thoroughly, rather than ten halfway. Use transliteration at first and move gradually to the Hebrew letters.
5. **Begin the mitzvah early.** A boy starts tefillin about two months before. See [starting tefillin](/learn/starting-tefillin-before-bar-mitzvah). A girl can begin lighting her candle on the next Friday.
6. **Choose a short speech** and write it in the child's own words.
7. **Plan the celebration last.** See [a meaningful celebration](/learn/meaningful-bar-bat-mitzvah-celebration).

## Learning together

The best motivation is a parent who also learns. Sit down together over the alphabet. Many parents discover that they, too, never had a Jewish education, and that learning with their child is a gift. If a parent wants to do this too, see [Hebrew reading for adults](/learn/hebrew-reading-for-adults) and [adult Bar and Bat Mitzvah](/learn/adult-bar-bat-mitzvah).

For girls, local Chabad centers offer pre-Bat Mitzvah classes and a Bat Mitzvah Club that meets for events and activities, and the site's program page says that girls at Chabad of Lamorinda join the Bat Mitzvah Club. See the [Bar and Bat Mitzvah program](/programs/bar-bat-mitzvah). If you also want your child to learn alongside peers, ask about [Hebrew school](/programs/hebrew-school).

## When there is less time, or a different need

If the date is only a few weeks away, say so on the first call. A shorter plan, with the blessings, one mitzvah and one Torah thought, can still be moving. If your child has learning differences, see [a Bar or Bat Mitzvah with special needs](/learn/special-needs-bar-bat-mitzvah).

## Common questions

### Can my child have a Bar Mitzvah without Hebrew school?
Yes. The child becomes Bar Mitzvah on the Hebrew birthday regardless, and a meaningful celebration can be built around the mitzvot, the blessings and a short talk. Call the Rabbi to make a plan.

### How long does it take to prepare for a Bar Mitzvah?
Many families start a year ahead, but a shorter plan of a few months, or even weeks, can work. The Rabbi can shape it around your time.

### Do we have to read Hebrew to have a Bar or Bat Mitzvah?
Not to be Bar or Bat Mitzvah. Reading helps, and many children learn the basics quickly. Start with the letters and vowels and add the blessings.

### Is it too late if the date is close?
Rarely. Even a few weeks can be used for the blessings, tefillin or candles and a short Torah thought. Call right away.

### Can my child join other children who are preparing?
Girls can join the Bat Mitzvah Club. Boys and girls can also ask about Hebrew school. Call 925-384-7242 to ask about what is planned.

### What if neither parent knows Hebrew?
That is fine. Learn alongside your child, and ask the Rabbi to help. See [Hebrew reading for adults](/learn/hebrew-reading-for-adults).
