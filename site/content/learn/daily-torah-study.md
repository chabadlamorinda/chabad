Torah study is a daily mitzvah for every Jew, man or woman, young or old. Even a few minutes each day connects you to G-d's wisdom and gives your whole day a different feeling.

## Why study every day

The Torah says, "These words which I command you today shall be on your heart... and you shall speak of them when you sit in your home and when you walk on the way, when you lie down and when you rise up" (Deuteronomy 6:6 to 7). Joshua was told, "This book of the Torah shall not leave your mouth, and you shall meditate on it day and night" (Joshua 1:8).

Torah study is not only about gaining information. Chassidic teaching explains that when you learn Torah, you are connecting your mind with G-d's own wisdom. It is a form of closeness to G-d unlike anything else.

## The Chabad daily study cycles

To help every Jew learn something every day, Chabad Rebbes established fixed daily study cycles:

- **Chumash:** each day, a section of the [weekly parsha](/learn/weekly-parsha) with Rashi's commentary, one aliyah per day of the week.
- **Tehillim:** a portion of [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) each day, completing the book every month.
- **Tanya:** a short daily section of the [Tanya](/learn/tanya), the foundational book of Chabad philosophy, completed every year.

These three are called Chitas, an acronym of Chumash, Tehillim and Tanya, set up by the Previous Rebbe.

- **Rambam:** in 1984 [the Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe) launched the daily study of the Rambam's Mishneh Torah, with tracks of three chapters a day, one chapter a day, or the Book of Mitzvot. See our guide to [Rambam](/learn/rambam).

Learning the same section as Jews worldwide on the same day creates a beautiful unity.

## How to start

1. **Pick a time.** Morning coffee, the commute, lunch, or before bed.
2. **Start small.** Five or ten minutes is a real beginning. Consistency matters more than length.
3. **Choose a topic you enjoy:** the weekly portion, Jewish law, Jewish ideas, or Chassidic teachings.
4. **Find a partner.** Learning with a chavruta, a study partner, makes learning deeper and keeps you going.
5. **Join a class.** A weekly class builds knowledge and community.
6. **Use good books and recordings.** Many classic texts are available in English.

## Women and Torah study

Women study Torah too. Jewish women learn the laws they need to keep, Jewish ideas and Chassidic teachings, and many study the daily cycles. The Rebbe strongly encouraged advanced Torah learning for women and girls. Read more in our guide to [Jewish women](/learn/jewish-women).

## Common questions

### What if I miss a day?
Just continue the next day. If you can, make up the missed section. Do not let one missed day end the habit.

### Does reading in English count?
Yes. Learning Torah in any language you understand is a mitzvah. Over time, learning some of the original Hebrew adds richness.

### What should I learn first?
Many start with the weekly parsha or practical Jewish law, since knowing how to keep mitzvot is basic. Ask the Rabbi for a recommendation suited to you.

### Is listening to a class the same as learning?
Yes. Listening attentively to Torah being taught is Torah study. Saying the words yourself, even softly, adds to the mitzvah.
