The Tanya's gift to parents is a way of seeing: every Jewish child has a G-dly soul, and your job is to help that soul shine, with patience instead of anger and with joy instead of heaviness. This guide applies teachings of the Tanya, as Chabad.org presents them, to everyday parenting. The Tanya itself is not a parenting manual, and what follows is encouragement, not professional advice.

## Your child has a soul, and it is "a part of G-d"

Chapter 2 of the [Tanya](/learn/tanya) describes the G-dly soul of every Jew as, in the Alter Rebbe's words, truly a part of G-d above. Chabad.org's text goes on to say that the soul itself is given from Above, and it is not set by what parents do; a very lofty soul can be born to a parent who feels ordinary. What parents influence is the "garment" the soul uses to express itself.

For a parent in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda who is looking at a messy room or a stubborn four-year-old, this is a big thought: behind the behavior stands a soul that came from a very high place. Seeing it does not remove discipline, but it changes our tone. See [disciplining children](/learn/disciplining-children).

## Anger and patience: Epistle 25

Chabad.org's page on the Rebbe's guidance about anger says the Rebbe often recommended Epistle 25 of the Tanya. It teaches that anger is a lapse of faith: in that moment we forget that all that happens is in G-d's hands. It also says the person who causes us trouble remains responsible for his choices.

A parent can use the first part of this thought as a pause. The Rebbe's own practical advice, quoted on that page, is that "the first step is to wait," and that if we hurt someone, even in anger, we should apologize in person. For a parent, that can mean apologizing to a child, which also teaches the child how to do it. For more, see [anger](/learn/anger-jewish-view) and [patience](/learn/patience).

Epistle 25 also says that anger can be right in a religious duty, such as stopping someone from doing wrong. Parents sometimes need to step in firmly. The line is between a firm, calm "no" and a personal outburst.

## Bring joy to the work

Chapter 26 compares serving G-d to wrestling. A sluggish wrestler loses even to a weaker opponent because he is heavy and slow. Alacrity, which comes from joy and an open heart, is what wins. The same is true of parenting. A parent who is anxious and heavy has less to give than one who has recovered some joy.

The chapter also says that sadness over failings should be kept to set times and then followed by trust in G-d's forgiveness. If you had a bad day with your kids, you can note what you want to change, then let it go and begin again tomorrow. See [forgiving yourself](/learn/forgiving-yourself).

## Teaching by doing

Chapter 4 describes thought, speech and action as the garments of the soul. Children watch what we do. A child who sees a parent give tzedakah, light Shabbat candles or say a kind word learns something that a lecture cannot teach. See [raising kind children](/learn/raising-kind-children) and [bedtime Shema](/learn/bedtime-shema).

## Your child's struggle is real too

If the Tanya teaches that each of us has an inner struggle, a child also has one. When a child struggles, you can help the G-dly side by being a calm mind for a stormy heart. That is an application of the Tanya, not a quotation from it. If you are worried about your child, talk to a professional and ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### What does Tanya say about parenting?
The Tanya does not have a section on parenting, but its teachings about the soul, patience, joy and example apply directly to raising children.

### Does every child have a G-dly soul?
Chapter 2 teaches that every Jew has a G-dly soul that is truly a part of G-d above.

### What does Tanya say about anger at children?
Epistle 25 says anger is a lapse of faith. The Rebbe's advice, as Chabad.org quotes it, is to wait before reacting and to apologize if you hurt someone.

### How can I stay joyful when parenting is hard?
Chapter 26 teaches that joy brings energy, and that heaviness hurts our work. If sadness or worry feels too heavy, ask the Rabbi and also seek professional help.

### Is it good to apologize to a child?
Chabad.org quotes the Rebbe saying that if you hurt someone, you should apologize in person.
