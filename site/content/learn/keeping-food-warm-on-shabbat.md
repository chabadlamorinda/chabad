You keep food warm on Shabbat by setting up a heat source before Shabbat begins, such as a covered stove (a blech) or a single-setting hot plate, and by putting on it only food that is fully cooked and dry. You may not cook on Shabbat, so the plan is made on Friday and the cooking is finished before candle lighting.

## Why can't I just heat food up?

Cooking is one of the [39 categories of work](/learn/thirty-nine-melachot) forbidden on Shabbat. But there is an important principle: "there is no cooking after cooking." Reheating food that is already fully cooked and dry is not considered cooking, so it may be kept warm. Liquids are stricter, and food that is not yet cooked may never be warmed.

The same rule explains why a hot Shabbat lunch is possible, from [cholent](/learn/cholent) to a warm kugel, even though the stove cannot be adjusted on Shabbat.

## What is a blech?

A blech (בְּלֶעךְ, Yiddish for "tin") is a metal sheet placed over the stove burners before Shabbat. It covers the flames and spreads the heat. Chabad.org explains that it has zones: directly over the flame food would cook, farther away it is only warm. The zones decide where food may be put.

How to use one:

1. **Before Shabbat, turn on the flame** (low) and cover the stove with the blech.
2. **Place the pots on the blech before Shabbat.**
3. **Only fully cooked food** may be put back later, and only on the cooler part, away from the flame.
4. **Do not move a pot from a cooler area over the flame.** You may move it from the hot area to a cooler one.
5. **Do not add raw food** or partly cooked food to the blech on Shabbat.

## Hot plates and warming drawers

A **single-setting hot plate**, turned on before Shabbat, can be used to keep fully cooked food warm. A plate with variable settings is a problem, even on its lowest setting, because the setting can be changed. A warming drawer is allowed in the same way if it was on before Shabbat and has one temperature. An ordinary oven cannot be used to reheat food on Shabbat, even in "Shabbat mode." Because details differ by model, ask the Rabbi before relying on any appliance. For electric appliances see [electricity on Shabbat](/learn/electricity-on-shabbat).

## Dry food, liquids and drinks

- **Dry, fully cooked food** such as chicken, kugel, rice or challah is the easy case.
- **Food with liquid,** like soup, is more strict. Under Ashkenazi custom, a liquid that has cooled completely may not be reheated, while one that is still warm may be kept warm.
- **Hot water:** a Shabbat urn or kettle that was filled and turned on before Shabbat keeps water hot for tea and coffee. For a baby's bottle, ask the Rabbi how to warm it.

Ask the Rabbi about soups, sauces, baby food and anything with fat, because the rules depend on the food.

## A simple Friday routine

- Cook everything fully on Friday.
- Cover the stove or turn on the hot plate before candle lighting. See the time on [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times).
- Put the pots in place, label them, and leave them alone.
- On Shabbat morning, take food from the warm area and serve it.

## Common questions

### How do you keep food warm on Shabbat?
Use a blech or a single-setting hot plate that was turned on before Shabbat, and keep on it only food that is fully cooked and dry.

### What is a blech?
A blech is a metal sheet that covers the stove flames before Shabbat, so that pots on it may be kept warm and not cooked.

### Can you reheat food on Shabbat?
Fully cooked dry food can be warmed by a pre-set heat source such as a blech. You cannot put food in an oven to heat it up, even in "Shabbat mode."

### Can I use a hot plate on Shabbat?
Yes, if it has a single setting and was turned on before Shabbat. A hot plate with adjustable settings is not allowed.

### Can I use a hot water urn on Shabbat?
Yes, an urn filled and turned on before Shabbat can give hot water for drinks. Ask the Rabbi about the details.
