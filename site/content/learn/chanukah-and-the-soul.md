"The soul of man is the candle of G-d" is a line from the Book of Proverbs (20:27), and it is the key to Chanukah's deepest lesson. The Chanukah lights are not only about a miracle long ago: they are a picture of the G-dly soul that burns inside every Jew, and of our task to make it shine, and to help light another's candle.

## What does the verse say?

On Chabad.org the verse is translated: "Man's soul is the Lord's lamp, which searches out all the innermost parts." Rashi explains that the soul inside a person testifies about him, so that it is, in a way, our inner voice. Other Chabad.org writings use the words "candle," "lamp" and "flame" for the same idea. In Hebrew, the word *neshamah* (נשמה) is the soul, and the word *ner* (נר) is a candle.

For a wider look at the soul, see [the soul](/learn/the-soul).

## Oil, wick and flame

The second Rebbe of Chabad, Rabbi DovBer of Lubavitch, wrote a Chanukah discourse that begins with this verse. It uses the parts of a lamp, the oil, the wick and the flame, to explain how each of us should serve G-d. Practical mitzvah observance is compared to oil, which joins the flame to the wick and keeps the soul's light burning in the body. Contemplation and inner growth are important, the discourse says, but it is the doing of mitzvot that is most essential.

Another Chabad.org essay says the flame is like Torah study and the wick is like mitzvot, the action. We need both, learning and doing, for the light to burn. See [Chanukah and Torah study](/learn/chanukah-and-torah-study).

## A light that cannot be put out

Rabbi Yitzi Hurwitz explains on Chabad.org that the soul has five levels, and that the highest, the *yechidah*, is the part of us that cannot be touched by negative influences. Reaching it does more than push the darkness away. It changes the darkness, so that "the darkness itself begins to shine." The menorah's light is the symbol of this. So when you feel that you are not "religious enough," Chanukah's message is that your soul's flame is never gone.

## One person lights another

In Chabad.org's commentary on the verse "when you light the lamps" (Numbers 8:2), the verse about the soul is quoted, and the lesson is: each Jew should make his own candle shine brightly and help light another Jew's candle, through learning Torah, doing mitzvot and inspiring others to do the same. For a Rebbe's call to bring Chanukah's light outside, see [the Rebbe on Chanukah](/learn/the-rebbe-on-chanukah).

## A true story about one more light

A story on Chabad.org, told by the person it happened to, shows what this means in practice. Alan Magill was a recreation therapist in a New York rehabilitation center, a kind of nursing home, where open flames were not allowed and the only menorah was a small electric one. Each night he lit one more bulb. On the eighth night, the new bulb would not light. Many residents had memory loss, and he felt that the right number of lights mattered to them, so he went searching. A hardware store had no bulb. At a nearly empty synagogue, an older man, Morris Gallant, remembered that an electric menorah had been stored downstairs for twenty years, and he climbed down to find it. Magill tested the old bulbs one by one. The first eight did not work, but the last one lit.

The author sees in this the same faith as the search of the Maccabees for pure oil. The effort itself had value, even before it succeeded. Our guide [Chanukah in senior living](/learn/chanukah-in-senior-living) is about bringing Chanukah's light to those who may not be able to come out.

## Common questions

### What does "the soul of man is the candle of G-d" mean?
It means that every person's soul is a G-dly light, a lamp of G-d. The verse is from Proverbs 20:27, and Chabad.org's teachings use it to explain the Chanukah lights.

### Where is the verse "ner Hashem nishmat adam" from?
It is from Proverbs (Mishlei) 20:27. Rashi explains that the soul inside a person testifies about him.

### What is the yechidah?
It is the highest level of the soul, according to Chabad.org, the part that is always connected to G-d and cannot be touched by negative influences.

### How can I help light another person's candle?
Chabad.org says a person can help another by learning Torah, doing mitzvot and inspiring others to do the same.

### Why do Chanukah lights point to the soul?
Like a flame, the soul rises upward, and like the menorah, its purpose is to give light. One teaching says the darkness itself can shine.
