Chametz is any food made from wheat, barley, rye, oats or spelt that has risen by coming into contact with water for more than 18 minutes. On Passover, Jews may not eat, own or benefit from chametz, so before the holiday we clean it out, search for it, sell it and burn it.

## What counts as chametz?

Chametz includes bread, cake, cookies, pasta, cereal, crackers, beer and whiskey, and any food containing these grains or their derivatives that is not certified kosher for Passover. Many processed foods contain hidden chametz, such as grain alcohol, malt, or wheat starch.

Matzah is made from the same flour, but it is baked quickly under careful supervision so it never has a chance to rise.

Ashkenazi Jews also avoid kitniyot during Passover: rice, corn, beans, lentils and similar foods. These are not chametz, but the custom has been kept for centuries.

## Why is chametz forbidden?

When the Jewish people left Egypt, they left in such a hurry that their dough had no time to rise (Exodus 12:39). The Torah commands us not to eat or even own chametz during Passover.

Chassidic teaching explains that chametz, which puffs itself up, represents ego and arrogance. Matzah, flat and simple, represents humility. Clearing out the chametz is also a chance to clear out pride.

## How to get rid of chametz, step by step

### 1. Clean

In the weeks before Passover, clean every place where chametz may have gotten: the kitchen, pantry, car, bags, pockets and desk drawers. The goal is to remove chametz, not to spring-clean the whole house. Crumbs that are dirty and inedible are not a problem, though we still clean carefully.

### 2. Kasher or cover the kitchen

Kitchens need special preparation. Many families use separate Passover dishes, and counters, sinks and stoves are cleaned and covered or kashered. See our [kosher kitchen guide](/learn/kosher-kitchen) and ask the Rabbi for details.

### 3. Sell what you keep

Chametz you do not want to throw away, such as an open bottle of whiskey or a pantry of pasta, can be sold to a non-Jew for the duration of Passover through a rabbi. Put it in a closed cabinet or room, marked off, and do not use it during the holiday. The rabbi buys it back for you after Passover.

### 4. Search: bedikat chametz

On the night before Passover, after nightfall, search the house by candlelight or flashlight. Many hide ten small wrapped pieces of bread around the house to be found during the search. Say the blessing "al biur chametz" before you begin, and afterward say the declaration nullifying any chametz you did not find.

### 5. Burn: biur chametz

The next morning, burn the chametz you found, before the deadline in the late morning. Then say the final declaration nullifying all chametz. Check the exact times for your location.

## Common questions

### Is it really necessary to sell chametz?
If you have chametz you do not want to throw out, selling it is the way to avoid owning it on Passover. Many people sell to be safe, in case some chametz was missed. Use our online form or call us.

### What if I find chametz during Passover?
Do not eat it. On a weekday of the holiday, burn it or flush it away right away. On Shabbat or a full holiday day, cover it and dispose of it afterward. Ask the Rabbi if you are not sure.

### Are pet foods a problem?
Many pet foods contain chametz. Since we may not own or benefit from chametz on Passover, find a kosher for Passover pet food or ask the Rabbi for guidance.

### What happens to chametz owned by a Jew during Passover?
Chametz that a Jew owned during Passover may not be eaten or used even after the holiday. That is why many stores wait to restock, and why selling chametz before the holiday matters.
