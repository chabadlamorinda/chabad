On Chanukah we light one candle on the first night and add one more each night, until eight burn on the last night, because Jewish law follows Beit Hillel, who taught that in matters of holiness we go up and not down. Their rivals, Beit Shammai, said to begin with eight and decrease.

Behind this old argument is a lesson that many people find useful all year: how to change your life.

## The debate in the Talmud

The Talmud (Shabbat 21b) records a disagreement between two great schools of sages, the House of Shammai and the House of Hillel (see [Hillel and Shammai](/learn/hillel-and-shammai) for who they were).

- **Beit Shammai:** light eight candles on the first night, then one fewer each night.
- **Beit Hillel:** light one candle on the first night, then add one each night, until eight on the last.

We follow Beit Hillel. In fact, the name of the holiday is read as a hint to this ruling: "eight candles, and the law follows Beit Hillel." You can read about the name in [What Does Chanukah Mean?](/learn/what-does-chanukah-mean).

## The idea of "always going up"

Why does Beit Hillel's way win? Chabad.org explains that one reason is the principle in Hebrew called *ma'alin bakodesh v'ein moridin*: in matters of holiness we raise up, we do not bring down. A mitzvah done tonight is not erased tomorrow, so tomorrow should be more. Another reason offered is that each night we add to match the days that have already passed.

There is a practical consequence. If someone cannot manage the full number of candles on a certain night, the guidance depends on which reason one follows. Ask the Rabbi about your own situation.

## What Shammai was saying

Shammai's view is not foolish. Chabad teaching gives a thoughtful explanation of it.

- **Shammai says: fight the darkness head-on.** Start with all your strength, with the most light, because that is when the fight is hardest. Each night the darkness is weaker, so you need less and less, until it is gone.
- **Shammai's worry about small steps:** small steps are fragile, like a sandcastle that one wave sweeps away.

## What Hillel was saying

- **Hillel says: build the good.** Start small, with a step anyone can take, then add a little each day. Consistency counts more than intensity.
- **A little light beats a lot of darkness.** One small flame drives away darkness much larger than itself, and the darkness cannot put it out.
- **A mitzvah's light is permanent.** The light of a mitzvah stays with a person and carries its blessing forward, and each day brings the light of the days before it.
- **Actions, not plans.** Intentions do not give light by themselves. Only what a person actually does makes light.

Chabad.org suggests that Shammai's method suited a time when people had the spiritual strength for a direct battle, while in our times of exile a positive approach is what works. Even so, the best life often needs both: to stop the bad and to build the good.

## How to use this in your own life

Chabad teaching connects this to the Rebbe's emphasis on always growing: whatever we did yesterday, we try to do a little more today. Here are ideas for a family in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda:

1. Choose one small mitzvah and do it each day of Chanukah: giving tzedakah, a moment of prayer, or lighting Shabbat candles.
2. Add something on each night, such as one more blessing, one more page of learning or one more kind call.
3. Keep it going after the holiday. Chanukah is the training for the rest of the year.

## Common questions

### Why do we add a candle each night of Chanukah?
We follow Beit Hillel's opinion, which says to light one candle on the first night and add one each night. The principle is that in matters of holiness we go up and do not go down.

### What did Beit Shammai say about the Chanukah candles?
Beit Shammai said to light eight candles on the first night and reduce by one each night. The law does not follow this view.

### Why is the halachah like Beit Hillel?
The Sages accepted Beit Hillel's view that holiness always increases. Many also see their approach, a small beginning and daily growth, as the best way to change.

### On which side do I add the new candle?
In Chabad custom the first candle goes at the right end of the menorah and each new candle is added to its left. See our [menorah guide](/learn/chanukah-menorah) for the lighting order, or ask the Rabbi.

### What does adding a candle teach me?
It teaches that growth comes from steady steps and that the good we do stays with us and builds on itself.
