A kosher Chanukah menorah has eight lights in a straight row, all at the same height, with a ninth place set apart for the shamash. If you are buying a menorah in Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda or anywhere in the East Bay, these are the points to check.

## What a Chanukah menorah is

A Chanukah menorah, also called a chanukiah (חֲנֻכִּיָּה), holds the lights of the holiday: eight for the eight nights, plus the shamash. It recalls the Temple menorah, though it is a different object. See [how to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah).

## The must-haves

According to Chabad.org:

1. **Eight lights, plus a ninth for the shamash.** The shamash sits apart from the eight, higher, lower or to the side. See [what is the shamash](/learn/what-is-the-shamash).
2. **A straight line.** The eight lights are in a straight row, not in a curve or a circle.
3. **The same height.** All eight are level, or on a slope that is even, so that none looks higher than the others.
4. **Clear spaces between the lights.** Each flame should be seen as a separate flame, not merge into a blaze.
5. **Cups that can hold enough oil.** For an oil menorah, each cup should hold enough to burn at least an hour and a half, which covers Friday, when we light early. See [Chanukah on Shabbat](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat).
6. **A stable base.** Choose a menorah that will not tip, preferably with a back plate that protects the wall.

A candle menorah needs candle holders that fit your candles snugly. See [oil or candles](/learn/menorah-oil-and-candles).

## Style and material

Chabad.org encourages a beautiful menorah, such as silver, but any material that holds flames safely is fine. The Chabad custom is a menorah whose lights are all in one straight line, as described above, and that is the kind we recommend. Whether you choose a simple tin menorah or an heirloom, beauty adds to the mitzvah, but the points above are what make it kosher. For general buying advice for Judaica, see [the Judaica buying guide](/learn/judaica-buying-guide).

## Real flames, please

The mitzvah is fulfilled with fuel, a wick and a flame, as the Temple menorah had, so an electric menorah does not fulfill the mitzvah. Chabad.org says it is nice for publicizing the holiday along with a real menorah. If flames are a problem where you live, ask the Rabbi; see [menorah in an apartment or condo](/learn/menorah-in-an-apartment-or-condo).

## Common questions

### What makes a Chanukah menorah kosher?
Eight lights in a straight row at the same height, plus a separate ninth for the shamash, and holders or cups that can burn long enough.

### How do I choose a menorah?
Check that the eight lights are in a line and level, that the shamash is set apart, and that it is stable. Then choose a style you love.

### Can I use a menorah with lights in a curve or circle?
No. The eight flames should be in a straight line.

### Can a menorah have the shamash in the same line?
It should be set apart, usually higher or lower, so we know which light is the helper.

### Is an electric menorah kosher?
No, it does not fulfill the mitzvah, though it can decorate a window. Ask the Rabbi for special situations.

### Can I use tea lights?
Yes, in a pinch, as long as they burn at least half an hour after nightfall. A tray under them is a good idea.
