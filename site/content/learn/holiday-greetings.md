The most common Jewish holiday greeting is "Chag Sameach" (Happy holiday), and on Shabbat people say "Shabbat Shalom" (a peaceful Sabbath). Each holiday also has its own special wishes, in Hebrew or Yiddish, and knowing them is a warm way to connect with Jewish friends, neighbors and coworkers.

## Shabbat

- **Shabbat Shalom** (shah-BAHT shah-LOHM): "A peaceful Sabbath." Said from Friday afternoon through Shabbat.
- **Gut Shabbos** (Yiddish): "Good Shabbat." Very common in Chabad and Ashkenazi communities.
- **Shavua Tov** (Hebrew) or **A gute voch** (Yiddish): "A good week." Said after [Havdalah](/learn/havdalah) on Saturday night.

## Any holiday

- **Chag Sameach** (CHAHG sah-MAY-ach): "Happy holiday." Works for almost every festival.
- **Gut Yom Tov** (Yiddish): "Good holiday." Used for the major festivals, when work is not done.
- **Moadim L'Simcha**: "Times of joy." Said on the middle days of Sukkot and Passover. The traditional answer is "Chagim U'zmanim L'sasson," "holidays and seasons for gladness."

## Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur

- **Shana Tova** or **Shana Tova U'metukah**: "A good and sweet year."
- **K'tiva V'chatima Tova**: "May you be written and sealed for a good year." Said before and on [Rosh Hashanah](/holidays/rosh-hashanah).
- **G'mar Chatima Tova**: "A good final sealing." Said between Rosh Hashanah and [Yom Kippur](/holidays/yom-kippur), and on Yom Kippur itself.
- **Tzom Kal** or **Have an easy fast**: said before Yom Kippur and other fast days. Some prefer "Have a meaningful fast."

## Sukkot and Simchat Torah

**Chag Sameach** and **Gut Yom Tov** are used throughout. On [Hoshana Rabbah](/learn/hoshana-rabbah), many wish each other **A gute kvitel**, "a good note," the final sealing of the year's judgment.

## Chanukah and Purim

- **Happy Chanukah** or **Chanukah Sameach**. In Yiddish, **A freilichen Chanukah**.
- **Purim Sameach**: "Happy Purim." Or **A freilichen Purim**.

## Passover

- **Chag Kasher V'sameach**: "A kosher and happy Passover." Used in the weeks before and during [Passover](/holidays/passover).
- **Chag Sameach** and **Gut Yom Tov** also work.

## Other times and occasions

- **Chodesh Tov**: "A good month," said on [Rosh Chodesh](/learn/rosh-chodesh).
- **Mazal Tov**: "Congratulations," for a birth, engagement, wedding, birthday or bar mitzvah.
- **Yasher Koach**: "May your strength increase." Said to someone who received an aliyah or did a mitzvah. The reply is **Baruch tihyeh**, "may you be blessed."
- **Refuah Shleimah**: "A complete recovery," to someone who is ill.
- **Tisha B'Av**: on this day of mourning, people do not greet each other.

## Common questions

### How do you respond to "Shabbat Shalom"?
Simply say "Shabbat Shalom" back. The same is true of "Chag Sameach," "Shana Tova" and "Gut Yom Tov": repeat the greeting, or say "Same to you."

### Is it okay to say "Happy Yom Kippur"?
Yom Kippur is a solemn but hopeful day, so "Happy" can sound odd. Better choices are "G'mar Chatima Tova" or "Have an easy and meaningful fast."

### What do I say to a Jewish coworker if I am not Jewish?
"Happy holiday" or "Chag Sameach" is always appreciated. On Rosh Hashanah, "Happy New Year" or "Shana Tova" is perfect.

### What is the difference between Chag Sameach and Gut Yom Tov?
They mean nearly the same thing. "Chag Sameach" is Hebrew and common in Israel; "Gut Yom Tov" is Yiddish and common among Ashkenazi and Chassidic Jews, especially on the major festivals.
