Judaism teaches that the body is a gift from G-d and the home of the soul, so taking care of your health is a mitzvah. Eating well, sleeping, exercising and getting medical care are part of serving G-d, not a distraction from it.

## The body and the soul

In Jewish thought, the body is not an enemy of the [soul](/learn/the-soul). They are partners. The soul gives the body life, and the body allows the soul to do mitzvot in this world: lighting a candle, giving tzedakah, helping a friend. Chassidic teaching stresses that G-d wants a home in the physical world, and our healthy, active bodies make that possible.

## A mitzvah to stay healthy

The Torah says, "Guard yourselves very carefully" (Deuteronomy 4:15), which the Sages understood as a command to protect our health. Other teachings follow from this:

- **Life comes first.** Saving a life, pikuach nefesh, overrides almost every mitzvah, including Shabbat and Yom Kippur.
- **Avoiding danger.** We may not put ourselves at unnecessary risk.
- **Medical care.** Jewish law permits and encourages going to doctors. The Torah's words "he shall surely heal" are understood as permission and a duty for doctors to heal.

## Maimonides on healthy living

[Maimonides](/learn/maimonides), who was also a renowned physician, wrote that "keeping the body healthy and strong is part of the way of G-d." In his law code, he gives practical advice:

- Eat when hungry and stop before you are completely full.
- Exercise regularly.
- Get enough sleep.
- Eat simple, wholesome food and avoid overindulging.
- Keep calm and avoid excessive anger and worry.

Of course, follow your own doctor's advice for your personal health.

## Thanking G-d for the body

Each time we use the restroom, we say a blessing called **Asher Yatzar**, thanking G-d for forming the human body with wisdom, with openings and passages that must work just right. It is a daily reminder never to take good health for granted. You can find it near the start of every [siddur](/learn/siddur).

## Respecting the body

Because the body belongs to G-d and holds a holy soul, Jewish law asks us to treat it with respect:

- Dress with dignity.
- Avoid harming the body. The Torah forbids tattoos (Leviticus 19:28).
- After death, the body is treated with great honor and buried in the earth.

## Health and joy

Chassidic teaching emphasizes joy and calm faith, which are good for the body and the soul. The Rebbe often encouraged people to take care of their health, to get proper rest and to approach life with trust in G-d.

## Common questions

### Is it a mitzvah to exercise?
Exercise is part of guarding your health, which is a mitzvah. Maimonides specifically recommends regular exercise. Doing it with the intention of having strength to serve G-d makes it even more meaningful.

### Do I have to fast on Yom Kippur if I am sick?
Not if fasting would endanger your health. Someone who is ill should speak with their doctor and ask the Rabbi. Jewish law values life over fasting. See [Yom Kippur fast](/learn/yom-kippur-fast).

### What does Judaism say about organ donation and medical decisions?
These are serious questions with detailed answers in Jewish law. Every case is different, so please talk with the Rabbi, who can guide you through it.

### Why is there a blessing for using the restroom?
Because a healthy body is a daily miracle. Asher Yatzar thanks G-d that everything works as it should, which anyone who has been sick knows is no small thing.
