Chanukah is easy to love: the candles, the latkes, the dreidels, the kids. But the menorah in the window is also a teacher. The Rebbe returned to its lessons year after year. Here are a few of them.

## Add a little more light every day

The Talmud records a debate (Shabbat 21b). The school of Shammai said to light eight candles the first night and one fewer each night. The school of Hillel said to start with one and add one each night, "because in holy matters we go up, not down." We follow Hillel.

The lesson is about how to grow. Yesterday's good is not enough for today. A person who gave tzedakah last year, studied a little, came to a class, is asked to add one more candle: one more mitzvah, one more act of kindness, one more minute of learning. Not a huge leap, just one more than yesterday.

## A little light dispels a lot of darkness

Chassidic teaching repeats this line often: *a little light pushes away much darkness*. You do not fight darkness with a broom; you light a candle. One small flame in a dark room is seen by everyone.

That is a practical teaching. When the world feels dark, when a family or a workplace feels tense, when you feel far from where you want to be, the answer is not to despair over how much darkness there is. It is to do one bright thing: a kind word, a phone call to someone lonely, a candle on Friday night. The darkness has no choice but to step back.

## Light at the doorway, not in the closet

Jewish law asks us to light the menorah where it can be seen: at the door or in a window facing the street. The reason is *pirsumei nisa*, publicizing the miracle. Most mitzvot are done quietly; the menorah is meant to be seen.

The Rebbe took this literally and asked that menorahs be lit in public places: city squares, shopping centers, in front of government buildings, so that every Jew, however far from Jewish life, would see one and remember who they are. Today public menorah lightings take place in thousands of cities around the world, and Chabad of Lamorinda's grand menorah lighting is our own. See [Chabad](/learn/chabad).

For each of us, the lesson is: do not hide your Judaism. Light that is shared lights others. See [Kiddush Hashem](/learn/kiddush-hashem).

## Quality over quantity: the pure oil

When the Maccabees entered the Temple, they could have used oil that was ritually impure; Jewish law would have allowed it in that emergency. Instead they searched until they found one small jug with the seal of the High Priest, enough for one day. G-d answered their stubbornness with eight days of light.

The teaching: in matters of the soul, do not settle for "good enough." A child's Jewish education, the honesty of a business deal, the sincerity of a prayer, these deserve the pure oil, even if there seems to be only a little. G-d takes care of making it last.

## Bring the lesson home

Light every night, and each night add one small good thing to your day. Put the menorah in a window. Come to a public lighting and bring a friend who would not have come alone. ## Common questions

### Why do we add candles instead of taking away?
We follow the school of Hillel, who taught that in holy matters we always go up (Shabbat 21b). Each night the light grows, and so should we.

### Why is the menorah placed in a window?
To publicize the miracle, *pirsumei nisa*. The menorah is one of the few mitzvot meant to be seen by others, which is why Chabad lights menorahs in public squares around the world.

### What does "a little light dispels much darkness" mean in practice?
That one good deed changes the atmosphere of a room, a home or a town more than a long list of what is wrong. Pick one bright thing and do it today.

### What is the lesson of the pure oil?
Do not compromise on what matters most. The Maccabees insisted on pure oil even when the law would have allowed otherwise, and G-d made the little they had last eight days.
