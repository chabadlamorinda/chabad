A Kohen (כֹּהֵן, priest) is a Jewish man descended in the male line from Aaron, a Levi (לֵוִי) is descended in the male line from the tribe of Levi, and everyone else is a Yisrael (יִשְׂרָאֵל). Each status passes from father to son, so the best way to know yours is your family tradition, and the Rabbi can help you check.

## The twelve tribes

The Jewish people descend from the twelve tribes, the children of Jacob. Over history, most tribal identities were lost, and most Jews today do not know their tribe. What was carefully kept is the family line of the Kohanim and the Leviim, because they have special roles. Everyone else is simply called Yisrael.

## How the status passes

- **Kohen:** you are a Kohen if your father is a Kohen, and so on back to Aaron, Moses' brother.
- **Levi:** you are a Levi if your father is a Levi.
- **Yisrael:** if your father is neither, you are a Yisrael.

This follows the father, but only a Jew counts at all, and being Jewish itself follows the mother, as explained in [who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew). A woman is the daughter of a Kohen or Levi from her father, and that matters for some laws, such as [pidyon haben](/learn/pidyon-haben).

## What about a surname or a DNA test?

Many families remember "my grandfather was a Kohen." That memory is valuable.

- **Surnames** such as Cohen, Kagan or Katz (in Russian, Коган, Каган, Кац) or Levi, Levin or Levine are often clues, but they are not proof. Some families changed names, and not everyone with such a name is a Kohen.
- **Family tradition** counts for a great deal: that your grandfather was called to the Torah first, gave the priestly blessing, or was told not to enter a cemetery.
- **A Jewish cemetery** may help: some tombstones show a pair of hands for a Kohen or a pitcher for a Levi.
- **A DNA test** can show that you share ancestry, but Chabad.org notes that there is no "Jewish gene," and genetics cannot settle Jewish status. A Kohen marker is a hint, not a ruling.

Please **ask the Rabbi** about your family. He can ask the right questions and tell you whether to treat yourself as a Kohen, Levi or Yisrael.

## What a Kohen does today

- **Birkat Kohanim** (the priestly blessing), recited on holidays. See [Birkat Kohanim](/learn/birkat-kohanim).
- **The first aliyah** to the Torah on Shabbat, and a Levi receives the second. See [aliyah](/learn/aliyah).
- **Pidyon haben**: a Kohen receives the redemption of a firstborn son. A father who is a Kohen or Levi, or a mother who is the daughter of one, does not need it.
- **Certain laws of purity**, such as not entering a cemetery except for close relatives, and some marriage restrictions. Ask the Rabbi about specifics.

A Levi also washes the hands of the Kohanim before the blessing.

## Common questions

### How do I know if I am a Kohen?

You are a Kohen if your father is, going back in the male line to Aaron. Ask your family about the tradition and ask the Rabbi to help you check.

### Is the surname Cohen proof that I am a Kohen?

No. It is a clue, but not proof. Names changed over time, and some people named Cohen are not Kohanim, while Kohanim may have other surnames.

### Can a DNA test show that I am a Kohen?

It can hint at shared ancestry, but it cannot decide Jewish status or Kohen status in Jewish law. Please ask the Rabbi.

### What if only my mother is a Kohen's daughter?

Kohen status passes only through the father, but a Kohen's daughter has her own laws, such as for pidyon haben. Ask the Rabbi about your case.

### Do I need to know my tribe to be Jewish?

No. Most Jews do not know their tribe, and that does not affect being Jewish.
