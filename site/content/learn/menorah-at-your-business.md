Many Jewish business owners and employees wish to display or light a Chanukah menorah at work, and it is a beautiful way to publicize the miracle. Whether the blessings are said depends on the details, such as whether the menorah is kosher, when it is lit, and who sees it, so it is best to ask the Rabbi before you light.

One rule is clear and comes first: lighting at work never replaces lighting at home. The laws of Chanukah are explicit: even the synagogue lighting does not exempt anyone from kindling the menorah at home, and people at a public lighting are urged not to rely on it but to light at home with the usual blessings.

## Why the menorah is meant to be seen

The Chanukah lights are lit to publicize the miracle, a mitzvah called *pirsumei nisa* (פרסומי ניסא). Jewish law describes the Chanukah menorah as a mitzvah of public life: it is lit at home in a doorway or window, in the synagogue, and in public places. For why Chanukah goes public, read [public menorah lightings](/learn/public-menorah-lighting).

## What Halacha says about public lightings

The laws of Chanukah say that kindling a menorah at a public outdoor gathering is a maximum of publicizing the miracle, and may be done with a blessing. A separate teaching explains why blessings are said at most public lightings, even outside a synagogue:

- The synagogue lighting is already established in the Code of Jewish Law with blessings, partly to publicize the miracle and partly for people who do not light at home.
- A public square or mall publicizes the miracle even more, so the same reasoning applies.
- The blessings apply only if the menorah is kosher, meaning it has properly spaced, fuel-burning flames and is lit at the right time in the evening. Otherwise it is purely symbolic.
- Some authorities hold that blessings are said only in a synagogue, and others disagree, so the question is discussed. If a minyan prays at the public ceremony, many rule that the place counts as a synagogue.

If the lighting is electric or at the wrong time of day, the law says the traditional blessings may not be recited.

## A workplace is a different question

A business, store or office is not a public square. The laws of Chanukah do not give a simple rule for a menorah lit inside a private workplace. Whether it is lit for customers outside, for a few employees inside or just as a display, and whether the setting is open to the public, may change whether the blessings are said. These are the kind of questions to take to the Rabbi, who can help with your actual space. See also [Chanukah at work and college](/learn/chanukah-at-work-and-college).

If you want a display, Jewish law notes that electric menorahs are acceptable for display only, and do not fulfill the mitzvah. Halacha also advises a sturdy, fireproof surface, out of children's reach and away from anything flammable.

## Car menorahs: lights on the move

Many people have seen cars with a menorah on the roof. The law tells how car menorahs began with yeshivah students from Brooklyn during the Rebbe's 1973 call for Chanukah awareness, and how they spread worldwide as a way of publicizing the miracle. A business that wants to share the holiday can learn from them: the aim is to let the light be seen.

## Lighting at home still comes first

However you mark Chanukah at work, make sure to light your own menorah at home each night. Read [how to light the Chanukah menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah) and [Chanukah for friends and coworkers](/learn/chanukah-for-friends-and-coworkers). ## Common questions

### Can I light a menorah at my office?
Many people do, but whether to say a blessing and how to light depend on the details. Ask Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 about your office.

### Do I say a blessing on a menorah at work?
It depends. Jewish law says blessings are said at most public lightings if the menorah is kosher and lit at the right time. For a store or office, ask the Rabbi.

### Does lighting at work count for the mitzvah at home?
No. The laws of Chanukah say that even the synagogue lighting does not exempt anyone from lighting at home, and people at a public lighting should still light at home with the usual blessings.

### Can I use an electric menorah at my business?
An electric menorah is acceptable for display only, and does not fulfill the mitzvah. A real flame menorah must be lit for the mitzvah.

### Is a public lighting a way to publicize the miracle?
Yes. Halacha calls lighting at a public outdoor gathering a maximum of publicizing the miracle.
