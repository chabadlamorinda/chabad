Your first time at synagogue can feel a little unfamiliar, but you do not need to know anything to walk in. Come as you are, take a seat, and let the people around you help you follow along. At Chabad, every Jew is welcome, whatever their background or knowledge.

## Before you go

- **Check the time.** Services start at set times, and Shabbat morning services often run two to three hours, followed by Kiddush. Arriving partway through is completely normal.
- **Dress respectfully.** Many men wear a jacket or nice shirt, and women often wear a skirt or dress with sleeves. Nobody will turn you away, so do not stress about it.
- **On Shabbat,** it helps to leave your phone at home or turned off, since phones are not used in synagogue on Shabbat.

## When you arrive

1. **Men put on a [kippah](/learn/kippah).** There are usually some at the entrance.
2. **Take a [siddur](/learn/siddur),** the prayer book. Many have English translation.
3. **Find a seat.** In traditional synagogues, including Chabad, men and women sit in separate sections divided by a mechitzah, a partition. This helps everyone focus on prayer.
4. **Ask for the page.** Page numbers are often announced or posted, and a neighbor will gladly point to the right place.

## What happens during the service

A Shabbat morning service includes:

- **Morning blessings and psalms** of praise
- **The [Shema](/learn/shema),** the declaration of G-d's oneness
- **The [Amidah](/learn/amidah),** a silent standing prayer
- **The [Torah reading](/learn/torah-reading),** when the scroll is taken out and read aloud
- **The Haftarah** and often a short talk by the Rabbi
- **Musaf,** an additional Amidah for Shabbat
- **Closing prayers and songs**

You will notice people standing, sitting, bowing and sometimes swaying. Follow along as best you can. Nobody is checking.

## Helpful etiquette

- **Stand** when the ark is open and when others stand for key prayers.
- **Stay quiet** during the Amidah and the Torah reading.
- **Do not walk in front of** someone saying the silent Amidah.
- **Answer "Amen"** when you hear a blessing, if you feel comfortable.
- **On Shabbat,** avoid photos, writing and using electronics inside.

## After the service

Most Shabbat services end with Kiddush, a blessing over wine followed by food and conversation. This is the best time to meet people and ask questions. Stay a while, introduce yourself, and enjoy.

## Common questions

### Do I need to read Hebrew?
No. Many people follow in English or simply listen. Over time you may want to learn; see our [Hebrew alphabet](/learn/hebrew-alphabet) guide.

### Can I bring my kids?
Yes. Children are part of Jewish life, and you will often see them in synagogue. If a little one gets restless, stepping out for a few minutes is perfectly fine.

### Will people expect me to know what to do?
Not at all. Everyone was new once. People are glad you came, and the Rabbi is happy to explain anything.

### Do I need to be a member?
No. Chabad is known for welcoming every Jew, whether or not they belong anywhere. If you have questions before coming, just call.
