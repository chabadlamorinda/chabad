Rosh Hashanah is over, and Yom Kippur is not here yet. The days in between are not a break. They are the *Aseret Yemei Teshuvah*, the Ten Days of Repentance (counting Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur themselves), and the Jewish tradition treats them as the most precious days of the year for setting things right.

## What these days are

On Rosh Hashanah, the sages teach, the book of life is opened and our year is written; on Yom Kippur it is sealed. The days between are the chance to change what is written. The prophet Isaiah says, "Seek G-d when He is to be found, call on Him when He is near," and the Talmud explains: these are the ten days between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur (Rosh Hashanah 18a). In these days G-d is, so to speak, closer, and a small effort reaches further.

## Shabbat Shuvah

The Shabbat that falls in these days is called **Shabbat Shuvah**, the Shabbat of Return, after the first word of its haftarah: "Return, O Israel, to the L-rd your G-d" (Hosea 14). In many communities the Rabbi gives a longer talk on teshuvah, and the meals are a natural time to think about the year behind and ahead. See [Shabbat](/shabbat) and the [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times) for Lafayette.

## Extra care

During these days, many Jews take on something they do not keep all year, as a way of saying to G-d: see, I can. Some pray three times a day who usually do not; some are more careful about what they say of others. The point is to show ourselves what we are capable of. See [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah) and [lashon hara](/learn/lashon-hara).

The daily prayers also change. We add a few lines to the Amidah asking to be "remembered for life," and we say *Avinu Malkeinu*, "Our Father, our King," each weekday morning and afternoon (not on Shabbat). If you pray on your own, these additions are in every siddur. See [the Amidah](/learn/amidah).

## Tzedakah

"Repentance, prayer and charity avert the harshness of the decree," we say on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur. Of the three, tzedakah is the one you can do in a minute. Many families give extra during these days, before Yom Kippur especially, and the custom of [kapparot](/learn/kapparot) ends with money given to the poor. Give a little more than feels comfortable, and let a child put the coins in the [pushka](/learn/pushka). You can also [give online](/donate). See [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).

## Asking forgiveness

Yom Kippur atones for what is between us and G-d. For what is between us and other people, the Mishnah is clear: Yom Kippur does not atone until we have made peace with the person we hurt (Yoma 8:9). That is why these days are the time for the hard phone call, the apology to a sibling, the honest word to a spouse or a coworker.

A real apology names what you did, does not explain it away, and asks, "Will you forgive me?" If the answer is no, Jewish law says to try again, up to three times, sincerely. And when someone asks us, forgive, and mean it. See [forgiveness and making peace](/learn/forgiveness-and-making-peace).

## Small resolutions

Rosh Hashanah is full of big feelings. These ten days are for turning them into something small and real. The Rebbe often urged people to take on one specific, modest resolution rather than a grand one, because a small change that lasts is worth more than a large one that does not. Ideas:

- Light [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) every Friday.
- Say Modeh Ani each morning. See [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani).
- Learn a few lines of Torah a day. See [daily Torah study](/learn/daily-torah-study).
- Check the mezuzahs. See [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah).

Write it down. Tell someone. Then walk into Yom Kippur with one promise you intend to keep. See the guides to [Rosh Hashanah](/holidays/rosh-hashanah) and [Yom Kippur](/holidays/yom-kippur).

## Common questions

### What is Shabbat Shuvah?
The Shabbat between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, named for its haftarah from Hosea, "Return, O Israel." A wonderful Shabbat to come to services.

### Do I have to apologize to everyone I ever wronged?
Start with the people you know you hurt this year, especially those closest to you. A sincere, specific apology is what matters. For a situation that feels complicated, ask the Rabbi.

### What if I was not in synagogue on Rosh Hashanah?
You are not behind. Teshuvah can begin on any day, and these are the best days of the year to begin. Come for Shabbat Shuvah or Yom Kippur, and bring one small resolution with you. See [starting Jewish observance](/learn/starting-jewish-observance).
