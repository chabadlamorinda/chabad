Chanukah (חֲנֻכָּה) is the eight-day Jewish festival of lights. Each night we light a menorah, adding one more flame every evening, to remember the Maccabees' victory over the Greek rulers who tried to end Jewish life and the oil in the Holy Temple that burned for eight days.

The word means "dedication," for the rededication of the Holy Temple in Jerusalem. Nothing about it requires experience. If you can light a candle, you can celebrate Chanukah.

## What Chanukah is about

More than 2,000 years ago, the Seleucid Greek rulers of the Holy Land tried to replace Jewish observance and belief in G-d with Greek culture. A small, poorly armed band led by Judah the Maccabee defeated a much larger army and reclaimed the Temple. When they went to light the Temple menorah, they found only one flask of pure olive oil. It burned for eight days, the time needed to prepare new pure oil. The Sages established Chanukah to thank G-d and to publicize these miracles. Read the full story in our [Chanukah story guide](/learn/chanukah-story) and meet [the Maccabees](/learn/the-maccabees).

## How to light the menorah

The Chanukah menorah has nine flames: eight for the nights, plus the shamash, the "attendant" light used to kindle the others. Here is the Chabad custom in short:

1. **Set up the lights in a straight row**, with the shamash set apart, higher or to the side. Olive oil with cotton wicks, or candles, are used. Electric lights do not fulfill the mitzvah.
2. **Place the menorah in a doorway**, opposite the mezuzah, so you pass between two mitzvahs. Use a sturdy surface away from curtains and children.
3. **Light shortly after sunset.** On Friday, light before the Shabbat candles. On Saturday night, light after Shabbat ends.
4. **Add one light each night.** Night one has one light, on the right. Each next night, add one to the left and kindle the newest light first.
5. **Say the blessings.** Two blessings every night, and a third, Shehecheyanu, on the first night only.
6. **Sit near the lights** for the first half hour and enjoy them. Do not use their light for reading or work; use the shamash.

Men and women both light, and children should have their own menorahs. ## Foods, dreidel and gelt

- **Fried foods** remember the oil. Potato latkes (pancakes) and sufganiyot (jelly doughnuts) are the classics. See [Chanukah foods](/learn/chanukah-foods).
- **Dairy foods** are also customary, recalling Yehudit, a brave woman of the story.
- **The dreidel** is a four-sided top marked נ, ג, ה, ש, for "A great miracle happened there." Play for coins, nuts or candy: [how to play dreidel](/learn/dreidel), or [spin one online](/dreidel).
- **Gelt** is money given to children, especially to reward learning and kindness. Children are encouraged to give part to tzedakah. See [Chanukah gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt).

## Prayers, giving and joy

Through the eight days we recite full Hallel in the morning prayers and add V'al Hanissim to the Amidah and Grace After Meals ([learn more](/learn/al-hanissim-and-hallel)). It is customary to give extra tzedakah, and double on Friday. After lighting, families sing songs such as Maoz Tzur and Haneirot Halalu ([Chanukah songs](/learn/chanukah-songs)). Work is permitted, but women customarily rest from housework while the candles burn.

## Common questions

### What is Chanukah in simple words?
Chanukah is an eight-day Jewish winter festival of lights that celebrates the Maccabees' victory and the oil miracle in the Holy Temple. We mark it by lighting a menorah each night, adding a flame, and eating fried foods.

### How do you celebrate Chanukah for the first time?
Get a menorah and candles or oil, light them at home shortly after sunset with the blessings, and add one flame each night. Then add songs, latkes or doughnuts, a dreidel game and gelt for the children.

### How many candles do you light on Chanukah?
You light one on the first night and add one each night until eight are lit, plus the shamash, the helper light, every night. Always light the newest candle first.

### Do you need to be religious to celebrate Chanukah?
No. Every Jew is welcome to light the menorah, and the community lighting is open to everyone. Call 925-384-7242 if you want help getting started.

### Is Chanukah a major Jewish holiday?
Chanukah is a rabbinic holiday and is not mentioned in the Bible, so work is permitted. Observing it is still a mitzvah, and the menorah is its heart.
