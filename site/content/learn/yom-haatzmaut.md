Yom Haatzmaut ("Independence Day") is the day Israel celebrates the founding of the modern State of Israel in 1948. It is observed on the fifth of the Hebrew month of Iyar, with Israel moving the day when it would fall next to Shabbat. It is a day of gratitude for Jewish life in our homeland, and Jews of different communities mark it in different ways.

## What the day is

On 5 Iyar in 1948, the State of Israel was declared, after nearly two thousand years in which Jews lived scattered among the nations while praying daily to return to Zion. In Israel it is a national holiday, preceded by a day of remembrance for fallen soldiers and victims of terror. In the evening there is a ceremony, followed by singing and dancing in the streets, and the next day, picnics and barbecues.

For many Jews around the world, the day is a moment to say thank you for a Jewish home, and to remember the soldiers and families who gave everything. Read about the [bond between the Jewish people and Israel](/learn/israel-and-the-jewish-people) and about [Jerusalem](/learn/jerusalem).

## How communities mark it

Observance differs widely, and each approach comes from sincere religious conviction:

- Many Religious Zionist communities add special prayers, and some recite Hallel, the psalms of praise, with or without a blessing.
- Many Jewish communities hold festive gatherings, concerts and Israeli food.
- Many Chassidic communities, including Chabad, do not change the regular prayers and do not add a special liturgy for the day. They share the joy of Jewish life in Israel, and keep the day through Torah learning and charity.

## What Chabad teaches

Chabad has always stressed love for every Jew in Israel and the importance of Jewish safety there. The Rebbe called on Jewish men to put on tefillin as a merit for the protection of the Jewish people in Israel. Chassidic teaching also reminds us that the full redemption, with the Holy Temple rebuilt, is still ahead. See [the Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple). For questions about how our community's custom applies in your home, please ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### What is Yom Haatzmaut?
It is Israel's Independence Day, marking the founding of the State of Israel in 1948.

### When is Yom Haatzmaut?
On the fifth day of the Hebrew month of Iyar, though Israel shifts it when the date would be next to Shabbat. Check the [Jewish calendar](/learn/jewish-calendar) for this year.

### How do Jews celebrate Yom Haatzmaut?
Religious Zionists often add prayers and Hallel; many communities hold gatherings, singing and festive meals; Chabad keeps the regular prayers. In Israel it is a national holiday with ceremonies and barbecues.

### Does Chabad celebrate Yom Haatzmaut?
Chabad shares the joy of Jewish life in Israel and gives thanks for it, but does not add special prayers or a Hallel for the day. Please ask the Rabbi about details.

### Is Yom Haatzmaut a religious holiday?
It is a national holiday, and Jews relate to it religiously in different ways. Many say Hallel, many do not.

### What is the difference between Yom Haatzmaut and Yom Yerushalayim?
Yom Haatzmaut marks the founding of the State in 1948; Yom Yerushalayim marks the reunification of Jerusalem in 1967.
