The most traditional Jewish housewarming gifts are bread and salt, a kosher mezuzah, a charity (tzedakah) box and Jewish books. Chabad.org describes bread and salt as the first items brought into a new home, an age-old custom, and the mezuzah as the main requirement when you move in.

## Bread and salt

According to Chabad.org's article "Moving to a New Home," bringing bread and salt in first is an age-old Jewish custom. The reason is unknown, but it is suggested that bread stands for prosperity and salt, which never spoils, for the home's permanence. Another Chabad.org article adds that, together, they invoke G-d's blessing that the home will never lack food or necessities and will be a place of lasting stability. A loaf of challah and a small jar of salt make a simple, meaningful gift. Learn why salt matters in [your table is an altar](/learn/the-table-as-an-altar) and [kosher salt](/learn/kosher-salt).

## A kosher mezuzah

Chabad.org calls affixing a kosher mezuzah to the doorways of a home the primary requirement when moving in, one that protects everyone in the home. A mezuzah is a handwritten parchment scroll in a case. The scroll is what makes it kosher, so a gift mezuzah should contain a properly written scroll, not only a decorative case. See [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) and [checking mezuzahs](/learn/checking-mezuzahs-and-tefillin).

Mezuzahs are put up with a blessing. Chabad of Lamorinda helps families buy kosher mezuzahs and put them up; use the [mezuzah form](/forms/mezuzah-tefillin) or call 925-384-7242.

## Jewish books and a tzedakah box

Chabad.org says to bring Jewish books and a charity box into the new home before the movers bring in the other boxes, so the home begins as a place of study and kindness. In Chabad circles the customary books are a Chumash (the Five Books of Moses), a book of Psalms and the Tanya. A tzedakah box is easy to give as a gift too. Read [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah), and more ideas in [Jewish gift ideas](/learn/jewish-gift-ideas).

## The housewarming party

Soon after moving in, it is customary to hold a *chanukat habayit* (חנוכת הבית), a housewarming. Words of Torah are spoken, and guests offer blessings for a fruitful and happy stay. If you are invited, bring one of the gifts above and a blessing. If you are hosting, read [chanukat habayit](/learn/chanukat-habayit).

## Simple gift ideas

- A challah and salt.
- A kosher mezuzah scroll and case, to be put up with a blessing.
- A tzedakah box, with the first coins inside.
- A Chumash, a book of Psalms or the Tanya.
- A [Shabbat table](/learn/setting-the-shabbat-table) set, such as candlesticks.

If your friend keeps kosher, ask before bringing food. [Bay Kosher](/programs/bay-kosher), a project of Chabad of Lamorinda, is the local source for kosher items and is always kosher.

## Common questions

### What is a traditional Jewish housewarming gift?
Bread and salt are the traditional first items brought into a new home, and a kosher mezuzah, tzedakah box and Jewish books are classic gifts. Chabad.org describes all of these as customs for a new home.

### Why bread and salt?
Chabad.org says the reason is unknown, but bread may stand for prosperity and salt for permanence. Another Chabad.org article connects them to a blessing that the home never lack food or necessities.

### Is a mezuzah a good housewarming gift?
Yes, if it contains a kosher handwritten scroll. A mezuzah is the main requirement for a Jewish home. Ask the Rabbi if you are unsure about a scroll.

### Do I have to bring a gift to a housewarming?
The Chabad.org description of a *chanukat habayit* mentions words of Torah and blessings from the guests, not a required gift. A warm blessing is the heart of it.

### Which day is best for moving in?
Chabad.org says Tuesday is the most propitious day for a move, and that Shabbat and Jewish holidays are not for moving. Call the Rabbi if your date is tight.
