Many Jewish families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda include a husband or wife who is not Jewish. If that is your family, the first thing to know is simple: you are welcome at Chabad of Lamorinda. There is no membership, no test and no judgment. Come to Shabbat, to the holidays and to classes, together.

## The Jewish partner is always fully Jewish

A Jew is always a Jew. Marrying someone who is not Jewish does not change that, and it never closes a door. The Lubavitcher Rebbe taught that every Jew has a holy soul, a spark of G-d, that nothing can put out. Every mitzvah you do, lighting Shabbat candles, putting up a menorah, giving tzedakah, saying the Shema, is complete and precious.

## Your spouse is welcome too

A husband or wife who is not Jewish is welcome as our guest at Shabbat dinners, holiday celebrations, the public menorah lighting and classes. Many come to understand their spouse's heritage, to support their children, or simply because they enjoy the warmth of a Shabbat table. Some parts of Jewish practice, such as being counted in a minyan or being called up to the Torah, are for Jewish participants, and nobody will put anyone on the spot.

Judaism does not ask non-Jews to become Jewish. It teaches that every person can have a close relationship with G-d by keeping the [Seven Noahide Laws](/learn/noahide-laws). If someone wishes to explore conversion, it is a serious, personal path to discuss with the Rabbi, never a condition for being welcome.

## Are the children Jewish?

This is the question many couples ask first. According to Jewish law, Jewish identity passes through the mother:

- If the mother is Jewish, the children are Jewish, fully and forever, whatever the father's background.
- If the father is Jewish and the mother is not, the children are not Jewish according to Jewish law, but they have a real connection to the Jewish people, and the Rabbi is glad to talk with you about it.

We know this can be sensitive. Read [who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew), and bring any question about your own family privately to the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin.

## A Jewish home, one step at a time

Whatever your family looks like, Jewish life at home grows from small, steady steps:

- **Friday night.** Light Shabbat candles before sunset, say Kiddush over wine or grape juice and share challah. See [Shabbat for beginners](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners).
- **Chanukah.** Put the menorah in the window and light it together each night. December can raise questions in an interfaith home; read [Jews and Christmas](/learn/jews-and-christmas).
- **A mezuzah** on the front door, a pushka for tzedakah, Jewish books for the children.
- **Jewish education.** The [preschool](/programs/jewish-preschool) and [Hebrew School](/programs/hebrew-school) give children a joyful Jewish start. Call to ask about your child.

## Talking with each other

Couples do best when they talk openly and kindly about what matters to each of them: holidays, the children's education, grandparents' expectations. Respect your spouse's feelings and family, and be clear and proud about your own Jewish identity. When questions get hard, a private conversation with the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin can help; see [shalom bayit](/learn/shalom-bayit).

## Common questions

### Are interfaith families welcome at Chabad of Lamorinda?
Yes. Every Jew is welcome, together with their family. A spouse who is not Jewish is welcome as a guest at Shabbat dinners, holidays, events and classes in Lafayette. There is no membership.

### Is my child Jewish if I am Jewish and my spouse is not?
According to Jewish law, a child of a Jewish mother is Jewish. A child of a Jewish father and a non-Jewish mother is not Jewish by Jewish law, though connected to the Jewish people. Talk privately with the Rabbi about your family.

### Does my spouse have to convert?
No. Judaism does not seek converts, and nobody will pressure your spouse. If your spouse ever wishes to explore conversion, it is a sincere, personal decision to discuss with the Rabbi.

### How do we handle the holidays in an interfaith home?
Make the Jewish holidays bright and joyful at home, especially Shabbat and Chanukah, and talk kindly with your spouse and extended family ahead of time. Ask the Rabbi when a particular question comes up.

### Can the Rabbi help with a bris, baby naming or burial in an interfaith family?
Every situation is different and deserves a personal answer. Call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242; he will listen and explain what is possible.
