Antisemitism hurts, whether it is a slur at school, a swastika on a wall, a hostile post or a threat. Here is what to do, and how to answer it in a Jewish way.

## First: safety

- **If anyone is in danger, call 911.**
- If there is a threat of violence, report it to the police right away, even if it was online.
- Write down what happened, when and where, and take photos or screenshots before anything is removed.

## Reporting

- **At school:** tell the teacher and the principal in writing, with the details. Schools must respond to harassment, and a written report creates a record. Ask what will be done and follow up.
- **At work:** report to your manager or human resources in writing.
- **Vandalism or graffiti:** report it to the police before it is cleaned.
- **Online:** report the post to the platform; for threats, also to the police.
- **Tell the Rabbi.** We can help you think through next steps, stand with you, and connect you with the right people.

## Talking with your children

- **Listen first.** Let them tell the whole story. Thank them for telling you.
- **Make clear it is not their fault**, and that hatred says something about the hater, not about them.
- **Give them pride, not fear.** Remind them that the Jewish people have outlived every empire that hated them, and that they belong to something beautiful.
- **Stay in touch with the school** until the situation is resolved.

## Answering with more light

The Lubavitcher Rebbe's answer to darkness was always more light. After a hateful act, do one more mitzvah, and encourage others to as well:

- Put up a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) proudly, or check the ones you have.
- Light [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) this Friday; put on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin).
- Give [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah), come to a class, bring a friend to Shabbat.
- Light the [menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah) in the window at Chanukah.

## Common questions

### Should I hide my Jewishness to stay safe?
Use good judgment about safety, but hiding who we are has never protected us. Communities that stand proudly and together are stronger. Talk with the Rabbi about your situation.

### My child was bullied for being Jewish. What should I ask the school?
Ask for a written response, what steps will be taken with the student involved, how your child will be protected, and whether education will be given to the class.

### Is criticism of Israel antisemitism?
Criticizing a government's policy is not antisemitism. Denying the Jewish people's right to a homeland, holding all Jews responsible, or using old antisemitic images and myths is.
