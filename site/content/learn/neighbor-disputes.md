"Love your neighbor as yourself" is a great principle of the Torah, and sometimes it is hardest with the people who actually live next door. Jewish law has a whole section on neighbors, written long before HOAs and city codes.

## What Jewish law teaches

- **Don't cause damage or nuisance.** A person may not use their property in a way that harms a neighbor: smoke, noise at night, water running onto their land, or a tree whose roots damage their foundation.
- **Privacy matters.** The sages forbade building a window that looks directly into a neighbor's home or yard. Respecting your neighbor's privacy is a Torah value.
- **Be generous where it costs you little.** The sages called it a bad trait to refuse a neighbor something that benefits them and costs you nothing.
- **A good neighbor is a blessing.** "Better a near neighbor than a distant brother" (Proverbs 27:10).

## How to settle a dispute

1. **Talk in person, early and calmly,** before anger builds. A knock on the door beats an angry note.
2. **Assume good faith.** They may not even know there is a problem.
3. **Offer a solution, not only a complaint.** "Could we trim the tree together and split the cost?"
4. **Don't complain about them to others.** It travels, and it hardens hearts. See [lashon hara](/learn/lashon-hara).
5. **Bring in a third party** if needed: a rabbi, a community mediator, or your HOA.
6. **Legal steps last.** For a dispute between Jews, Jewish law asks to go to a Beth Din before a civil court. See [disputes](/help/disputes).

## Being the good neighbor

Bring a challah or treats when someone moves in, keep an eye on their home when they travel, and invite them for a holiday meal. Kindness is the best prevention.

## Common questions

### My neighbor is not Jewish. Does Jewish law still apply?
The Torah's values of honesty, kindness and avoiding harm apply to everyone. Local law and HOA rules apply too.

### The noise is unbearable and talking didn't help. What now?
Write down what happens and when, try mediation, and check local noise rules. The Rabbi can help you think it through.

### My neighbor complains about our sukkah or holiday guests. What should I do?
Speak kindly, explain the holiday, keep noise down late at night, and invite them to see the sukkah. Most people respond warmly.
