Tiberias (Tveriah, טְבֶרְיָה) is a city on the shore of the Kinneret, the Sea of Galilee, and one of the four holy cities of Israel, together with Jerusalem, Hebron and Tzfat. It is the resting place of great Jewish sages, including Maimonides (the Rambam), Rabbi Akiva and Rabbi Meir Baal Haness, and for centuries it was the center of Jewish learning in the Land of Israel.

## A city of Torah

Tiberias was built by Herod Antipas and named for the Roman emperor Tiberius. The site had an ancient cemetery, which made it ritually impure, and many Jews avoided it at first. Tradition tells that Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai purified the city, and it became a center of Torah.

After the destruction of the Second Temple, the Sanhedrin, the supreme Jewish court, met in several places, including Yavneh, Usha and Tzippori. After the passing of Rabbi Judah the Prince, around the year 220, it moved to Tiberias. The Jerusalem Talmud was compiled here, around the year 400. It is called the Jerusalem Talmud because it records the learning of the Land of Israel (see [The Talmud](/learn/talmud)).

## The Masoretes: how the Torah is read

In the centuries that followed, scholars in Tiberias called the Masoretes preserved the exact text of the Hebrew Bible and developed the system of Hebrew vowel signs (nikud) that we still use. When a child learns to read Hebrew from a siddur, the vowels come from Tiberias (see [The Hebrew Alphabet](/learn/hebrew-alphabet)).

## Sages buried in Tiberias

- **Rabbi Akiva**, the great teacher of the Mishnah, whose tomb is on a hillside (see [Rabbi Akiva](/learn/rabbi-akiva)). His wife Rachel is also buried in the area.
- **Rabbi Meir Baal Haness**, Rabbi Akiva's student. He died outside the Land of Israel and was brought to Tiberias for burial (see [Rabbi Meir Baal Haness](/learn/rabbi-meir-baal-haness)).
- **Maimonides (the Rambam)**, who died in Egypt, and whose body was brought to Tiberias as he wished (see [Maimonides](/learn/maimonides)).
- **Rabbi Moshe Chaim Luzzatto (the Ramchal)**, author of *Mesillat Yesharim*, buried near Rabbi Akiva.
- **Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk**, a Chassidic leader who moved to Tiberias with several hundred followers in the 1700s.

Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai is buried in Meron, in the Galilee, and thousands visit his grave on Lag BaOmer (see [Lag BaOmer](/learn/lag-baomer)). Tzfat, nearby, is known as the city of Kabbalah (see [Tzfat](/learn/tzfat)).

## The Kinneret and the hot springs

The Sea of Galilee lies hundreds of feet below sea level. Tiberias is nicknamed the "City of Water," and it has hot springs that have been known for healing for centuries. There are seventeen natural springs, and Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai reportedly was cured by bathing there. If you plan to use the springs, ask the Rabbi about Shabbat and kosher questions that can arise with hot springs.

## A city that was rebuilt again and again

Tiberias was destroyed and rebuilt many times. In the 1500s, Doña Gracia and her nephew Joseph Hanassi restored its walls and built a yeshiva there. In the 1740s, Rabbi Chaim Abulafia resettled it. A strong earthquake in 1837 destroyed almost the entire city, and the Jewish community rebuilt it.

## Visiting the Galilee

The graves of the sages are places to pray and to say [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim). Check current travel guidance for your dates and plan with a reliable guide. Other places of interest in the north include Meron, Tzfat, Mount Hermon and the ancient town of Gamla in the north.

## Common questions

### Why is Tiberias one of the four holy cities of Israel?
Because of its great scholars and mystics, and because the Sanhedrin and the Jerusalem Talmud are linked to it. The four are Jerusalem, Hebron, Tzfat and Tiberias.

### Who is buried in Tiberias?
Rabbi Akiva, Rabbi Meir Baal Haness, Maimonides, the Ramchal and Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk, among others.

### Where is the Rambam buried?
In Tiberias. He passed away in Egypt, and his body was brought to Tiberias according to his wishes.

### What are the Masoretes?
They were scholars in Tiberias who preserved the exact text of the Bible and created the Hebrew vowel signs.

### Is it good to pray at the graves of tzaddikim?
It is a custom to pray at the graves of the righteous and ask them to plead for us. Ask the Rabbi for guidance about the customs.
