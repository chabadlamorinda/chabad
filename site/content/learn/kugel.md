Kugel is a baked Jewish casserole, most often made from grated or blended potatoes or from egg noodles, and served at Shabbat and holiday meals. The word is Yiddish, and the dish is a staple on tables in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda and everywhere Jews gather for Shabbat.

## What is kugel?

Kugel (קוגל) is a Yiddish word for a baked pudding or casserole. Potato kugel is savory, with a crisp golden top and a soft middle, and noodle kugel can be savory or sweet. Because it can be fully baked before Shabbat and served warm, it fits the Shabbat meal perfectly. It pairs well with [cholent](/learn/cholent), [chicken soup](/learn/jewish-chicken-soup) and the other foods of a [Shabbat meal](/learn/shabbat-meals).

## An easy potato kugel, adapted from Chabad.org

This recipe is retold in our own words from a potato kugel on Chabad.org. It is pareve and gluten-free, and takes a little over an hour.

**Ingredients**
- 4 large potatoes, peeled and cut into cubes
- 1 onion, cut into cubes
- 1/2 cup of oil
- 4 eggs
- 1 1/2 teaspoons of salt
- 1/2 teaspoon of black pepper

Use kosher ingredients, and ask the Rabbi about specifics.

**Steps**
1. Heat the oven to 450 degrees Fahrenheit.
2. Grease a baking dish, and put it in the oven for about 5 minutes so that it gets very hot.
3. While the dish heats, keep the potato and onion cubes in a bowl of cold water so they do not darken.
4. Drain them, then blend them smooth with the eggs, oil, salt and pepper.
5. Take the hot dish out and pour the mixture in right away. It should sizzle.
6. Bake for 30 minutes at 450 degrees.
7. Lower the oven to 350 degrees and bake 30 minutes more, until golden.

**A bigger batch:** make a second round of the mixture, pour it over the first partway through baking, and return the dish to the oven until it is done.

## Noodle kugel

Many families also love noodle kugel, made with egg noodles baked in a casserole. We did not find a noodle kugel recipe on Chabad.org to share here, so ask the Rabbi or a friend who bakes one and cook it together.

## Pareve, make-ahead and warm

This potato kugel is pareve, meaning it contains no meat or dairy, so it fits a meat meal or a dairy meal. Read more about [pareve foods](/learn/pareve). It can be baked on Thursday or Friday and reheated. Since cooking is not done on Shabbat, see [keeping food warm on Shabbat](/learn/keeping-food-warm-on-shabbat), and ask the Rabbi about your own oven.

## Common questions

### What is kugel?
Kugel is a baked Jewish casserole, usually made of potatoes or egg noodles. It is traditionally served at Shabbat and holiday meals.

### What does the word kugel mean?
Kugel is a Yiddish word for a baked pudding or casserole. Ask the Rabbi if you would like to hear more about where the name came from.

### Is potato kugel pareve?
This potato kugel, made with potatoes, onion, eggs and oil, is pareve. It can be served with meat or with dairy.

### Can I make kugel ahead of Shabbat?
Yes. Bake it before Shabbat and keep it warm as your family does. Ask the Rabbi about the details for your oven.

### Why serve kugel on Shabbat?
It is a beloved Shabbat food that keeps well and feels festive. Many families have served it for generations.

### Is kugel gluten-free?
The potato kugel here has no flour, so it is gluten-free as written. Always check your ingredients.
