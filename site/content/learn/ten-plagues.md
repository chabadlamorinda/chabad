The ten plagues of Egypt, in order, are: blood, frogs, lice, wild animals, pestilence, boils, hail, locusts, darkness and the death of the firstborn. They are the story of how G-d brought the Jewish people out of slavery, told in the Torah portions Va'eira and Bo, and every Passover Seder recalls them.

## The ten plagues in order

Here is each plague with its Hebrew name, from the account on Chabad.org:

1. **Blood (דם, *dam*).** The Nile and all Egyptian waters turned to blood for a week, the fish died and there was nothing to drink.
2. **Frogs (צפרדע, *tzefardeia*).** Frogs filled homes and bedrooms. Pharaoh promised to let the people go, then broke his word once they left.
3. **Lice (כנים, *kinim*).** Dust became swarms of insects. Pharaoh's magicians could not copy it and said, "This is the finger of G-d."
4. **Wild animals (ערוב, *arov*).** Wild creatures destroyed property in Egypt, but Goshen, where the Jews lived, was spared.
5. **Pestilence (דבר, *dever*).** A disease killed Egyptian livestock but not the Jews' animals.
6. **Boils (שחין, *shechin*).** Soot thrown toward the sky caused painful boils.
7. **Hail (ברד, *barad*).** Hail with thunder and lightning destroyed crops and anyone left outside; those who heeded Moses' warning stayed safe.
8. **Locusts (ארבה, *arbeh*).** Locusts devoured what the hail left.
9. **Darkness (חושך, *choshech*).** A thick darkness covered Egypt for six days, while Goshen had light.
10. **Death of the firstborn (מכת בכורות, *makat bechorot*).** At midnight the firstborn of Egypt died, and Pharaoh sent the Jews out that same night.

Commentators differ on some of the animals (for example whether *arov* means wolves or swarms of insects), so do not worry if you have heard another version.

## Rabbi Yehuda's code: Detzach Adash B'achav

Rabbi Yehuda grouped the plagues into three memory words: *Detzach* (blood, frogs, lice), *Adash* (wild animals, pestilence, boils) and *B'achav* (hail, locusts, darkness, firstborn). Chabad.org shares an explanation: each group taught the Egyptians a lesson. The first showed that G-d exists, the second that G-d watches over the world, and the third that G-d can do anything.

## Why do we spill wine at the Seder?

During the Haggadah we remove a bit of wine from our cup as we say each plague and again at the three words of Rabbi Yehuda, sixteen drops in all, according to a custom going back at least a thousand years. Chabad.org gives three reasons: the letters of a name for G-d's judgment; that a blessing is not said over a cup of punishment; and that our joy is not complete because it came at the cost of others' suffering. The Lubavitch practice is to pour the wine directly from the cup (not to dip a finger), and then refill the cup. See [the four cups](/learn/four-cups) and [the Haggadah](/learn/haggadah).

## What do the plagues teach us?

Chabad.org teaches that each plague also holds a lesson for everyday life, especially in dealing with difficult people:

- **Blood:** Moses showed gratitude even to the Nile that saved him, so we are grateful even to those we find hard.
- **Frogs:** Hitting the frog only made more frogs: anger harms the angry one.
- **Lice:** Difficult people are placed in our lives by Divine design, so meet them with dignity.
- **Wild animals:** Keep your Torah values distinct, as Goshen did.
- **Pestilence:** Bring your reactive "animal soul" inside and respond with restraint.
- **Boils:** Do your best, then trust G-d with the rest.
- **Hail (ice and fire):** Answer attacks with warmth, and sometimes pause to cool down.
- **Locusts:** *Arbeh* sounds like *harbeh* ("abundance"): choose to focus on your blessings.
- **Darkness:** When confused, Torah is the light.
- **Firstborn:** G-d cherishes His people and works beyond nature for them.

Chassidic teaching adds that the plagues broke Egypt's arrogance, like cracking a nut to reach the fruit, and prepared the world for Sinai.

## At the Seder with children

Let children say each plague aloud and add a drop of wine together, or use ten small toys. Pause after each plague and ask, "What would you do if you had a plague of frogs?" See [Passover with kids](/learn/passover-with-kids) and [the Exodus story](/learn/exodus-story).

## Common questions

### What are the ten plagues of Egypt in order?
Blood, frogs, lice, wild animals, pestilence, boils, hail, locusts, darkness and the death of the firstborn.

### What are the Hebrew names of the ten plagues?
*Dam, tzefardeia, kinim, arov, dever, shechin, barad, arbeh, choshech* and *makat bechorot*.

### What is Detzach Adash B'achav?
It is Rabbi Yehuda's mnemonic that groups the ten plagues into three sets of three, three and four, to teach that G-d exists, watches the world and can do anything.

### Why do we spill wine at the Seder?
We spill sixteen drops because our joy is not complete when others suffered. Chabad.org gives additional reasons, and the Lubavitch practice is to pour from the cup.

### Were the Jews hurt by the plagues?
The Torah says Goshen was spared in several plagues. Some commentators say the first three struck everyone, others say the Jews were spared from all.

### Where are the plagues read in the Torah?
In the portions Va'eira and Bo (Exodus 7:14 to 12:36). See [this week's page](/this-week) for the current portion.
