Families give tzedakah together by keeping a pushka (charity box) in the home, adding coins every day and before Shabbat, and choosing together where it goes. Chabad.org teaches that a pushka in the house turns a home into a center of kindness, and that giving regularly matters more than the amount.

## Make giving part of the home

Chabad.org's guide to the pushka (פּוּשְׁקֶע, a Yiddish word for the charity box) says the Rebbe suggested keeping it as a permanent fixture, mounted on the wall at home or in the office. He said a charity box "redefines the entire space," making the home a center of kindness. Each day family members drop in a few coins, and extra coins are added just before Shabbat, because money is not handled on Shabbat or Yom Tov.

Chabad.org quotes two teachings: Maimonides held that regularity matters more than the amount, and the Baal Shem Tov taught not to let a day pass without an act of giving. When the box is full, the family chooses a worthy cause together. See [The Pushka](/learn/pushka).

## Before Shabbat candles

Chabad.org's basics of tzedakah mention several good times to give: before prayers, and, for women and girls, before lighting Shabbat and holiday candles. A pregnant woman, Chabad.org adds, is encouraged to give charity every day. See [Tzedakah Before Candle Lighting](/learn/tzedakah-before-candle-lighting) and, for this week's candle-lighting time, [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times).

A family can make this a gentle Friday habit: a child places the coins in the box, and everyone sees that Shabbat begins with giving. For the youngest children, see [Children, Money and Tzedakah](/learn/children-money-and-tzedakah).

## Whom to help first

Chabad.org gives a simple order of priorities: family members in serious financial difficulty first, then local charities before distant ones, and charities in Israel before other faraway ones. Torah schools and Jewish institutions are among the worthy recipients. So a family can ask together: who near us needs help? See [Tzedakah Priorities](/learn/tzedakah-priorities) and [Local Tzedakah in Lamorinda](/learn/local-tzedakah-in-lamorinda).

Chabad.org also teaches that how we give matters more than how much: give warmly, with a smile, and give anonymously when you can, so the recipient is not embarrassed.

## A tenth for the family

Many families set aside maaser, a tenth of income, for tzedakah. Maaser is where the Torah itself invites us to test G-d. The prophet Malachi (3:10) says to bring the tithes and "try Me now," and the Talmud (Taanit 9a) teaches: give a tenth so that you will become rich. Chabad.org presents both. Rabbi Labkowski teaches that maaser is Hashem's promise of success. This is said of maaser alone, and it is never a reason for anyone to feel guilty. If money is tight, or you are not sure how maaser applies to your family, please call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242. See [Maaser](/learn/maaser).

## Common questions

### How can we give tzedakah as a family?
Keep a pushka in the home, give a few coins every day and before Shabbat, and choose together where the money goes. Chabad.org presents this as a simple, lasting habit.

### Where should the family pushka be placed?
Chabad.org says the Rebbe suggested a permanent place, mounted on the wall at home or in the office, where it is seen every day.

### Can we give on Shabbat?
Money is not handled on Shabbat or Yom Tov, so the pushka is filled before.

### Why do many families give maaser, a tenth?
Because the Torah itself invites us to "try Me" with the tithe (Malachi 3:10), and the Talmud teaches to give a tenth so that you will become rich. Rabbi Labkowski teaches that maaser is Hashem's promise of success. For your own situation, call the Rabbi.

### Who should get our tzedakah first?
Family members in serious need come first, then local causes, and Israel before other distant causes, as Chabad.org presents Jewish law. Ask the Rabbi about your own case.

### Can grandparents give together with grandchildren?
Yes. Giving together passes the tradition on. See [Jewish Grandparents](/learn/jewish-grandparents) for ideas.
