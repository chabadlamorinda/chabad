A Jew who was part of an interfaith family is still entitled to a Jewish burial in a Jewish cemetery, and the community is obligated to provide it. Questions about a non-Jewish spouse, children or relatives are sensitive, so please call Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski at 925-384-7242 and talk it through with him privately.

**Jewish burial is of the utmost importance, and it can all be arranged even if there is no budget. Financial help is available. Please call at once: Rabbi Labkowski 925-384-7242, Rebbetzin Labkowski 925-384-7241.** Whatever your family looks like, you will be met with warmth and without judgment. Families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda come from many backgrounds.

## The Jewish person

Chabad.org (from Rabbi Maurice Lamm's guide) says that a Jewish partner in an intermarriage who remained Jewish is entitled to burial in a Jewish cemetery. Every Jew deserves a dignified Jewish burial, whether or not they were observant. See [why Jewish burial is so important](/learn/why-jewish-burial-matters).

## The non-Jewish spouse or relative

This is the part that is hard to hear, and we say it gently. Chabad.org explains that, because a Jewish cemetery is for Jews (see [what makes a cemetery Jewish](/learn/buying-a-jewish-cemetery-plot)), a spouse who is not Jewish and has not converted may not be buried in it. Chabad.org adds that a man who was circumcised for reasons other than conversion is still considered non-Jewish. The Rabbi can talk with you about the options and about where your loved ones may rest. Please do not decide in a hurry or in pain; call first.

## Children and who is counted as Jewish

According to Chabad.org:

- A child of a Jewish mother may be buried in a Jewish cemetery, even if the child was not circumcised.
- A child of a non-Jewish mother who has not converted may not be buried there, even if raised or educated in a Jewish school, unless there was intent to convert.
- A person who truly converted is buried as a full Jew, in the Jewish spouse's plot or a new family plot.

Questions of who is Jewish can be painful. See [who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew) and [converting to Judaism](/learn/converting-to-judaism), and ask the Rabbi about your family's case.

## Non-Jewish loved ones at the funeral

Non-Jewish family and friends are surely grieving too. We do not want anyone to feel unwelcome at a time like this. Please ask the Rabbi about your family's situation; he will answer with care.

## If there are conflicts in the family

Sometimes relatives disagree about what the person who died would have wanted. If the person left written wishes, the Rabbi can help you see which can be carried out. Chabad.org says that wishes that go against Jewish law should be set aside and a proper Jewish burial arranged, and that requests that only differ from local custom should be discussed with a rabbi. See [planning a Jewish funeral in advance](/learn/planning-a-jewish-funeral-in-advance).

## Common questions

### Can a Jew be buried in a Jewish cemetery if their spouse is not Jewish?

Yes. According to Chabad.org, a Jewish partner who remained Jewish is entitled to be buried in a Jewish cemetery. The question is where the non-Jewish spouse may be buried. Call the Rabbi.

### Can a non-Jewish spouse be buried in a Jewish cemetery?

Chabad.org says an unconverted non-Jewish spouse may not be buried in a Jewish cemetery. A rabbi can discuss respectful options with your family.

### Can a child of an interfaith couple be buried in a Jewish cemetery?

A child of a Jewish mother can, even if uncircumcised. A child of a non-Jewish mother who has not converted cannot, according to Chabad.org. Ask the Rabbi about your child's case.

### Can non-Jewish relatives attend a Jewish funeral?

Please ask the Rabbi about your family's situation. He will answer with care, and with respect for everyone who is grieving.

### Does it matter if the person was not religious?

No. A Jew who remained Jewish is entitled to a Jewish burial whether or not they observed Jewish practice.

### What if we cannot afford a Jewish burial?

Call the Rabbi. It can all be arranged even if there is no budget, and financial help is available.
