A chosson is a groom. Just as a bride learns with a teacher before her wedding, a Jewish groom traditionally meets privately with a rabbi for chosson classes. They prepare him for the wedding day and, more importantly, for being a loving and responsible Jewish husband.

## What a chosson learns

- **[Family purity](/learn/family-purity) from his side.** The laws of taharat hamishpacha belong to both husband and wife. A groom learns what the times of separation mean for him, how to be supportive, and why this monthly rhythm brings renewal to a marriage.
- **Shalom bayit.** How Judaism sees marriage: the Rambam writes that a husband should honor his wife more than himself. Classes talk about respect, listening, kind speech and handling disagreements. See [Shalom Bayit](/learn/shalom-bayit).
- **The wedding day.** The meaning of the customs: the fast, the prayers, the veiling of the bride, the [ketubah](/learn/ketubah), the ring, the chuppah and the breaking of the glass. See [The Jewish Wedding](/learn/jewish-wedding).
- **Mitzvot of a Jewish husband.** In many communities, including Chabad, a man begins wearing a [tallit](/learn/tallit) at morning prayers once he is married. A groom who does not yet put on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) every weekday is encouraged to begin; the Rabbi is glad to show you how.
- **A Jewish home.** Mezuzahs, Shabbat, kosher, and the groom's part in making the home a place of warmth and Torah.

## When to start

A few months before the wedding is ideal, so there is time to learn calmly and ask questions. If the wedding is sooner, call anyway; classes can be arranged to fit.

## How the classes work

Chosson classes are private, usually one-on-one with the Rabbi, and arranged at times that suit the groom. Everything is confidential, and no question is too basic. No background is needed; many grooms come knowing very little, and that is fine.

## Some customs before the wedding

- **An aliyah to the Torah** on the Shabbat before the wedding, celebrating the groom.
- **The wedding-day fast.** Bride and groom traditionally fast until the ceremony and add the confession prayers of Yom Kippur at the afternoon prayer, because the wedding day is like a personal Yom Kippur, a fresh start.
- **The kittel.** In Chabad, the groom wears a kittel, a simple white robe, under the chuppah. Other communities have different customs; ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### Do grooms really need classes too?
Yes. A marriage is built by two people, and the laws of family purity and shalom bayit are for both. Grooms who learn before the wedding often say it made them more patient, aware and supportive husbands.

### I have never put on tefillin. Is it too late?
Never. Many men start before their wedding. The Rabbi is glad to help you get started and to help you get a pair of kosher tefillin.

### What if we are having a small or simple wedding?
The classes are about the marriage, not the size of the wedding. Every groom is welcome.
