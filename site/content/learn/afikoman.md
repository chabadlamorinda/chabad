The afikoman is the piece of matzah set aside early in the Passover seder and eaten at the end of the meal as the last food of the night. It recalls the Passover offering, which was eaten at the end of the seder meal in Temple times.

## What the word means

Afikoman comes from a Greek word for dessert or after-meal entertainment. The Mishnah teaches that after eating the Passover offering, we do not move on to an "afikoman," meaning another course or party. Over time, the word came to mean the final piece of matzah itself.

## Why we eat it

In the days of the Holy Temple, every family ate the Passover lamb at the end of the seder meal, so that its taste would remain in their mouths. Today, without the Temple, the afikoman matzah takes its place. That is why nothing is eaten after it, except the last two of the four cups of wine.

## The afikoman during the seder, step by step

1. **Yachatz (breaking).** Early in the seder, the leader takes the middle of the three matzahs and breaks it into two pieces.
2. **Set aside the larger piece.** The larger half becomes the afikoman. It is wrapped in a napkin or cloth and put away until the end of the meal.
3. **Eat the meal.** Enjoy the festive dinner, Shulchan Orech.
4. **Tzafun (hidden).** After the meal, the afikoman is brought out and shared. Add more shmurah matzah so everyone has enough.
5. **Eat while reclining** to the left, as a sign of freedom.
6. **Finish before midnight.** The afikoman should ideally be eaten before halachic midnight (chatzot), which is usually around 1:00 am in daylight saving time. Check the exact time with our [zmanim tool](/tools/zmanim).
7. **Continue the seder** with Birkat Hamazon (Grace after Meals), the third cup of wine, Hallel and the fourth cup.

## How much to eat

Each person should eat at least a kezayit, an olive-sized amount, of matzah for the afikoman. Many eat two such portions: one for the Passover offering and one for the matzah that was eaten with it. A kezayit is roughly half a handmade shmurah matzah, but opinions vary. Ask the Rabbi if you are unsure, and do not overeat if it is hard for you.

## The hiding custom

In many homes, children "steal" or the parents hide the afikoman, and the children search for it and receive a reward or promised gift for returning it. The idea is to keep children awake and curious, since the whole seder is built around their questions.

Chabad custom does not play the stealing game. Instead, the afikoman is kept wrapped near the leader, and children are kept engaged with the [Four Questions](/learn/four-questions), prizes for questions and answers, and lively storytelling. Every family can find what works best.

## Common questions

### Can we eat dessert after the afikoman?
No. The afikoman should be the last taste of the night. Have dessert before Tzafun, then eat the afikoman. Only the remaining cups of wine and water or tea may follow.

### What if the afikoman is lost?
Use other shmurah matzah. Any matzah can serve as the afikoman, which is why it helps to have plenty on the table.

### What if we finish the meal after midnight?
Still eat the afikoman. Try to plan the evening so that it is eaten before chatzot, and ask the Rabbi about your situation.

### Do we use the same piece for everyone?
The broken piece is shared, and extra matzah is added so that each person has a full portion. Learn more in our [matzah guide](/learn/matzah).
