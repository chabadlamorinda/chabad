Jewish prayer follows the seasons. Twice a year the words of the Amidah change, so that in the rainy season of the Land of Israel we ask G-d for rain, and in the dry season we ask for dew. Here in California, where every rainy winter matters, these short phrases feel very close to home.

## Two additions to the Amidah

1. **"Mashiv haruach u'morid hageshem,"** "He makes the wind blow and the rain fall." This is praise, not yet a request. It is said in the second blessing of the Amidah from Shemini Atzeret (the day after Sukkot) until the first day of Passover. In summer, many say "morid hatal," "He makes the dew fall," instead.
2. **"V'ten tal u'matar livracha,"** "Give dew and rain for a blessing." This is the actual request for rain, said in the weekday Amidah, in the blessing for the year's produce. In summer we say "v'ten bracha," "give blessing," instead.

## When we start asking for rain

- **In the Land of Israel:** from the evening of 7 Cheshvan, about two weeks after Sukkot. The Sages waited that long so that pilgrims returning home from the Holy Temple in Jerusalem would reach their homes, even far away, before the rains.
- **Outside Israel:** from the evening of December 4 (December 5 in the year before a leap year), based on the ancient Babylonian count of 60 days after the autumn season begins. Jews all over the diaspora follow this date, wherever they live.
- The request continues until the first day of Passover.

## Why ask at all?

The Torah describes the Land of Israel as a land that "drinks water from the rain of heaven," always looking up to G-d. Rain reminds us that our livelihood, our food, everything, comes from Above. The Sages say that the day rain falls is as great as the day the Torah was given.

## If you forgot

It happens to everyone, especially in the first weeks. Whether you need to go back depends on which phrase you missed and when you noticed. A common custom is to say the correct words 90 times on the first day to get used to them. If you are unsure, ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### Why is it a December date outside Israel and not a Jewish date?
It follows the solar seasons, counted the way the Talmud's sages in Babylonia did, so it falls on a fixed date of the regular calendar.

### Do we pray for rain in California too?
Yes. Jews outside Israel ask for rain from early December, the same as everyone in the diaspora. If a community has a special need, such as a drought, there are extra prayers; ask the Rabbi.

### Is it said on Shabbat?
"Mashiv haruach" is said every day, including Shabbat. "V'ten tal u'matar" is only in the weekday Amidah.
