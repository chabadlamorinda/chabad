The Passover Seder was designed with children in mind. Many of its customs exist for one reason: to make kids curious, keep them awake and get them asking questions. With a little planning, the Seder can be the night your children remember all year.

## Why the Seder is built for children

The Torah says, "And you shall tell your son on that day" (Exodus 13:8). The heart of the Seder is passing the story of the Exodus from parents to children. That is why we do unusual things at the table, like dipping vegetables and leaning, so children will notice and ask, "Why is this night different?" Their questions open the door to the story.

The Haggadah even speaks of four kinds of children, the wise, the rebellious, the simple and the one who does not know how to ask, and teaches that each one deserves an answer in a way he or she can understand.

## The Four Questions

Traditionally, the youngest child who is able asks the [Four Questions](/learn/four-questions) (Mah Nishtanah). Practice in the weeks before, and let every child who wants to have a turn, in Hebrew, English or both. Give them a big round of applause.

## Ideas to keep the Seder lively

- **Plague props.** Bring out toy frogs, plastic bugs, ping-pong "hail," sunglasses for darkness and cotton balls for boils as you read the Ten Plagues.
- **Act out the Exodus.** Kids can put on robes, tie their matzah in a cloth over their shoulders, and march around the table as if leaving Egypt.
- **The afikoman.** In many homes the children "steal" and hide the [afikoman](/learn/afikoman), and the parents promise a prize to get it back, so the Seder can end. It keeps everyone alert right to the end.
- **A kids' Haggadah.** A Haggadah with pictures helps children follow along. Let them point to each step on the [Seder plate](/learn/seder-plate).
- **Give every child a job.** Someone can pour the wine or grape juice, hand out the karpas, open the door for Elijah, or ask a question at each step.
- **Prizes for questions.** Hand out nuts, candies or small treats for every good question. Our Sages mention giving children treats on Seder night so they stay awake and ask.
- **Rest beforehand.** The Seder starts late and runs long. A good afternoon nap makes a huge difference for young children (and grown-ups).

## Getting ready in the days before

The excitement can begin well before the Seder:

- **Crafts.** Make a matzah cover, a pillow for leaning, an Elijah's cup or a decorated Seder plate.
- **Tell the story ahead of time.** Read the [Exodus story](/learn/exodus-story) at bedtime during the weeks before.
- **Search for chametz.** On the night before Passover, the family searches the house for chametz by the light of a candle, and many families use a flashlight for safety. Kids love following the light into every corner. Parents often hide ten wrapped pieces of bread for them to find. See [chametz](/learn/chametz).
- **Practice songs.** Mah Nishtanah, Dayeinu and Chad Gadya are easy and fun to learn together.

## Toddlers and older kids

**Toddlers** do best with short bursts: a song, a prop, a snack, then free play nearby. Feed them something before the Seder, keep a few quiet toys handy, and do not worry if they fall asleep. See [holidays with toddlers](/learn/holidays-with-toddlers).

**Older children** can take real roles: reading a section aloud, explaining a part of the Seder plate, preparing a short "news report from Egypt," or leading a song. Ask them big questions too: what does freedom mean to you today?

## Common questions

### What if my child falls asleep before the end?
That is fine, especially for little ones. The most important parts for children are the Four Questions, the story and the matzah. Try to do those while they are awake.

### Do children need to drink four cups of wine?
Children who are old enough to understand are given four cups too, usually of grape juice. For exact amounts and ages, ask the Rabbi.

### Which child asks the Four Questions?
Usually the youngest child who is able to ask them, but every child is welcome to join in. If there are no children, an adult asks, and even someone alone at the Seder asks himself.

### How can we keep older kids interested?
Give them real responsibility: reading, leading, explaining or acting. When children feel the Seder is theirs, they stay involved.
