Before booking a hall, it is wise to check the Jewish calendar. Some days are not used for weddings, some are especially favored, and the bride's own calendar matters too. A short call to the Rabbi before you sign a contract can save a lot of trouble.

## Days when weddings are not held

- **Shabbat and Yom Tov.** Weddings are not held on Shabbat or on the holidays themselves, including Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, and the festival days of Sukkot, Passover and Shavuot.
- **Chol Hamoed.** The intermediate days of Passover and Sukkot are already days of joy, and we do not "mix one joy with another."
- **Most of the Omer.** The weeks between Passover and Shavuot are a time of semi-mourning. Customs differ about exactly which days are affected; Lag BaOmer is a common day for weddings. See [Counting the Omer](/learn/counting-the-omer).
- **The Three Weeks.** From the fast of the 17th of Tammuz until Tisha B'Av, in mourning for the Holy Temple. See [The Three Weeks](/learn/three-weeks).
- **Fast days**, such as Tisha B'Av and the other [fast days](/learn/fast-days).

Because customs vary, especially for the Omer, ask the Rabbi about any specific date.

## The first half of the month

There is an old custom, followed in Chabad according to the Rebbe's guidance, to hold weddings in the first half of the Hebrew month, while the moon is growing. The waxing moon is a sign of blessing and growth for the new couple. The [Jewish calendar](/learn/jewish-calendar) guide explains how the Hebrew months follow the moon.

## The bride's cycle and the mikvah

A bride immerses in the [mikvah](/learn/mikvah) shortly before her wedding, and the laws of [family purity](/learn/family-purity) affect the days around the wedding. For this reason, the bride should plan the date together with her kallah teacher, privately, before the date is fixed. Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski is glad to help with this confidentially.

## The wedding-day fast

The wedding day is like a personal Yom Kippur: the couple's past mistakes are forgiven and they begin a new life together. So the bride and groom traditionally:

- **Fast** from morning until after the chuppah, breaking the fast together right after the ceremony.
- **Add the Yom Kippur confession** (Al Chet) to their afternoon prayer.
- **Say Tehillim** and pray for themselves and for others.

On certain festive days, such as Rosh Chodesh and Chanukah, the couple does not fast. Ask the Rabbi about your date.

## Common questions

### Can we get married on a Sunday?
Yes, as long as it is not one of the days above. Sunday is a popular choice. Weddings on Saturday night after Shabbat are also possible, but remember that in summer Shabbat ends late.

### Can we have a wedding during Chanukah or Purim?
Generally yes, and Chanukah weddings are common. Purim itself raises its own questions, so ask the Rabbi.

### Why is it important to involve the Rebbetzin before setting a date?
Because the timing of the bride's mikvah immersion depends on her cycle, the date should be planned privately with her kallah teacher.
