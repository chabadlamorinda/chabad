Jewish jewelry, like a Star of David, a chai or a Hebrew name necklace, is a beautiful way to show Jewish pride and keep a reminder of who you are close to your heart. Jewish law has a few guidelines, especially for jewelry with holy words and for Shabbat.

## Popular Jewish jewelry and what it means

- **Star of David (Magen David).** The six-pointed star has been a Jewish symbol for centuries. Read about the [Star of David](/learn/star-of-david).
- **Chai.** The Hebrew letters chet and yud spell chai, "life," with a numerical value of 18. See the [chai symbol](/learn/chai-symbol).
- **Hebrew name necklaces.** Your Hebrew name, or your English name in Hebrew letters. Make sure the spelling is correct; see [Hebrew names](/learn/hebrew-names).
- **Shema or verse pendants.** Some contain verses from the Torah or the Shema.
- **Hamsa.** The hand-shaped design is a popular folk symbol, especially among Israeli and Sephardi Jews. It is not a Torah mitzvah or a religious requirement.
- **Rings with Hebrew inscriptions,** often a verse from the Song of Songs, "I am my beloved's and my beloved is mine."

For more symbols, see [Jewish symbols](/learn/jewish-symbols).

## Jewelry with holy words

Jewelry that includes G-d's name or verses from the Torah needs some care:

- **Bathrooms.** A pendant with a Torah verse or a Hebrew name of G-d should be covered, for example under your shirt, or removed before entering a bathroom.
- **Treat it with respect.** Do not let it drop on the floor or be thrown away. If it breaks, ask the Rabbi what to do.
- **Choose wisely.** Many people prefer jewelry without G-d's name, so they do not need to worry about these rules.

## Jewelry on Shabbat

On Shabbat, carrying objects in a public area is forbidden unless there is an [eruv](/learn/eruv). Jewelry that you wear as part of your outfit is generally not considered carrying. There are some details for specific items, like loose pins or watches, and customs differ, so ask the Rabbi about your jewelry.

## Jewelry and the mikvah

Before immersing in a [mikvah](/learn/mikvah), all jewelry is removed, since nothing may separate the body from the water.

## The wedding ring

At a Jewish wedding, the groom gives the bride a simple ring of solid metal without stones. Its value must be clear and its look simple, since the ring is part of the legal act of marriage. After the wedding, any ring may be worn.

## Jewelry as a gift

Jewish jewelry is a popular gift for a Bar or Bat Mitzvah, a birthday or a new baby. A chai or Star of David can be a daily reminder of Jewish identity. Pair it with a mitzvah, like lighting Shabbat candles, for a gift that lasts.

## Common questions

### Can I wear a Star of David or chai every day?
Yes. Wearing Jewish jewelry is a lovely way to show Jewish pride. There is no special blessing or rule for wearing a Star of David or chai.

### Is the hamsa a Jewish symbol?
The hamsa is a popular folk symbol in many Jewish communities, but it does not come from the Torah and is not a mitzvah. If you have questions about it, ask the Rabbi.

### Can I wear a necklace with G-d's name into the bathroom?
It should be covered or removed. Many people tuck it under their clothing. To avoid the issue, choose a design without G-d's name.

### Can a man wear Jewish jewelry?
Yes. Many men wear a chai, Star of David or a ring. Jewish tradition encourages modest, dignified dress, and the Torah forbids men and women from wearing clothing meant for the other gender.
