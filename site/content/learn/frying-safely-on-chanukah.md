To fry latkes safely, use a deep, heavy pan, a thermometer, dry food and low-to-medium heat, keep children away from the stove, and keep a lid nearby. If oil catches fire, slide a lid over the pan, turn off the heat and never pour water on it.

## Why take care?

Chanukah's foods are fried in oil, a custom that recalls the miracle of the oil (see [Chanukah foods](/learn/chanukah-foods)). Frying is also the part of the holiday where a kitchen is most likely to have a burn or a fire. Fire departments warn that unattended cooking and splashing hot oil are common causes of home fires and burns. A calm kitchen keeps the joy in the holiday.

## Choosing the oil

Chabad.org's Chanukah recipes call for vegetable or canola oil for frying. Use an oil that stays stable at frying heat, such as vegetable or canola oil. Extra-virgin olive oil is wonderful on salads and on the menorah, but it starts to smoke at lower heat, so many cooks use another oil for deep frying. If anyone in your home has a nut allergy, avoid peanut oil. Look for a kosher symbol on the bottle ([kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols)). More on the meaning of oil: [olive oil in Jewish tradition](/learn/olive-oil-in-jewish-tradition).

## Before you start

1. **Clear the stove.** Move towels, paper and menus away.
2. **Use the right pan.** A heavy, deep pan or pot. Fill it only about one-third to one-half with oil, so it will not bubble over.
3. **Dry the food.** Water makes oil spatter. Squeeze grated potatoes well and pat things dry.
4. **Have a lid ready.** Keep a lid that fits the pan within reach before you begin.
5. **Know where the smoke alarm is,** and check that it works.

## While you fry

- **Watch the heat.** Heat oil gently until it shimmers or reaches the temperature in your recipe. Chabad.org's split pea latkes call for about 365°F. A kitchen thermometer helps. If the oil smokes, turn it down.
- **Lower food in carefully,** placing it away from you.
- **Do not crowd the pan.** This also gives you crisper latkes.
- **Stay with the pan.** Do not walk away while oil is hot.
- **Keep a kid-free zone.** A common fire-safety rule is to keep children at least three feet from the stove. Let them mix, set the table and choose toppings ([baking with kids](/learn/chanukah-baking-with-kids)).
- **Turn handles inward** so no one bumps them.
- **Keep meat and dairy separate.** Use separate pans for each ([kosher kitchen](/learn/kosher-kitchen)).

## If oil catches fire

Fire departments give the same steps:

1. Slide a lid over the pan to smother the flames.
2. Turn off the heat.
3. Do not move the pan.
4. **Never use water.** It can make burning oil explode outward.
5. Keep the lid on until it is completely cool.
6. If the fire spreads or you are not sure, get everyone out and call 911.

For an oven fire, turn off the heat and keep the door shut.

## Cleaning up and storing oil

Let used oil cool completely. Strain it, and pour cooled oil into a sealed container for the trash rather than down the drain. If you fried latkes, [leftover latkes](/learn/latkes) freeze well in a single layer and reheat in the oven.

## The menorah too

A burning menorah also needs a safe place: a flat, non-flammable surface, away from curtains. See [oil or candles](/learn/menorah-oil-and-candles). Light the menorah and the stove with care. If you need to leave the kitchen to light, turn off the burner first.

## Common questions

### What oil is best for frying latkes?
A neutral oil with a high smoke point, such as vegetable or canola oil, which Chabad.org's recipes use. Check for a kosher symbol.

### How do I put out a grease fire?
Cover the pan with a lid, turn off the heat, leave it covered until cool, and never use water. Call 911 if it spreads.

### How deep should the oil be?
Fill the pot only a third to a half full. Latkes can fry in a shallow layer, while doughnuts need more, so follow your recipe.

### Can children help with frying?
It is safest for adults to do all frying. Children can mix, shape, decorate and serve.

### Can I bake latkes instead?
Yes. See [healthier Chanukah foods](/learn/healthier-chanukah-foods).

### Can I use the same pan for meat and dairy?
No. If you keep kosher, use separate pans, and ask the Rabbi about your kitchen.
