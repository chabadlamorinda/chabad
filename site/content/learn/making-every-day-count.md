"Teach us to number our days," Moses prays in Psalm 90, "so we may gain a heart of wisdom." Judaism treats time as the most precious thing we have. Money can be earned back; a day, once gone, never returns. And yet the Jewish view of time is not anxious. It is hopeful: every day is a gift, given on purpose, with its own chance to do good.

## Each day has its own mission

The Torah says that Abraham, in old age, "came with his days." Jewish teachers explain: he arrived at the end of his life with all his days, none of them empty. Each day had been filled with kindness, faith and good deeds.

Chassidic teaching adds that G-d gives each person exactly the number of days they need for their mission in this world. So no day is extra and no day is wasted when we use it for good.

## A Jewish rhythm for the day

- **Wake up with thanks.** [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani), said before getting out of bed, starts the day with gratitude: G-d gave me back my soul for one more day.
- **Fix a time for learning.** The Sages said, "Make your Torah study a fixed habit." Even five minutes at the same time each day adds up to a lifetime of learning. See [daily Torah study](/learn/daily-torah-study).
- **Give something every day.** A coin in a [pushka](/learn/pushka) on weekday mornings makes giving a habit.
- **End the day with an honest look back.** Before sleep, the [bedtime Shema](/learn/bedtime-shema) includes a moment to review the day, forgive others, and resolve to do better tomorrow.
- **Stop once a week.** [Shabbat](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners) gives us a day that is not for producing but for living: family, rest, prayer and joy.

## "If not now, when?"

Hillel's famous saying in Pirkei Avot is a gentle push against putting off good things: a phone call to a parent, a visit to a sick friend, a mitzvah you have been meaning to try. Rabbi Tarfon added, "The day is short and the work is great," and also, "It is not up to you to finish the work, but you are not free to stop doing it." We do what we can today, and that is enough.

## When time feels wasted

Everyone has seasons that feel lost: illness, a hard year, a long wait. Judaism teaches that it is never too late. One sincere moment of [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah), returning to G-d, can fill the past with meaning, and every new day is a fresh start.

## Common questions

### Does Judaism say I must be productive every minute?
No. Rest, sleep, family time and Shabbat are part of a meaningful life. The goal is not busyness; it is that our time, including our rest, serves what matters.

### How do I start if my days feel too full?
Choose one small thing: Modeh Ani in the morning, or five minutes of learning. Small habits, kept every day, change a life.

### Is there a prayer for using time well?
Psalm 90, with its line "Teach us to number our days," is a beautiful one to read slowly. You can also ask G-d in your own words.
