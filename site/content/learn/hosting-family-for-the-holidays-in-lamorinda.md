Hosting out-of-town family for a Jewish holiday takes planning in three areas: arrivals timed before candle lighting, a place to stay within walking distance of services, and meals prepared in advance. Do these well and Rosh Hashanah, Passover or a family simcha in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda will feel calm and joyful.

## Plan arrivals before candle lighting

Shabbat and holidays begin at sunset, with candle lighting beforehand. After that, travelers who keep the holiday cannot drive, fly or carry things outside, and even guests who do not keep the holiday will want to arrive without stress. Check [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times) and the [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays) calendar for exact dates and candle-lighting times, which the site computes for you, and aim for guests to land hours before.

Chabad.org encourages saying the Traveler's Prayer, Tefilat Haderech (תפילת הדרך), when setting out on a journey. Read more in [Tefilat Haderech](/learn/tefilat-haderech), and see [traveling on Shabbat](/learn/traveling-on-shabbat) if a flight is near the holiday.

## Getting from the airport

BART's Yellow Line runs between the San Francisco airport and Antioch, and it stops in Orinda, Lafayette, Walnut Creek and Pleasant Hill, according to the line's public description. That makes it an easy ride for guests landing at SFO who are staying in Lafayette or Orinda. Check bart.gov for current schedules and any transfers, since service patterns change. Remember that on Shabbat and holidays, guests who observe will not ride BART or use phones, so plan travel for before the holiday begins.

## Staying close enough to walk

If your guests will walk to services on a holiday, choose a place they can reach on foot. Consider how far the walk is, whether older relatives can manage it, and whether the route has sidewalks. Having everyone sleep in your home or nearby, rather than driving in on the holiday, makes the day much easier. For where services are held and what is planned this year, call 925-384-7242.

## Two-day holidays and cooking: eruv tavshilin

Rosh Hashanah is two days, and many other holidays have two days outside Israel. When a holiday falls right before Shabbat, cooking for Shabbat on the holiday is only allowed with an *eruv tavshilin* (עירוב תבשילין), a simple ritual performed before the holiday. Without it, you cannot cook or prepare for Shabbat on the last day of the holiday. Read [Eruv Tavshilin](/learn/eruv-tavshilin) in advance and ask the Rabbi if you are unsure.

On the holiday itself, cooking from an existing flame is allowed for the holiday meal, but kindling a new fire is not. Prepare as much as you can ahead of time. Cook, set the table, and put out water, wine and grape juice before candle lighting.

## Rooms, kids and the family

- **Sleeping:** Kids can share rooms or sleep on mattresses on the floor, and grandparents may need a quiet bedroom near a bathroom.
- **Food:** Ask about allergies and dietary needs. If guests keep kosher and you do not, see [hosting a kosher guest](/learn/hosting-a-kosher-guest).
- **Seating and honor:** The mitzvah of honoring parents extends to in-laws and grandparents, so give them comfortable seats and a role at the table. See [Honoring Parents](/learn/honoring-parents).
- **Hosting your first seder?** See [Hosting Your First Seder](/learn/hosting-your-first-seder).

## When family tension comes along

Holidays can bring old tensions. Decide in advance to keep the table peaceful. Set a gentle topic or a game, thank people for coming, and avoid heated arguments. If family tension is a worry, read [family tension at holiday meals](/learn/family-tension-at-holiday-meals), or call the Rabbi privately at 925-384-7242.

## Common questions

### How early should my family arrive before a Jewish holiday?
Aim for several hours before candle lighting, so guests can settle in and nobody rushes. The exact time is on the [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times) page.

### Is there BART from the airport to Lafayette?
Yes. BART's Yellow Line runs from the San Francisco airport and stops at Orinda and Lafayette, according to the line's public description. Check bart.gov for current schedules.

### Can guests drive to services on a holiday?
Observant Jewish law forbids driving on Shabbat and holidays, so guests who keep them should stay within walking distance. Ask the Rabbi about your situation.

### What is an eruv tavshilin and do I need one?
It is a ritual done before a holiday that falls before Shabbat, allowing you to cook for Shabbat on the holiday. See [Eruv Tavshilin](/learn/eruv-tavshilin).

### How do I keep the peace at a family holiday meal?
Plan the seating, pick light topics and focus on honoring one another. See [Family Tension at Holiday Meals](/learn/family-tension-at-holiday-meals).
