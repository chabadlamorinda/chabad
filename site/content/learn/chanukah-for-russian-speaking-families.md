For Jewish families from Russia, Ukraine, Belarus and the rest of the former Soviet Union, Chanukah can be a holiday of memory as well as light: for generations, many could not light a menorah openly, and today you can light one in your window in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda. Chabad of Lamorinda welcomes Russian-speaking families, and this website is available in Russian.

## A menorah where no menorah was allowed

For most of the twentieth century, the Soviet government discouraged religion, and Jewish practice was often punished. Even so, Jews kept Judaism alive in secret, as explained in [Judaism behind the Iron Curtain](/learn/soviet-jewry-history). Many families in the East Bay are the children and grandchildren of those people.

The story is told of Reb Berke Chein, a devoted Chabad chassid who was imprisoned in a Soviet prison after trying to cross the border. Prisoners were interrogated, and holding any forbidden object could bring a terrible charge. As Chanukah came, he worried about how to get matches, wicks and oil. A non-Jewish cellmate asked what he needed. With what he had, the man made a menorah from half an onion, used butter from his ration as oil, and pulled threads from his uniform as wicks. For a flame, he rubbed threads against the stone floor with the sole of a shoe until they smoldered. Reb Berke called it his personal Chanukah miracle.

The story reminds us that the wish to light a Chanukah (חֲנֻכָּה) light is stronger than fear, and that kindness comes from surprising places. It may also remind you of a grandmother's whisper, a candle in a hidden corner, or a holiday remembered only in pieces.

## Lighting in the open, today

The idea of Chanukah is publicizing the miracle, so the menorah is meant to be seen. Here in California you can place it in a window or doorway, in full view. In 1973 the Rebbe launched a campaign to bring Chanukah out of the home and into public life, and public menorahs have since spread to town squares in many cities. Lafayette has its own, at [Light Up Lafayette](/learn/light-up-lafayette).

How to light, in short:

1. Set eight lights in a row at the same height, with a ninth, the helper (shamash), apart.
2. Light the helper, say the blessings, then light the newest candle first and continue from left to right.
3. Sing and sit by the lights for about half an hour.

The full steps and blessings are in [How to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah), and [tonight's candles](/menorah) shows the order. Do not worry if you have never done this. Many people light for the first time as adults. If you have not lit before, a short question to the Rabbi is welcome.

## Making it your family's

- **Tell the story in Russian.** Tell your children and grandchildren what your family remembers. Read [the story of Chanukah](/learn/chanukah-story) together.
- **Keep your own foods,** and add latkes. Fried foods recall the miracle of the oil. See [Chanukah foods](/learn/chanukah-foods).
- **Give gelt,** and teach children to give a little to tzedakah. See [gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt).
- **Invite the older generation.** A grandparent who lit in secret may feel something special seeing a child light openly. See [honoring the elderly](/learn/honoring-the-elderly).
- **Name your past.** If you find a family story, write it down. Ask the Rabbi if you would like to learn more about your family's Jewish roots.

## Common questions

### How do Russian-speaking Jews celebrate Chanukah?
By lighting the menorah each night for eight nights, with blessings, singing, eating fried foods and giving gelt and tzedakah, like Jews everywhere. Families add their own memories and stories.

### Is there Chanukah information in Russian?
Yes. This whole website is available in Russian. Tap **RU** at the top of any page, including the Chanukah guides.

### Can I light the menorah if I never did as a child?
Yes. Many Jews from the former Soviet Union light for the first time as adults. It is a mitzvah for every Jew, and no experience is needed. Read [How to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah) or ask the Rabbi.

### Is there a Chanukah event for families near Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda?
Chabad of Lamorinda's Grand Chanukah Menorah Lighting, Light Up Lafayette, is on Sunday, December 6, 2026 at 4:00 PM at Lafayette Plaza Park. It is free and open to everyone. See [events](/events).

### What is the story of Chanukah?
Chanukah recalls the victory of the Maccabees over a regime that forbade Jewish practice, and the oil that lasted eight days in the Temple. See [the story of Chanukah](/learn/chanukah-story).
