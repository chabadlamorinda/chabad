Barchu (בָּרְכוּ, "Bless!") is the call to prayer in which the leader invites the congregation to bless G-d, and everyone answers. It opens the blessings before the Shema in the morning and evening services.

## What is said

The leader says: **Barchu et Adonai hamevorach.** ("Bless G-d, the blessed One.")

The congregation answers: **Baruch Adonai hamevorach le'olam va'ed.** ("Blessed is G-d, the blessed One, forever and ever.")

In the Chabad custom, the leader says the response together with the congregation, and no one answers "Amen" to this line. Many stand for Barchu. Follow the people around you, and ask the Rabbi about bowing customs.

## Why it needs a minyan

Barchu is a public call, said when a [minyan](/learn/minyan) of ten Jewish adults is present. Prayer is fundamentally communal, and Barchu turns individuals into a congregation. If you pray alone, you simply skip Barchu and continue with the blessings.

## The blessings before the Shema

After Barchu come the blessings that frame the [Shema](/learn/shema); the Sages set seven blessings around it each day. In the morning the first is Yotzer Or, praising G-d as creator of light, and the next speaks of G-d's love for Israel and the gift of Torah. These lead directly into the Shema. One should not interrupt between Barchu and these blessings.

You can find them in the [Shacharit morning service](/learn/shacharit-morning-service), after [Ashrei](/learn/ashrei).

## Step by step for newcomers

1. Stand when the leader begins Barchu.
2. Listen for the call, then answer aloud.
3. Say the blessings quietly, following in a siddur with translation.
4. Join in the Shema together.

## Common questions

### What is Barchu?
Barchu is the call to prayer where the leader says "Bless G-d" and the congregation answers.

### What do you answer to Barchu?
"Baruch Adonai hamevorach le'olam va'ed."

### Does Barchu need a minyan?
Yes, it is said with a minyan of ten.

### What if I am praying alone?
Skip Barchu and begin the blessings before the Shema.

### What are the blessings before the Shema?
The first praises G-d for light and creation, and the next speaks of His love and Torah.
