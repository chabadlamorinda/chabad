According to traditional Jewish law (halacha), a person is Jewish if their mother is Jewish, or if they converted to Judaism according to Jewish law. Jewish identity is not lost by lack of observance, belief or knowledge: a Jew is always a Jew.

This question can touch on family, love and identity. Whatever your background, you are welcome at Chabad, and any question can be asked privately and without judgment.

## Jewish through the mother

For thousands of years, Jewish identity has passed through the mother. This is based on the Torah, as explained in the Talmud, and is accepted by traditional Jewish communities worldwide.

Why the mother? Jewish teachings offer several explanations:

- A mother forms the child's body and nurtures the child's earliest identity.
- Chassidic thought teaches that the Jewish soul is connected to the mother's spiritual essence.
- Maternity is always known with certainty.

What this means in practice:

- If your mother is Jewish, you are Jewish, regardless of your father.
- If your mother's mother was Jewish, your mother is Jewish too, even if she was not raised Jewish.
- If only your father is Jewish, you are not Jewish according to halacha, though you have a meaningful connection to the Jewish people.

## A Jew is always a Jew

Being Jewish is not a membership that can expire. The Talmud says, "A Jew, even if he has sinned, remains a Jew." Someone raised with no Jewish practice, who never had a bar or bat mitzvah, or who married outside the faith is still fully Jewish. The Rebbe taught that every Jew has a holy soul, a spark of G-d, that can never be extinguished.

## Conversion

A person not born Jewish can become Jewish through conversion (giyur). A traditional conversion involves:

1. **Sincere study and commitment** to living by the Torah and its mitzvot.
2. **Acceptance of mitzvot** before a rabbinic court (beit din) of three qualified judges.
3. **Brit milah** for men.
4. **Immersion in a [mikvah](/learn/mikvah).**

A sincere convert is fully Jewish and is especially beloved. Ruth, the great-grandmother of King David, was a convert. Jewish tradition traditionally does not seek converts, so a Rabbi will usually encourage reflection to be sure the decision is sincere.

## If you are not Jewish

Judaism teaches that you do not need to be Jewish to have a close relationship with G-d. Every person can live a meaningful, G-dly life through the [Seven Noahide Laws](/learn/noahide-laws).

## Common questions

### How can I find out if I am Jewish?

Start with your maternal line: your mother, her mother, and so on. Documents like a ketubah, a grandparent's burial record, or synagogue records can help. The Rabbi can help you look into it with care.

### My father is Jewish but my mother is not. Am I Jewish?

According to traditional Jewish law, Jewish identity follows the mother. We understand this can be painful to hear. You are warmly welcome in our community, and the Rabbi is glad to talk about your options and your connection.

### Can you stop being Jewish?

No. A person born to a Jewish mother remains Jewish for life, no matter what they believe or practice. Every Jew can always reconnect.

### Does a DNA test prove I am Jewish?

No. A DNA test can suggest ancestry, but Jewish status follows the maternal line as shown by records and family history, or through conversion. Ask the Rabbi to help you understand your results.
