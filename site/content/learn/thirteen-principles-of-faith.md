Jews believe in one G-d who created and runs the world, who gave the Torah at Sinai, and who will bring Moshiach. Maimonides (the Rambam, 1138 to 1204) gathered these core beliefs into thirteen principles, which Jews around the world sing and say in the siddur.

## What are the 13 principles of faith?

Each principle begins in the prayer with the words Ani Maamin (אֲנִי מַאֲמִין), "I believe with complete faith." In one line each:

1. **G-d exists** and is the Creator of everything.
2. **G-d is One,** truly unique, with no partners.
3. **G-d has no body** and no physical form.
4. **G-d is eternal,** with no beginning and no end.
5. **We pray to G-d alone,** and to no one else.
6. **The words of the prophets are true.**
7. **Moses was the greatest of the prophets.**
8. **The whole Torah we have today is the Torah given to Moses** by G-d.
9. **The Torah will not be replaced or changed.**
10. **G-d knows everything we do and think.**
11. **G-d rewards the good and holds us accountable.**
12. **Moshiach will come,** and we wait for him every day. See the [Moshiach guide](/learn/moshiach).
13. **The dead will live again** in the time of the redemption.

## Why Maimonides wrote them

Maimonides lived in Spain, Morocco and Egypt, and was a physician and the great codifier of Jewish law. He wrote the principles in his commentary on the Mishnah to give every Jew, scholar or beginner, a clear summary of the foundations. Not every Jewish thinker counted the beliefs the same way, but the thirteen became the best-known list. Learn more about [Maimonides](/learn/maimonides) and his life.

## Where you meet them in the siddur

The [siddur](/learn/siddur) is the Jewish prayer book. Many siddurim print the thirteen Ani Maamin statements near the morning prayers. Yigdal (יִגְדַּל) is a poem based on the same thirteen principles, sung in many congregations. Ask the Rabbi where they appear in the siddur you use, since the arrangement varies from community to community.

Jews have sung Ani Maamin in the hardest times of history, including the Holocaust. Its melody has a rich story, and when you hear it sung at a Jewish gathering, it is hard to forget.

Judaism puts great weight on what we do, not only on what we think. Beliefs give the mitzvahs their meaning, and the mitzvahs make belief real. ## Common questions

### What do Jews believe?
Jews believe in one G-d, the Torah given at Sinai, reward and responsibility for our actions, and the coming of Moshiach. The 13 Principles are the best-known summary.

### What are Maimonides' 13 principles of faith?
They are thirteen foundations of Jewish belief written by Maimonides, from G-d's existence and unity to Moshiach and the revival of the dead.

### What does Ani Maamin mean?
Ani Maamin means "I believe." It is the opening of each of the thirteen statements, which many Jews say after prayer.

### What is Yigdal?
Yigdal is a Jewish hymn that retells the thirteen principles in poetry, sung to several melodies.

### Do all Jews believe the same things?
Not exactly. Jews differ in practice and emphasis, but these principles are widely accepted in traditional Judaism. Ask the Rabbi if you wonder where you stand.
