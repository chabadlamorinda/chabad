The Torah forbids getting a tattoo. At the same time, a Jew who already has one is fully Jewish, fully welcome in the synagogue, and can be buried in a Jewish cemetery. The popular idea that a tattoo keeps you out of a Jewish cemetery is a myth.

## The source

The prohibition comes from the Torah itself: "You shall not make cuts in your flesh for the dead, nor put tattoo marks on yourselves; I am the L-rd" (Leviticus 19:28). It is one of the 613 mitzvot.

The prohibition covers a permanent mark made by inserting ink or dye under the skin. Temporary tattoos, henna and face paint, which sit on the surface and wash away, are not included, though some avoid them so as not to look like a real tattoo.

## Why no tattoos?

Several reasons are given:

- **Our bodies belong to G-d.** Judaism teaches that the body is on loan to us, a holy vessel for the soul. We care for it and do not permanently change it without a real need.
- **Distance from idolatry.** In ancient times, tattooing was part of pagan worship and mourning rites. The Torah set the Jewish people apart from these practices.
- **A clean slate.** Chassidic thought sees the body as complete as G-d created it, in His image. We express who we are through our deeds, not through marks on the skin.

Like many mitzvot, it is ultimately a decree of G-d, and we keep it even beyond the reasons we understand.

## If you already have a tattoo

Many people got tattoos before they knew about this mitzvah, or at a different stage of life. Here is what to know:

- **You are welcome.** A tattoo does not change anyone's Jewish identity or place in the community. You can be called to the Torah, wear tefillin and take part in every mitzvah.
- **You do not have to remove it.** Removal is not required. Some choose to, and some cover it in synagogue out of modesty, but that is a personal choice.
- **Looking forward.** Judaism always looks ahead. The way of [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah) is to commit not to get more, and to keep growing.

## Burial in a Jewish cemetery

A person with a tattoo can be buried in a Jewish cemetery. Jewish law does not exclude anyone from Jewish burial because of a tattoo. If you have heard otherwise, rest assured. Read more about the [Jewish funeral](/learn/jewish-funeral).

## Holocaust numbers

The numbers tattooed on prisoners in the Nazi camps were forced on them and are no violation at all. Survivors' numbers are a testimony of courage and survival, and many families treat them with deep honor.

## Common questions

### What about medical tattoos, like markers for radiation?
Medical needs are treated differently, and there are often ways to do what is needed while respecting Jewish law. Ask the Rabbi before the procedure if you can.

### Is cosmetic tattooing, like permanent eyebrows, allowed?
Permanent makeup raises its own questions in Jewish law, and opinions vary depending on the technique. Ask the Rabbi before having it done.

### Are ear piercings the same as tattoos?
No. Piercing is not part of this prohibition, and ear piercing for women has a long tradition. Other body modifications raise separate questions about caring for the body; ask the Rabbi.

### Can I get a tattoo of something Jewish, like a Star of David?
The prohibition applies to every tattoo, including Jewish symbols. A Star of David necklace or a mezuzah on your door is a better way to wear your pride.
