The best way to tell your children about your will and a charitable gift is to tell them yourself, in your own words, while you are able, and to explain the values behind your choices. It is wise to discuss your plans with your family, and a gift to a Jewish cause is easier for children to understand when they hear why it matters to you.

## Why tell them yourself

Talk over your wishes with your family and a financial advisor. A will is a legal document, but children also wonder what their parent meant. Explaining it ahead of time can spare them from guessing. Family quarrels over an estate hurt, and the Torah takes peace seriously; see [Family Feuds and Inheritance](/learn/family-feuds-and-inheritance).

## Say what you value, not only what you give

Beyond the numbers, your children want to know what you believe in. For a gift to Chabad of Lamorinda that may be: Jewish education for future generations, keeping Judaism alive, and Jewish continuity in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda. Say it in your own words, as you would at a Shabbat table.

## An ethical will

An ethical will is a letter that passes on your values, memories and blessings. One example was written by Dvora Waysman to her four children. She says her possessions are modest and would fit in a few lines, and she leaves them what she considers more valuable: memories, an extended family, Jewish faith and heritage, pride, and her love and blessing. A letter like this can sit beside your will and explain what the legal words cannot. See [An Ethical Will](/learn/ethical-will) and [Recording Your Family Story](/learn/recording-your-family-story).

## Ideas for the conversation

These are simple suggestions, not rules:

1. Choose a calm time, not a moment of stress.
2. Begin with your values and your gratitude, then say what you have decided.
3. Listen to questions and feelings.
4. Say who will carry out your wishes, and where your documents are kept.
5. Ask your attorney if anything legal needs to be said differently.

## Common questions

### How do I tell my children about my will?
Tell them yourself, in a calm moment, beginning with your values. It is wise to discuss your wishes with family.

### Do I have to tell my children about a gift to charity?
That is your decision. Many people find that telling their children is kind and avoids surprises. Ask your attorney about legal points.

### What is an ethical will?
A letter that passes on your values and blessings. See [An Ethical Will](/learn/ethical-will).

### What if my children disagree?
Listen, and explain. If a quarrel grows, the Rabbi can help; see [Family Feuds and Inheritance](/learn/family-feuds-and-inheritance).
