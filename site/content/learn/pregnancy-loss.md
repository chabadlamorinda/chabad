Losing a pregnancy or a baby is a deep and often silent grief. Many parents feel alone, and the people around them do not know what to say. Judaism recognizes this loss, and offers comfort and guidance.

## Your grief is real

Whether the loss came early or late, you lost a child you already loved and a future you imagined. It is natural to feel sadness, anger, guilt or emptiness, and for each parent to grieve differently. It is not your fault.

## What Jewish law says

- **Burial.** A baby who was stillborn or lived only a short time, and in many cases a later miscarriage, is buried in a Jewish cemetery with dignity. The Chevra Kadisha (burial society) cares for the baby. Call the Rabbi as soon as possible, ideally before the hospital makes arrangements, so everything is done properly.
- **Mourning.** For a baby who lived less than 30 days, the formal mourning rituals (shiva, Kaddish) are not observed. This is not because the loss is small; Jewish law spares the parents obligations at a fragile time. Parents are fully entitled to grieve and to be comforted.
- **Naming.** Many give a baby boy who died before his brit milah a Hebrew name, and a baby girl a name as well. Ask the Rabbi.

Every case has details, so please call; the Rabbi will guide you gently, step by step.

## Ways to remember and heal

- **Tehillim:** saying Psalms, especially Psalm 23, can be a comfort. See [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim).
- **Tzedakah** in the baby's memory.
- **Lighting a candle** on a hard day.
- **Talking:** with your spouse, a friend, the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin, and a counselor who specializes in pregnancy loss.

## For family and friends

- Say "I'm so sorry." Then listen.
- Don't say "It was meant to be" or "You can try again."
- Bring a meal, help with older children, and remember the due date and anniversary.
- Both parents are grieving; check on the father too.

## Common questions

### Is there a funeral for a stillborn baby?
There is a burial, usually small and private, arranged by the Chevra Kadisha. Call the Rabbi right away for guidance.

### Do we sit shiva?
Not for a baby who lived less than 30 days. You may still receive visitors and comfort, and you should.

### Will this affect future pregnancies?
That is a medical question for your doctor. Spiritually, many couples find strength in prayer, mitzvahs and the support of the community.
