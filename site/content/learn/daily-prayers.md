Jews pray three times a day: in the morning, the afternoon and the evening. Our Sages teach that Abraham, Isaac and Jacob each began one of these prayers, and they also correspond to the daily offerings in the Holy Temple. Prayer is a daily conversation with G-d, a few minutes to stop, give thanks and ask for what we need.

## Shacharit: the morning prayer

**When:** in the morning, ideally in the first part of the day. The exact times change with the seasons; ask the Rabbi for today's times.

**What's in it:**
- **Morning blessings,** thanking G-d for the new day.
- **Pesukei d'Zimra,** psalms of praise, including Ashrei.
- **The [Shema](/learn/shema)** with its blessings.
- **The [Amidah](/learn/amidah),** the silent standing prayer, the heart of every service.
- **Closing prayers,** including Aleinu.

On Mondays and Thursdays the Torah is read. Men wear [tallit](/learn/tallit) and [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) for weekday Shacharit.

## Mincha: the afternoon prayer

**When:** in the afternoon, until sunset.

**What's in it:** Ashrei, the Amidah and Aleinu. It is the shortest of the three. Our Sages say Mincha is especially precious, because it means stopping in the middle of a busy day to remember G-d.

## Maariv: the evening prayer

**When:** after nightfall, and it may be said until late at night.

**What's in it:** the Shema with its blessings, the Amidah and Aleinu. Before sleep, there is also the [bedtime Shema](/learn/bedtime-shema).

## Alone or with a minyan?

You can pray anywhere: at home, at work, on a hike. Praying with a [minyan](/learn/minyan), ten Jewish men over 13, is especially valuable. Certain parts are said only with a minyan: Kaddish, Barchu, Kedushah and the Torah reading. If you pray alone, try to do so at the same time the community prays.

## Starting small

You do not have to say everything at once. A good beginner's prayer:

1. [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani) on waking.
2. The morning blessings.
3. The first paragraph of the Shema.
4. A short personal request in your own words, in any language.

Add a little at a time. A few words said with heart are worth more than many said in a rush. A [siddur](/learn/siddur) with English translation and transliteration helps.

## Women and daily prayer

Women are obligated to pray every day. In the Chabad view, based on the Alter Rebbe's Shulchan Aruch, women say Shacharit and Mincha, centered on the Amidah; Maariv is optional for women. Many women also say the morning blessings and the Shema. A busy mother with small children can ask the Rabbi or Rebbetzin what is right for her.

## Common questions

### What if I don't read Hebrew?
You may pray in any language you understand. A siddur with translation and transliteration lets you follow along, and reading Hebrew can come in time. See [Hebrew reading for adults](/learn/hebrew-reading-for-adults).

### How long does each prayer take?
A full weekday Shacharit with a minyan takes 30 to 45 minutes; Mincha and Maariv take about 10 to 15 minutes each. A beginner's version can take five minutes.

### What if I missed a prayer?
In some cases there is a way to make it up in the next prayer. Ask the Rabbi.

### Do I need to face a certain direction?
Yes, Jews face toward Jerusalem when praying. In America that is generally toward the east. If you are not sure which way to face, simply pray with your heart directed to Jerusalem.
