The Tanya teaches that your workday is not outside your spiritual life: honestly earned money and effort can be raised to holiness, and heavy spiritual brooding in the middle of business is not holy at all, but a trick of the yetzer hara. Chapter 26 says that heartfelt sadness belongs to prayer and Torah study, not the middle of a business day.

For people in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda who commute, run businesses or work from home, this is a freeing idea. You can be fully at work and fully a Jew at the same time.

## Chapter 26: a trick for the middle of the day

Chapter 26 on Chabad.org is about sadness and a heavy heart. It says the evil inclination cannot be defeated with laziness and heaviness, which come from sadness. The Alter Rebbe compares the inner struggle to a wrestling match: a lazy wrestler loses even to a weaker opponent. We need alacrity that flows from joy and an open heart.

Then the chapter makes a point about the workday. Sadness about spiritual matters that strikes during business or work, it says, is a trick of the yetzer hara, whose goal is to make a person depressed so that he later falls into temptation. Genuine sadness, rooted in love or awe of G-d, arises naturally during prayer or Torah study. If it appears in the middle of business affairs, it is likely artificial. The remedy is to recognize that this is not the time for sadness, even over serious mistakes.

## Set a time for reflection

The chapter does not tell you to ignore your conscience. It advises setting aside fixed times, when the mind is calm, to reflect on G-d's greatness and on wrongs you have done. The chapter says the proper timing is explained in Iggeret HaTeshuvah (Chapters 7 and 11, per the footnotes); this page does not specify it. After that heart-searching, it says, remove the sorrow completely: G-d "pardons abundantly." Believing this is itself, in the chapter's words, the true joy in G-d.

So the Tanya gives each part of life its own time: reflection at fixed times, prayer, and then work with a clear head and a light heart. See [what the Tanya says about prayer](/learn/tanya-on-prayer).

This guide is about spiritual heaviness, not clinical depression or anxiety. If you are struggling, professional help is also the right step, and you can talk to the Rabbi. Ask the Rabbi for personal guidance.

## What about earning money?

Chapter 37 says something surprising about work. Tzedakah is special, it explains, because the money comes from one's labor, so the full energy of the animal soul goes into earning it. Giving it to charity lifts all that energy to G-d. Even someone who does not work for a living gives "the life of his soul," since the money could have sustained his life. See [Tanya on Money](/learn/tanya-on-money).

Chapter 37 also says that things obtained through theft cannot be lifted into holiness. Honesty is part of the picture; see [business ethics](/learn/business-ethics).

## Calm trust

Epistle 11 of the Iggeret HaKodesh on Chabad.org offers a spiritual perspective on livelihood: a person who truly trusts G-d knows that his needs and affairs come from G-d, and does not have to be anxious. The epistle gives no practical advice on earning or budgeting; its concern is the inner attitude. For the practical side of livelihood, see [parnassah, work and livelihood](/learn/parnassah-work-and-livelihood).

## A way to carry this into your week

These are ideas that follow from the chapters above, not rules from the Tanya:

- When a heavy thought about your faults arrives in the middle of a meeting, remember that this may not be the time. Set it aside for prayer or a calm moment.
- Begin the workday with thanks. See [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani).
- Take your earnings seriously as a tool for good. See [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).
- Learn a little each week. See [Jewish wisdom for busy professionals](/learn/jewish-wisdom-for-busy-professionals).

## Common questions

### How can I be a good Jew at work?
The Tanya's answer is to be honest, to keep serving with joy and to give the heavy spiritual thoughts their own time in prayer and reflection. Chapter 26 says that sadness in the middle of business is likely a trick of the yetzer hara.

### What does the Tanya say about work and business?
Chapter 26 says that spiritual sadness in the middle of business is likely a trick of the yetzer hara, and Chapter 37 says the money you earn through labor carries your energy and can be lifted to holiness when given to tzedakah.

### Is it wrong to think about my sins during the workday?
Chapter 26 says that sorrow over your mistakes belongs at set times, when the mind is calm, and not in the middle of business. After that, remove the sorrow and believe that G-d pardons abundantly.

### Does Judaism say I should not worry about money?
Epistle 11 says that someone who truly trusts G-d knows his needs come from G-d. It does not say to ignore practical responsibilities. If worry is heavy, call the Rabbi. See also [money worries](/learn/money-worries).

### Is this advice for depression?
No. The Tanya's guidance is spiritual. If you are struggling with sadness or anxiety, professional help is also right, and the Rabbi is available to talk.
