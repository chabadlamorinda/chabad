Maaser means "a tenth." It is the Jewish custom of giving ten percent of your income to tzedakah, charity. Many Jews keep this practice as a basic part of their financial life, giving with gratitude for the blessings G-d has given them.

## Where maaser comes from

In the Torah, farmers in the Land of Israel gave a tenth of their crops to support the Levites and the poor. Jacob also promised G-d, "Of all that You give me, I will surely give a tenth to You" (Genesis 28:22).

From these roots, the Sages established the custom of *maaser kesafim*, giving a tenth of one's money. Jewish law says that giving a tenth is the standard, and giving up to a fifth is especially praiseworthy. Giving more than a fifth is generally discouraged, so a person does not end up needing help themselves.

## Why give maaser?

- **It is not really ours.** Judaism teaches that our money is entrusted to us by G-d, and part of it is meant for others. Giving tzedakah is not just generosity; it is justice.
- **It brings blessing.** The prophet Malachi says that G-d invites us to test Him with tithing, and He will pour out blessing. This is the one area where the Sages say testing G-d is allowed.
- **It makes giving a habit.** Setting aside a fixed amount means you give consistently, not only when you happen to feel moved.

## How to calculate maaser

1. **Start with your income.** This includes salary, business profit, gifts and investment gains.
2. **Decide on gross or net.** Many calculate on net income, after taxes and business expenses. Others give from gross. Ask the Rabbi which approach fits your situation.
3. **Set aside ten percent.** Many people put it aside right away, in a separate account or a note on a spreadsheet.
4. **Track your giving.** Keep a simple record of what you set aside and what you have given.

## What maaser can go to

Maaser is for tzedakah. Common uses include:

- Helping the poor and people in need.
- Supporting Torah study and Jewish education.
- Funding synagogues and Jewish community programs.
- Helping a relative in financial need, which is a top priority in Jewish law.

Some expenses, such as paying for your own children's basic needs, normally do not count. Ask the Rabbi about specific cases.

## Tips for keeping maaser

- **Give a little every day,** such as a coin in your [pushka](/learn/pushka) before morning prayers.
- **Give before Shabbat candle lighting** each Friday.
- **Make a plan for the year,** dividing your maaser among causes that matter to you.
- **Start where you are.** If ten percent feels like too much right now, begin with a smaller amount and grow.

## Common questions

### Is maaser required by Jewish law?
Giving tzedakah is a Torah obligation. Giving exactly a tenth is a widely accepted standard set by the Sages and kept by many as a binding practice. If you are unsure how it applies to you, ask the Rabbi.

### Can I give maaser to my synagogue?
Yes. Supporting a synagogue or Jewish community center, Torah study and Jewish education are fine uses of maaser. Membership dues and tickets for your own family may be different, so ask.

### What if I cannot afford ten percent?
Tzedakah is about giving according to your means. Give what you can, even a small amount. G-d values every gift.

### Do I need to tell the recipient it is maaser?
No. It is simply your tzedakah. What matters is that you set aside and give the amount.
