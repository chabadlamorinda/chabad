A brit milah (often called a bris) is the Jewish circumcision ceremony, performed on a baby boy on the eighth day of his life. It marks his entry into the covenant G-d made with Abraham, and it is one of the oldest and most universally kept traditions in Jewish life.

## What is a brit milah?

The Hebrew words brit milah mean "covenant of circumcision." In the Torah, G-d commanded Abraham to circumcise himself and his descendants as "an eternal covenant" (Genesis 17). For nearly 4,000 years, Jewish families in every land and circumstance have kept this mitzvah, often at great personal risk.

The bris is performed by a mohel, a person trained in both the medical procedure and the Jewish laws involved. It is a religious ceremony, not only a medical one, and it is followed by a festive meal called a seudat mitzvah.

## Why the eighth day?

The Torah specifies the eighth day. Our sages explain that the number seven represents the natural world (the seven days of creation), while eight represents something beyond nature. The covenant connects a child to G-d in a way that transcends the physical.

Some practical points:

- The bris takes place on the eighth day even if it falls on Shabbat or Yom Kippur, as long as the baby was born naturally.
- It is held during daylight hours, ideally in the morning, to show eagerness for the mitzvah.
- If the baby is not healthy enough, the bris is postponed until the doctor and mohel agree he is well. A child's health always comes first.

For timing questions in your specific case, ask the Rabbi.

## Who takes part

Several honors are given to family and friends:

- **Kvatter and kvatterin:** a couple who carries the baby into the room.
- **Sandek:** the person who holds the baby during the circumcision, often a grandfather or respected elder. It is considered one of the highest honors.
- **Kisei shel Eliyahu (Chair of Elijah):** the baby is briefly placed on a special chair, since tradition teaches that the prophet Elijah attends every bris.
- **Blessings:** the father recites a blessing, and those present respond, "Just as he has entered the covenant, so may he enter into Torah, marriage and good deeds."

## The naming

At the end of the ceremony, a blessing is said over a cup of wine and the baby's Hebrew name is announced for the first time. Many parents find this the most moving moment. Learn more in our guides to [baby naming](/learn/baby-naming) and [Hebrew names](/learn/hebrew-names).

## After the bris

Guests share a festive meal, sing, and wish the family mazel tov. The mohel gives instructions for caring for the baby over the next few days.

## Common questions

### Does a bris have to be on the eighth day?

Yes, when the baby is healthy. The day of birth counts as day one, so a boy born on a Monday has his bris the following Monday. Remember that a Jewish day begins at sunset, so a birth after sundown counts as the next day.

### Is a hospital circumcision the same as a bris?

No. A bris is a religious ceremony with specific laws and blessings, performed by a mohel. A medical circumcision does not fulfill the mitzvah, though there is a procedure for an older child or adult who was circumcised medically. Ask the Rabbi about your situation.

### Can an adult have a brit milah?

Yes. Jewish men who did not have a bris as babies can have one at any age, performed under proper medical care. It is never too late, and it is a deeply meaningful step.

### What should guests bring or wear?

Dress as you would for synagogue. A gift for the baby is a kind gesture but not required. Your presence and blessings are what matter most.
