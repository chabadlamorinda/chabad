Jewish teens are asked it at school, Jewish adults hear it from friends and coworkers, and many people simply wonder: "Why don't Jews believe in Jesus?" Here is a simple, respectful answer. It is not about anyone's sincerity or goodness. It is about what Judaism has always believed, from the Torah itself.

## The short answer

Judaism does not believe Jesus was the Messiah, because the Messiah described by the Hebrew prophets has a clear mission that has not yet been fulfilled. Judaism also does not believe that G-d becomes a human being, or that the Torah's commandments were ever replaced. These beliefs come straight from the Hebrew Bible and have been held by Jews for thousands of years.

## 1. What the prophets say the Messiah will do

The Hebrew word Moshiach means "anointed one." The prophets describe him as a human leader, a descendant of King David, who will:

- Bring the Jewish people back to the Land of Israel (Isaiah 11:12).
- Rebuild the Holy Temple in Jerusalem (Ezekiel 37:26-28).
- Bring peace to the whole world, so that "nation will not lift up sword against nation" (Isaiah 2:4).
- Fill the world with the knowledge of G-d (Isaiah 11:9).

Maimonides, the great 12th-century sage, wrote that the Messiah is recognized by actually completing this mission. Since the world is still waiting for peace and for the Temple, Jews are still waiting for Moshiach, and we pray for him every day. Read more in [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach).

## 2. G-d is One

The central Jewish declaration is the [Shema](/learn/shema): "Hear, O Israel, the L-rd is our G-d, the L-rd is One." Judaism teaches that G-d is One, without parts, and has no body or human form. G-d does not become a person, and no person is G-d. Maimonides included this among the [Thirteen Principles of Faith](/learn/thirteen-principles-of-faith).

## 3. No go-between is needed

In Judaism, every person may speak directly to G-d, in any language, at any moment. We pray to G-d alone, not through an intermediary. Teshuvah, returning to G-d, is open to everyone, always.

## 4. The Torah is forever

The Torah calls its commandments "an everlasting covenant" for all generations. Jews believe the mitzvot, Shabbat, kosher, tefillin and the rest, were never cancelled or replaced. They remain G-d's living instructions to the Jewish people.

## Respect for our neighbors

Judaism teaches respect for every person, each created in the image of G-d. Our Christian friends and neighbors hold their beliefs sincerely, and we respect them as people while holding to our own faith. Judaism does not ask anyone to become Jewish; every person can live a good, G-dly life through the [Seven Noahide Laws](/learn/noahide-laws).

For Jews, the answer is not about rejecting others. It is about staying faithful to the covenant our ancestors accepted at Sinai, and to the hope the prophets gave us.

## Common questions

### Why don't Jews accept Jesus as the Messiah?
Because the Messiah of the Hebrew prophets is a human descendant of King David who will gather the Jewish people to Israel, rebuild the Temple in Jerusalem and bring world peace. Those things have not yet happened, so Jews are still waiting and praying for the Messiah.

### Do Jews believe in the Messiah?
Yes. Belief in Moshiach is one of Maimonides' Thirteen Principles of Faith. Jews pray for his coming every day and believe it can happen at any time.

### What do Jews believe about Jesus?
Judaism sees Jesus as a historical figure who lived in the time of the Second Temple, but not as the Messiah, a prophet or divine. Judaism teaches that G-d is One and never takes human form.
