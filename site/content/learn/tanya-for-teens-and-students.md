Tanya's idea for teens and students is simple: every Jew has two souls, a G-dly soul that wants what is true and good and an animal soul that wants comfort and self, and you are the one who chooses between them. The pull you feel in two directions is not a defect. It is how the Tanya says a person is built.

## Why do I feel pulled two ways?

You want to do well, and you want to relax. You want to be kind, and you get angry. You want to belong, and you want to be yourself. The [Tanya](/learn/tanya), written by Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi, the founder of Chabad, says this is normal.

In Chapter 1, on Chabad.org, the Alter Rebbe teaches that every Jew has two souls. One animates the body and is the source of natural drives, including anger, pride, appetite and laziness, and also some good traits like compassion. The other, the G-dly soul, is described in Chapter 2 as "a part of G-d above." For a longer explanation, see [the soul](/learn/the-soul).

The Tanya does not say the first soul is "bad." It says it needs direction.

## You are not what you feel

Chapter 12 describes the ordinary person the Tanya calls a beinoni. He still feels the pulls of the animal soul, but his thought, speech and action are guided by the G-dly soul. He answers anger or jealousy toward others with kindness. See [the beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni).

For a teenager that means: a feeling is not an order. You can feel the urge to snap, to gossip or to give up, and still choose differently. The Tanya says the mind can guide the heart. See also [Jewish identity for teens](/learn/jewish-identity-for-teens).

## Applying it: pressure, screens, stress

These are ways to think with the Tanya, not statements from the text.

- **Peer pressure.** Ask yourself which soul is talking. Not every voice you hear is you.
- **What you say and type.** Tanya (Chapters 12 and 37) speaks of thought, speech and action as the ways the soul acts in the world, and calls thought and speech "garments" of the soul. A message you send is speech. It leaves a mark. See [lashon hara](/learn/lashon-hara).
- **Exam stress.** Epistle 11, on Chabad.org, teaches that G-d gives life at every moment and that the good is often hidden. Do your best, then let go of the outcome. See [exam stress: Jewish wisdom](/learn/exam-stress-jewish-wisdom) and [Tanya on worry and trust](/learn/tanya-on-worry-and-trust).
- **Feeling not Jewish enough.** See [Tanya on self-worth](/learn/tanya-on-self-worth).
- **Heading to campus.** See [Jewish college students](/learn/jewish-college-students) and [sending a Lamorinda teen to college](/learn/sending-a-lamorinda-teen-to-college).

If you feel anxious or very low, talk to a parent, school counselor or doctor. That is also right, and the Tanya's guidance is spiritual, not medical. You can also ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### What does the Tanya say about teenagers?
It does not speak about age. It says every Jew has a G-dly soul and an animal soul, and everyone chooses. That fits the teen years, when the inner pull is strong.

### What are the two souls?
The animal soul gives natural drives and the G-dly soul is "a part of G-d above." You choose which one guides your actions. See [the soul](/learn/the-soul).

### Is it normal to feel like two people?
Yes. The Tanya treats the inner struggle as normal and as the place where growth happens. It is not proof that something is wrong.

### Can Tanya help with exam stress and anxiety?
It offers a spiritual view of trust: effort, then letting go. If stress is overwhelming, professional help is also right. Ask the Rabbi.
