After her wedding, a Jewish woman traditionally covers her hair. For many women it is a daily, quiet expression of who they are: a married Jewish woman with a private, precious bond with her husband.

## Where it comes from

- **The Torah** hints at it in the passage of the sotah (Numbers 5:18), where the kohen uncovers a woman's hair. The Talmud learns from this that married Jewish women normally cover their hair (Ketubot 72a).
- **Halacha.** The Shulchan Aruch codifies that a married woman covers her hair in public. This is the accepted law in every traditional community.

## Why cover hair?

Covering hair is part of [tzniut](/learn/tzniut), Jewish modesty. Modesty in Judaism is not about hiding; it is about dignity and keeping the most personal things private. A woman's hair is considered part of her beauty, and after marriage it is reserved for her home.

Jewish tradition also sees great blessing in this mitzvah. The Zohar teaches that a woman who covers her hair brings blessing to her husband, her children and her home. The Talmud tells of Kimchit, whose sons served as High Priests, and who attributed this honor to her great care in covering her hair.

## Sheitel, tichel or hat

There are three common ways to cover hair:

- **Sheitel (wig).** Looks natural and stays put all day.
- **Tichel (scarf)** or snood. Comfortable and often worn at home or in casual settings.
- **Hat or beret.** A simple, stylish option for outside.

Customs vary about how much hair is covered and which kind is preferred. Ask the Rebbetzin what applies to you.

## The Chabad view

The Rebbe strongly encouraged married women to wear a sheitel. He explained that a sheitel covers all the hair, and that a woman is less tempted to take it off in situations where a scarf or hat might feel awkward. He also spoke of the blessings this mitzvah brings to the whole family, and encouraged women to wear a sheitel that is attractive, so the mitzvah is a joy.

## Starting gradually

Many women did not grow up with this mitzvah. If it feels like a big step, that is normal.

- **Start where you can.** Some begin by covering their hair on Shabbat, when lighting candles, or for prayer, and grow from there.
- **Find what feels like you.** A good sheitel or a beautiful scarf can feel natural and even make you feel more beautiful.
- **Talk to other women** who cover their hair. Most are happy to share tips.
- **Ask the Rebbetzin.** She is glad to talk privately and without judgment.

Every step counts. A mitzvah is never "all or nothing."

## Common questions

### When does a woman start covering her hair?
After her wedding. Customs vary slightly about the exact moment; ask the Rebbetzin.

### Do unmarried women cover their hair?
No. In most communities, including Chabad, hair covering begins with marriage. Women who were married before, such as widows or divorced women, continue to cover their hair.

### Does a woman cover her hair at home?
Customs and opinions vary about the home, especially in front of her own husband and children. Ask the Rebbetzin.

### Isn't a sheitel just as attractive as hair?
The halacha is about covering one's own hair, which a sheitel does completely. A sheitel should still be modest and dignified. Ask the Rebbetzin for guidance.
