Your health always comes first: never stop or delay a prescription without your doctor, and Jewish law permits whatever treatment you need. The kosher rules for medicine are lenient, especially for a pill swallowed whole when you are ill, but they are not identical for every case, so for any specific medicine, vitamin, Passover, fast day or Shabbat question, ask the Rabbi.

## Health comes first

Judaism holds that saving and protecting life overrides almost every commandment (see [pikuach nefesh](/learn/pikuach-nefesh)). No one should skip a needed medicine because of a kosher or Shabbat concern. If you are unsure, take what your doctor ordered and call the Rabbi right away: he will help you with the rest. For more, read [Jewish views on health and the body](/learn/jewish-health-and-body).

## Is medicine kosher? The general idea

The laws of kosher food apply to things eaten in the normal way. A medicine is not ordinary food, so the rules are more flexible, and the answer depends on a few things:

- **How the medicine is taken.** A tasteless tablet or capsule swallowed whole is treated more leniently than a chewable, a liquid or a flavored syrup, which are pleasant to taste and are closer to food.
- **Who is taking it.** A person who is ill, even if not in danger, has more room for leniency than a healthy person taking a vitamin.
- **What is in it.** Gelatin (often in capsules and gummy vitamins), glycerin, lactose and flavorings can come from non-kosher sources (see [hidden kosher ingredients](/learn/hidden-kosher-ingredients)).

Where a kosher or plant-based capsule exists in the same strength, many people choose it. For every specific product, ask the Rabbi; do not rely on a label or on a friend.

## Vitamins and supplements

Vitamins are usually not prescribed, so it is easier to choose a kosher one. Gummy vitamins and chewables often contain gelatin and flavorings and are the most common concern. Look for a reliable symbol, or call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242 with the name of the product.

## Medicine on Passover

Some tablets use starch or other ingredients that can be chametz. Many authorities allow a person who needs a pill to take it even on Passover, while liquids and chewables need more care. Ask the Rabbi before Passover, so that you have your answer in advance. Read [kosher for Passover](/learn/kosher-for-passover) for the larger picture.

## Medicine on fast days and Shabbat

**Fast days.** On Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av, someone who is ill, elderly, pregnant or on essential medication may need to eat or drink. Do not decide this alone: talk to your doctor and the Rabbi before the fast. See [fast days](/learn/fast-days).

**Shabbat.** Medicine that you need for a chronic condition, and any medicine in an emergency, is permitted. Treatments for mild discomfort have special rules. Call the Rabbi before Shabbat for a clear answer, and in any emergency call your doctor or 911 right away.

## A prayer for healing

The Talmud records a short prayer to say when going for treatment or medicine, asking that it bring a cure. Many also say a short personal prayer or Psalms for a healing. See [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) for how to add a name to the prayer list. Ask the Rabbi if you would like a Mi Sheberach said for you.

## Common questions

### Do vitamins need to be kosher?
It is preferable to choose kosher vitamins, since you are not ill and have easy alternatives. Gummies and chewables with gelatin are the main concern. Ask the Rabbi about a specific product.

### Are gelatin capsules kosher?
Gelatin capsules are kosher only if the gelatin is from a kosher source. For a person who is ill and swallows a capsule whole, there is room for leniency. Ask the Rabbi.

### Do I have to take only kosher-for-Passover medicine?
Ask the Rabbi in advance. Many people who need a medicine are permitted to keep taking it, and the Rabbi can tell you which forms need care. Never stop prescription medicine without your doctor.

### Can I take medicine on Yom Kippur?
Yes, if you need it. Pikuach nefesh overrides the fast. Speak to your doctor and the Rabbi before Yom Kippur so you know exactly what to do.

### Can I take medicine on Shabbat?
Medicine for an ongoing or serious condition is permitted. For smaller matters, ask the Rabbi before Shabbat, and in an emergency do not wait.
