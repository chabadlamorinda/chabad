Yes, Jews celebrate birthdays, and in Chabad a birthday is a very special day: a personal new year, a time to give thanks, take stock and grow. The Lubavitcher Rebbe encouraged every Jew, young and old, to mark their birthday with joy and with meaningful Jewish customs.

## What Jewish tradition says

Some people are surprised to hear that birthdays have a place in Judaism, since the only birthday party mentioned in the Torah is Pharaoh's. But Jewish sources tell a fuller story:

- **Moses** passed away on his birthday, the 7th of Adar, completing his years "to the day." The Talmud learns from this that G-d completes the years of the righteous.
- The Jerusalem Talmud teaches that on a person's birthday, their *mazal*, their spiritual source of blessing, is especially strong.
- **The Rebbe** taught that just as Rosh Hashanah is the birthday of the world, each person's birthday is his or her own Rosh Hashanah.

In 1988, after the passing of his wife, Rebbetzin Chaya Mushka, the Rebbe launched a worldwide birthday campaign, encouraging every Jew to celebrate their birthday as a day of spiritual growth, and to gather friends for a joyful celebration.

## Which date: Hebrew or English?

The special customs are kept on the Hebrew birthday, which falls on a different English date each year. Use our [Hebrew birthday tool](/tools/hebrew-birthday) to find yours. Many people celebrate both. Our guide to the [Hebrew birthday](/learn/hebrew-birthday) lists all the customs in detail.

## Chabad birthday customs at a glance

- **An aliyah** to the Torah on the Shabbat before.
- **Tzedakah** on the day itself, before morning prayers if you can.
- **Extra prayer and Torah study**, even a few minutes.
- **Your Psalm:** start saying the chapter of [Tehillim](/tehillim) matching your new year (your age plus one).
- **A good resolution** for the coming year.
- **A new fruit** with the [Shehecheyanu](/learn/shehecheyanu) blessing.
- **A gathering** of family and friends with words of Torah and blessings.

## A Jewish birthday party for kids

A child's birthday is a wonderful way to make Judaism joyful. Ideas:

1. **Start with tzedakah.** Have the birthday child put coins in a [pushka](/learn/pushka), and invite guests to do the same.
2. **Share a short Torah thought**, a story or a lesson the child chose.
3. **Say a few Torah verses** that Jewish children learn by heart, together.
4. **Make a good resolution**: the birthday child chooses one mitzvah to work on this year.
5. **Kosher food and treats** so every friend can join in.
6. **Blessings:** guests give the birthday child a blessing for the year ahead.

For a boy's third birthday, Jewish tradition marks his first haircut with an [upsherin](/learn/upsherin).

## Common questions

### What do you say to wish someone a happy Jewish birthday?
"Happy birthday" is fine, or "Mazal tov!" Many add "Ad meah v'esrim," "until 120," the age of Moses. In Yiddish: "Biz hundert un tzvantzik."

### Is it okay to have a birthday cake and candles?
Yes. A cake and a celebration are part of the joy. Make sure the food is kosher if guests keep kosher, and add a Jewish touch, like tzedakah or a Torah thought.

### What if my birthday falls on Shabbat?
Celebrate on Shabbat with a festive meal and words of Torah. Tzedakah is given before Shabbat, on Friday, or after Shabbat ends.

### Do adults celebrate too?
Absolutely. The Rebbe encouraged people of every age, including seniors, to celebrate their birthdays with friends and to make the day one of renewed purpose.
