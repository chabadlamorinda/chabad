Yes, you can make Kiddush on kosher grape juice, and it carries the same blessing as wine, *Borei pri hagafen*. If you cannot or do not drink alcohol, for recovery, pregnancy, medication or any other reason, grape juice is the simple solution for Friday night Kiddush, Havdalah and the four cups of the Seder.

## Why grape juice works

Kiddush (קִדּוּשׁ) is the blessing over a cup that declares Shabbat holy. The Sages made it over "the fruit of the vine." Grape juice is the fruit of the vine and carries the same blessing as wine, so it is fully acceptable. Many families use it every week, and no one needs to explain. Read more in [Kiddush](/learn/kiddush).

Choose pure grape juice that has a reliable kosher symbol, since grape products have special rules (see [kosher wine](/learn/kosher-wine)). Sparkling grape juice is fine too. Other drinks, like apple juice or soda, are not the same on Friday night.

## You are not alone, and there is no shame

Many Jews do not drink alcohol, and the reasons are all respected: recovery, pregnancy, certain medications, health conditions, driving or simply a preference. Judaism values health and sobriety above any custom (see [pikuach nefesh](/learn/pikuach-nefesh)). If alcohol is a struggle for you or someone you love, read [addiction and recovery](/learn/addiction-and-recovery), and know that the Rabbi will speak with you privately.

## Friday night Kiddush

1. **Use grape juice** in a clean cup, filled to the brim if possible.
2. **Say the Kiddush** (see [Kiddush](/learn/kiddush)), ending with *Borei pri hagafen*.
3. **Drink a mouthful,** or ask the Rabbi how much is needed. Everyone at the table may share from the cup.

If you have no grape juice, Kiddush can be made over two challahs instead. Ask the Rabbi to show you how.

## Shabbat morning Kiddush

The daytime Kiddush is shorter. If you do not drink wine, grape juice is again the best choice. In some cases other drinks may be used for the morning Kiddush: ask the Rabbi, because the options depend on the drink.

## Havdalah without wine

Havdalah (הַבְדָּלָה) marks the end of Shabbat with a cup, spices and a candle (see [Havdalah](/learn/havdalah)). The cup is usually wine or grape juice. Water is not suitable, and other drinks are limited, so ask the Rabbi what is right for you.

## The four cups at the Seder

At the Passover Seder we drink four cups. Grape juice is acceptable, and many people dilute wine with juice or use red grape juice. A person who must avoid alcohol completely can use grape juice for all four cups. Ask the Rabbi about the amount to drink. Read the [Passover Seder](/learn/passover-seder).

## Common questions

### Can you make Kiddush on grape juice?
Yes. Kosher grape juice carries the same blessing as wine, *Borei pri hagafen*, and it is fully acceptable for Kiddush.

### What can I use for Kiddush if I cannot drink alcohol?
Grape juice is the best choice. If none is available on Friday night, Kiddush can be made over challah. Ask the Rabbi.

### Can I make Havdalah on grape juice?
Yes, grape juice is acceptable for Havdalah. Water is not suitable.

### Can I use grape juice for the four cups at the Seder?
Yes. Many people do, especially those who avoid alcohol. Ask the Rabbi about the amount.

### Is it all right to ask for grape juice at a Kiddush or Shabbat meal?
Yes. Many hosts keep it ready. If you are a guest in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda, simply tell the host, and no one needs to ask why.
