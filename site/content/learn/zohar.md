The Zohar (זֹהַר, "Splendor") is the primary text of Kabbalah, the Jewish mystical tradition. It is a commentary on the Torah, written mostly in Aramaic, that contains the teachings of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his disciples.

## What the Zohar is

The Zohar is a set of mystical teachings on the Five Books of Moses, with additional sections on other subjects. It explores the hidden meaning of the Torah, the nature of the soul, the structure of creation and how mitzvot affect the world above. Chabad.org calls it the primary text of Kabbalah. See the [Kabbalah](/learn/kabbalah) guide for the big picture.

## Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai

Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai (often called Rashbi) was a great sage of the Mishnah, living in the Land of Israel in the second century. The Talmud tells that he and his son hid in a cave for years from the Roman authorities, and spent the time in Torah study. Chabad.org says that Rashbi himself wrote some of the Zohar, such as the section called "the First Mishnah," while hiding in the cave, but that the Zohar describes his disciple Rabbi Abba as the one who wrote down most of the teachings.

Rabbi Shimon's yahrzeit is celebrated on [Lag BaOmer](/holidays/lag-baomer) with bonfires and joy.

## How the Zohar reached us

The book first became widely known in the thirteenth century, when it was published in Spain by one of the leading Kabbalists of the time, Rabbi Moshe de Leon (c. 1240 to 1305). The story of how the manuscript reached him is surrounded by many legends and traditions.

Chabad.org presents the question honestly. It notes that the prevailing academic opinion is that Rabbi Moshe de Leon himself wrote it, with some dissenters. Those who accept the Zohar as authentic, as Chabad does, generally hold that Rashbi wrote part but not all of the text, and that later hands added to it. This guide does not settle the scholarly debate; if you want to go deeper, ask the Rabbi.

## How Chassidus makes it understandable

The Zohar is deep and hard to approach alone. Chassidus, and especially the [Tanya](/learn/tanya), explain Kabbalistic ideas in words an ordinary person can understand and apply to daily life. This is why the Chabad approach prefers starting with Chassidus rather than diving into the Zohar.

## Should I read the Zohar?

Many people are curious. Here is a sensible way to start:

1. **Start with the basics.** Know the [Torah](/learn/torah), [midrash](/learn/midrash) and the weekly portion.
2. **Learn with a teacher.** Kabbalah is not meant to be self-taught, and a teacher keeps you from misunderstandings.
3. **Read about Kabbalah first.** See [Kabbalah](/learn/kabbalah) and the [Tanya](/learn/tanya).
4. **Be wary of fashion.** Red string bracelets, celebrity "Kabbalah" and magic promises are not what the Zohar teaches. Ask the Rabbi what is authentic.

## Common questions

### What is the Zohar?
The Zohar is the primary text of Kabbalah, a mystical commentary on the Torah written mostly in Aramaic. It contains the teachings of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his students.

### Who wrote the Zohar?
Tradition, including Chabad, says Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his disciples, with Rabbi Abba writing down much of it. Academic scholars generally say that Rabbi Moshe de Leon wrote it in the thirteenth century.

### Who was Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai?
A second-century sage of the Land of Israel who, according to the Talmud, hid in a cave from the Romans. His yahrzeit is celebrated on Lag BaOmer.

### Is the Zohar a holy book?
Yes. Jews have long considered it a deeply sacred work, and parts of it are studied by many communities.

### Should I read the Zohar?
Start with a teacher and with introductory books such as the Tanya. Ask the Rabbi where to begin.
