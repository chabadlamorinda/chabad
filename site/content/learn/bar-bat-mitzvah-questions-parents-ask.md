Parents planning a bar or bat mitzvah usually have the same handful of questions. Here are simple answers, from the basics to the day itself. If yours is not here, call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242. Families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda ask us everything, and no question is too small.

## The basics

**What is a bar or bat mitzvah?** The day a Jewish child becomes responsible for the mitzvot: a boy at 13, a girl at 12. "Bar mitzvah" means "son of the commandment," and "bat mitzvah" means "daughter of the commandment." Read more about the [bar mitzvah](/learn/bar-mitzvah) and the [bat mitzvah](/learn/bat-mitzvah).

**Why 12 for girls and 13 for boys?** Jewish tradition teaches that girls mature earlier, so a girl reaches the age of mitzvot a year sooner.

**Does it happen without a ceremony?** Yes. Your child becomes bar or bat mitzvah on the Hebrew birthday regardless. The ceremony celebrates it.

## The date

**How do we find the date?** It is the Hebrew birthday, which usually differs from the English one. Use the [date calculator](/tools/bar-bat-mitzvah), and read [the Hebrew birthday date](/learn/bar-mitzvah-hebrew-birthday-date).

**Can we celebrate on a different day?** The milestone itself comes on the Hebrew birthday, and the celebration can often be held on a nearby day that suits the family. Ask the Rabbi.

## The preparation

**When should we start?** About a year ahead is ideal, and a shorter time also works. See the [step-by-step timeline](/learn/bar-bat-mitzvah-timeline).

**How much Hebrew does my child need?** None to begin. Lessons start wherever your child is.

**Who teaches?** At Chabad of Lamorinda, the Rabbi and the Rebbetzin teach each child personally, at the child's own pace. Boys learn to read from the Torah and put on tefillin; girls join the Bat Mitzvah Club.

**Does my son have to read the whole Torah portion?** No. Reading from the Torah is a beautiful option. The essentials are taking on mitzvot, especially [tefillin](/learn/tefillin), and being called up for an [aliyah](/learn/aliyah).

**What does a girl do?** A bat mitzvah usually centers on learning, a talk and taking on mitzvot such as lighting Shabbat candles, often with a [mitzvah project](/learn/bat-mitzvah-projects). See [bat mitzvah ideas](/learn/bat-mitzvah-ideas).

## The day

**What happens on the day?** For a boy, usually an aliyah to the Torah, a few words of Torah and a festive meal; see [his first aliyah and tefillin](/learn/bar-mitzvah-first-tefillin-and-aliyah). For a girl, often a celebration where she shares what she learned.

**Do we need a party?** No. A simple, joyful meal is enough. See [a bar or bat mitzvah on a budget](/learn/bar-bat-mitzvah-on-a-budget).

**Does the food have to be kosher?** The festive meal is itself a mitzvah, so kosher food is the way to honor it, and we are glad to help. See [kosher catering for a simcha](/learn/kosher-catering-for-a-simcha-in-lamorinda).

## Afterwards

**What changes after the day?** Your child now keeps the mitzvot as a Jewish adult, one step at a time. See [what changes after a bar or bat mitzvah](/learn/after-the-bar-mitzvah).
