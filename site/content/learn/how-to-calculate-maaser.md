To calculate maaser, take your income after mandatory taxes and business expenses, and set aside a tenth of it for tzedakah. Jewish law teaches that maaser applies to all income, including salary, gifts, inheritance and interest, but not to loans you must repay.

## Net or gross: which one?

Maaser is taken from your income after the amounts that are taken from you before you ever receive it. That means:

- **Deducted:** income tax, Social Security and disability insurance withheld from your pay, and, for a business, real business expenses.
- **Generally not deducted:** your personal living costs, such as rent, food and clothing. Those are part of ordinary life, and the tenth is calculated before them.

If you do not yet know your final tax bill (for example, if you are self-employed), you can calculate once a year when your taxes are known, or estimate your taxes and adjust later. Someone in financial need should ask a rabbi whether sales and property taxes may be deducted. Your tax advisor can help with the tax side.

## What counts as income?

According to Jewish law, maaser applies to all income: salary, gifts, inheritance, interest or even money found with no identifiable owner. A bonus is simply more salary, so it falls under the same rule.

**Gifts.** Cash gifts are normally subject to maaser, for example a check from a relative. The exception is money given for a specific purpose, such as rent, tuition or basic needs. Using that money for something else, even tzedakah, may be a problem without the giver's permission. Store gift cards are, by common custom, not subject to maaser. Opinions differ on other kinds of gift cards.

**Help for basic needs.** Money a parent gives for your basic needs generally is not subject to maaser, but spending money you can use freely is.

**Public aid.** Separating maaser from food stamps and similar aid is not just unnecessary, it is prohibited.

## What is not income?

- **Loans** you must repay.
- **Money earmarked** by the giver for a specific purpose.

When calculating profit on something like a home sale, the cost of purchase, renovation and maintenance comes off first. If you sold property, ask the Rabbi, because the details matter.

## A simple example (only an example)

These are made-up round numbers to show the idea, not a rule for anyone:

- Your pay after taxes is **$4,000** in a month. Maaser is $400.
- Someone gives you a **$200** cash gift with no stated purpose. Maaser is $20.
- Your total to set aside is **$420**.

If you have a friend or relative who needs help, or tuition, or a debt, those are separate questions. See [What Maaser Can and Cannot Pay For](/learn/what-maaser-can-and-cannot-pay-for).

## When your income is small

Your own basic needs come first. If you cannot cover food, clothing and shelter, you are not obligated to give maaser, and if you are just getting by, some opinions allow figuring maaser after basic living expenses. Debts that are due now, such as unpaid wages or loans, take precedence over giving maaser. The Rebbe encouraged giving a partial amount where needed. There is warm guidance in [Giving When Money Is Tight](/learn/giving-when-money-is-tight). For your case, ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### Do I calculate maaser on net or gross income?
The rule is to take maaser after mandatory taxes and business expenses, but not after personal living costs. If your situation is complicated, ask the Rabbi.

### Do I give maaser on a bonus?
A bonus is income, and maaser applies to all income. So yes, include it, after taxes withheld.

### Do I give maaser on a gift of money?
Normally yes. The exception is money given for a specific purpose, like rent or tuition.

### Do I give maaser on a loan?
No. Loans you must repay are not income.

### What about my retirement account?
There is no one simple answer here, so ask the Rabbi, and your tax advisor for the tax side.

### Can I calculate maaser once a year?
Yes. You may calculate at any point in the year, though it is ideal to separate maaser when income is received. See [Starting Maaser Step by Step](/learn/starting-maaser-step-by-step).
