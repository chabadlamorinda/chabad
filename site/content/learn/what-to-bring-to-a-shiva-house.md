At a shiva house, you are not expected to bring anything, because your presence matters most. If you want to bring something, bring kosher food, sent or delivered in coordination with whoever is organizing meals, or a photograph or memory of the person who died. Flowers are not part of the traditional Jewish custom; many families prefer a donation to charity in memory of their loved one.

This guide is for friends, neighbors and colleagues in Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda and the East Bay who are going to a shiva house for the first time, and want to do it right.

## Start with this: you do not need to bring anything

Chabad.org says that visitors are generally not expected to bring anything. A shiva visit is about comforting mourners, and the *mitzvah* (commandment) is the visit itself. What matters is to come with a patient and sensitive heart, ready to listen. See [comforting mourners](/learn/comforting-mourners) and [shiva](/learn/shiva).

## Bringing food

Friends often provide food for the mourners and their guests, because the family is in shock and should not need to cook. Bringing food is optional, and if you do, here is how:

1. **Ask first.** Contact whoever is organizing meals for the family, so they do not receive ten lasagnas on the same day.
2. **Keep it kosher.** If the family keeps kosher, bring kosher take-out or food that is clearly kosher, and ask them what they need. Chabad.org suggests kosher take-out as a simple option. If you are not sure, call the Rabbi.
3. **Keep it simple.** Shiva is not a party. Plain, comforting food is perfect.
4. **Make it easy.** Disposable plates, a note, no need to be thanked.

Many communities organize a "meal train," where friends sign up to bring a meal each day.

## The first meal after the funeral

The first meal the mourners eat after the funeral is called the *seudat havra'ah* (meal of condolence), and it is traditionally provided by neighbors, not the family itself. Chabad.org explains that it includes at least bread or rolls and hard-boiled eggs, and may include lentils, cooked vegetables and a drink. The custom reflects wisdom: a mourner may be tempted not to eat, and the neighbors' visit turns them from spectators into participants. If you are a neighbor or a close friend, ask the family or the Rabbi whether you can help provide it.

## Other things you can bring

- **A photo or a memory.** Shiva is a time to share stories. A picture of the person can be a precious gift.
- **A prayerbook, or tallit and tefillin** for a man who will attend morning services at the house.
- **A small packet of tissues,** which are useful in a crowded, emotional room.
- **A written note.** See [writing a condolence note](/learn/writing-a-condolence-note).

## Why not flowers?

Flowers are not part of the traditional Jewish way of comforting mourners, and a house of mourning is quiet and plain. In Jewish tradition, the way to honor the person is with deeds and tzedakah, such as charity, learning or a mitzvah in their memory. Ask the family whether they have a favorite cause. If they do not, you can [give in memory](/donate), and tell the family you did.

## What to say

The traditional words, said as you leave, are *HaMakom yenachem etchem b'toch she'ar aveilei Tzion v'Yerushalayim*, "May G-d comfort you among the other mourners of Zion and Jerusalem." You can also simply say, "I am so sorry." Let the mourner speak first. See [shiva](/learn/shiva).

## Common questions

### What do you bring to a shiva house?
You are not required to bring anything. If you want to, bring kosher food coordinated with the person organizing meals, or a photo of the person who passed.

### Do you bring flowers to a shiva house?
Flowers are not part of the traditional Jewish custom for a house of mourning. Many families prefer a donation to charity in memory of their loved one, so ask, or give to a cause the person cared about.

### Does the food have to be kosher?
If the family keeps kosher, yes, and the safest choice is kosher take-out or a kosher caterer. If you are unsure, call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242.

### Should I call before visiting a shiva house?
It is customary to just come, because shiva is open to visitors, but check for posted hours or ask a family member. Do not stay too long.

### What should I avoid bringing?
Avoid anything festive, such as party platters or gifts for fun, and anything that adds work for the family. Keep it simple.

### Can I send something if I live far away?
Yes. Send a note, a donation in memory, or kosher food through the person organizing meals. A phone call is also a mitzvah.
