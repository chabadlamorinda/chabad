Moses (Moshe, משה) was the greatest prophet who ever lived, the leader who took the Jewish people out of slavery in Egypt and brought them the Torah at Mount Sinai. Jews call him Moshe Rabbeinu, "Moses our Teacher," because everything we learn goes back to what he taught.

## A baby in a basket

Moses was born in Egypt while the Israelites were slaves and Pharaoh had ordered every newborn Hebrew boy thrown into the Nile. His parents, Amram and Yocheved, of the tribe of Levi, already had Miriam and Aaron. When Yocheved could no longer hide the baby, she set him in a waterproof basket among the reeds while Miriam watched from a distance. Pharaoh's daughter found him and raised him in the palace. She named him Moshe, "drawn out" of the water.

## From the palace to the shepherd's field

As a grown man Moses saw the suffering of his people and tried to protect a Hebrew from an Egyptian taskmaster. He had to flee to Midian, where he married Tzipporah, daughter of Jethro, and became a shepherd. At age eighty, near Mount Sinai, he saw a bush that burned and was not consumed. G-d spoke from it and sent him to free the Jewish people. Moses doubted himself, saying he was not a man of words, but he went with his brother Aaron.

## The Exodus and Sinai

After the Ten Plagues Pharaoh let the people go, and Moses led them through the sea. About six weeks later they stood at Mount Sinai and heard G-d speak the [Ten Commandments](/learn/ten-commandments). Moses then spent forty days on the mountain receiving the Torah. When the people made the golden calf, he broke the tablets, prayed for the people, and came down again with a second set. The story of this journey is told in the [Exodus story](/learn/exodus-story) and relived every year at the Passover Seder.

## Forty years of leading

For forty years in the desert Moses cared for the people like a nurse with a baby. He pleaded for them again and again, even when they complained. After the spies frightened the nation about entering the Land, that generation was to live out its years in the desert. Later Moses struck a rock instead of speaking to it, and he was told he would not enter the Land of Israel. On his final days he spoke to the people, which became the Book of Deuteronomy, appointed Joshua to lead, climbed Mount Nebo, saw the Land from afar, and passed away at 120. The place of his burial is unknown.

## The humblest of men

The Torah calls Moses the most humble person on earth. Chassidic teaching explains this simply: Moses never took credit for what he did. He knew G-d had chosen him, and he felt that anyone else given the same gifts would have done even better. Real [humility](/learn/humility) is not thinking little of yourself; it is knowing your gifts are a trust, to be used for others.

## A spark of Moses in every generation

Chassidic and Kabbalistic teaching says every generation has a spark of Moses, a power to carry the Torah forward for that time. It reminds each of us that we receive the [Torah](/learn/torah) from a chain of teachers back to Sinai, and that we each help pass it on. The next prophet in that chain, who reappears in Jewish life at every bris and Seder, is [Elijah](/learn/elijah-the-prophet).

## Common questions

### Who was Moses in the Bible?
Moses was the Jewish people's greatest prophet and leader, who took them out of Egypt, received the Torah at Sinai and led them for forty years in the desert.

### Why is Moses called Moshe Rabbeinu?
Moshe Rabbeinu means "Moses our Teacher." The name expresses that Moses taught the Torah to the whole people, and every Jewish teacher since is a student in his chain.

### Why did Moses not enter the Land of Israel?
The Torah says he struck a rock instead of speaking to it, as G-d had told him. He was allowed to see the Land from Mount Nebo. Ask the Rabbi for the deeper explanations.

### How old was Moses when he died?
He was 120 years old. The Torah says his eye had not dimmed and his strength had not left him.

### Where is Moses buried?
Nobody knows. The Torah says his burial place is unknown to this day.

### Did Moses write the Torah?
Jewish tradition holds that G-d dictated the Torah to Moses and he wrote it down, which is why it is called the Five Books of Moses.
