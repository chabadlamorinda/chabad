A mohel (מוֹהֵל) is a person trained in both the Jewish laws of circumcision and the medical procedure, who performs the bris on a baby boy's eighth day. To choose one, ask about training, experience and how he or she works with parents, and ask your rabbi and your pediatrician for advice.

## What a mohel does

The brit milah (בְּרִית מִילָה) is a religious ceremony, not only a medical one. The mohel performs the circumcision, recites the blessings and guides the family through the ceremony. He also advises on caring for the baby in the days afterward. The mohel is chosen for both skill and knowledge of Jewish law. See [Brit Milah: a simple guide](/learn/brit-milah) for the whole ceremony.

## Questions to ask a mohel

Do not be shy about asking. A good mohel welcomes questions.

1. **How were you trained, and for how long?** Ask about both Jewish law and the medical technique.
2. **How many brises have you performed?** Experience matters.
3. **Can you give references** from other families?
4. **What method and tools do you use?** Chabad.org notes particular rules here, and the Rabbi can tell you what Chabad custom requires. Ask the Rabbi before you decide.
5. **What do you do before and after the bris?** Ask about the baby's health check, feeding before the ceremony, and how you will be told to care for him afterward.
6. **What if the baby is not healthy on the eighth day?** A good mohel will tell you plainly that health comes first. See [when a bris is postponed](/learn/postponing-a-bris).
7. **Are you available on the date and time we need?** The bris is on the eighth day, usually in the morning.
8. **Will you explain the ceremony** to me, to my wife and to our guests?

## Skill first

Chabad.org advises that when you can choose between two mohelim, you choose the one who is highly skilled rather than an elderly mohel who is deeply learned but less skilled. Skill comes first, and learning and piety are a bonus.

## Doctor, mohel and Rabbi

Your pediatrician cares for your baby's health. The mohel performs the bris. The Rabbi advises on Jewish law and the plans for the day. They work together. If you have any health concern, mention it to the mohel and the doctor early. Our guide to [preparing for a bris](/learn/preparing-for-a-bris) has a checklist.

## Common questions

### What is a mohel?
A mohel is a Jewish person trained in the laws of brit milah and in the procedure itself, who performs the circumcision at a bris. A mohel is not necessarily a doctor, and many are trained in both fields.

### How do I find a mohel near Lafayette?
Ask your rabbi, your pediatrician, and other Jewish parents. You can call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 and he will help you find a qualified mohel for your family.

### When should I choose a mohel?
As early as possible, even during the pregnancy, since the bris is only a week after the birth. See [new parents in Lamorinda](/learn/new-parents-in-lamorinda).

### Can a pediatrician or surgeon do the bris?
A bris is a religious act and must be performed by someone who knows Jewish law, which is why a mohel is used. Ask the Rabbi about your specific questions.

### What if the mohel says my baby needs to wait?
Then you wait. The baby's health always comes first, and the bris is held when he is ready.

### Who pays the mohel?
Fees vary from one mohel to another, so ask the mohel directly when you call.
