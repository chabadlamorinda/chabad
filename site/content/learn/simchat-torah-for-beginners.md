Simchat Torah means "Rejoicing with the Torah." It is the day we finish the yearly cycle of Torah reading and begin again, and we celebrate by dancing in circles with the Torah scrolls. You do not need to know any Hebrew: the whole point of the day is that everyone dances.

## What the holiday is

Simchat Torah comes right after [Sukkot](/learn/sukkot-for-beginners), together with [Shemini Atzeret](/learn/shemini-atzeret). Outside Israel they are two days in a row, and the second one is Simchat Torah. In Israel they are a single day.

On Simchat Torah we read the last section of the Torah, which ends with the passing of Moses, and immediately read the first chapter of Genesis. The Torah is never "finished": as soon as the end is reached, the beginning is waiting. Our sages saw this as a lesson that learning is endless.

## Why we dance

Chassidic teaching explains something beautiful about this day. All year we study the Torah, and a scholar knows more than a beginner. On Simchat Torah we dance with the scrolls closed, wrapped in their covers. Nobody is reading, so nobody is ahead. A professor and a child who cannot read yet hold the same Torah and dance in the same circle, because our bond with G-d and His Torah is the same for every Jew. That is why the day feels so joyful and so equal.

## Hakafot: the circles of dancing

The dancing is called [hakafot](/learn/hakafot), meaning "circling." The scrolls are taken out of the ark and carried around the reading table, with singing, and then everyone dances. There are seven rounds. Hakafot take place on the evening of Simchat Torah and again the next morning. Many communities, including Chabad, also hold them on the evening of Shemini Atzeret.

Everyone is invited to carry a scroll for a round. If you have never held a Torah, ask: someone will gladly show you how to hold it, close to your heart.

## What the Torah reading looks like

On the morning of Simchat Torah, every man in the room is called up for an aliyah, and the section is read again and again so that all can join. Children are called up together for a special aliyah, and their blessing is said with an adult beside them. Two honors go to the people who finish the Torah and begin it again. The evening service is mostly the hakafot; the Torah reading is in the morning. Read more about [the Torah reading](/learn/torah-reading).

## Kids, flags and treats

This is the children's holiday. Kids wave flags (often topped with an apple) and sing along, and they receive candy and treats. It is easy, noisy and happy, and it is a perfect first experience of a synagogue for a young family. For ideas, see our [kids' corner](/kids).

## Common questions

### What is Simchat Torah?
It is the Jewish holiday of celebrating the Torah. We complete the yearly Torah reading, begin it again from Genesis, and dance with the scrolls.

### What do you do on Simchat Torah?
We go to synagogue in the evening and morning, dance seven rounds of hakafot with the Torah scrolls, read the end and the beginning of the Torah, and give the children flags and candy.

### Why do we dance with the Torah on Simchat Torah?
Because the joy of the Torah belongs to every Jew equally. The scrolls are closed, so nobody is reading and nobody is more learned than anyone else. We celebrate our simple love for G-d and His Torah.

### What are hakafot?
Hakafot ("circles") are the rounds of singing and dancing with the Torah scrolls around the synagogue's reading table. There are seven.

### What is the difference between Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah?
Shemini Atzeret is the first of the two days, when we recite Yizkor and the prayer for rain. Simchat Torah is the second day, with the dancing, Torah reading and the children's celebration. In Israel both are one day.
