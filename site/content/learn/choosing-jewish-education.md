The best Jewish education for your child is the one that fits your family's time, your child's age and your hopes for Jewish life at home. Options range from a full-time Jewish day school to a once-a-week Hebrew School, an afterschool program or summer camp, and Chabad of Lamorinda in Lafayette offers several of these for families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda.

## What Jewish education is for

The Hebrew word is *chinuch* (חינוך), education. Chabad.org explains that parents have a duty, from the rabbis, to teach children the mitzvahs as soon as each child is able to do them. The Torah places the duty of teaching Torah on the father, who may entrust it to a teacher or school, and the mother often does the most effective teaching. See [Chinuch](/learn/chinuch).

Chabad.org also says the goal is for children to value each mitzvah for itself and for the closeness to G-d it brings, not just for rewards. That is a useful test for any program: will my child come away loving being Jewish?

Chabad.org notes that this learning begins early: blessings and simple prayers around age three, and short Torah verses and the Shema as soon as a child can speak.

## The main formats

There is no single right answer. Think about these in turn:

- **Full-time day school.** Jewish learning every school day, alongside general studies. It is the most immersive, and usually the largest commitment of time and money.
- **Weekly Hebrew School.** A few hours a week, usually on a Sunday. It builds Hebrew reading, holidays and traditions, and leads toward bar or bat mitzvah.
- **Afterschool.** Jewish activities folded into the school week, with homework help and a meal.
- **Preschool.** A first Jewish classroom, where songs, Shabbat and holidays are part of the day. See [choosing a Jewish preschool](/learn/choosing-a-jewish-preschool).
- **Summer camp.** Informal, joyful learning. Chabad.org calls camp especially valuable for children who do not attend a Jewish school during the year.

## Questions to ask about any program

1. **How many hours a week, and what is the schedule?** Be honest about what your family can keep up.
2. **What will my child learn?** Hebrew reading, holidays, Jewish values, Torah stories.
3. **What does it cost, and is help available?** Ask about payment plans and scholarships.
4. **Who are the teachers?** Meet them if you can.
5. **What happens at home?** Education works best when Shabbat, blessings and holidays are also lived in the family. See [Shabbat in Lamorinda](/learn/shabbat-in-lamorinda).

## Common questions

### How do I choose between Hebrew School and Jewish day school?
Choose by time, goals and budget: day school offers daily immersion, while Hebrew School offers a weekly Sunday program that fits alongside regular school. Talk it through with the Rabbi, who can help you weigh it for your child.

### Is Hebrew School worth it?
A good one gives children Hebrew reading, a sense of belonging and a path toward bar or bat mitzvah. Its value grows when the family also keeps Jewish traditions at home.

### What does chinuch mean?
*Chinuch* means education, and in Jewish law it is the parents' duty to teach children mitzvahs as they become able to do them.
