The Four Kinds are a lulav (palm branch), an etrog (citron), hadassim (myrtle branches) and aravot (willow branches), held together and waved each day of Sukkot. It is one of the most beautiful and joyful mitzvot of the year.

## What are the Four Kinds?

The Torah says: "On the first day you shall take for yourselves the fruit of a beautiful tree, branches of date palms, a branch of a thick-leaved tree, and willows of the brook, and you shall rejoice before G-d" (Leviticus 23:40). The Sages explain these as:

- **Etrog:** a citron, a fragrant yellow citrus fruit.
- **Lulav:** a closed frond of a date palm.
- **Hadassim:** three myrtle branches.
- **Aravot:** two willow branches.

The lulav, hadassim and aravot are bound together. The etrog is held separately.

## What the Four Kinds mean

The Midrash compares the four species to four kinds of Jews:

- The **etrog** has taste and fragrance, like a person with both Torah learning and good deeds.
- The **lulav** (date palm) has taste but no fragrance: learning without deeds.
- The **hadas** has fragrance but no taste: good deeds without learning.
- The **aravah** has neither.

On Sukkot we bind them all together. Every Jew is essential, and together we are complete.

## How to assemble the set

1. Hold the lulav with its spine (the center rib) facing you.
2. Place the three hadassim on the right side of the lulav and the two aravot on the left. The hadassim should be slightly higher than the aravot.
3. Bind them together with the holder made of palm leaves (koishiklach). Chabad custom is to add rings of palm leaf along the lulav as well.

## How to shake the lulav, step by step

1. **Hold the lulav** in your right hand, spine facing you. A left-handed person holds it in the left hand.
2. **Say the blessing** (below). On the first day you take the Four Kinds, also say Shehecheyanu.
3. **Pick up the etrog** in your other hand, right side up, with the stem end facing down. (Chabad custom is to pick up the etrog after the blessing. Many others hold it upside down during the blessing and then turn it over.)
4. **Bring the two hands together** so the etrog touches the lulav.
5. **Wave in six directions.** Extend and draw back the set three times in each direction. Chabad order: south (right), north (left), east (forward), up, down, and west (behind you).

## The blessing

**Hebrew:** בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו, וְצִוָּנוּ עַל נְטִילַת לוּלָב

**Transliteration:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu al netilat lulav.

**Translation:** Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us concerning taking the lulav.

## When to shake

The Four Kinds are taken every day of Sukkot except Shabbat, during the daytime. Many shake them in the morning before prayers, and again during Hallel in synagogue. They are not used on Shemini Atzeret or Simchat Torah.

## Choosing a kosher set

The etrog should be whole, clean and unblemished, especially on its upper part, with no missing tip if it grew with one. The lulav should be straight and closed, with its middle leaf not split. Myrtles should have leaves growing in groups of three. Willows should have long, smooth-edged leaves. Buying from a reliable source makes this much easier.

## Common questions

### Can women shake the lulav?
Women are not obligated, since it is a time-bound mitzvah, but many women do it and it is very meaningful. Ask the Rabbi about saying the blessing according to your custom.

### Why is the lulav not shaken on Shabbat?
The Sages were concerned that someone might carry the lulav in a public area to learn how to use it, which is forbidden on Shabbat.

### Can I use someone else's set?
Yes. On the first day, the set must belong to you, so the owner gives it to you as a gift, and you may give it back. On the other days, borrowing is fine.

### What do I do with my lulav after Sukkot?
Many keep the aravot or use the lulav to help burn chametz before Passover. Since it was used for a mitzvah, it should not be thrown in the trash casually.
