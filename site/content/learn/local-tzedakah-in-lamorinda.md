According to Jewish law, a person's first duty in giving tzedakah (charity, literally "justice") is to family in need, then to the poor of their own city, and only then to causes elsewhere. In a comfortable suburb like Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda, "your city" means the neighbors, seniors and families nearby, whose need is often quiet and unseen.

## What Jewish law says about priority

Maimonides, in his Laws of Gifts to the Poor, as presented on Chabad.org, sets the order. A poor relative comes before everyone else. The poor of your own household come before the poor of your city, and the poor of your own city come before the poor of another city. He adds that the poor of the Land of Israel come before those of other places, but not before the poor of your own city. If a person gives to "the poor" without saying where, the money goes to the poor of their own city.

Chabad.org's overview of tzedakah describes the same idea as concentric circles: family first, then local needs and institutions, then the wider world. Tzedakah also means giving a tenth of your income, called maaser, and those who can aim for a fifth. Money is understood as a trust from G-d, given to us to share.

This is the general rule, and it is not a command to ignore far-away suffering. Real life is full of exceptions, so for your own situation, ask the Rabbi. Read more in [tzedakah priorities](/learn/tzedakah-priorities) and [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).

## What "your city" means in a quiet suburb

Need in Lamorinda rarely looks like need. It can be a neighbor who has lost a job, a family dealing with a long illness, a senior living alone on a fixed income, or a household that suffered a fire or flood. People are proud and private, and they may not ask. That is why Jewish teaching values giving that is discreet, and why checking in on people is itself tzedakah.

Here are ways that need can hide in a comfortable neighborhood, and what to do:

- **A neighbor after a job loss or illness.** A simple meal, a ride, or an anonymous gift helps without embarrassment.
- **A senior who is alone.** A weekly call or visit can matter more than money. See [Jewish seniors in Lamorinda](/learn/jewish-seniors-in-lamorinda).
- **Families after a disaster.** Ask what they actually need before giving. Follow official guidance from the fire district and local agencies on how to help.
- **Local Jewish life.** Programs for children, classes, Shabbat meals and holiday celebrations all depend on neighbors who give.

## Ways to give close to home

Here are some ways to begin, using only what already exists at Chabad of Lamorinda:

1. **Maot chitim before Passover.** Literally "money for wheat," this is the old custom of making sure that every Jewish family can afford the holiday. See [maot chitim](/learn/maot-chitim), and call 925-384-7242 to hear what is planned.
2. **A gemach.** A gemach (גמ"ח) is an interest-free loan fund, a quiet way to help neighbors in a bind. See [gemach interest-free loans](/learn/gemach-interest-free-loans).
3. **The Chai Club.** Monthly partners help support the Jewish programs of Lamorinda. See the [Chai Club](/chai-club).
4. **Sponsoring a Kiddush.** A Kiddush after Shabbat services can be sponsored in honor of a birthday or in memory of a loved one.
5. **Volunteering.** Time is tzedakah too. See the [volunteer form](/forms/volunteer).
6. **A pushka at home.** A small charity box in the kitchen, where children place a coin before Shabbat, teaches giving every week. See [pushka](/learn/pushka).
7. **A gift to Chabad of Lamorinda.** If you are moved to give, the page is [donate](/donate). There is never any pressure.

## Why start small, and start at home

Chabad teaches that tzedakah is not only about the amount; it is about turning a person's heart outward. A child who learns to give a coin before Shabbat becomes an adult who looks around. Start with one household, one family, one habit.

Many Jews give a small amount of tzedakah every day, and you will find more in [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah). For how Chabad of Lamorinda is supported, see [supporting Chabad of Lamorinda](/learn/supporting-chabad-of-lamorinda).

## Common questions

### Who should I give tzedakah to first?
According to Maimonides, a poor relative comes first, then the poor of your own household and city, and then others. Ask the Rabbi about your own situation.

### What does "the poor of your city come first" mean?
It means that the needy people closest to you take priority over those far away. In Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, that means the neighbors and families around you.

### How much tzedakah should I give?
Chabad.org describes giving a tenth of income as the proper amount, with a fifth for those who are able. Speak to the Rabbi about how to start.

### What is maot chitim?
Maot chitim (מעות חיטים), "money for wheat," is the custom of giving so that every Jewish family can celebrate Passover. See [maot chitim](/learn/maot-chitim).

### Can I give tzedakah anonymously?
Yes, and giving discreetly is a highly praised form of tzedakah. See [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) and ask the Rabbi if you wish.

### How can I help a neighbor who will not ask for help?
Offer something concrete, like a meal or a ride, and respect their privacy. The Rabbi can advise on discreet help at 925-384-7242.
