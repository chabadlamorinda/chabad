Jews do not believe Jesus was the Messiah because, according to the Torah and the prophets, the Messiah (Moshiach) must complete specific things that have not yet happened, such as bringing lasting peace to the world, and because Judaism teaches that G-d is One and is never a human being. This is a difference of belief, not a judgment on anyone: Judaism does not ask Christians to become Jewish.

## What Judaism expects of Moshiach

Moshiach (מָשִׁיחַ) means "anointed one," a human king from the family of King David. Maimonides (the Rambam) sums up the Jewish tradition in his Laws of Kings: the Messiah will be a person who follows the Torah, rebuilds the Holy Temple in Jerusalem, gathers Jews from the corners of the earth, and brings the whole world to know G-d. The prophets describe the result: "Nation shall not lift up sword against nation, and they shall no longer learn war" (Isaiah 2:4).

The Jewish view is simple: these things have not happened yet, so Moshiach has not yet come. Read the [Moshiach guide](/learn/moshiach) for the full picture, and why we still wait with hope every day.

## G-d is One, and never a person

The heart of Judaism is the [Shema](/learn/shema): "Hear, O Israel, the L-rd our G-d, the L-rd is One." The Rambam's Thirteen Principles of Faith teach that G-d has no body and no form, and that we pray to G-d alone, with no go-between. So Judaism cannot accept the idea that G-d became a human, or that G-d is divided into parts. This is the deepest reason, more than any single verse. See [belief in G-d](/learn/belief-in-g-d) for how Jews think about the Creator.

## Why Jews read the verses differently

Christians often point to verses in the Hebrew Bible that they see as describing Jesus. Jewish tradition reads these passages in their own context, in the original Hebrew, and understands them differently. For example, the "suffering servant" in Isaiah is explained in Jewish tradition as the people of Israel. Each verse deserves a patient, unhurried conversation, not a quick text message. That is why we invite questions rather than argue them here.

## Respect for Christian neighbors

Jewish teaching holds that the righteous of all nations have a share in the World to Come, and that non-Jews are asked to follow the [Seven Noahide Laws](/learn/noahide-laws), not to convert. Judaism does not seek converts. Christians in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda are our neighbors, friends and often family. Many Jewish families in the East Bay and across the Bay Area celebrate with Christian relatives; see [Jewish in December](/learn/jewish-in-december) for gentle ideas about the season.

## Common questions

### Do Jews believe in Jesus?
Jews respect that Jesus was a historical Jew, but do not believe he was the Messiah or G-d. Judaism holds that Moshiach has not yet come and that G-d is not a human being.

### Why don't Jews believe Jesus was the Messiah?
Because the Messiah, in Jewish tradition, is a human king who brings world peace and knowledge of G-d and rebuilds the Temple, and none of that has happened yet.

### What do Jews think of Jesus?
There is no single official Jewish teaching about his personality. Jews generally see him as a Jew of the first century who is not the Messiah, and ask for respect in how this is discussed.

### What is the Jewish view of the Messiah?
He will be a righteous, Torah-observant descendant of King David who ushers in an age of peace, as Maimonides describes. See [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach).

### Do Jews celebrate Christmas?
No. It is a Christian holiday, but many Jewish families spend it with loved ones. Ask the Rabbi how your family can respect both traditions.
