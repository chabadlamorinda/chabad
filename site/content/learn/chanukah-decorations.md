The most important Chanukah decoration in a Jewish home is the menorah itself, lit each night where others can see it. Everything else, from handmade wrapping paper to blue and white colors, is a joyful extra that makes the home feel like Chanukah.

In Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, a menorah glowing in a doorway or window tells the neighborhood that this is a Jewish home celebrating the miracle of light.

## The menorah is the real decoration

Chanukah's central mitzvah is to light the menorah and publicize the miracle. Chabad.org teaches that the menorah is best lit in the doorway, outdoors or in a window facing the street, so passersby can see it. If that is not possible, it may be lit in the doorway of the room the family uses most. The Chabad custom is to place it in an interior doorway opposite the mezuzah, so you pass between two mitzvahs. If you place it in a window facing the street, use a menorah without a back plate so the lights can be seen from the inside and the street. Read more in [the Chanukah menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah) and [a menorah at home in Lamorinda](/learn/menorah-at-home-in-lamorinda).

Keep the display safe: use a sturdy, fireproof surface, out of children's reach, away from curtains and anything flammable. Electric menorahs are fine for display only, but they do not fulfill the mitzvah. A real flame menorah must also be lit, as Chabad.org explains.

## Decorate with Jewish symbols

Jewish symbols are natural decorations for the holiday: the menorah, the dreidel with its Hebrew letters, and the Hebrew letters themselves. To learn what the symbols you already own mean, read [Jewish symbols](/learn/jewish-symbols). Any candle color is acceptable for the menorah, according to Chabad.org, and blue and white are popular because they match Israel's colors.

## Craft your own

Chabad.org's kids' pages offer craft ideas such as making your own dreidel, crafting a unique menorah, a Chanukah apron and coloring pages. A favorite is homemade wrapping paper, which you can also use for napkins, an apron, table cards or a tablecloth. Here is how Chabad.org describes it:

1. **Gather** Chanukah-shaped metal cookie cutters, a sharp knife, a large russet or sweet potato, paper towels, acrylic paint (fabric paint works better for fabric), a metal tray or paper plate for the paint, and paper or fabric to print on.
2. **Cut and stamp the shape.** Cut the potato in half and press a cookie cutter into the cut side at least half an inch deep.
3. **Carve the design.** Use a paring knife to cut away the potato around the cutter's outline, leaving the shape raised. Pat the stamp dry.
4. **Print.** Dip the stamp in paint and press it onto your paper or fabric.

Chabad.org suggests buying a few extra potatoes when you shop for latke ingredients. Craft menorahs and decorations are for display; the mitzvah uses real lights in a straight row. See more in [Chanukah crafts for kids](/learn/chanukah-crafts-for-kids).

## Decorations that give

Chabad.org suggests visiting hospitals and nursing homes with menorahs, treats and handmade cards, especially for people without family nearby, and bringing children along. For help with visits, see [Chanukah in the hospital](/learn/chanukah-in-the-hospital).

## Common questions

### What are traditional Chanukah decorations?
The menorah is the central one. Dreidels and Hebrew letters are also popular symbols, and many homes use blue and white because they match Israel's colors.

### Can I put my menorah in the window?
Yes. If you put it in a window facing the street, Chabad.org says to use a menorah without a back plate so the lights can be seen from the street. Chabad's own custom is an interior doorway opposite the mezuzah.

### Is an electric menorah a good decoration?
Electric menorahs are acceptable for display only. A real flame menorah must also be lit, since electric lights do not fulfill the mitzvah.

### What can kids make for Chanukah?
They can make a dreidel, a menorah craft, an apron, coloring pages or potato-stamp wrapping paper. See [Chanukah crafts for kids](/learn/chanukah-crafts-for-kids).

### Are there any rules for the height of a window menorah?
Chabad.org says a window menorah should be no higher than 20 cubits, about 30 feet, above street level, so it can be seen from the street.
