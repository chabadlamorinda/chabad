Hallel (הַלֵּל, "praise") is a collection of Psalms 113 to 118, sung in the morning service on Jewish holidays and on Rosh Chodesh, the new moon. It is a song of thanks to G-d for the Exodus from Egypt and for the miracles in every generation, and it comes in two versions: full Hallel and half Hallel.

## What is Hallel?

The six chapters of Tehillim (Psalms) that make up Hallel praise G-d for taking the Jewish people out of Egypt, which is why it is also called "Egyptian Hallel." On the days it is said, it is sung joyfully, and many of the psalms have traditional tunes. It follows the Amidah, the central silent prayer; see [the Amidah](/learn/amidah) and the [Shacharit morning service](/learn/shacharit-morning-service).

Hallel is preceded by a blessing in which we thank G-d for making us holy and commanding us to say it, and followed by a closing blessing that ends "Blessed are You, G-d, King who is extolled with praises." The final verses, from Psalm 118:21, are repeated twice.

## Which days have full Hallel?

According to Chabad.org, full Hallel, the entire six psalms, is said on:

- **Sukkot**, every day (and Shemini Atzeret). On Sukkot the Four Kinds are held during Hallel and the lulav is waved in six directions at four points; see [the four kinds](/learn/four-kinds).
- **Chanukah**, every day. Work is allowed and it is not a biblical festival, but Hallel marks the miracles of Chanukah. See [al hanissim and Hallel](/learn/al-hanissim-and-hallel).
- **Passover**, the first day (the first two days outside Israel).
- **Shavuot**.

Together with Sukkot, Passover and Shavuot, these are the [three pilgrimage festivals](/learn/shalosh-regalim) when the Torah calls for rejoicing.

## Which days have half Hallel?

Half Hallel, where two paragraphs are left out, is said on **Rosh Chodesh** and on the **remaining days of Passover**. Chabad.org describes it as "more like 85 percent" of the whole. The customary skips are the first half of Psalm 115 and the first half of Psalm 116, since their themes repeat in the second halves.

During half Hallel, the blessings are recited by the prayer leader, and the congregation answers "Amen." Rosh Chodesh is a day the Torah calls a festival (a mo'ed), but work is not forbidden, so the joy is a bit more restrained. Learn more in [Rosh Chodesh](/learn/rosh-chodesh).

Chabad.org gives reasons why Passover's last days get half Hallel while every day of Sukkot gets full Hallel. On Sukkot each day had a different offering in the Temple, so each day is its own holiday. On Passover the same offering was brought daily, and the Midrash says the final days recall the Egyptians drowning at the Red Sea, when G-d said it was not a time for full joy.

## Days without Hallel

Hallel is not said on Rosh Hashanah or Yom Kippur. The Talmud says G-d told the angels it is not fitting for Israel to sing while the books of life and death are open. It is also not said on Shabbat alone, or on Purim. For Purim, one reason given is that reading the Megillah is itself a kind of Hallel.

## Hallel at the Seder

On Passover night, part of Hallel is recited within the Haggadah, and the rest after the Grace After Meals. No blessing is said before this Hallel. The second and fourth cups of wine are raised during it, and "the Great Hallel" (Psalm 136) is also recited. See [the Passover Seder](/learn/passover-seder).

## Common questions

### What is Hallel in Judaism?
Hallel is a set of six psalms, 113 to 118, of praise and thanks, said in the morning service on Jewish holidays and Rosh Chodesh.

### What is the difference between full Hallel and half Hallel?
Full Hallel includes all the psalms; half Hallel leaves out two paragraphs, as on Rosh Chodesh and the later days of Passover.

### Is Hallel said on Rosh Chodesh?
Yes, half Hallel is said on Rosh Chodesh, and only the prayer leader recites the blessings.

### Why is Hallel not said on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur?
Because these are solemn days of judgment and repentance, and the joyful tone of Hallel does not fit them.

### Is Hallel said on Chanukah?
Yes, full Hallel is said on every day of Chanukah.
