No: according to the Tanya, the animal soul is not simply bad. It is your natural self, with energy and drive, and it also has good traits such as compassion and kindness. The goal is not to crush it but to turn its power toward good.

This changes how many people see their own struggle. You do not have to hate the part of you that wants things.

## What Chapter 1 says

In Chapter 1 the Tanya describes the animal soul and the pulls it can bring, such as anger, pride, love of pleasure and laziness. Then it says that the Jewish animal soul also produces good natural traits, such as compassion and kindness. As the Tanya explains in this chapter, this is because it comes from kelipat nogah, a realm that contains good. See [the glossary](/learn/tanya-glossary-for-beginners) and [the two souls](/learn/the-two-souls-explained).

## Raising up the natural

Chapter 37 of the Tanya goes further. It teaches that when you use physical things for a mitzvah, they are raised into holiness. The parchment of tefillin, an etrog and money given to tzedakah are examples. Things connected with a sin, like stolen money, cannot be raised in this way.

The same chapter says the animal soul itself is raised when it is used for a mitzvah:

- **Speech:** when you speak words of Torah or prayer aloud, you use the body and the animal soul, and so bring that energy into holiness.
- **Food and drink:** the energy that comes from food is raised when it is used for mitzvot. See [eating as a mitzvah](/learn/eating-as-a-mitzvah).
- **Charity:** the chapter says tzedakah is equal to all the other mitzvot together, because the giver's entire energy of earning goes into it. See [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).

The chapter also says the soul's purpose in coming down into the world is to refine the body and the animal soul, and to unite them with G-d. See [what is holiness?](/learn/what-is-holiness).

## So what do I do with my desires?

The beinoni of Chapter 12 still feels the pull of the animal soul, but chooses what to do. Chassidic teaching explains that this struggle is the point, and that old temptations lose their pull while new ones appear. See [the beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni) and [mind over heart](/learn/mind-over-heart-tanya).

This does not mean that every desire is fine to follow. Case by case, ask the Rabbi.

## A note

This is spiritual guidance. If a drive such as eating, spending or anger feels out of control, please also speak with a professional.

## Common questions

### Is the animal soul evil in Tanya?
No. Chapter 1 says it also has good natural traits, such as compassion and kindness, and Chapter 37 says it can be raised up through mitzvot.

### What is kelipat nogah?
It is a mixed realm of good and evil, which contains good. Tanya says the Jewish animal soul comes from it, and that physical things from this realm can be raised through a mitzvah.

### Should I try to destroy my natural desires?
The Tanya's aim is to refine and raise the animal soul, not to crush it. For personal questions, ask the Rabbi.

### How can eating or working be holy?
Chapter 37 teaches that the energy gained from food and drink is raised when it is used for mitzvot. See [eating as a mitzvah](/learn/eating-as-a-mitzvah).

### Does feeling temptation mean I am bad?
No. The beinoni still feels it and chooses what to do. See [the beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni).
