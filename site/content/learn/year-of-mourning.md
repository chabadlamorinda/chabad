When a parent passes away, Jewish tradition sets aside twelve months of mourning. During this year the child says Kaddish, gives tzedakah and does good deeds in the parent's memory, while gently limiting celebrations.

## Why a full year for a parent?

Honoring parents is one of the Ten Commandments, and the obligation continues after their passing. The year of mourning, called the *yud-beis chodesh* (twelve months), is the child's way of honoring a father or mother more deeply than any other relative.

For other close relatives (spouse, sibling or child), formal mourning ends after [sheloshim](/learn/sheloshim), the first thirty days.

## How the year is counted

The twelve months are counted by the Hebrew calendar from the day of death. In a Jewish leap year, which has thirteen months, mourning still lasts twelve months. The first [yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit), the anniversary of the passing on the Hebrew calendar, comes after that. Our [yahrzeit tool](/tools/yahrzeit) can help you find the dates.

## Saying Kaddish for eleven months

The mourner's [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish) is the most well-known part of the year. It is said at prayer services with a minyan. It praises G-d and brings merit and elevation to the soul of the parent.

Kaddish is said for eleven months, not twelve. Tradition teaches that the soul's purification after death lasts up to twelve months, and that only the very wicked need the full time. Stopping after eleven months shows that we think of our parent as a good person.

If you cannot attend a minyan every day, someone else can say Kaddish on your behalf. Chabad of Lamorinda can help [arrange Kaddish](/kaddish).

## Customs during the year

Mourners for a parent traditionally:

- **Avoid parties and celebrations** for the full twelve months, especially those with live music and dancing.
- **Limit attending weddings and festive meals.** Mourners can often attend a family wedding with some limits. Ask the Rabbi.
- **Do not buy notable new clothing** for a period, according to custom.
- **Lead prayer services** when possible, which is considered a special honor for the parent's soul.

## Good deeds for the soul

The year is about more than restrictions. Each good deed you do in your parent's memory brings their soul higher. Some ideas:

- Give [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) regularly in their name.
- Study Torah, especially Mishnah, in their memory.
- Take on a new mitzvah in their honor.
- Do acts of kindness they would have loved.

## The headstone

Many families set up the headstone and hold an [unveiling](/learn/unveiling) during the first year, often near the end of it. Customs vary, and the Rabbi can help you decide.

## Common questions

### Do daughters say Kaddish?
Kaddish is traditionally said by sons. Daughters honor their parent in many powerful ways during the year, including tzedakah, Torah study, prayer and good deeds, and can arrange for Kaddish to be said. Speak with the Rabbi about meaningful options.

### When exactly does Kaddish end?
Kaddish ends a little before the end of the eleventh month. Since the exact day depends on the date of death, ask the Rabbi or call us and we will work it out with you.

### Can I go to a bar mitzvah or wedding during the year?
Often you can attend part of it, such as the ceremony, while avoiding some of the festivities. Every case is different. Ask the Rabbi.

### What happens after the year ends?
Mourning customs end, and the family marks the yahrzeit each year by lighting a candle, saying Kaddish and giving tzedakah. Yizkor is also said four times a year.
