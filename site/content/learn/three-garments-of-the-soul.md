In the Tanya, the three garments of the soul are thought, speech and action. Chapter 4 says that, like clothes, they can be put on or taken off, and that the soul uses them to express itself in the world.

This simple picture explains why your small daily choices matter: they are what your soul wears.

## What are the three garments?

According to Chabad.org's page on Chapter 4, every divine soul has three garments:

- **Thought:** for the divine soul, this is grasping Torah as much as one can, on the levels of plain meaning, hint, interpretation and the hidden.
- **Speech:** Torah study aloud, which explains the mitzvot and their laws.
- **Action:** doing the mitzvot, such as putting on tefillin.

The chapter says that when a person engages in all three, the soul is "clothed" and its intellect and emotions find expression. Omit a mitzvah, and its place in the soul goes without a garment. See [what is a mitzvah](/learn/what-is-a-mitzvah).

## Why "clothing"?

A Chabad.org article by Tzvi Freeman makes the image come alive. Clothes reveal a person's inner self, and they also shape the wearer: dress as an officer and you become more orderly, dress in track pants and you relax. Thoughts and feelings become real, the article says, when you put them into words or deeds. It quotes the Baal Shem Tov: "Wherever you put your thoughts, that is where you are, all of you."

## Higher than the soul?

Chapter 4 says something surprising: these garments are higher than the soul itself. The reason is that Torah and G-d are one, so when you learn Torah or do a mitzvah, you connect to G-d Himself. The chapter explains that G-d "compressed" His infinite wisdom into the mitzvot and the Torah so that people could take hold of it, and compares this to an embrace: it makes no difference whether the king wears one robe or many, as his body is within.

## What this means for you

The article on the garments says that a beinoni's task is to master his clothing, meaning his words and deeds, even when feelings still stir. Having an angry feeling is not failure; speaking in anger, or deliberately dwelling on negative thoughts, is the thing to avoid. When you stumble, it says, acknowledge it, express regret to G-d, resolve to continue and move on.

You can also change what you wear in the moment: choose a kind word, a mitzvah, a few minutes of Torah, even when your mood is low. See [mind over heart](/learn/mind-over-heart-tanya), [the beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni) and [lashon hara](/learn/lashon-hara) on the power of speech.

## Common questions

### What are the three garments of the soul?
Thought, speech and action. Chapter 4 of the Tanya says the divine soul uses them to express itself, and they are used in Torah and mitzvot.

### Why does the Tanya call them garments?
Because, like clothes, they can be put on and taken off, and they express the soul.

### Can I change my thoughts, words and actions even if I cannot change my feelings?
The Tanya teaches that this is the choice open to every person. See [mind over heart](/learn/mind-over-heart-tanya).

### How does a mitzvah connect me to G-d?
Chapter 4 says that Torah and G-d are one, so performing mitzvot and learning Torah connect the soul to G-d.

### What is meant by the thought garment?
For the divine soul, it is understanding Torah as far as one can. Chabad.org's article uses the Hebrew word machshavah for the inner "thought" of ideas and feelings.
