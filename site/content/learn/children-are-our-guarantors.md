When G-d was about to give the Torah at Mount Sinai, the Midrash says, He asked the Jewish people for a guarantee that they would keep it. The answer He accepted was: "Our children will be our guarantors." Ever since, Jewish education has come first, because the future of the Jewish people rests on what our children learn.

## The story from the Midrash

The Midrash (Shir HaShirim Rabbah 1:4) tells that at Sinai, G-d said to the Jewish people: "I am giving you My Torah. Bring Me good guarantors that you will keep it."

They said: "Our forefathers, Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, will be our guarantors." G-d answered: "Your forefathers need guarantors themselves."

They said: "Our prophets will be our guarantors." G-d answered: "The prophets too need guarantors."

Then they said: "Our children will be our guarantors." And G-d said: "These are surely good guarantors. For their sake I will give it to you."

## Why children?

The forefathers were great, but they belonged to the past. The prophets were great, but they were few. Children are the future. A tradition lives on only when it is handed to the next generation, and when children learn Torah, the Torah is guaranteed for another generation.

There is a second lesson here. G-d did not ask for scholars or for perfect people. He asked for children, just as they are: curious, energetic and still learning. Every child, from every kind of Jewish home, is part of that guarantee.

## What it means for parents today

**Jewish education is not an extra.** In a full week of school, sports and activities, Jewish learning can feel like one more thing. The Midrash says it is the thing on which everything else depends.

**Start early.** The Talmud says that as soon as a child can speak, a parent teaches the verse "Torah tzivah lanu Moshe", "The Torah that Moses commanded us is the inheritance of the congregation of Jacob." The Torah already belongs to your child; teaching it is handing over an inheritance. See [chinuch](/learn/chinuch).

**Make it joyful.** A guarantor who loves the Torah keeps it gladly. Children who meet Torah through songs, games, crafts and celebrations grow up wanting more. See [the benefits of Torah study for children](/learn/benefits-of-torah-study-for-children).

**Do not wait until you know enough.** Many parents in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda did not grow up with much Jewish learning. That is fine. Your child's learning can be the start of the family's learning, and many parents learn alongside their children.

## Children at Sinai, children today

The Lubavitcher Rebbe often spoke of this Midrash and urged that Jewish children be gathered to learn Torah, especially before Shavuot, so that they could stand again "at Sinai" for the reading of the Ten Commandments. He also asked that every child learn [twelve Torah verses](/learn/twelve-torah-verses) by heart. To the Rebbe, every Jewish child was a guarantor of the whole Jewish people, and no child was to be left out.

## Common questions

### Where does "our children are our guarantors" come from?
From the Midrash on the Song of Songs (Shir HaShirim Rabbah 1:4), which tells how G-d accepted the Jewish children as guarantors before giving the Torah at Sinai.

### Why are children better guarantors than the forefathers?
The forefathers and the prophets belonged to their own times. Children carry the Torah forward. When they learn it, the Torah is assured for another generation.

### Should children come to synagogue on Shavuot?
Yes. The Rebbe encouraged bringing every child, even babies, to hear the Ten Commandments on Shavuot, as at Sinai. Call 925-384-7242 for the time at Chabad of Lamorinda.

### What can I do if I never learned much myself?
Start with your child. Enroll them in Hebrew school, ask them what they learned, and learn alongside them. Ask the Rabbi for a class for yourself too.
