Gefilte fish is a traditional Jewish dish of ground fish mixed with onion and egg, shaped into balls or a loaf, simmered, and served cold with horseradish. It is a classic first course at Shabbat and holiday meals in Jewish homes in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda and around the world.

## What is gefilte fish?

"Gefilte" (געפילטע) is Yiddish for "stuffed." The dish is made from ground fish, onions, eggs, salt and sugar. It is served cold, with a slice of cooked carrot on top and chrein (חריין), a sharp horseradish and beet relish, on the side. Chabad.org calls it a dish some people consider the quintessential Jewish food. It is a fish dish, so the fish must be kosher; see our guide to [kosher fish](/learn/kosher-fish).

## Why on Shabbat?

Fish is a long-standing food of the Shabbat table, and many Jewish families make gefilte fish for Friday night or Shabbat lunch. People give a number of explanations for the custom, and the Chabad.org recipe we read does not give one, so ask the Rabbi if you would like to hear more. For the full picture of the meal, see [Shabbat meals](/learn/shabbat-meals) and [Jewish foods](/learn/jewish-foods).

## Homemade gefilte fish, adapted from Chabad.org

This is retold in our own words from a Chabad.org recipe. It makes about 60 fish balls, and it halves easily.

**Fish mixture**
- 5 pounds ground fish (the recipe uses a mix of pike and whitefish)
- 2 medium onions, peeled and ground
- 2 tablespoons kosher salt
- 5 eggs
- 3 tablespoons water
- 1 1/2 cups sugar

**Cooking stock**
- 16 cups water
- 4 teaspoons kosher salt
- 1/2 cup sugar
- 2 onions and 2 carrots, peeled and cut into chunks

Use kosher ingredients, and ask the Rabbi about specifics.

**Steps**
1. Bring the stock ingredients to a boil in a big, sturdy pot.
2. Mix the fish ingredients. The mixture should be soft but firm enough to roll into balls.
3. When the stock is boiling hard, gently drop in the fish balls, a few at a time. Wet your hands now and then to make rolling easier.
4. When all the balls are in, lower the heat and simmer for one hour.
5. Lift out the fish and carrots and let them cool. Throw out the liquid.
6. Refrigerate for up to 3 days, or freeze for later.

**Matzah meal:** this recipe has none, since many people do not wet matzah on Passover. Outside Passover, or if you do not keep that custom, you can fold in about half a cup of matzah meal, a little at a time, to firm the mixture.

## Serving, and a Passover note

Serve it chilled, with the carrot and chrein. Because the Chabad.org version has no matzah meal, it is made to suit Passover, and families often prepare it ahead for the Seder. See the [Passover shopping list](/learn/passover-shopping-list) and [Passover](/holidays/passover). Whether you may eat certain ingredients on Passover is a question for the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### What is gefilte fish?
Gefilte fish is ground fish mixed with onion and egg, formed into balls or a loaf, and simmered. It is served cold with horseradish.

### What does gefilte mean?
Gefilte is Yiddish for "stuffed." Ask the Rabbi if you would like more about the name.

### Why do Jews eat gefilte fish on Shabbat?
Fish is a traditional food of Shabbat and holiday meals. Explanations differ, so ask the Rabbi.

### Is gefilte fish kosher?
It is kosher only if the fish and all ingredients are kosher. See [kosher fish](/learn/kosher-fish), and ask the Rabbi about specifics.

### Can I make gefilte fish ahead of time?
Yes. It keeps in the refrigerator for up to 3 days, and it freezes well.

### What do you eat gefilte fish with?
Traditionally with chrein, a horseradish and beet relish, and a piece of cooked carrot.
