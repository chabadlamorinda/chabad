Yes, you can start keeping Jewish traditions at any age, and Jewish teaching says it is never too late: the Lubavitcher Rebbe taught that every additional day of life carries a mission, and that later years are an especially good time for learning. Whether you are 50, 70 or 90, any mitzvah you add is a real and complete act, and you can begin with just one.

## Why later in life can be a good time

The Rebbe taught that the Torah has no concept of retirement from purpose. In his talks on the elderly, he pointed out that people who are freed from the pressures of business may have more time and calm for Torah study, and he saw this as a gift. He also said that wisdom grows with years, and that older people can become mentors for the young. So a person who comes to Jewish practice later in life brings something special: life experience, and a deep wish to understand.

Read more in [aging with purpose](/learn/aging-with-purpose) and [a Jewish view of retirement](/learn/retirement-jewish-view).

## Rabbi Akiva started at forty

The Sages tell that Rabbi Akiva, one of the greatest sages, was an unlearned shepherd who began to study Torah at the age of 40, and went on to teach thousands. His story is told in [Rabbi Akiva](/learn/rabbi-akiva). You can also read about [a Bar Mitzvah at 83](/learn/bar-mitzvah-at-83) and about [adult Bar and Bat Mitzvah](/learn/adult-bar-bat-mitzvah).

## Simple first steps

You do not have to take on everything. Chabad teaching encourages us to add one mitzvah at a time. Here are some friendly places to begin:

1. **Learn your Hebrew name.** See [getting a Hebrew name as an adult](/learn/getting-a-hebrew-name-as-an-adult).
2. **Light Shabbat candles.** See [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) and [Shabbat for beginners](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners).
3. **Put up a kosher mezuzah.** See [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah), or ask about [mezuzah and tefillin](/forms/mezuzah-tefillin).
4. **Put on tefillin, if you are a man, or take part in another mitzvah.** See [tefillin](/learn/tefillin).
5. **Join a Shabbat meal or a class.** Sign up for a [Shabbat meal](/forms/shabbat-meal) or a class in [adult education](/programs/adult-education).
6. **Give tzedakah.** Start a [pushka](/learn/pushka) at home.

For a longer path, see [starting Jewish observance](/learn/starting-jewish-observance).

## Don't worry about being "behind"

Many people in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda worry that they will be asked questions they cannot answer, or that everyone else already knows what to do. The truth is that every Jew was once a beginner, and every Jew is welcome. Chabad of Lamorinda does not ask for membership or for experience. If you want to ask questions in private, call the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### Is it too late to start keeping Jewish traditions at 60 or 70?
No. The Rebbe taught that every day carries a mission and that later years are a good time for Torah. Any mitzvah you add is complete and meaningful.

### Where do I begin if I know almost nothing?
Begin with one thing: a Hebrew name, Shabbat candles or a mezuzah. Read [starting Jewish observance](/learn/starting-jewish-observance), or call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242.

### Do I have to change everything at once?
No. Add one mitzvah at a time, at a pace that feels right. For questions about your own life, ask the Rabbi.

### Can I have a Bar or Bat Mitzvah as an adult?
Yes. Many adults celebrate one. Read [adult Bar and Bat Mitzvah](/learn/adult-bar-bat-mitzvah) and the story of [a Bar Mitzvah at 83](/learn/bar-mitzvah-at-83).

### Will I feel out of place if my family is not Jewish or I was not raised with Judaism?
Please ask the Rabbi. Every Jew is welcome at Chabad of Lamorinda, and we are happy to talk with you and your family privately.

Yes, see [adult education](/programs/adult-education) and [Torah classes in Lamorinda](/learn/torah-classes-in-lamorinda), or call 925-384-7242.
