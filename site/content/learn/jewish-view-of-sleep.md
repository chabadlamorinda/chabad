In Judaism, sleep is not wasted time: it is rest that the body needs and a nightly journey for the soul. We bracket it with prayer, saying the bedtime Shema before sleep to forgive, let go and entrust our soul to G-d, and Modeh Ani on waking to thank G-d for returning it.

For busy parents and professionals in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, these small practices can turn the first and last minutes of the day into calm.

## Sleep and the soul

Chabad.org teaches that the soul rises to heaven each night to recharge, and each morning G-d mercifully returns it to the body. A new day is a sign that G-d still trusts you with something to do. This is why the first words of the day thank Him.

The sages compare sleep to a small taste of death, and waking to a renewal of life. That is a gentle idea: each morning is a fresh start.

## Before bed: the bedtime Shema

The bedtime Shema is a short set of prayers found in the siddur. Chabad.org explains that the last minutes before sleep shape your dreams and affect how you perform the next day, so a calm bedtime supports both rest and tomorrow.

Chabad.org lays out six steps:

1. **Relax.** Slow down and let go of the day's worries.
2. **Re-examine.** Review the day. Notice the good, and set aside mistakes with the intention to do better.
3. **Refresh.** Forgive anyone who hurt you, using the short paragraph composed by Rabbi Isaac Luria.
4. **Refocus.** Recite the Shema with concentration.
5. **Repent.** Reflect on G-d's kindness and say the confession that follows.
6. **Re-entrust.** Say the blessing *Hamapil* (הַמַּפִּיל), asking for a peaceful night and entrusting your soul to G-d.

After the blessing, avoid food or drink until you wake. If you have trouble falling asleep, Chabad.org suggests saying, thinking or picturing the words of the Shema. See the full guide to the [bedtime Shema](/learn/bedtime-shema) and [the Shema](/learn/shema).

## Forgiving others before sleep

Chabad.org notes that the best way to release your own mistakes is to release the wrongs others did to you. The Talmudic sage Rava taught that those who let go of revenge have their own sins overlooked. Forgiving before sleep means you do not carry anger into the night. See [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah).

## Waking up: Modeh Ani

*Modeh Ani* (מוֹדֶה אֲנִי) is the first prayer of the day, said in bed. It thanks G-d, the living and eternal King, for mercifully returning our soul, and it affirms how great His faithfulness is.

Chabad.org says it is generally said on waking after a real sleep, when you intend to start your day. Our first conscious moments are spent on gratitude, with nothing from the previous day to taint them.

Chabad.org also admits waking up is a battle between "I want" (the snooze button) and "I should" (getting up), and offers a trick: picture a king waiting for you to begin your mission. The true King is the One who renews you each day. See [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani).

It is also customary to wash your hands on rising. Ask the Rabbi for the details.

## Rest is part of caring for your body

Judaism teaches that caring for your health is a duty, since your body is a gift on loan. Maimonides, the great medieval sage, wrote that a person should sleep about eight hours. Rest helps you serve G-d, and your family, with a clear head. Shabbat is the weekly reminder: one day to stop, rest and recharge. See [Jewish health and the body](/learn/jewish-health-and-body).

If you have long-term trouble sleeping, please see a doctor. The Jewish tradition values medical care.

## A simple Jewish sleep routine

1. Put down screens a little earlier.
2. Review one good thing from the day.
3. Forgive someone, and yourself.
4. Say the bedtime Shema, or even just the first line.
5. In the morning, say Modeh Ani before you reach for your phone.

## Common questions

### What is the Jewish view of sleep?
Sleep is rest that the body needs and a nightly journey for the soul. We frame it with the bedtime Shema and Modeh Ani.

### What is Modeh Ani?
It is a short prayer of thanks said on waking, thanking G-d for returning our soul. It is said in bed, before washing.

### What is the bedtime Shema?
It is a set of prayers from the siddur said before sleep, including a statement of forgiveness, the Shema, and the *Hamapil* blessing.

### Why forgive others before sleeping?
Letting go of grudges lets you rest in peace and, according to the Talmud, helps your own mistakes be overlooked.

### How many hours should a Jew sleep?
Maimonides mentions about eight hours, but needs vary. Take care of your health and ask a doctor.
