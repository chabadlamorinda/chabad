A Jewish funeral is a simple, dignified service followed by burial in the earth, ideally as soon as possible after death. Its customs focus on two values: honoring the person who passed away (kavod hamet) and comforting the family who remains.

If you are reading this because you have just lost someone, we are deeply sorry. You do not have to know everything. Please call us and we will help.

## First steps

When a death occurs:

1. **Call the Rabbi.** He can guide you on what to do next and help coordinate with a Jewish funeral home and burial society.
2. **Contact a funeral home** familiar with Jewish practice.
3. **Do not leave the body alone.** It is customary for someone to stay with the deceased and recite [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) (Psalms). This is called shemirah, "guarding."

## Preparing the body: tahara

Jewish tradition treats the body with great respect, since it housed a holy soul. Members of a chevra kadisha (holy burial society) perform tahara, a gentle ritual washing and purification. Men care for men, and women care for women.

The deceased is then dressed in tachrichim, simple white linen shrouds. Everyone is dressed alike, rich or poor, because we all stand equal before G-d. A man is often buried with his tallit.

## The casket and burial

- **Simple casket:** a plain wooden casket with no metal is traditional, so the body can return to the earth naturally.
- **Burial, not cremation:** Jewish law calls for burial in the ground. "For dust you are, and to dust you shall return" (Genesis 3:19).
- **Quick burial:** burial usually takes place within a day or so, unless a short delay is needed for family to travel or because of Shabbat or a holiday.
- **No embalming or viewing:** the casket is kept closed out of respect.

## The funeral service

A Jewish funeral is usually short and modest:

- **Kriah (tearing):** immediate family members tear a garment or wear a torn ribbon, expressing grief.
- **Psalms and eulogies:** a Rabbi and family members share words about the person's life.
- **El Malei Rachamim:** a memorial prayer for the soul.
- **Burial:** family and friends help fill the grave with earth, an act of kindness that cannot be repaid.
- **Kaddish:** mourners recite [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish) at the graveside.
- **Comforting:** guests form two lines for the mourners to pass through as they offer words of comfort.

Flowers are not a Jewish custom. Instead, people often give tzedakah in the person's memory.

## After the funeral

Mourners return home to begin [shiva](/learn/shiva), the seven days of mourning. Friends usually prepare a first meal for them, called the seudat havraah, which often includes round foods like eggs and lentils.

## Common questions

### Why are Jewish funerals so quick?

The Torah teaches that the deceased should not be left unburied overnight. A prompt burial honors the person and allows the soul to move on. A short delay for family or Shabbat is acceptable.

### Is cremation allowed in Judaism?

Traditional Jewish law requires burial and does not permit cremation, since we return the body to the earth whole. If you are facing a hard decision, please talk to the Rabbi privately and with no judgment.

### What should I wear to a Jewish funeral?

Dress modestly in dark, respectful clothing. Men usually wear a kippah, and kippot are often available at the service.

### What do I say to the mourners?

Simple words are best: "I am so sorry for your loss." The traditional phrase is "May G-d comfort you among the mourners of Zion and Jerusalem." Your presence means more than any words.
