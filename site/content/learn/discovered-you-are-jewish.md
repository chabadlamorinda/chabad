According to traditional Jewish law, you are Jewish if your mother is Jewish, meaning her own mother was, and so on back, or if you converted properly. A DNA test can point to Jewish ancestors, but it cannot by itself say whether you are Jewish under Jewish law, so the kind next step is to ask the Rabbi, privately and without pressure.

## What a DNA result can and cannot tell you

Many people in the Bay Area open a DNA report and see "Ashkenazi Jewish" for the first time. That is real information about ancestry. But Jewish status in Jewish law is not decided by a percentage or a gene. It follows the mother's line, as the Torah teaches and the Talmud explains. Chabad.org notes that there is no "Jewish gene" and that genetics cannot conclusively say whether a person is Jewish. So a result is a doorway, not a verdict.

## The mother's line

Here is the traditional rule, plainly and kindly:

- If your mother is Jewish, you are Jewish, whatever your father's background.
- If your mother's mother was Jewish, your mother is Jewish too, even if she never knew it or was not raised Jewish.
- If only your father is Jewish, you are not Jewish according to halacha, though you have a real connection to the Jewish people and a warm welcome here.

For each person the details differ, so please ask the Rabbi about your own case.

## A Jew is always a Jew

A Jew who never had a bar or bat mitzvah, never entered a synagogue, or grew up without any Judaism is no less Jewish. In Chassidic teaching, every Jewish soul is a spark of G-d that nothing can put out. Many families in the former Soviet Union, where Jewish life was suppressed for generations, are in just this situation. See [Russian-speaking Jews in Lamorinda](/learn/russian-speaking-jews-in-lamorinda).

## What to do first

1. **Talk to family.** Ask older relatives for names, towns, stories and old papers.
2. **Gather what you have.** See [how to show you are Jewish](/learn/proving-jewish-ancestry) and [Jewish genealogy](/learn/jewish-genealogy).
3. **Talk to the Rabbi.** He can explain what your family information means and what, if anything, is needed.
4. **Do one mitzvah.** You do not have to change your life overnight. Light Shabbat candles, learn your [Hebrew name](/learn/hebrew-names), put up a mezuzah, or come to a class.

## If it turns out the mother's line is not Jewish

Many people discover that they have Jewish ancestors but are not Jewish by halacha. The path of [converting to Judaism](/learn/converting-to-judaism) is open to anyone sincere, and a Jewish ancestor is often a beautiful reason to begin. Nobody will press you. Ask the Rabbi what is right for you.

## Common questions

### My DNA test says I am Jewish. Am I Jewish?

A DNA test shows ancestry, not Jewish status under Jewish law, which follows the mother's line. Ask the Rabbi, who can look at your family history with care.

### My grandmother was Jewish. Am I Jewish?

If she was your mother's mother, then yes, your mother and you are Jewish by halacha. If she was your father's mother, the line passes through your father, so ask the Rabbi about your case.

### Can my father's Jewishness make me Jewish?

According to traditional Jewish law, Jewish identity follows the mother. You are warmly welcome, and the Rabbi can talk with you about your connection and your options.

### I was raised with no religion. Am I still Jewish?

Yes, if your mother is Jewish. A Jew who knew nothing of Judaism is still Jewish, and can begin learning at any time.
