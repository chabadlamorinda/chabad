Adar (אדר) is the Hebrew month in which, as the Talmud says, "when Adar begins, we increase in joy" (*mishenichnas Adar marbim besimchah*). It is the month of Purim, and in a leap year it appears twice: Adar I and Adar II.

## Why do we increase in joy?

The Talmud teaches that Adar is a fortunate month for the Jewish people, and Chabad.org adds that the name Adar is related to *adir*, which means strength and power. Haman cast lots to choose Adar for his decree, thinking it the worst month for the Jews, and the opposite happened: it became the month of rescue.

Joy is a mitzvah in itself. Chassidic teaching stresses that joy breaks through barriers, and the Lubavitcher Rebbe taught that joy hastens the final redemption. So make this month a month of smiles, kindness and celebration. See [simcha, Jewish joy](/learn/simcha).

## What happens in the month?

Chabad.org gives nine points about Adar. Among them: Moses was both born and passed away on 7 Adar, and the Talmud teaches that his birthday helped avert the decree. The sign of Adar is Pisces (fish), a symbol of blessing, and the Jewish people thrive immersed in Torah, as fish thrive in water.

Haman was hanged in Nissan, not in Adar; Purim commemorates the rest after the victory. Adar was also the last month the Jewish people spent in Egypt before the Exodus, so its joy leads right into [Passover](/holidays/passover).

## Adar I and Adar II: the leap year

The Jewish calendar follows the moon but must stay in step with the seasons, so seven times in a 19-year cycle (about every three years) an extra month is added. The extra month comes before Adar and is called Adar I, and the regular Adar becomes Adar II. See [the Jewish leap year](/learn/jewish-leap-year-and-calendar).

In a leap year, Purim is celebrated in Adar II, the month closest to Nissan, to link redemption with redemption. This year the calendar on [the holidays page](/jewish-holidays) shows you the exact days for Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda.

## What is Purim Katan?

Purim Katan ("Small Purim") falls on the 14th of Adar I, in a leap year. Chabad.org lists these customs: Tachanun, the penitential prayers, is omitted, beginning from the preceding afternoon prayer; there is no fasting; eulogies are generally not given. Some add a little to their joy and festive eating, but that is not required.

The Rebbe taught that joy should not depend on big events: even on an ordinary day like this, be joyful.

## Common questions

### Why do we increase in joy when Adar begins?
The Talmud says that Adar is a fortunate month for the Jewish people. It is the month of the Purim miracle, so we add to our happiness.

### What are Adar I and Adar II?
In a leap year, an extra month called Adar I is added before the regular Adar, which becomes Adar II. Purim is in Adar II.

### What is Purim Katan?
Purim Katan is the 14th of Adar I in a leap year. We do not fast or say Tachanun, and some add a little festive joy.

### How often is there a leap year in the Jewish calendar?
Seven times in a 19-year cycle, roughly every three years.
