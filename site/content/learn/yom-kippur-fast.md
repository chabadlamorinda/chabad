On Yom Kippur, Jews fast for about 25 hours, from before sunset until after nightfall the next day, with no food or drink at all. The fast is part of the holiest day of the year, a day of prayer, [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah) and closeness to G-d.

## Why we fast on Yom Kippur

The Torah says, "On the tenth day of this seventh month... you shall afflict your souls" (Leviticus 23:27). The Sages explain that this means fasting and refraining from physical comforts.

On Yom Kippur, we are compared to angels, who do not eat or drink. Setting aside the body's needs for one day helps us focus on the soul. It is not meant as punishment, but as a chance to connect with our deepest self.

## The five restrictions

On Yom Kippur, we refrain from:

1. **Eating and drinking**, including water.
2. **Washing or bathing**, except washing hands to the knuckles in the morning and after using the bathroom.
3. **Applying lotions, oils or creams.**
4. **Wearing leather shoes.** Many wear canvas sneakers or slippers.
5. **Marital relations.**

All the laws of Shabbat also apply on Yom Kippur.

## When the fast begins and ends

The fast begins before sunset on the eve of Yom Kippur, after the final meal, and before candle lighting. It ends after nightfall the next evening, after the Neilah prayer, the final shofar blast and Havdalah. Check the exact times for your location on our [zmanim tool](/tools/zmanim).

## Who should not fast

Health always comes first in Jewish law.

- **Children** under bar or bat mitzvah age do not fast. Older children may practice by fasting for part of the day.
- **Pregnant and nursing women** are generally required to fast, but must consult a doctor and the Rabbi if there is any concern.
- **The sick or elderly**, and anyone with a medical condition such as diabetes, should speak with their doctor and the Rabbi before the holiday. Sometimes the instruction is to eat or drink in small amounts at set intervals.

If a doctor says you must eat, then eating is the mitzvah. Never risk your health to fast.

## Tips for an easier fast

1. **Hydrate** well in the days before Yom Kippur.
2. **Cut back on caffeine** gradually over the week before, to avoid headaches.
3. **Eat a balanced final meal.** Include complex carbohydrates and protein, and avoid very salty or spicy foods.
4. **Rest.** Spend the day in synagogue and avoid exertion.

## The day before Yom Kippur

It is a mitzvah to eat and drink well on the day before Yom Kippur. The Talmud says that whoever eats on the ninth is credited as if they fasted on both the ninth and the tenth. Many also give tzedakah, ask forgiveness from others, and perform the custom of kapparot.

## Breaking the fast

After Havdalah, break the fast gently with a drink and light foods. Many families have a festive "break-fast" meal. It is also customary to begin building the sukkah right after Yom Kippur.

## Common questions

### Can I brush my teeth on Yom Kippur?
No. Brushing teeth and using mouthwash are not permitted. Some people find a mouth rinse right before the fast begins helpful.

### Can I take my medication?
Essential medication should be taken. Pills can often be swallowed without water. Ask your doctor and the Rabbi before the holiday.

### What if I accidentally eat?
If you accidentally eat or drink, stop as soon as you remember and continue fasting. Then do teshuvah, which is what the day is about.

### Is it okay to go to work on Yom Kippur?
No. Yom Kippur is a full holiday on which all work is forbidden, just like Shabbat.
