It is one of the most natural questions there is. G-d knows everything; He knows what we need better than we do. So what is the point of asking? And if G-d has already decided, how can our words change anything?

## Prayer is connection, not information

The Hebrew word for prayer, *tefillah*, comes from a root that means "to attach" or "to join." Chassidic teaching explains that prayer is not about telling G-d something He does not know. It is about binding ourselves to Him. A child who asks a parent for something is not only getting a request answered; the asking itself is a moment of closeness.

## G-d wants to be asked

The Talmud teaches that G-d "longs for the prayers of the righteous." Our matriarchs Sarah, Rebecca and Rachel waited years for children, and the sages explain that G-d wanted their heartfelt prayers. G-d gives blessings, but He wants us to be partners: to want them, to ask for them, and to recognize where they come from.

## Prayer changes us

When we pray, we stop and think about what truly matters. We remember to be thankful, we feel for others, we see our life in a bigger light. Chassidic teaching explains that sincere prayer makes a person into a better "vessel" for blessing, and a changed person may now deserve, and be able to receive, what was not right for them before.

## Can prayer change what will happen?

Judaism teaches that it can. The sages say that even when a sharp sword rests on a person's neck, they should not give up on prayer. Sincere prayer, together with [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah) and [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah), can transform a decree. That is why we pray for the sick, for our families and for the world.

## When the answer is "no" or "not yet"

Sometimes we pray and do not see the answer we wanted. Judaism does not promise that every request will be granted the way we imagine. It does promise that no prayer is wasted: every sincere word brings us closer, and its good is stored up, sometimes for us, sometimes for others, sometimes for later. See [why bad things happen](/learn/why-bad-things-happen) and [trust in G-d](/learn/bitachon).

## Common questions

### Do I have to pray in Hebrew?
No. G-d understands every language, and a prayer you understand is better than one you do not. Hebrew has its own special power, so many people use both. See [talking to G-d in your own words](/learn/talking-to-g-d-in-your-own-words).

### Is it selfish to pray for myself?
No. Asking G-d for health, livelihood and peace acknowledges that everything comes from Him. Many people add others to their prayers, which the Talmud says brings blessing to the one who prays too.

### How do I start praying if I never have?
Begin with Modeh Ani in the morning and the Shema at night, and say a few words of your own whenever you like. See [learning to pray](/learn/prayer).
