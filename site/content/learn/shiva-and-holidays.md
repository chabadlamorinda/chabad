Chanukah and Purim do not end shiva. Because every case depends on the exact day, please call the Rabbi.

## Shabbat during shiva

Shabbat does not end shiva. Chabad.org explains that it counts as one of the seven days, but "overt mourning" is forbidden on Shabbat. In practice:

- Mourners wear regular shoes, sit on regular chairs and wear clothes with no signs of mourning.
- They may attend synagogue and say Kaddish at the appropriate times, but do not lead the service and are not called up to the Torah. (If called by mistake, they may accept.)
- On Friday night, the usual welcoming song *Shalom Aleichem* is skipped.
- Before Shabbat, mourners may bathe and put on festive clothing starting about two and a half hours before sunset. Shiva stops a little before Shabbat starts, for preparation.
- Mourning resumes after Shabbat, once evening prayers are done and they are at home.

See [shiva](/learn/shiva) and [kaddish](/learn/kaddish).

## Major holidays end shiva

If Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Passover or Shavuot begins during shiva, the remaining days of shiva are canceled. This works only if some mourning was observed before the holiday began; even a short time counts, according to Chabad.org, as the equivalent of the seven days. The mourner then joins the holiday fully: shofar on Rosh Hashanah, the sukkah and lulav on Sukkot, the Seder and chametz on Passover. The shiva candle may stay lit but should not be in the room where holiday meals are held.

## If the death or burial is during a holiday

If the death or burial happens during a major holiday, shiva begins only after the holiday ends. If the burial happens just before Shabbat or a holiday, the counting depends on exact times, such as when the family heard of it and when the sun set. This is a case for the Rabbi, not for guessing.

## What about sheloshim?

Sheloshim (שְׁלוֹשִׁים, "thirty") is the 30 days from burial. A holiday during shiva counts toward the thirty days, and when shiva is cut short by a festival, parts of the thirty days may be shortened as well. Details differ by holiday and by how much mourning was kept, so ask the Rabbi to count with you. See [sheloshim](/learn/sheloshim).

## Chanukah and Purim

These are minor holidays and do not cancel shiva.

- **Chanukah.** Chabad.org says it is not among the holidays that end shiva. Mourners light the menorah with the blessing. See [Chanukah](/holidays/chanukah).
- **Purim.** Shiva continues in its softened form: no low stool and ordinary shoes, but no parties. A mourner still hears the Megillah (the Book of Esther), gives gifts of food to a friend, charity to at least two poor people and takes part in a meal. Joyous foods and drinks are left out of the gifts, and visitors may bring food to the family but should not give mishloach manot to the mourner. See [Purim](/holidays/purim).

## Common questions

### Does a holiday cancel shiva?
A major festival (Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Passover or Shavuot) ends the rest of shiva if some mourning was observed before it began. Minor holidays like Chanukah and Purim do not. Call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242 for your case.

### Does Shabbat count as one of the seven days of shiva?
Yes. Shabbat counts as a day, but public signs of mourning are not observed on it.

### Can you sit shiva on Shabbat?
You do not sit shiva publicly on Shabbat, but it is still counted. Mourners may go to synagogue and wear regular shoes.

### What happens if someone dies during Passover or Sukkot?
Generally, shiva begins after the holiday ends. The exact timing depends on the day, so please ask the Rabbi.

### Is shiva kept on Chanukah or Purim?
Yes. These minor holidays do not end shiva. A mourner still lights the menorah, or hears the Megillah, with the modifications explained above.
