Shalom Aleichem is the song sung at home on Friday night before Kiddush, welcoming the angels who accompany us home from synagogue on Shabbat. Its name means "peace be upon you," and it opens the Shabbat meal with warmth and blessing.

## The story behind the song

The Talmud (Shabbat 119b) teaches that two angels, one good and one opposite, escort every person home from synagogue on Friday night. If they find the candles lit, the table set and the home peaceful, the good angel blesses: "May it be this way next Shabbat too," and the other angel must answer "Amen." If the home is not ready for Shabbat, the opposite happens.

Shalom Aleichem greets these angels and asks them to bless us. It was composed in the 1600s, based on Kabbalistic teachings, and quickly spread to Jewish homes everywhere.

## The words

Each of the four verses is traditionally sung three times.

**1. Welcome**
*Shalom aleichem, malachei hasharet, malachei Elyon, mi'Melech malchei hamelachim, HaKadosh Baruch Hu.*
Peace be upon you, ministering angels, angels of the Most High, from the King of kings, the Holy One, blessed be He.

**2. Come in peace**
*Bo'achem l'shalom, malachei hashalom, malachei Elyon...*
Come in peace, angels of peace, angels of the Most High...

**3. Bless me**
*Barchuni l'shalom, malachei hashalom, malachei Elyon...*
Bless me with peace, angels of peace, angels of the Most High...

**4. Go in peace**
*Tzeitchem l'shalom, malachei hashalom, malachei Elyon...*
May your departure be in peace, angels of peace, angels of the Most High...

Each verse ends with the same words: "from the King of kings, the Holy One, blessed be He."

## When and how to sing it

1. **Come home** from Friday night services, or finish lighting candles and the evening prayers.
2. **Gather around the Shabbat table,** which is set with the challah, wine and candles.
3. **Sing Shalom Aleichem,** often standing.
4. Many then say a short prayer, "Ribon kol ha'olamim," and sing [Eishet Chayil](/learn/eishet-chayil).
5. **Make Kiddush** over wine and begin the meal. See [Kiddush](/learn/kiddush).

There are many melodies, and every family seems to have its own. Children often love to sing it loudly.

## Why it matters

The song reminds us that Shabbat is not just a day off. It brings real holiness into our homes, so much that angels come to visit. A peaceful, joyful home on Friday night invites blessing for the whole week.

## Common questions

### Why do we say "go in peace" right after welcoming the angels?
Commentators offer several answers. Some explain that once we begin the meal, we no longer need the angels' help, so we send them off with respect. Others say Shabbat itself is so holy that even angels step back.

### Can I sing Shalom Aleichem if I did not go to synagogue?
Yes. Anyone can sing it at their Shabbat table, whether or not they went to services. It is a beautiful way to start any Friday night meal.

### Is Shalom Aleichem the same as the greeting "shalom aleichem"?
They share the words. As a greeting, "shalom aleichem" means "peace be upon you," and the reply is "aleichem shalom." The Friday night song uses the same phrase to greet the angels.

### Do I have to sing it in Hebrew?
Hebrew is traditional, but understanding matters. If you are new to it, sing along in Hebrew and read the English to know what you are saying. See our guide to [Shabbat meals](/learn/shabbat-meals).
