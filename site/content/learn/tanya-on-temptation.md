Tanya teaches that you do not have to be perfect or "feel spiritual" to resist temptation; you only have to choose right in this one moment. Chapter 14 says that anyone can be a beinoni, a person who does not act on a wrong desire, "at any time or hour," because the power to choose in action, speech and thought is in everyone's hands.

That turns a huge life question, "Can I ever change?", into a small one that can be answered today: "What will I do right now?"

## Think in moments, not in years

Many people give up because they imagine a lifetime of saying no. Chabad.org's presentation of Chapter 14 describes the beinoni level as available "at any time or hour," and it explains that it is about conduct, not feeling. A person may not yet hate the wrong desire in his heart, since feelings change with the circumstance. But everyone can turn away from evil and do good in deed, word and thought. See [the beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni).

## Mind over heart

Chapter 12 gives the central tool. The mind can guide the heart. When a desire rises, the beinoni understands that it is wrong and, using that understanding, turns his attention elsewhere, especially to holy matters. If a wrong thought comes, he pushes it aside "with both hands" and does not dwell on it or plan to act on it. See [unwanted thoughts](/learn/tanya-unwanted-thoughts).

The chapter says the same approach applies to anger, jealousy and resentment toward others: hold them back and answer with kindness. So "temptation" in Tanya is wide: it includes anger, jealousy and grudges, not only the dramatic things.

## A simple sequence for the tempting moment

Using Chapters 12 and 14, one can follow these steps:

1. **Recognize the pull.** Feeling it is normal; it does not mean you failed.
2. **Tell your heart what you want.** Chapter 14 suggests telling the heart that you do not want to be a rasha, even for a moment, because wrongdoing cuts the bond with G-d.
3. **Let the mind lead.** Remind yourself why this desire is wrong and turn your attention to something good.
4. **Do not act, speak or plan.** Choosing not to act is a real victory. Tanya's beinoni does not let the desire reach his thought, speech or action.
5. **Do one good thing next.** Chapter 14 also speaks of deliberately binding yourself to G-d through good deeds, words and thoughts.

## Train the heart in calm moments

The chapter does not leave it to the heat of the moment. It says to set aside times to cultivate distaste for worldly pleasures, for example by reflecting on how fleeting they are, and to cultivate joy in G-d by contemplating His greatness. Repeated practice becomes second nature. So strength in a tempting moment grows from calm moments before it. For habits, see [building good habits with Tanya](/learn/building-good-habits-tanya) and [changing a bad habit](/learn/changing-a-bad-habit).

## When it is more than temptation

Tanya's guidance is spiritual. If a pattern feels beyond your control, such as addiction, please get professional help; that is a medical matter and help is available. See [addiction and recovery](/learn/addiction-and-recovery). For a personal situation, ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### How do I resist temptation according to Judaism?
Tanya teaches mind over heart: understand why the desire is wrong, turn your attention elsewhere and do not act, speak or plan on it. Anyone can do this in any single moment.

### Does Tanya say temptation goes away?
No. The beinoni still feels the pull. What he does is choose not to act on it. Only the tzaddik's inner nature is fully transformed.

### What if I fail?
Falling does not cancel your effort, and [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah) is always open. Tanya says not to be deeply discouraged.

### Can I really change without feeling inspired?
Yes. Chapter 14 says the beinoni level depends on choosing in action, speech and thought, not on feeling.

### What if I cannot stop?
Please see a doctor or counselor. Tanya's guidance is spiritual and professional help is also right when needed.
