A pareve Chanukah dish has no meat and no dairy, and a vegan dish also has no eggs, honey or any animal product. Eggs are pareve but not vegan, so a vegan latke needs a recipe without eggs, such as split pea latkes.

## Pareve is not the same as vegan

[Pareve](/learn/pareve) (פרווה) is a kosher category: foods that are neither meat nor dairy, so they can be served with either. Fruits, vegetables, grains, fish and eggs are pareve. Vegan is a way of eating that excludes all animal products, including eggs. So:

- A potato latke with egg is pareve, but not vegan.
- A vegan latke is always pareve if every ingredient is kosher.
- "Vegan" does not mean "kosher." Processed foods still need a kosher symbol, and vegetables still need checking. See [vegan and vegetarian kosher](/learn/vegan-and-vegetarian-kosher).

## A vegan latke: Indian-style split pea

Chabad.org has a pareve, vegan recipe for split pea latkes. Here it is retold:

1. Soak 1 cup of yellow split peas for about three hours, then drain.
2. Pulse the peas in a food processor with a little dried red pepper, a piece of fresh ginger, a clove of garlic, fresh cilantro, ground coriander and cumin, until coarse.
3. Stir in a small minced onion, salt and pepper, and a quarter cup of flour if the batter is loose.
4. Fry quarter-cup scoops in about three inches of hot oil until crisp, about four minutes, and keep warm in a low oven.

Chabad.org also has a collection of fifteen latkes, from sweet potato and carnival squash to carrot-parsnip. Many are pareve, but the page does not mark which are vegan, so read each recipe for eggs.

## Doughnuts without dairy or eggs

Traditional doughnut dough often has eggs, and some use milk or butter. Choose a recipe that is clearly labeled vegan, or read each ingredient, and use a kosher oil. Sufganiyot are traditionally pareve, and see [sufganiyot](/learn/sufganiyot). Fill with jam, and check that the jam and any glaze are kosher certified.

## Sides and dessert for a vegan table

- Applesauce with no added dairy.
- Roasted vegetables, like carrots and squash.
- A big salad with oil and lemon, after you [check the greens](/learn/checking-for-insects).
- Fresh fruit and kosher dark chocolate gelt marked pareve. Read the label, since many chocolates contain milk.

## Kosher tips for any recipe

1. **Look for a symbol** on every packaged ingredient ([kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols)).
2. **Check vegetables, herbs and leafy greens** for insects, which is more important when a recipe is full of fresh herbs.
3. **Use clean, kosher utensils**, and ask the Rabbi if a pot or pan was used with non-kosher food.
4. **Hidden ingredients**: animal fats, gelatin and some colorings can hide in packaged foods ([hidden kosher ingredients](/learn/hidden-kosher-ingredients)).

## Common questions

### Are vegan Chanukah foods automatically kosher?
No. A vegan dish still needs kosher-certified ingredients and checked produce. Ask the Rabbi if you are unsure.

### What is the difference between pareve and vegan?
Pareve means neither meat nor dairy, and it may include eggs and fish. Vegan means no animal products at all.

### Are eggs pareve?
Yes, kosher eggs are pareve (see [kosher eggs](/learn/kosher-eggs)), though they are not vegan.

### Can I make vegan latkes?
Yes. Chabad.org's Indian-style split pea latkes are vegan, and other vegetable latkes can be made without eggs.

### Can I make vegan sufganiyot?
Yes, if you use a vegan dough recipe and kosher ingredients. Many traditional doughs have eggs or dairy, so read the recipe.

### Can pareve food go on a meat table?
Yes, if it is truly pareve and was not cooked in a meat or dairy pan. Ask the Rabbi if the pan or oven is not clear.
