## What is Purim?

Purim (פּוּרִים) is the joyful holiday that celebrates the rescue of the Jewish people in ancient Persia, told in the Book of Esther. The Megillah (מְגִילָּה, the "scroll") is the handwritten scroll of that story. If you are new to the holiday, start with [Purim for beginners](/learn/purim-for-beginners). The exact date every year is on the [Jewish holidays page](/jewish-holidays).

## Where to hear the Megillah near me

Hearing the Megillah is the heart of Purim, and it is read twice: once on Purim night and once the next morning. Every word counts, so the custom is to listen carefully to each one. Children are welcome and are traditionally given noisemakers (graggers) to blot out the name of Haman whenever it is read.

Chabad of Lamorinda in Lafayette holds Megillah readings for the community of Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda and nearby Pleasant Hill and Martinez. Times are posted on the [events calendar](/events). Cannot get away? Call 925-384-7242 and ask the Rabbi about a reading for a person who is homebound or ill. Read more in [the Megillah](/learn/megillah).

## The four mitzvahs of Purim, done locally

1. **Hear the Megillah** at night and again by day.
2. **Send mishloach manot** (מִשְׁלוֹחַ מָנוֹת, "sending portions"): a gift of two different ready-to-eat foods to at least one friend. Bring one to a neighbor in Moraga or Orinda who may not know it is Purim. See [mishloach manot](/learn/mishloach-manot).
3. **Give matanot la'evyonim** (מַתָּנוֹת לָאֶבְיוֹנִים): gifts of money to at least two people in need. You can also give through the [donate page](/donate).
4. **Enjoy the Purim meal** (seudah) with family and friends, as in [the Purim seudah](/learn/purim-seudah).

All four are explained in [the four mitzvahs of Purim](/learn/the-four-mitzvahs-of-purim).

## Costumes, hamantashen and the Purim party

Dressing up recalls that on Purim the hidden hand of G-d is behind everything. Hamantashen are triangle-filled cookies, and Chabad of Lamorinda's kids' hamantashen bake lets children fill and fold their own. The Purim party is for the whole community, so come in costume. Find ideas in [Purim costumes](/learn/purim-costumes) and [Purim for kids](/learn/purim-for-kids). Call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 to hear the schedule for this year.

## Common questions

### Where can I hear the Megillah on Purim in Lafayette?
Chabad of Lamorinda holds Megillah readings in Lafayette. See the [Purim page](/holidays/purim) and the [events calendar](/events) for times, or call 925-384-7242.

### How many times do you hear the Megillah on Purim?
Twice: once on Purim night and once on Purim day. Women and children are welcome to hear it too.

### Is there a Purim party for kids near Moraga and Orinda?
Yes. Chabad of Lamorinda holds a Purim party for the whole community and a kids' hamantashen bake in Lafayette, about ten minutes from Moraga and a few minutes from Orinda. Costumes are encouraged.

### What is mishloach manot and who do I send it to?
Mishloach manot is a gift of at least two different ready-to-eat foods, sent to at least one Jewish friend or neighbor on Purim day. Many people send several, so the day is full of friendship.

### Do I have to wear a costume to come to Purim at Chabad?
No, but it is encouraged. Come as you are or dress up; every Jew is welcome, with no membership needed.

### Can I give charity on Purim through Chabad of Lamorinda?
Yes. Matanot la'evyonim, gifts for people in need, is one of the four mitzvahs, and you can give on the [donate page](/donate) or ask the Rabbi at 925-384-7242.
