Most practical Shabbat questions come down to one idea: on Shabbat we do not create, cook, kindle or switch things on and off, so we set things up before Shabbat starts. Lights can be left on or put on timers, food is kept warm on a covered stove or hot plate, and the fridge light is turned off ahead of time.

## Why these questions come up

The Torah forbids 39 categories of creative work on Shabbat, called melachot, such as cooking, lighting a fire and writing. Modern questions about electricity, appliances and phones are answered by applying these categories. Read about the [39 melachot](/learn/thirty-nine-melachot) and see our [Shabbat guide](/shabbat).

## Lights

- **Leave on what you need** before Shabbat, such as the kitchen, hallway and bathroom lights.
- **Timers** set before Shabbat may turn lights on and off during Shabbat. Many families use them for bedrooms.
- **Do not flip switches** during Shabbat.
- **Motion sensor lights** on porches can be disabled or covered before Shabbat so walking by does not turn them on.

## The refrigerator

You may open and close the fridge on Shabbat, but the inside light would turn on. Before Shabbat, unscrew the bulb, tape the switch or use the "Sabbath mode" many fridges have. Some ice and water dispensers also need to be turned off.

## Food and hot water

- **Cooking** must be done before Shabbat. Cooked food can be kept warm on a blech (a metal sheet over a low flame) or a Shabbat hot plate, set before Shabbat.
- **Slow cookers** can be set up before Shabbat with fully cooked food.
- **Hot water** for tea and coffee can come from an urn filled and plugged in before Shabbat.
- **Hot water from the tap** can involve a water heater refilling with cold water; ask the Rabbi about your system.

Read more about [Shabbat meals](/learn/shabbat-meals).

## Phones, screens and the doorbell

- **Phones, computers and TVs** are not used on Shabbat. Many people turn them off or put them away before candle lighting. This is also one of the great gifts of Shabbat: a day of real presence.
- **Doorbells and video doorbells** should not be used; leave the door unlocked if guests are coming, or knock.
- **Security cameras** and smart home devices raise detailed questions. Ask the Rabbi about your setup.
- **In a real emergency** that may endanger life, call for help. Saving a life overrides Shabbat.

## Elevators

Pressing an elevator button is not allowed on Shabbat. Some buildings, hospitals and hotels have a "Shabbat elevator" that stops on every floor automatically. Opinions differ about using it, so ask the Rabbi.

## Other everyday tips

1. **Pre-tear toilet paper** or buy tissues.
2. **Open packages and bottles** before Shabbat when you can.
3. **Set alarm clocks** before Shabbat.
4. **Carrying** outside is allowed only within an [eruv](/learn/eruv).

## Common questions

### Can I leave the lights on all Shabbat?
Yes. Leaving lights on, or setting them on timers before Shabbat, is the common practice. What we avoid is switching them on or off during Shabbat.

### Is it okay to open the refrigerator on Shabbat?
Yes, as long as the inside light will not go on. Remove or unscrew the bulb, tape the switch, or use the Sabbath mode before Shabbat starts.

### Can I use a Shabbat elevator?
There are different opinions about Shabbat elevators. Ask the Rabbi about the building you will be in.

### What if I am just starting to keep Shabbat?
Start with what feels doable, like candle lighting and a Friday night dinner, and add one practice at a time. See [Shabbat for beginners](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners).
