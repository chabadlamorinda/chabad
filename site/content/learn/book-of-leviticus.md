The Book of Leviticus, called Vayikra (וַיִּקְרָא, "and He called") in Hebrew, is the third of the five Books of the Torah. It is mostly about how to come close to G-d through the offerings brought in the Mishkan, and how to live a holy life every day.

## What does "Vayikra" mean?

The Hebrew name is simply the first word of the book: "And He called to Moses." The book is also called Torat Kohanim, "the teaching of the priests," because so many of its laws concern the Kohanim, the descendants of Aaron. It follows the [Book of Exodus](/learn/book-of-exodus), which ends with the building of the [Mishkan](/learn/the-mishkan), and it begins with what happens inside.

Jewish tradition holds that children traditionally begin their Torah learning with Leviticus. The Midrash explains that pure children should begin with the laws of purity. In many families that is where a child first meets the Torah.

## What are the korbanot, the offerings?

The book opens with the laws of the offerings, the korbanot (קָרְבָּנוֹת). According to Chabad.org's summary, five main types are described:

- **Olah (עוֹלָה), the burnt offering,** burned entirely on the altar.
- **Minchah (מִנְחָה), the grain offering,** flour with oil and frankincense. Part was burned, and the rest was eaten by the priests.
- **Shelamim (שְׁלָמִים), the peace offering,** shared among the altar, the priests and the person who brought it.
- **Chatat (חַטָּאת), the sin offering,** for sins committed by accident.
- **Asham (אָשָׁם), the guilt offering,** for specific wrongs.

The word korban (קָרְבָּן) comes from the root karov, "near." Chabad teachers stress that its point is not giving something up but getting close to G-d. A person brought something of value, and something of himself, in order to come near.

## What took the place of the offerings?

Since the destruction of the [Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple), there are no offerings. Jewish tradition teaches that our daily prayers parallel the daily offerings, and the times of the services correspond to them. The Lubavitcher Rebbe taught that the offering that is required of us today must come from within us: our time, our effort and our heart. If you would like to know more about the order of the prayers, ask the Rabbi.

## What else is in Leviticus?

According to the summary on Chabad.org, the book also covers:

1. **The inauguration of the Mishkan.** For seven days Moses served as High Priest, and on the eighth day Aaron began. The same day, two of Aaron's sons died after bringing an offering that had not been commanded, and Aaron responded in silence.
2. **Holiness and purity.** Which animals a holy people may eat (the laws of kashrut), and the laws of ritual purity and impurity.
3. **Laws for the Kohanim.** Whom they may marry and what they may not do.
4. **The holidays.** Passover, Shavuot, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur and Sukkot are listed. The service of the High Priest on Yom Kippur is described in detail.
5. **Social and economic laws.** The Sabbatical year (Shemitah), when the land rests and debts are released, and the Jubilee (Yovel) every fiftieth year, when fields return to their original families.
6. **Blessings and warnings.** Peace and security for following the Torah, and a promise that G-d will remember His covenant even after hard times.

## "Love your fellow as yourself"

In the middle of the book, in chapter 19, comes the commandment "Love your fellow as yourself" (Leviticus 19:18). Rabbi Akiva called it a great principle of the Torah. It sits among laws about honest scales, caring for the poor, respecting the elderly and not gossiping, showing that holiness is found in ordinary life, at the office, in the kitchen and in the neighborhood. See [ahavat Yisrael, love of a fellow Jew](/learn/ahavat-yisrael).

## When is Leviticus read?

We read Leviticus on Shabbat mornings from late winter until spring, beginning with Parshat Vayikra, usually in March. Check [the weekly Torah portion](/parsha) to see where we are.

## Common questions

### What is the Book of Leviticus about?
Leviticus is about holiness: how to come close to G-d through the offerings in the Mishkan and how to live a holy life every day through laws of food, purity, justice and kindness.

### What does Vayikra mean?
Vayikra (וַיִּקְרָא) means "and He called." It is the first word of the book, which is why the book carries this name.

### What does korban mean?
Korban (קָרְבָּן), usually translated "offering" or "sacrifice," comes from the root meaning "near." It is about drawing close to G-d.

### Why did Jews bring sacrifices?
They brought offerings in the Mishkan and the Holy Temple to come close to G-d, to express thanks and to atone for mistakes, as the Torah commanded.

### Do Jews still bring sacrifices today?
No. There is no Temple today. Our prayers parallel the daily offerings, and we pray for the Temple to be rebuilt.

### Why do children begin learning Torah with Leviticus?
It is a long-standing Jewish custom. The Midrash says pure children should begin with the laws of purity.
