Taharat hamishpacha, "family purity," is the set of Torah laws that guide the intimate life of a Jewish husband and wife. It is one of the pillars of Jewish life, kept by Jewish couples for thousands of years, and it is often called the foundation of the Jewish home.

## A monthly rhythm

At its heart, family purity is a rhythm of separation and reunion that follows a woman's monthly cycle:

1. **Separation.** When a woman's period begins, husband and wife refrain from physical intimacy and from physical closeness.
2. **Counting.** After her period ends, she counts seven "clean" days.
3. **Immersion.** At the end of the count, after nightfall, she immerses in a [mikvah](/learn/mikvah).
4. **Reunion.** The couple is together again, until the next cycle begins.

These are the broad lines. The practical details are specific and personal, and they are learned privately with a teacher. The Rebbetzin teaches this privately, one-on-one.

## Meaning and blessing

Family purity is a "chok," a mitzvah whose deepest reason is beyond us; we keep it because G-d asked. Yet many couples find in it great wisdom:

- **Renewal.** The Talmud teaches that the time apart keeps a marriage fresh, so that the reunion is "like the day of the wedding."
- **Communication.** During the days apart, couples learn to connect through words, attention and kindness.
- **Respect.** A wife's body and rhythm are honored, never taken for granted.
- **Holiness.** The physical bond between husband and wife becomes a mitzvah, brought into a sacred framework.

Jewish tradition teaches that keeping these laws brings blessing to the marriage and to the children born of it.

## Who keeps it?

Family purity is a mitzvah for every married Jewish couple, whatever their background or level of observance. Brides learn it before the wedding in [kallah classes](/learn/kallah-classes), and grooms learn their side in [chosson classes](/learn/chosson-classes). Couples who have been married for years can begin at any time, and many describe starting as a turning point in their marriage.

## Modesty and privacy

Judaism treats this part of life with great modesty. Questions are asked privately, the mikvah is a discreet and private place, and a teacher keeps everything confidential. No question is embarrassing.

## Common questions

### We have been married for years. Is it too late to start?
Never. Couples begin at every stage of life. The Rebbetzin is glad to guide you gently, step by step.

### What if a question comes up during the month?
Questions are normal, especially at first. Women call their teacher or a rabbi privately; that is exactly what they are there for. Ask the Rabbi or Rebbetzin.

### Does family purity apply after menopause?
A woman who has stopped menstruating immerses once more, and after that the monthly cycle no longer applies. Details depend on the situation; ask the Rebbetzin.
