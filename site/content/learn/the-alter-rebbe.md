The Tanya was written by Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi (1745 to 1812), known as the Alter Rebbe, "the Old Rebbe," the founder of Chabad Chassidism. He was a main disciple of the Maggid of Mezritch, who was the successor of the [Baal Shem Tov](/learn/baal-shem-tov).

This guide tells the story of his life, as Chabad.org presents it, so that you know who is speaking when you open the book.

## A young genius from Liozna

According to Chabad.org, he was born in Liozna, near Lubavitch, and his father was Rabbi Baruch. As a boy he studied Talmud and Kabbalah, and in his spare time science and mathematics, with a teacher who is said to have told his father that there was nothing more he could teach him. At twelve he moved to Vitebsk, where he was recognized as a genius. Chabad.org gives his birth date as 18 Elul 1745.

## Meseritch and the Maggid

At twenty he left home to look for guidance in serving G-d. Chabad.org says he weighed going to Vilna, the center of opposition to Chassidism, and chose Meseritch, where Rabbi Dovber, the Maggid, taught. He joined the Maggid's inner circle. When he was about twenty-five, the Maggid asked him to rewrite the Code of Jewish Law, the Shulchan Aruch, with the later rulings. That work is known as the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, and Chabad.org says scholars of many kinds praised it.

After the Maggid died on 19 Kislev, 1772, his disciples dispersed. The Alter Rebbe took up the task of spreading Chassidus in Lithuania, and Chabad.org says the Chabad movement was founded then. See [Chassidus](/learn/chassidus) and the [Chabad Rebbes](/learn/chabad-rebbes).

## The book: Tanya

The Alter Rebbe gave his book three names, according to an introduction to the Tanya on Chabad.org:

- **Likkutei Amarim** ("Collected Teachings"), a modest name, as if he had only gathered teachings. It was first printed in Slavita in 1796.
- **Tanya**, from the book's first word, the name it carries today.
- **Sefer shel Beinonim**, "The Book of the Intermediates," because it is about the ordinary person who struggles. See [the beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni).

The same introduction says that before the book, he taught orally for about twelve years, and that his students' copies of these teachings had errors, which was one reason for the book. His followers came to call the Tanya the "Written Torah" of Chassidus.

The title page, as Chabad.org presents it, says the book is based on a verse from Deuteronomy: the matter is very near to you, in your mouth and in your heart, to do it. The book sets out to explain how this closeness is true.

## Arrest and release

In 1798 he was denounced and taken to St. Petersburg. He was freed on 19 Kislev, on the orders of Czar Paul I, and Chassidim have celebrated that day ever since. The story is told in [Yud Tes Kislev](/learn/yud-tes-kislev).

## His last years

He died on 24 Teves, 1812, in the village of Piena, near Kursk, after fleeing from the advancing French armies, and was buried in Haditz. Chabad.org sums up his legacy with a saying: Vilna knew how to study and Meseritch knew how to pray, while he knew how to do both.

## Common questions

### Who wrote the Tanya?
The Tanya was written by Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, the Alter Rebbe, the founder of Chabad. It was first printed in 1796.

### Why is he called the Alter Rebbe?
Alter Rebbe means "Old Rebbe" in Yiddish. It is the name Chabad Chassidim use for the first Chabad Rebbe.

### Who was the Alter Rebbe's teacher?
His teacher was Rabbi Dovber, the Maggid of Mezritch (Meseritch), who in turn passed on the teachings of the Baal Shem Tov, as Chabad.org describes it.

### What else did the Alter Rebbe write?
Chabad.org mentions the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, his rewriting of the Code of Jewish Law, along with the Tanya.

### Why do Chabad Jews celebrate 19 Kislev?
It is the day he was freed in 1798. Read [Yud Tes Kislev](/learn/yud-tes-kislev).
