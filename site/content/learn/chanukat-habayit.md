Chanukat HaBayit, "dedication of the home," is the Jewish way to celebrate moving into a new house or apartment. The heart of it is putting up kosher mezuzahs, often followed by a festive gathering with words of Torah, blessings and good food.

## Where the custom comes from

The Torah mentions a person "who has built a new house and not yet dedicated it" (Deuteronomy 20:5). King David composed Psalm 30, "A song for the dedication of the House," and many say this psalm at a home dedication. Just as the Holy Temple was dedicated with joy, a Jewish home, which the Sages call a small sanctuary, is dedicated with joy too.

## Step by step

1. **Get kosher mezuzahs.** Every doorway used by people, except bathrooms, needs a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) written by a trained scribe. Order them ahead of time.
2. **Put them up promptly.** In a home you own, put the mezuzahs up as soon as you move in. In a rental outside Israel, the full obligation starts after 30 days, but many put them up right away.
3. **Say the blessing** before attaching the first mezuzah, with all the others in mind.
4. **Invite family and friends.** Make a gathering with food, music and joy.
5. **Share words of Torah.** A short teaching, a story, or a few verses of Psalm 30.
6. **Say a blessing over food or something new.** Many make the Shehecheyanu blessing on a new fruit or a new garment at the gathering. Ask the Rabbi whether and how to say a blessing over the new home itself.

## Filling the home with meaning

The Rebbe encouraged every Jewish home to be built on three things: Torah, prayer and tzedakah. A great way to dedicate a new home is to place in it:

- **Holy books.** A Chumash, a [siddur](/learn/siddur), a book of [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) and a Tanya. See [a Jewish home library](/learn/jewish-home-library).
- **A tzedakah box.** A [pushka](/learn/pushka) in the kitchen and one in each child's room.
- **Shabbat candlesticks**, ready for Friday night.
- **A kosher kitchen**, or a first step toward one.

## Popular customs

- **Bread and salt.** Many bring bread and salt to a new home, symbols of sustenance and of the covenant.
- **First guests.** Opening the home to guests early on begins it with the mitzvah of [hospitality](/learn/hachnasat-orchim).
- **Inviting the Rabbi.** Many families invite a rabbi to help put up the mezuzahs and say a few words.
- **A siyum.** Some time the dedication with completing a tractate of Talmud or a book of Torah, adding to the joy.

## Common questions

### When should I make the Chanukat HaBayit?
The mezuzahs go up right when you move in. The party can be anytime soon after, once you are settled. Many hold it within the first month.

### What gift should I bring to a Jewish housewarming?
Bread and salt, a challah cover, Shabbat candlesticks, a Jewish book, or a pushka are all meaningful. A beautiful mezuzah case is a lovely gift, though the scroll inside must be kosher.

### What if my home already had mezuzahs from the previous owner?
If the previous owners were Jewish, they should leave the mezuzahs up, and you should check that they are kosher. If you are unsure, have them checked by a scribe.

### Do I need a Chanukat HaBayit for a rental?
Yes, it is a wonderful custom for any new home, owned or rented. Every Jewish home deserves a joyful beginning.
