It is a Jewish custom to give a coin to tzedakah before praying in the morning. Chabad.org explains that the custom comes from the Talmud, which tells that Rabbi Eleazar gave a coin to a poor person and only then prayed.

## The Talmud's story

Chabad.org notes that giving charity before prayer is customary, and cites the Talmud (Bava Batra 10a): a small coin given to a poor person makes one worthy of the Divine Presence. It tells that Rabbi Eleazar would give a coin before he prayed. The idea is simple: before we ask G-d for what we need, we first help someone else with what they need.

Tzedakah (צְדָקָה) means "justice," not just "charity." Our money is entrusted to us by G-d so that we can share it (see [Tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah)). A coin before prayer reminds us of that before we begin to speak with Him.

## What the Ari taught

Chabad.org presents a teaching of Rabbi Yitzchak Luria, the Ari, from Kabbalah. He taught that charity given before prayer joins the first two letters of G-d's Name to the last two, which the sins of mankind had separated. In his picture, the coin is like the letter *yud*, the arm held out is like the *vav*, and the fingers of both hands are like the two letters *hei*.

The same page tells, from Rabbi Chaim Vital, that the Ari gave with great joy and did not check whether money remained for himself. The Ari's teaching is deep, and there are many details. If you want to learn more, ask the Rabbi.

## How to make it a habit

Chabad.org recommends keeping a charity box, a [pushka](/learn/pushka), at home and at work, and putting coins in it at least once a day. A simple routine is to place a coin in the pushka each morning before [Shacharit](/learn/shacharit-morning-service), the morning prayer. This is a custom for families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda and everywhere. See also [children, money and tzedakah](/learn/children-money-and-tzedakah).

Chabad.org teaches that how you give matters more than how much. See [how to give kindly](/learn/how-to-give-tzedakah-kindly).

## What about Shabbat?

Chabad.org notes that money cannot be handled on Shabbat, which is why the custom before Shabbat candles is to set aside the amount on Friday. On Shabbat and holidays, ask the Rabbi what is customary, and read about [giving before candle lighting](/learn/tzedakah-before-candle-lighting).

## Common questions

### Why do Jews give charity before praying?
Chabad.org explains that it is a custom based on the Talmud (Bava Batra 10a), where Rabbi Eleazar gave a coin to a poor person before he prayed. Giving first reminds us that we ask G-d for our needs after caring for another's.

### How much should I give before davening?
Chabad.org suggests putting coins in the pushka at least once a day. For how much to give overall, see [maaser](/learn/maaser) or ask the Rabbi.

### Does the custom apply on Shabbat?
Money is not handled on Shabbat. If you have a question about how to keep the custom on Shabbat or holidays, ask the Rabbi at 925-384-7242.

### Do women and children give before praying too?
Chabad.org recommends keeping a pushka at home and at work and giving at least once a day, and notes that women and girls customarily give before lighting Shabbat candles. Ask the Rabbi about your family's practice.

### What did Rabbi Yitzchak Luria teach about it?
Chabad.org presents the Ari's teaching that charity before prayer joins the parts of G-d's Name that sin had separated, and that one should give joyfully.
