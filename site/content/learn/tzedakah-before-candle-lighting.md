It is a Jewish custom for women and girls to give tzedakah (charity) just before they light Shabbat candles on Friday afternoon, by dropping a coin or bill into a charity box (a pushka). The same custom is kept before holiday candles, and because money cannot be handled once Shabbat begins, the giving is done beforehand.

## What the custom is

Tzedakah (צְדָקָה) is the Hebrew word for charity, and it literally means "justice" or "righteousness." Giving is not a favor; it is the right thing to do, because everything we have is entrusted to us by G-d.

Chabad.org's guide to Shabbat candles lists this as part of the "how": put some money in a charity box before lighting the candles. Chabad.org's explanation of tzedakah adds that, since money may not be handled on Shabbat, an extra sum is set aside and given before it begins.

## Why just before the candles?

The Rebbe, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson, wrote about linking candle lighting with tzedakah. In a 1975 letter he describes lighting Shabbat and Yom Tov candles as a special privilege given to Jewish women, one that brings light to the whole household. Giving tzedakah selflessly, he wrote, is a life-saving act, so the two fit together: a light in the home and a gift that helps someone else's life.

## How to do it, step by step

1. **Keep a pushka** where you will see it, in the kitchen or near the candlesticks. (See [The Pushka](/learn/pushka).)
2. **Give before the candles.** Shortly before lighting, put a coin or a few dollars in the box.
3. **Light the candles** at the time you normally do, and say the blessing. (See [How to Light Shabbat Candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).)
4. **Empty the box now and then** and send the money to a worthy cause. See [Tzedakah Priorities](/learn/tzedakah-priorities) for who to give to first.

Children can drop their coins in too.

## Before Yom Tov

The Rebbe's letter speaks of Shabbat and Yom Tov candles together, and the custom is kept before holiday candles too. The Rebbe also noted that money is set aside, not handled, on Shabbat and Yom Tov, so give before the holiday begins, not after.

## A small habit with a big idea

Chabad.org also mentions giving a coin before praying, and having a pushka in every Jewish home. The Friday coin is one of the easiest habits to begin: it takes only a moment, and it shows children that Shabbat begins with giving. If your family is just starting, one coin is a perfect beginning. For the bigger picture, see [Tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).

## Common questions

### Why do Jewish women give charity before lighting Shabbat candles?
It is a long-standing custom that pairs the light of the candles with the light of helping another person. The Rebbe suggested linking candle lighting with tzedakah, since giving selflessly is a life-saving act.

### Can I give tzedakah on Shabbat itself?
Money is not handled on Shabbat or Yom Tov, so charity is given before they begin. That is why an extra sum is put in the box on Friday afternoon.

### How much should I give before lighting candles?
There is no set amount for this custom. A coin or a few dollars is common. For the yearly total, a tenth of your income is the usual guideline; see [Maaser](/learn/maaser).

### Do men and boys give too?
Giving tzedakah is a mitzvah for everyone, and Chabad.org encourages giving every day, especially before praying. The candle-lighting custom is described for women and girls. For your own situation, ask the Rabbi.

### What if I forget to give before lighting?
Give when you remember, and add it to the box before the next Friday. The point is to build the habit, not to be perfect.

### Can children take part?
Yes. Girls begin lighting candles at about age three, and children can hold the coin and drop it in the pushka. See [Children, Money and Tzedakah](/learn/children-money-and-tzedakah).
