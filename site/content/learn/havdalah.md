Havdalah is the short ceremony on Saturday night that ends Shabbat and begins the new week. It uses a cup of wine, fragrant spices and a braided candle, and takes just a few minutes.

## What Havdalah means

Havdalah (הַבְדָּלָה) means "separation." Just as we welcome Shabbat with Kiddush, we say goodbye to it with Havdalah, which separates the holy day from the ordinary week. It helps us carry some of Shabbat's peace and holiness into the days ahead.

## When to make Havdalah

Shabbat ends on Saturday night when three stars appear, roughly 40 to 50 minutes after sunset depending on location and season. Check the time on our [Shabbat page](/shabbat). Until you hear or say Havdalah, do not eat or drink (except water). If you missed it on Saturday night, ask the Rabbi, since parts of Havdalah may still be said until Tuesday afternoon.

## What you need

- **A cup of wine or grape juice.** Fill it to the top, even letting it overflow a little, as a sign of blessing.
- **Spices** (besamim), such as cloves or cinnamon, in a spice box.
- **A Havdalah candle** with at least two wicks braided together, or two candles held together so the flames touch.

## How to make Havdalah, step by step

1. Light the Havdalah candle, and have someone hold it.
2. Hold the cup in your right hand and say the opening verses ("Hinei El yeshuati...") found in the siddur.
3. **Blessing on the wine:**
   Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, borei p'ri hagafen.
   "Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who creates the fruit of the vine."
   Put the cup down.
4. **Blessing on the spices:**
   Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, borei minei v'samim.
   "Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who creates various kinds of spices."
   Smell the spices and pass them around.
5. **Blessing on the flame:**
   Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, borei m'orei ha'eish.
   "Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who creates the lights of fire."
   Hold your fingers up toward the flame and look at the light reflected on your fingernails.
6. **The Havdalah blessing:** Pick up the cup again and say the final blessing, which ends:
   Baruch atah Ado-nai, hamavdil bein kodesh l'chol.
   "Blessed are You, L-rd, who separates between holy and ordinary."
7. **Drink** most of the cup while sitting.
8. **Put out the candle** by dipping it in leftover wine poured into a dish.
9. **Wish everyone "Shavua Tov,"** a good week.

## Why spices and fire?

Our Sages teach that on Shabbat each Jew receives an "extra soul," and when it departs we feel a little faint. The pleasant smell of the spices comforts and revives us. The flame recalls that fire was first made by Adam on the first Saturday night, and it marks the start of the work week, since fire may be kindled again.

## Common questions

### Can I make Havdalah without wine?
Yes. Grape juice is fine. If you have neither, certain other important drinks can be used in some cases. Ask the Rabbi.

### Is Havdalah said after holidays too?
Yes, but after a holiday that falls on a weekday, Havdalah is said over wine alone, without spices or the candle. When a holiday begins right on Saturday night, a special form of Havdalah is included in Kiddush.

### Can women make Havdalah?
Women are obligated in Havdalah just like men. Usually one person says it for the household, and everyone listening answers "Amen" and fulfills the mitzvah.

### Why do we look at our fingernails?
A blessing on light should be said when we can benefit from it. Looking at the light on our nails, and the shadow on our palms, shows we can tell things apart by its light.
