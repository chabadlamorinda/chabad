Tachanun (תחנון) is a set of quiet, heartfelt prayers for mercy that Jews say after the morning and afternoon Amidah on ordinary weekdays. The moment when everyone rests their head on their arm is part of it, and it is skipped on happy days such as Shabbat, Rosh Chodesh and Chanukah.

## What Tachanun is

The word means "supplications," pleading prayers asked humbly. According to Chabad.org, Tachanun in the Chabad siddur has four parts: Viduy (confession), the Thirteen Attributes of Mercy (said only with a minyan, a group of ten), Nefilat Apayim (the prayer said with the head lowered) and a shortened Avinu Malkeinu.

The Thirteen Attributes of Mercy are the qualities of kindness that G-d revealed to Moses on Mount Sinai. Saying them is a way of reminding ourselves, and asking G-d, to treat us with that same compassion.

## Why do people put their head down?

This is the moment that puzzles visitors most. It is called Nefilat Apayim, "falling on the face." Chabad.org explains that the Torah describes Moses praying in three ways: sitting, standing and falling on his face. Today we lower our head onto the arm to show awe, humility and surrender to G-d, especially when we ask forgiveness. Rabbeinu Bechaye gives three meanings: fear of G-d, the pain of asking forgiveness, and setting aside our own wants before Him.

Which arm? The most common custom in the morning is to lean on the right arm, out of respect for the tefillin on the left arm. In the afternoon, and in the morning for someone not wearing tefillin on the left arm, people lean on the left. Some communities differ, so watch what the people around you do, or ask the Rabbi.

Joshua also "fell on his face" before the Ark of G-d (Joshua 7:6). This is the basis of the custom to do Nefilat Apayim mainly where a Torah scroll is present. The face is covered with an arm or garment, and the supplication is said with sincerity. It is not a pose.

## Why is Tachanun longer on Monday and Thursday?

On Mondays and Thursdays the morning Tachanun is much longer. Chabad.org calls these days especially fitting for seeking G-d's favor.

## When is Tachanun not said?

It is a prayer of humility, so it is left out when the day is joyful. Among others, it is not said:

- On Shabbat, starting from Friday afternoon, and on [Rosh Chodesh](/learn/rosh-chodesh)
- On [Chanukah](/holidays/chanukah) and on Purim (the 13th to 15th of Adar)
- Throughout the month of Nissan
- On Lag BaOmer, and for the first twelve days of Sivan
- On the days leading up to and after Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, and Tisha B'Av
- On Tu BiShvat and the 15th of Av

Chabad also skips it on several days that remember miracles of release, such as the 10th, 19th and 20th of Kislev. The site calendar shows the holidays, so do not rely on a list typed from memory. For other occasions, such as a bris or a wedding in the synagogue, ask the Rabbi.

## How to take part as a newcomer

1. After the Amidah, see what the community does. If everyone lowers their head, you may too.
2. Read the English silently if Hebrew is hard. The confession and the plea for mercy are a conversation you can have in your own words.
3. If no one is leaning, you do not have to. Many days have no Tachanun at all.
4. Come back on a Monday or Thursday to hear the longer version, then a Shabbat to enjoy the joy of a day without it.

## Common questions

### What is Tachanun in Judaism?
Tachanun is a short set of prayers asking for mercy, said after the Amidah on weekday mornings and afternoons. It includes confession and a prayer said with the head lowered.

### Why do Jews put their head on their arm during prayer?
It is called Nefilat Apayim, "falling on one's face." It shows humility and surrender to G-d, following Moses and Joshua, who fell on their faces in prayer.

### Which arm do you lean on for Tachanun?
The most common custom in the morning is the right arm, because the left arm has the tefillin. In the afternoon it is the left. Customs vary, so ask the Rabbi.

### Why is there no Tachanun on some days?
Tachanun is left out on festive days like Shabbat, Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, Purim and all of Nissan, because it is a prayer of sorrow and humility.

### Why is Tachanun longer on Monday and Thursday?
Chabad.org says these are especially auspicious days to seek G-d's favor, so the morning prayer is longer.

### Do I have to say Tachanun if I am new to prayer?
No. If you are just starting, listen first, and say what you can in English. The Rabbi can guide you.
