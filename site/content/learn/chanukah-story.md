Chanukah celebrates two miracles from about 2,200 years ago: a small band of Jews, the Maccabees, defeated the mighty Syrian-Greek army, and a single jar of pure oil lit the Temple menorah for eight days. We remember both by lighting the menorah for eight nights.

## The background

After Alexander the Great, the Land of Israel came under the rule of the Seleucid Greeks, based in Syria. At first Jews were allowed to practice their faith. Over time, Greek culture spread, and some Jews, called Hellenists, adopted Greek ways and turned away from the Torah.

Then King Antiochus IV set out to force Greek culture on the Jews. He outlawed the core of Jewish life:

- Studying Torah
- Keeping Shabbat
- Celebrating Rosh Chodesh, the new month
- Circumcision (brit milah)

Jews who refused risked their lives. The Holy Temple in Jerusalem was taken over, an idol was placed inside, and it was defiled.

## The revolt of the Maccabees

In the town of Modiin, an elderly Kohen (priest) named Matityahu refused to bow to an idol. He and his five sons fled to the hills and called out, "Whoever is for G-d, follow me!"

His son Judah, called the Maccabee, led a small, untrained army against one of the strongest armies in the world. Against all odds, they won battle after battle. In the Al Hanisim prayer we thank G-d for delivering "the strong into the hands of the weak, the many into the hands of the few."

## The miracle of the oil

When the Maccabees returned to Jerusalem, they cleaned and rededicated the Temple. The word Chanukah means "dedication." The rededication began on the 25th of the Hebrew month of Kislev.

To light the Temple menorah, they needed pure olive oil sealed by the High Priest. The Talmud tells that they found only one small jar, enough for a single day. Preparing new pure oil would take eight days. They lit it anyway, and the oil burned for eight days.

The following year, the Sages established Chanukah as a holiday of praise and thanks. Learn how to light in our [menorah guide](/learn/chanukah-menorah).

## Other Chanukah heroes

- **Yehudit (Judith)** showed great courage against an enemy general. Because of her, there is a custom to eat dairy foods on Chanukah.
- **Chana and her seven sons** refused to bow to an idol, and their story of faith is told at Chanukah time.

## What the story means today

The Greeks did not want to destroy the Jews physically. They wanted Jews to forget that the Torah is G-d's wisdom and to treat it as just another culture. The Maccabees fought for the right to live as Jews.

The message for us: a little light pushes away a lot of darkness. Just as we add a candle each night, we can always add more light, more kindness and more Judaism to the world. That is also why we place the menorah where it can be seen from outside.

## Common questions

### Is the Chanukah story in the Bible?
No. The events took place after the books of the Hebrew Bible were completed. The story is told in the Talmud, in the Al Hanisim prayer and in other ancient sources.

### Why do we celebrate the oil more than the military victory?
The victory was a great miracle, but the oil showed that the Jews' goal was spiritual: to restore the light of the Temple. Lighting the menorah keeps that focus.

### Why do we eat latkes and doughnuts?
Foods fried in oil recall the miracle of the oil. See our guide to [Jewish foods](/learn/jewish-foods).

### When is Chanukah?
Chanukah begins on the evening before the 25th of Kislev and lasts eight days, usually in December. Check our [Chanukah page](/holidays/chanukah) for this year's dates.
