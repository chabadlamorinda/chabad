You can light your Chanukah menorah with either olive oil or wax candles, but Chabad custom prefers olive oil, because the Temple menorah burned olive oil. Whatever you choose, there must be enough fuel to burn for at least half an hour after nightfall.

## Oil or candles: which is better?

Both are valid, and a Jew who lights candles has fully done the mitzvah. Chabad.org explains that olive oil is the preferred choice for the home, since it recalls the miracle in the Temple, while candles are the common choice in many synagogues. Tea lights in a small cup are fine in a pinch.

If you have never lit before, candles are the easy start. Many families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda begin with a box of candles and move to oil once the children are old enough to love the glow. See [how to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah) for the blessings and the order.

## How long must the lights burn?

The lights must burn for at least a half hour after nightfall. Chabad.org puts it simply: make sure you have enough fuel to burn into the night.

Standard Chanukah candles are made for this, so a box labeled for Chanukah is usually enough. For oil, fill each cup generously. The lights are for the mitzvah of publicizing the miracle, so do not blow them out early to save fuel.

## Wicks and cups

For oil, use cotton wicks that float in the oil or sit in a holder, and trim them so they stand just above the surface. Fill the cups before nightfall, so that when it is time to light, all you need is a flame.

Keep a flat, stable, non-flammable surface under the menorah, and keep it away from curtains and little hands. Candles go in from right to left, and are lit from left to right.

## Friday afternoon: the longer lights

On Friday, the menorah is lit before the Shabbat candles, well before nightfall, yet it must still burn for a half hour after nightfall. That means using more oil or larger candles. Ordinary Chanukah candles are not enough on Friday, so put in extra oil, or use long-burning candles that are made for it.

After the Chanukah lights, light the Shabbat candles, and keep the menorah in place as it burns. See [Chanukah on Shabbat](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat), and the weekly times on the [Shabbat times page](/shabbat-times).

## What about an electric menorah?

The mitzvah is fulfilled with a real flame: oil or candles. An electric menorah makes a nice decoration for a window or a school, but if you are unsure about using one, ask the Rabbi.

## Getting ready before night one

1. Choose oil or candles and buy enough for all eight nights, plus the extra for Friday.
2. Find a safe, visible spot, a window or a doorway if you can.
3. Have a shamash (helper candle) ready, and matches or a lighter.
4. Keep the blessings nearby; see [the Chanukah story](/learn/chanukah-story) to tell the children why we light.

Leftover oil and wicks are best handled by asking the Rabbi what to do. See also [the Chanukah holiday page](/holidays/chanukah) for this year's dates and events.

## Common questions

### Is it better to light Chanukah candles with oil or wax candles?
Both are valid, but olive oil is preferred in Chabad custom, because the Temple menorah used olive oil. Candles are fully acceptable and are the easier choice for most families.

### How long should Chanukah candles burn?
They must burn for at least a half hour after nightfall. Make sure you have enough fuel to last that long.

### What do I need to light a menorah with oil?
Use olive oil, cotton wicks, and a menorah with eight cups plus one for the shamash. Fill the cups before you light and trim the wicks.

### Why do Friday's lights need more oil?
On Friday you light before Shabbat begins, but the lights still have to burn a half hour into the night. So you need more oil or longer candles.

### Is an electric menorah kosher for the mitzvah?
The mitzvah is done with a flame, oil or candles. For questions about electric menorahs, ask the Rabbi.
