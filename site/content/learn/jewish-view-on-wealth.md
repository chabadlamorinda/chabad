Judaism does not see money as evil, nor as the goal of life. It sees wealth as a tool and a trust: G-d gives it to us so we can live well, support our families and do good.

## Wealth is a trust

"Remember the L-rd your G-d, for it is He who gives you the strength to make wealth" (Deuteronomy 8:18). Our talents and success are gifts. We are partners and caretakers, not absolute owners, which is why part of every income belongs to [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).

## Earning it honestly

The Talmud says that one of the first questions a soul is asked after life is: "Did you deal honestly in business?" Honest weights, fair prices, paying workers on time and keeping your word are mitzvahs. See [business ethics](/learn/business-ethics).

## Giving: tzedakah and maaser

- **Maaser:** giving a tenth of one's income to tzedakah, a widespread Jewish practice. See [maaser](/learn/maaser).
- **Tzedakah means justice,** not only charity: giving is the right thing to do with what we have.
- **Give joyfully and wisely:** the best tzedakah helps people stand on their own.

## Who is rich?

"Who is rich? One who is happy with his portion" (Ethics of the Fathers 4:1). Ambition is good when it builds; envy and endless comparing only drain us. Shabbat, one day a week without earning or spending, reminds us that we are more than what we produce.

## Teaching children about money

- Give an allowance and a [pushka](/learn/pushka): spend some, save some, give some.
- Let them see you give, and tell them why.
- Praise effort and honesty more than results.

## Common questions

### Is it wrong to want to be rich?
No, if wealth is earned honestly and used for good. The sages praised wealthy people who used their means generously.

### Do I give maaser before or after taxes?
There are different opinions and details. Ask the Rabbi for guidance for your situation.

### Can a donation be in someone's honor or memory?
Yes, a beautiful Jewish custom. See [sponsor in honor or memory](/sponsor).
