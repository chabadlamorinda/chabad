A mezuzah is a small handwritten parchment scroll containing the Shema prayer, placed on the doorposts of a Jewish home. It marks the home as a Jewish space and, as Jewish tradition teaches, brings G-d's protection to everyone inside.

## What a mezuzah is

Mezuzah (מְזוּזָה) literally means "doorpost." The mitzvah comes from the [Shema](/learn/shema): "And you shall write them on the doorposts of your house and on your gates" (Deuteronomy 6:9).

The mezuzah itself is the scroll, not the case. It contains two Torah passages, Deuteronomy 6:4 to 9 and 11:13 to 21, which speak of G-d's oneness, loving G-d and keeping His commandments. It must be handwritten in black ink on parchment by a trained scribe (sofer), following many detailed rules. A printed or photocopied "mezuzah" is not kosher, even inside a beautiful case.

## Why we have a mezuzah

- **A reminder.** Every doorway reminds us of G-d and our values.
- **Protection.** The Sages teach that the mezuzah guards the home and its residents, whether they are inside or away. On the back of the scroll is written the Divine Name Sha-dai, which is also read as an acronym for "Guardian of the doors of Israel."
- **Identity.** It proudly marks a Jewish home.

## Which doors need a mezuzah

Every doorway in the home used by people, including the front door, bedrooms and the kitchen, needs its own mezuzah. Bathrooms do not get a mezuzah, and some small storage spaces and closets are exempt. Since doorways vary (arches, sliding doors, doors without frames), ask the Rabbi about specific doors.

## How to put up a mezuzah, step by step

1. **Get a kosher scroll** from a reliable source. Roll it from the end of the text toward the beginning, so the first word, Shema, is on the outside.
2. **Put it in a case.** Wrapping it in plastic helps protect it from moisture.
3. **Find the spot:** on the right side of the doorway as you walk into the room or house, on the doorpost itself.
4. **Measure the height:** at the bottom of the top third of the doorway, usually around shoulder height. It should be at least a handbreadth (about 4 inches) from the top.
5. **Tilt it.** In Ashkenazi practice, including Chabad, the top of the mezuzah tilts inward toward the room. Sephardim usually hang it straight up.
6. **Say the blessing** (below), and right away attach the mezuzah with nails, screws or strong double-sided tape.

If you are putting up several mezuzahs at once, say the blessing once, at the first one, with all of them in mind.

## The blessing

**Hebrew:** בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו, וְצִוָּנוּ לִקְבּוֹעַ מְזוּזָה

**Transliteration:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu likbo'a mezuzah.

**Translation:** Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us to affix a mezuzah.

## Caring for your mezuzah

A mezuzah scroll can fade, crack or get damaged over time. Jewish law requires checking the scrolls twice every seven years. Many also check them in the month of Elul, before Rosh Hashanah.

## Common questions

### Why do people kiss the mezuzah?
Touching the mezuzah and kissing your fingers when passing through a door is a custom that shows love for the mitzvah and reminds us of G-d's presence.

### Do I need a mezuzah if I rent?
Yes. Renters also put up mezuzahs. Outside Israel, the full obligation in a rental begins after 30 days, but many put them up right away. Ask the Rabbi about the blessing and about what to do when you move out.

### Can I put a mezuzah on my office or dorm room?
A dorm room or apartment where you live needs a mezuzah. Offices and businesses have their own rules. Ask the Rabbi about your space.
