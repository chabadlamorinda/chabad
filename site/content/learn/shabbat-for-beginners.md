Shabbat is the Jewish day of rest, from just before sunset on Friday until nightfall on Saturday. You do not need to do everything at once: start with one or two parts, such as lighting candles and sharing a Friday night meal, and add more as you go.

## What Shabbat is

The Torah tells us that G-d created the world in six days and rested on the seventh. Shabbat is our weekly reminder that the world has a Creator and that we are more than our work. For 25 hours, we step away from creating and doing, and focus on family, prayer, rest and Torah.

Shabbat is a gift, not a burden. Many people who begin keeping it say it becomes the best part of their week. Read more in our main [Shabbat guide](/learn/shabbat).

## Your first Shabbat, step by step

**Before Shabbat (Friday)**

1. **Prepare food.** Cook dinner and Saturday lunch ahead of time. Keep food warm on a hot plate or in a low oven set before Shabbat.
2. **Set the table** with a white cloth if you have one, two challahs, a Kiddush cup and wine or grape juice.
3. **Give tzedakah.** Put a few coins in a charity box before candle lighting.
4. **Set your lights and devices.** Leave lights on where you need them, and put phones aside.

**Friday evening**

1. **Light Shabbat candles** 18 minutes before sunset. Women and girls traditionally light; if there is no woman in the home, a man lights. Check the time on our [Shabbat page](/shabbat). See our [candle lighting guide](/learn/shabbat-candles).
2. **Go to synagogue** for the welcoming Kabbalat Shabbat service if you can, or say a few psalms at home.
3. **Sing Shalom Aleichem,** welcoming the Shabbat angels, at the table.
4. **Make Kiddush** over a cup of wine or grape juice. See our [Kiddush guide](/learn/kiddush).
5. **Wash hands** with a cup for bread and say the blessing.
6. **Say Hamotzi** over two challahs, cut, and share them with salt.
7. **Enjoy the meal.** Talk, sing, share a thought from the [weekly parsha](/parsha).
8. **Say Birkat Hamazon,** Grace after Meals.

**Shabbat day**

1. **Go to synagogue** in the morning, or pray at home.
2. **Have a festive lunch** with Kiddush and challah.
3. **Rest, read, walk, visit friends** and spend time with family.
4. **Eat a light third meal** in the late afternoon.

**Saturday night**

**Make Havdalah** after nightfall, with wine, spices and a braided candle, to separate Shabbat from the week. See our [Havdalah guide](/learn/havdalah).

## What we refrain from

On Shabbat, we refrain from the 39 categories of creative work that went into building the Tabernacle in the desert. In daily life, this includes things like cooking, writing, driving, turning lights on or off, using electronics, shopping and handling money. Learn more in our guide to the [39 melachot](/learn/thirty-nine-melachot).

If that feels like a lot, start by choosing one thing to set aside, such as your phone during the Friday night meal.

## Tips for starting

- **Take one step at a time.** Every mitzvah counts on its own.
- **Invite friends.** Shabbat is better shared.
- **Be a guest first.** Experiencing a Shabbat meal in another home is the best way to learn.
- **Ask questions.** No question is too basic.

## Common questions

### Do I have to keep all of Shabbat to start?
No. Each Shabbat mitzvah you keep has value. Many people start with candles or a family dinner, and grow from there at their own pace.

### What if I cannot read Hebrew?
You can say blessings in transliteration or in English. Many prayer books have both. See our [Hebrew reading for adults](/learn/hebrew-reading-for-adults) guide if you want to learn.

### Can I keep Shabbat if my family does not?
Yes. Do what you can with respect and warmth for those around you. Ask the Rabbi for practical ideas for your situation.
