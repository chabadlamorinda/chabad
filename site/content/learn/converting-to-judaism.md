Conversion to Judaism according to Jewish law is a sincere commitment to accept the Torah and its mitzvot, completed before a recognized Orthodox Beth Din (rabbinical court) after a long period of study. A person who converts becomes fully Jewish, a beloved member of the Jewish people in every way.

## First: you may already be Jewish

Before anything else, it is worth checking. According to Jewish law, anyone born to a Jewish mother is Jewish, whatever their upbringing or beliefs. Some people discover a Jewish grandmother or great-grandmother on the maternal line. If that is your situation, you do not need to convert at all. Read more in our guide to [who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew).

## How Judaism views converts

The Torah commands us many times to love the convert and treat them with kindness. Ruth, the great-grandmother of King David, was a convert. Many great sages descended from converts. Once someone converts, it is forbidden to remind them of their past or treat them as different.

At the same time, Judaism does not seek converts. There is no need to become Jewish to have a meaningful relationship with G-d. Non-Jews who keep the seven [Noahide laws](/learn/noahide-laws) are called righteous among the nations and have a share in the World to Come.

## Why rabbis traditionally discourage at first

Traditionally, a rabbi gently discourages a potential convert at the start, as Naomi did with Ruth. This is not rejection. Becoming Jewish means taking on lifelong obligations, and the Beth Din must be sure the person's decision is sincere and well considered.

## The steps of conversion

While details vary, a conversion according to Jewish law includes:

1. **Sincere motivation.** A desire to join the Jewish people and serve G-d, not a decision made only for marriage or other outside reasons.
2. **Study.** Usually a year or more of learning Jewish beliefs, laws and practice, often with a mentor.
3. **Living Jewishly.** Keeping Shabbat, kosher and other mitzvot, and becoming part of an observant community.
4. **Acceptance of the mitzvot** before a recognized Orthodox Beth Din of three judges.
5. **Brit milah** for men, or a symbolic drop of blood if already circumcised.
6. **Immersion in a [mikvah](/learn/mikvah)** in the presence of the Beth Din.

Only a conversion done according to Jewish law, through a recognized Beth Din, is accepted universally, including for marriage and in Israel.

## Common questions

### How long does conversion take?
It varies from person to person. Most conversions take at least a year, and often longer, because it involves real learning and change, not just a ceremony.

### Can I convert for marriage?
A conversion must be sincere and for its own sake. If a relationship is what first brought you to Judaism, speak openly with the Rabbi about where you stand. See also our guide to [interfaith families](/learn/interfaith-families).

### Are children of a convert Jewish?
Yes. Children born to a woman after her conversion are fully Jewish from birth.

### I am not Jewish but love Judaism. Do I need to convert?
No. You can have a deep, meaningful relationship with G-d through the Noahide laws. The Rabbi is glad to talk with you about either path.
