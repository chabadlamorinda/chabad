The Holy Temple, called the Beit HaMikdash, was the central house of G-d in Jerusalem, where the Jewish people came to pray, bring offerings and feel G-d's presence. Two Temples stood on the same holy mountain, and Jewish tradition teaches that a Third Temple will be built in the time of Moshiach.

## What the Temple was

The Torah commands, "Make for Me a sanctuary, and I will dwell among them" (Exodus 25:8). In the desert, this sanctuary was the portable Tabernacle, the Mishkan. Once the Jewish people settled in the Land of Israel, it became a permanent building on Mount Moriah in [Jerusalem](/learn/jerusalem), the same place where Abraham bound Isaac.

Inside were:

- **The Holy of Holies**, which held the Ark with the Tablets of the [Ten Commandments](/learn/ten-commandments). Only the High Priest entered, once a year, on Yom Kippur.
- **The Holy**, with the golden menorah, the table with twelve loaves of showbread, and the golden incense altar.
- **The courtyard**, with the large altar where offerings were brought.

The Kohanim (priests) served in the Temple, and the Levites sang and played music. Three times a year, on Passover, Shavuot and Sukkot, Jews from everywhere made the pilgrimage to Jerusalem.

## The First Temple

King David prepared to build the Temple, and his son King Solomon built it. It stood for about 410 years until it was destroyed by the Babylonians, and the Jewish people were sent into exile.

## The Second Temple

About seventy years later, Jews returned and built the Second Temple. It was later greatly expanded by Herod. It stood for about 420 years until the Romans destroyed it in the year 70 CE. Both Temples were destroyed on the same day, the ninth of Av.

## Why they were destroyed

The Sages teach that the First Temple fell because of serious sins, and the Second Temple fell because of baseless hatred among Jews. The remedy, they teach, is baseless love: caring for every Jew without conditions.

## Remembering the Temple today

Though the Temple is not standing, it is part of daily Jewish life:

- **Prayer.** Our daily prayers take the place of the Temple offerings, and we face Jerusalem when we pray. The [Amidah](/learn/amidah) asks G-d to rebuild Jerusalem and restore the Temple service.
- **The Three Weeks and Tisha B'Av.** We mourn the destruction during the [Three Weeks](/learn/three-weeks), ending with the fast of [Tisha B'Av](/holidays/tisha-bav).
- **Weddings.** A glass is broken under the chuppah to remember Jerusalem even at our happiest moment.
- **Learning.** The Sages teach that studying the laws of the Temple is considered like building it. The Rebbe encouraged learning about the Temple, especially during the Three Weeks.
- **The Western Wall.** The [Western Wall](/learn/western-wall) is a remnant of the Temple Mount's retaining walls.

## The Third Temple

Jewish belief, as taught by Maimonides, holds that [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach) will rebuild the Temple and gather the Jewish people. Every good deed we do now helps bring that time closer.

## Common questions

### Where was the Holy Temple?
It stood on Mount Moriah, known as the Temple Mount, in Jerusalem. Jewish tradition identifies this as the place where Isaac was bound and where Jacob dreamed of the ladder.

### What were the offerings in the Temple?
There were daily offerings, holiday offerings, thanksgiving offerings and offerings brought to atone for mistakes. Many were animals, but there were also flour, oil, wine and incense offerings. Our prayers today correspond to them.

### Is there a "mini Temple" today?
The Sages call a synagogue and a house of study a "miniature sanctuary." The Rebbe taught that every Jewish home can be a small sanctuary when it is filled with Torah, prayer and kindness.

### When will the Third Temple be built?
We do not know the date. We pray for it every day and believe it will come with Moshiach, and we can bring it closer through acts of goodness and kindness.
