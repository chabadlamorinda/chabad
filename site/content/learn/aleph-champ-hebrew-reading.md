Aleph Champ is a Hebrew reading program that teaches children level by level. Each child moves up at their own pace, and each new level is celebrated. Children are never held back by the class and never pushed ahead before they are ready, so every child can feel like a champion. Lamorinda Hebrew School in Lafayette uses Aleph Champ to teach Hebrew reading.

## Why Hebrew reading matters

Hebrew is the language of the Torah and of Jewish prayer. A child who can read Hebrew can open a prayer book (siddur) in synagogue and follow along, say the blessings, read the Shema and, one day, prepare for a [Bar or Bat Mitzvah](/learn/hebrew-school-to-bar-bat-mitzvah) with confidence instead of worry. The letters themselves are holy: Jewish tradition teaches that G-d created the world with the letters of the Hebrew alphabet. See [the Hebrew alphabet](/learn/hebrew-alphabet).

## How Aleph Champ works

**Levels, not grades.** Hebrew reading is split into clear, small levels, from the first letters and vowels to fluent reading. Aleph Champ names its levels by colors, a little like belts in martial arts, so children can see how far they have come and what comes next.

**Each child at their own pace.** In one class, one child may be learning the first letters while another is reading whole prayers. That is fine. Each child works on their own level with the teacher's help.

**Moving up is a celebration.** When a child masters a level, they read for the teacher, move up, and the achievement is celebrated. Children feel real pride in each step, and they want to reach the next one.

**Practice that feels like a game.** Reading drills, letter games, flash cards and friendly challenges keep it fun. Children practice reading out loud, which builds fluency.

## Why it works so well for children

- **No child is left behind.** A child who needs more time gets it, without feeling slow.
- **No child is bored.** A quick learner keeps moving up.
- **Goals children can reach.** Small levels mean frequent success, and success builds motivation.
- **Parents can see progress.** You know which level your child is on and what they are working toward.

## How you can help at home

You do not need to read Hebrew to help. Ask your child to show you a letter or a word they learned. Celebrate each new level as a family. Five minutes of reading out loud a few times a week makes a real difference. Our guide to [teaching kids the Aleph-Bet](/learn/teaching-kids-the-aleph-bet) has more ideas, and parents who want to learn too can start with [Hebrew reading for adults](/learn/hebrew-reading-for-adults).

## Common questions

### What is Aleph Champ?
Aleph Champ is a Hebrew reading program that breaks reading into small levels. Each child moves up at their own pace, and each new level is celebrated.

### My child has never seen a Hebrew letter. Can they join?
Yes. Every child starts at the level that fits them, including the very first letters. See [starting Hebrew school at any age](/learn/starting-hebrew-school-at-any-age).

### How long does it take to learn to read Hebrew?
It depends on the child and on practice at home. Because each child moves at their own pace, there is no race. Ask the Rebbetzin at 925-384-7241 about your child.

### Will my child understand what they read?
Aleph Champ teaches reading first. Meaning comes through prayer, Torah stories and the other lessons at Hebrew school, where children learn what the words and prayers are about.

### Does my child need to read Hebrew for a Bar or Bat Mitzvah?
Reading Hebrew makes preparation much easier and more meaningful. Children who learn steadily come to the big day prepared and relaxed.
