There is no special blessing just for graduation in the Torah, but Jewish tradition gives us beautiful ways to mark it: thanking G-d and the people who helped, blessing your graduate, and giving tzedakah. Whether to say the Shehecheyanu blessing at a graduation is a question for the Rabbi, so please ask.

## The Shehecheyanu blessing

Shehecheyanu (שֶׁהֶחֱיָנוּ) is a blessing of thanks for being granted life, being sustained and reaching a special moment. It is said when experiencing something new or special, for example the first taste of a new fruit, wearing new clothes, or performing certain mitzvot. Graduation is not one of the classic examples. So practice may differ, and the right answer for you depends on the situation. Read more in [Shehecheyanu](/learn/shehecheyanu), and ask Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 whether and how to say it for your graduate.

Even without a formal blessing, a family can pause and say thank you to G-d for bringing everyone to this day. See also [a Jewish blessing for a new job](/learn/new-job-jewish-blessing).

## Gratitude: hakarat hatov

Jewish tradition places great value on saying thank you, known as hakarat hatov (הַכָּרַת הַטּוֹב, "recognizing the good"). A graduation is a perfect moment for it. You might:

- Thank parents, grandparents, teachers, coaches and mentors, in person or in a note.
- Say thank you to G-d at the family dinner or on Friday night.
- Ask your graduate to name three people who helped, and to thank them.

Read [hakarat hatov](/learn/hakarat-hatov) for more.

## Blessing your graduate

Parents bless their children on Friday night. See [blessing your children](/learn/blessing-your-children). You may also add a personal blessing in your own words, wishing your graduate health, joy and the strength to use their gifts for good. When you give a gift, you might add a donation of tzedakah in their honor. See [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).

## The Rebbe's advice to a student

In a letter to a young man who worried about how he would earn a living, the Lubavitcher Rebbe said that it was too early to worry about the future, and that learning with devotion is the best preparation for later life. He was writing to a yeshivah student, but the thought speaks to every graduate: you are not required to know your whole path today. Do the next step well, and keep growing. See also [choosing a career](/learn/choosing-a-career-jewish-view).

## Common questions

### Is there a Jewish blessing for graduation?
There is no blessing specifically for graduation that we can cite. Shehecheyanu is traditionally said on new or special occasions, so ask the Rabbi whether to say it for a graduation.

### Should we say Shehecheyanu at a graduation?
Ask the Rabbi. It is said for new or special experiences, and customs differ about what qualifies.

### How can parents bless a graduate?
Parents can bless their children on Friday night, and add a personal blessing. Read [blessing your children](/learn/blessing-your-children).

### Can we give tzedakah in honor of a graduate?
Yes, a donation of tzedakah in the graduate's honor is a meaningful gift. Ask the Rabbi for more ideas.
