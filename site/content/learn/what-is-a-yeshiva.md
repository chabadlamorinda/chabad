A yeshiva (יְשִׁיבָה, "sitting") is a place where Jews gather to study Torah and the rabbinic tradition, most often the Talmud, in an intense, question-filled way. Today the word covers everything from a school for young children with a strong Jewish curriculum to an advanced academy where scholars study all day.

## What a yeshiva is

The word comes from the Hebrew for "sitting," because students sit and learn. Jewish tradition traces study academies back to the time before the Torah was given, and after Sinai they were the way the Torah and the Oral Tradition were passed on. Through the centuries the great centers moved, from the land of Israel to Babylonia, then to Spain, France and Germany, and later to Eastern Europe.

In 1896 Rabbi Shalom DovBer Schneersohn, the fifth Chabad Rebbe, opened a yeshiva in Lubavitch that joined Talmud and Jewish law with daily study of Chassidic teachings. That model inspired Chabad yeshivas around the world, including an underground network in the Soviet Union.

## Chavruta: learning in pairs

The heart of yeshiva study is the **chavruta** (חַבְרוּתָא), a study partner. Two students sit across from one another with the same text, read it aloud, question it and argue about what it means. A good partner makes you sharpen your thinking, and you often understand a passage only when you have to explain it to someone else.

This is why a yeshiva study hall sounds like a busy marketplace of ideas, not a quiet library. Chabad.org notes that many authorities see learning in pairs as the ideal, since the Talmud itself recommends it.

## What is studied

- **Talmud**: the discussions of the Sages, which are the foundation of Jewish law and thought. See [the Talmud](/learn/talmud).
- **Halacha**: Jewish law, what to do in practice. See [what is halacha](/learn/what-is-halacha).
- **Chassidus**: in Chabad yeshivas, a significant part of the day is given to Chassidic teachings about G-d, the soul and spiritual growth. See [Chassidus](/learn/chassidus).
- **Bible, ethics and more**: some programs focus on Tanach, Jewish thought or ethics. See [Tanach](/learn/tanach).

## Words you may hear

- **Mesivta**: a Chabad high-school-level yeshiva.
- **Beit midrash** (בֵּית מִדְרָשׁ): "house of study," the study hall itself.
- **Kollel**: a program for married men who study full time, often with a small stipend.
- **Mashpia**: in Chabad yeshivas, a teacher of Chassidic texts who guides students' spiritual growth.
- **Mashgiach**: supervises students' progress and well-being.
- **Rosh yeshiva**: the head of the academy.
- **Seminary**: the usual English word for a women's school of Jewish learning. Chabad women's seminaries exist in Israel and elsewhere.

## Common questions

### What is a yeshiva in simple words?
A yeshiva is a Jewish school or academy where Torah and the Talmud are studied, often in pairs and with lively debate. The word means "sitting."

### What is a chavruta?
A chavruta is a learning partner. Two people study a text together, read it aloud, ask questions and challenge each other's understanding.

### What is the difference between a yeshiva and a seminary?
"Yeshiva" is usually used for men's study programs and "seminary" for women's, though the words vary by community. Both involve serious Jewish study. Ask the Rabbi what fits your family.

### What do they study in a Chabad yeshiva?
Talmud and Jewish law, along with a daily period of Chassidic teachings. A mashpia guides students in the spiritual side of learning.

### Can adults who never went to yeshiva study Torah?
Yes. Adults of every background learn Torah in classes or with a partner. No prior knowledge is needed.
