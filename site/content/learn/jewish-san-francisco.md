San Francisco has one of the oldest and most colorful Jewish stories in the American West. Whether you are a visitor with a free afternoon or a newcomer settling in, this guide points to long-standing places that tell the story. For anything practical, check each place's own website before you go. We have left out hours, prices and addresses on purpose, because they change.

## Where it all began

Jewish life in San Francisco goes back to the Gold Rush. Congregation Emanu-El describes the first High Holy Day services as held in 1849 in a tent, and was itself founded in 1850 and chartered in 1851. Sherith Israel was founded in the same years. Their story is told in [Jewish history of the Bay Area](/learn/jewish-bay-area-history).

Emanu-El's domed Byzantine Revival building was dedicated in 1926, after the earlier synagogue was destroyed in the 1906 earthquake and fire. Check its website if you would like to visit.

## The Contemporary Jewish Museum

The Contemporary Jewish Museum was founded in 1984 and, in 2008, opened its landmark building in the SoMa neighborhood. Architect Daniel Libeskind added a bold new design to a historic power substation. It is a museum without a permanent collection of its own, with rotating exhibitions about the many ways of being Jewish. Reports say the museum closed to the public at the end of 2024 and later announced plans to sell its building, so please check its current status before planning a visit.

## The Magnes Collection, across the bay

Berkeley is home to the Magnes Collection of Jewish Art and Life, founded in 1962 by Seymour and Rebecca Camhi Fromer and now part of UC Berkeley. It holds more than 45,000 artworks, objects, photographs and manuscripts, including the story of Jews in the American West. For the East Bay as a whole, see [Jewish life in the East Bay](/learn/jewish-east-bay).

## A short drive east

San Francisco is only part of the Bay Area Jewish picture. In the 2018 community study by the Jewish Community Federation, only about 17 percent of the region's Jews lived in the city, and the East Bay was growing. Families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda are just a BART ride or a short drive through the Caldecott Tunnel away. See [visiting Lamorinda](/learn/visiting-lamorinda).

## Common questions

### Where is the Jewish museum in San Francisco?
The Contemporary Jewish Museum is in the SoMa neighborhood. Reports say it closed to the public at the end of 2024, so check its website for its present status before you go.

### What is the oldest synagogue in San Francisco?
Congregation Emanu-El and Congregation Sherith Israel are the city's two historic congregations, both founded around 1849 to 1851 in the Gold Rush years.

### Is there a Jewish museum in the East Bay?
Yes. The Magnes Collection of Jewish Art and Life is in Berkeley, as part of UC Berkeley.

### How many Jews live in San Francisco?
A 2018 study of the Bay Area found roughly 61,000 Jews in the city itself, about 17 percent of the region's Jewish population.

### Is there a Chabad in San Francisco?
Yes, there are Chabad centers in the city. To learn how Chabad works in the Bay Area, read [Chabad in the Bay Area](/learn/chabad-in-the-bay-area).
