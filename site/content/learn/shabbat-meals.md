On Shabbat, it is a mitzvah to eat three festive meals: Friday night dinner, Shabbat lunch, and a third meal in the late afternoon. Each begins with challah, and the first two begin with Kiddush over wine.

## Why we eat festive meals on Shabbat

The prophet Isaiah says, "Call Shabbat a delight" (58:13). One of the main ways we delight in Shabbat is with good food, wine, songs and company. The Talmud teaches that the three meals are hinted to in the Torah, where the word "today" appears three times in the verse about eating the manna on Shabbat (Exodus 16:25).

Eating on Shabbat is not just physical. Chassidic teaching explains that the Shabbat meals elevate the food, the body and the soul, and bring blessing to the entire week.

## The Friday night meal, step by step

1. **Light the [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles)** before sunset.
2. **Sing Shalom Aleichem**, welcoming the Shabbat angels, and Eishet Chayil, "A Woman of Valor," in honor of the women of the home.
3. **Bless the children.** Many parents place their hands on each child's head and say the priestly blessing.
4. **Make [Kiddush](/learn/kiddush)** over a cup of wine or grape juice.
5. **Wash hands** for bread with a cup, and say the blessing al netilat yadayim.
6. **Say Hamotzi** over two whole loaves of [challah](/learn/challah), recalling the double portion of manna that fell before Shabbat. Dip the challah in salt and share it around.
7. **Enjoy the meal.** Share Torah thoughts, songs (zemirot) and stories.
8. **Say Grace after Meals** (Birkat Hamazon).

## Shabbat lunch

The second meal follows the morning prayers. It begins with the daytime Kiddush, which is shorter, followed by washing and Hamotzi over two challahs. Many serve cholent, a slow-cooked stew kept warm from before Shabbat.

## The third meal: Seudah Shlishit

The third meal is eaten in the late afternoon, after the afternoon prayers and before sunset. There is no Kiddush, but ideally it starts with washing and Hamotzi over challah. It is often lighter, with quiet, soulful melodies, as Shabbat is ending. Chassidic teaching calls this a time of great spiritual closeness.

## Traditional Shabbat foods

Every community has its favorites. Common dishes include:

- Challah, often homemade
- Gefilte fish or another fish course
- Chicken soup
- Roast chicken or meat
- Cholent for lunch, kept warm on a blech or hot plate
- Kugel, such as potato or noodle kugel

Foods must be cooked before Shabbat begins and kept warm in ways that follow the laws of Shabbat. See our guide to the [39 melachot](/learn/thirty-nine-melachot).

## Common questions

### Do I have to make all three meals?
All three meals are a mitzvah. If the third is hard, even a small amount of challah or other food at that time is good. Do what you can, and grow from there.

### Can I use grape juice for Kiddush?
Yes. Kosher grape juice may be used for Kiddush in place of wine.

### What if I have no challah?
Two whole loaves of any kosher bread or two matzahs can be used. The bread should be whole.

### Can I invite guests who are not observant?
Absolutely. Sharing a Shabbat meal is one of the warmest ways to welcome someone. See our guide to [hachnasat orchim](/learn/hachnasat-orchim), welcoming guests.
