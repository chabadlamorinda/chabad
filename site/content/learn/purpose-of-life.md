Sooner or later, everyone asks it: *Why am I here? Does my life matter?* Judaism gives a clear and hopeful answer. You are here on purpose, with a mission no one else can do, and you carry it out in the most ordinary places: at home, at work, at the dinner table.

## Why G-d made the world

The Midrash says that G-d "desired a dwelling place in the lower worlds." G-d did not need a world; He wanted one, and specifically this world, physical and busy and often confusing. Chassidic teaching explains that the goal is for this very world to become a home for G-d: a place where goodness and holiness are at home, not only in heaven, but here.

How does a world become a home for G-d? Through people. Every time a person does a [mitzvah](/learn/why-mitzvot), a kind act, an honest deal, a moment of prayer, a part of the world becomes a little more what it was meant to be.

## Why your soul came down

Every Jew has a [soul](/learn/the-soul) that the Tanya calls "truly a part of G-d above." The soul was perfectly content where it was. It came down into a body, with all its struggles, because there is something it can do only here: refine the world around it and bring light into places that were dark.

That means your life is not an accident. The family you were born into, the town you live in, the people you meet, the talents and even the difficulties you have are part of your mission. The Baal Shem Tov taught that a soul may come down for seventy or eighty years just to do a favor for another person.

## Meaning in ordinary things

Judaism does not ask you to leave the world to find meaning. It asks you to bring meaning into it:

- **Eating** becomes holy with a blessing before and after.
- **Work** becomes holy when it is honest and part of it goes to [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).
- **Home** becomes holy with a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) on the door and [Shabbat](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners) at the table.
- **Words** become holy when they encourage instead of hurt.

Maimonides wrote that a person should see the whole world as perfectly balanced between good and bad, so that one more good deed can tip the scale. Every small act counts.

## Where to start

You do not need to change everything at once. Pick one mitzvah and do it with intention this week: light Shabbat candles, put on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin), give a coin to tzedakah each morning, call a lonely relative. That is how a life of purpose is built: one good deed at a time.

## Common questions

### What is the meaning of life according to Judaism?
To make this world a home for G-d by doing good, learning Torah and keeping mitzvot, each person in their own place and in their own way.

### Does my life matter if I am not religious?
Yes. Every Jewish soul is precious and has a mission, and every mitzvah you do is complete and real, whatever else you do or do not do yet.

### How do I find my personal mission?
Look at what is in front of you: your family, your community, your talents, and the needs you notice. The Rabbi is glad to talk it through with you.
