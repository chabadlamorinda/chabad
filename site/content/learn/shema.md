The Shema is the central declaration of Jewish faith: "Hear O Israel, the L-rd is our G-d, the L-rd is One." Jews say it every morning and night, at bedtime, and it is traditionally the first prayer a child learns and the last words a Jew says in life.

## The words of the Shema

**Hebrew:** שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל, ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ, ה' אֶחָד

**Transliteration:** Shema Yisrael, Ado-nai Elo-heinu, Ado-nai Echad.

**Translation:** Hear, O Israel, the L-rd is our G-d, the L-rd is One.

It is followed by a line said quietly:

**Transliteration:** Baruch shem k'vod malchuto l'olam va'ed.

**Translation:** Blessed be the name of the glory of His kingdom forever and ever.

## The full Shema

The first verse comes from Deuteronomy 6:4. The complete Shema has three paragraphs from the Torah:

1. **V'ahavta** (Deuteronomy 6:5 to 9): loving G-d with all your heart, soul and might, teaching these words to your children, and the mitzvot of [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) and [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah).
2. **V'haya im shamoa** (Deuteronomy 11:13 to 21): reward for keeping the mitzvot and the consequences of turning away.
3. **Vayomer** (Numbers 15:37 to 41): the mitzvah of [tzitzit](/learn/tzitzit) and remembering the Exodus from Egypt.

## What the Shema means

The Shema expresses that G-d is One: not only that there is one G-d, but that nothing exists apart from Him. Chassidic teaching explains that everything we see is constantly created by G-d, and the Shema invites us to recognize His presence in every part of life. Saying it every day is called "accepting the yoke of Heaven," dedicating ourselves anew to G-d.

## When to say the Shema

- **In the morning,** as part of Shacharit, before about a quarter of the day has passed. Times change daily, so ask the Rabbi or check a Jewish calendar.
- **At night,** as part of Maariv.
- **At bedtime,** in the bedtime Shema (Kriat Shema al Hamitah).

The Torah says to speak these words "when you lie down and when you rise up," which is the source for saying them morning and night.

## How to say it, step by step

1. **Focus.** Stop what you are doing and concentrate on the meaning.
2. **Cover your eyes** with your right hand while saying the first verse, to block out distractions.
3. **Say the first verse aloud**, stretching out the word "Echad" and thinking that G-d is One and rules over all the heavens, the earth and the four directions.
4. **Whisper "Baruch shem..."**
5. **Continue** with the three paragraphs if you can.

## The bedtime Shema

Before going to sleep, many say the bedtime Shema. It begins with a short prayer forgiving anyone who hurt us, followed by the Shema and other verses for protection. It is a beautiful way to end the day with a clean heart, and a wonderful habit to teach children.

## Common questions

### Why do we cover our eyes during the Shema?
Covering the eyes helps us concentrate fully on the meaning of the words and shut out the world around us. It reflects the inner focus the Shema asks of us.

### Why is "Baruch shem" said quietly?
Tradition teaches that Moses heard this praise from the angels. Since it is an angelic praise, we whisper it all year. On Yom Kippur, when we are compared to angels, it is said aloud.

### Can I say the Shema in English?
Yes. The Shema may be said in any language you understand. Many people say at least the first verse in Hebrew, since it is so central and easy to learn.

### Is it okay to say only the first line?
Saying the first line is meaningful and important, especially at the beginning. The complete mitzvah includes the first paragraph and ideally all three. Add more as you are able.
