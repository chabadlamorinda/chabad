**Kiddush Hashem** means "sanctifying G-d's name." The Torah says, "I shall be sanctified among the children of Israel" (Leviticus 22:32). Every Jew represents G-d and the Jewish people, and every Jew can make G-d's name beloved in the world.

## Not only heroes

Throughout history, Jews who gave their lives rather than give up their faith were said to die "al Kiddush Hashem." We honor them always. But most Kiddush Hashem happens in ordinary moments: at the checkout counter, in an email, in traffic, at the office.

## What the Talmud says

The Talmud (Yoma 86a) explains the verse "You shall love the L-rd your G-d" to mean: make G-d's name beloved through you. When a person learns Torah, deals honestly in business and speaks gently to people, others say, "Fortunate is the one who taught him Torah. Look how fine his ways are."

A famous story: the sage Shimon ben Shetach bought a donkey from an Arab merchant, and his students found a precious gem tied to it. He insisted on returning it, saying he had bought a donkey, not a gem. The merchant exclaimed, "Blessed is the G-d of Shimon ben Shetach!" (Jerusalem Talmud, Bava Metzia 2:5). That is Kiddush Hashem.

## Kiddush Hashem day to day

- **Honesty:** return extra change, report income truthfully, keep your promises. See [Honesty and Truth](/learn/honesty-and-truth).
- **At work:** do a full day's work, treat coworkers and employees with respect, pay on time. See [Business Ethics](/learn/business-ethics).
- **Online:** before posting, ask whether it brings honor or shame to the Jewish people. Avoid mockery and [lashon hara](/learn/lashon-hara).
- **In public:** a kind word to a cashier, patience in a line, holding the door. People notice, especially if you wear a kippah or are known to be Jewish.
- **Kindness to everyone:** helping a neighbor of any background makes G-d's name beloved. See [Chesed](/learn/chesed).
- **Proud and open:** lighting a menorah in the window, keeping Shabbat at work politely and clearly. See [Jewish Pride](/learn/jewish-pride).

## The opposite: chillul Hashem

**Chillul Hashem**, "desecrating G-d's name," is when a person's behavior makes people think less of G-d, the Torah or the Jewish people. The Talmud (Yoma 86a) counts it among the most serious wrongs, and says the more a person is known as religious, the more careful they must be. A rabbi in the Talmud said that for him, buying meat and not paying right away would be a chillul Hashem.

The good news: [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah) works, and the best repair for a chillul Hashem is a Kiddush Hashem.

## Common questions

### Do I have to be observant to make a Kiddush Hashem?
No. Any Jew who acts with honesty and kindness, and is known to be Jewish, sanctifies G-d's name.

### Is it a Kiddush Hashem when non-Jews see good behavior?
Yes, especially then. The story of Shimon ben Shetach is about exactly that.

### What if I made a mistake in public?
Apologize, fix what you can, and move on. Admitting a mistake honestly is itself a Kiddush Hashem.

### How can I teach my children about Kiddush Hashem?
Point it out when you see it, and let them see you do it: returning a lost item, thanking a waiter, being patient. Children learn most from what they watch.
