Ezekiel (Yechezkel, יְחֶזְקֵאל) was a prophet who lived in Babylonia at the time of the destruction of the First Temple. He is best known for his vision of the Divine chariot and the valley of dry bones, a message that a people that thought it was finished would live again.

## Who was Ezekiel?

According to Chabad.org's summary, Ezekiel was born in the Land of Israel, the son of Buzi, a priest, and he began prophesying there. When Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylonia, conquered Jerusalem, he exiled King Jehoiachin and about ten thousand captives, including nobles, leaders and craftsmen. Ezekiel was among them. He spent the rest of his life in Babylonia and received most of his prophecies there, beside the Chebar River.

That matters. People thought that prophecy was tied to the Land of Israel. Ezekiel showed that G-d is with the Jewish people wherever they go, a message for every Jewish family far from home, and for any of us who feel far from our roots.

Ezekiel did not write his prophecies himself. According to the sources, the Men of the Great Assembly later wrote them down as the Book of Ezekiel.

## The vision of the Divine chariot

While he was by the Chebar River, Ezekiel saw a vision of G-d's throne, carried by four living creatures, each with four faces: a human, a lion, an ox and an eagle (the Hebrew word may also mean a vulture). The Talmud calls this subject Maaseh Merkavah, "the work of the chariot," and treats it as one of the deepest secrets of the Torah. Kabbalah and Chassidic teaching draw heavily on it.

This is why the passage is read as the haftarah on the first day of Shavuot, the holiday that celebrates the giving of the Torah. See [Shavuot for beginners](/learn/shavuot-for-beginners). Because the subject is so deep, we leave the details to teachers: if you want to study it, ask the Rabbi.

## The valley of dry bones

The most famous prophecy of Ezekiel is the valley of dry bones. G-d took Ezekiel to a valley full of dry human bones and told him to speak to them. The bones came together, were covered with sinews, flesh and skin, and then, when Ezekiel called to the spirit, they stood up as a great army.

The explanation is given in the text: the people of Israel were saying, "Our bones are dry, our hope is gone, we are cut off." G-d answered through Ezekiel that He would open their graves, bring them back to their land and place His spirit within them. This is a message of hope for anyone who feels finished: a person, a family or a people.

The Talmud debates whether the dry bones were a prophetic vision or a real event. One sage, Rabbi Yehudah ben Beteira, said he was a descendant of those who rose, and that he had inherited tefillin from them. Jewish tradition also draws on this passage when it speaks about [the resurrection of the dead](/learn/resurrection-of-the-dead).

## The Tenth of Tevet

G-d told Ezekiel to record the exact date: the tenth day of Tevet, the day the king of Babylonia began to besiege Jerusalem. That day is remembered with a fast every winter, the Tenth of Tevet, a day of repentance and reflection. See [the minor fast days](/learn/minor-fast-days).

Before the destruction, Ezekiel warned the people, sometimes with symbolic acts. After the news came that the city had fallen, he turned to words of comfort and the promise of return. The sources also credit him with a detailed vision of the future Temple.

## Lessons for today

- **Hope is never lost.** The dry bones teach that G-d can bring life where we see none.
- **G-d is with us everywhere.** A prophet can hear G-d by a river in a foreign land, and a Jew in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda can be close to G-d too.
- **Speak up.** Ezekiel was told he was like a watchman who must warn the people. Each of us has a role in encouraging others.

## Common questions

### Who was the prophet Ezekiel?
Ezekiel (Yechezkel) was a prophet of the Babylonian exile, a son of a priest named Buzi. He prophesied to the Jews exiled from Jerusalem and gave them hope of return.

### What is the vision of the chariot?
It is Ezekiel's vision of G-d's throne carried by four creatures with four faces each. Jewish tradition calls it Maaseh Merkavah and treats it as a deep mystical teaching.

### What does the valley of dry bones mean?
It is a promise that the Jewish people, who felt hopeless in exile, would be revived and return to their land. Jewish tradition also draws on it when speaking of the resurrection of the dead.

### Why is Ezekiel read on Shavuot?
His vision of G-d's throne is the haftarah on the first day of Shavuot, the holiday of the giving of the Torah.

### What is the Tenth of Tevet?
It is the winter fast day marking the beginning of the siege of Jerusalem. Ezekiel was told to record the date.

### Where did Ezekiel live?
He was born in the Land of Israel and spent most of his life in exile in Babylonia.
