A memorial plaque in the synagogue is a lasting way to remember a loved one, and at Chabad of Lamorinda a plaque can be added in the synagogue; please consult Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski at 925-384-7242 for the details. A plaque is a place where a name is kept in view, alongside the Jewish ways of remembering: Kaddish, tzedakah, learning and the yahrzeit.

## What a plaque is, and what it adds

A plaque is not a replacement for the grave or for the mitzvot done in memory of the person. It is a quiet place in the synagogue where family, friends and the next generation can see a name and remember. For the headstone at the grave, see [the headstone inscription](/learn/jewish-headstone-inscription) and [the unveiling](/learn/unveiling).

To add a plaque, consult the Rabbi: call 925-384-7242. We do not publish prices or sizes in a guide; the Rabbi will speak with you personally and with sensitivity. Families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, and those who now live far away, are all welcome to ask.

## The ways Judaism remembers, which a plaque joins

Jewish tradition for the yahrzeit, the anniversary of the death on the Hebrew calendar, lists the customs that keep a person's memory alive, and the plaque goes beautifully alongside them:

- **A yahrzeit candle.** The custom is to light a candle at nightfall on the evening before the anniversary. It is customary to let it burn out by itself, unless there is a real fire risk. (See [the yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit).)
- **Kaddish.** The Mourner's Kaddish is said at every service on the yahrzeit, and for 11 months after the death. Chabad of Lamorinda can arrange Kaddish for a loved one: use the form at [/kaddish](/kaddish), or see [saying Kaddish for someone else](/learn/saying-kaddish-for-someone-else).
- **Tzedakah.** Give to a synagogue, a school, a hospital or the poor in the person's memory. See [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).
- **Learning.** Standard guidance says Mishnah is the traditional study for a yahrzeit; if that is not possible, a chapter of the Bible. See [learning in memory of a loved one](/learn/learning-in-memory-of-a-loved-one).
- **Visiting the grave,** reading Psalms and saying the memorial prayer *El Malei Rachamim* yourself (see [El Malei Rachamim](/learn/el-malei-rachamim) and [visiting a grave](/learn/visiting-a-grave)).

At the holidays, the memorial service called [Yizkor](/learn/yizkor) remembers loved ones. We do not describe other synagogue customs here; for how names are read or remembered at services in Lamorinda, call the Rabbi.

## Passing it on

Many families like to teach their children about the person on the plaque: a story, a favorite saying, a mitzvah in their name. That is how a name becomes a legacy. If you would like to leave a gift that will support Jewish life in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda for generations, see [/legacy](/legacy).

## Common questions

### Can I add a memorial plaque for my loved one in the synagogue?

Yes. At Chabad of Lamorinda a plaque can be added in the synagogue. Please consult Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 for the details.

### How much does a memorial plaque cost?

We do not list prices here. Call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 and he will speak with you.

### Is a plaque a replacement for Kaddish or a headstone?

No. A plaque is a place of remembrance. Kaddish, tzedakah, learning and the yahrzeit continue as the Jewish ways of honoring a soul, and the headstone marks the grave.

### Can Kaddish be arranged for a loved one who passed away?

Yes. Chabad of Lamorinda can arrange Kaddish for a loved one. Fill in the form at [/kaddish](/kaddish) or call 925-384-7242.

### What do Jews do on a yahrzeit?

According to Standard guidance: light a yahrzeit candle, say Kaddish, give tzedakah, learn Torah, and visit the grave if possible. See [yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit).

### Can I remember a loved one if I live far away?

Yes. Call the Rabbi, who will tell you how you can take part in remembrance from a distance.
