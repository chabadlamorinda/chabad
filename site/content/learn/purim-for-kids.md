Purim is the happiest day of the Jewish year, and children love it: costumes, noise, treats and presents for friends. Every part of the day is a mitzvah that kids can do themselves.

## Tell the story simply

"A long time ago in Persia, there was a wicked man named Haman who wanted to hurt all the Jews. But a brave Jewish queen named Esther and her wise cousin Mordechai prayed, and the Jewish people came together. Esther spoke to King Achashverosh, Haman's plan was turned upside down, and the Jews were saved. So we celebrate with lots of joy!" The full story is in the Megillah, the Scroll of Esther. See our [Megillah](/learn/megillah) guide.

## The four mitzvahs, in kid words

1. **Listen to the Megillah, twice.** Once on Purim night and once on Purim day. Every word counts, so listen closely. When the reader says "Haman," everyone shakes a grogger or stamps their feet to "blot out" his name, then quiets down so the next words can be heard.
2. **Mishloach manot: food gifts to friends.** Give at least one friend a package with two different ready-to-eat foods, like a hamantasch and an apple. See [mishloach manot](/learn/mishloach-manot).
3. **Matanot la'evyonim: gifts to the poor.** Give money to at least two people in need on Purim day. Children can put coins in the [pushka](/learn/pushka) or hand them to the Rabbi to pass on. See [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).
4. **The Purim feast.** A happy, festive meal on Purim afternoon with family and friends. See [the Purim seudah](/learn/purim-seudah).

Girls and boys do all four mitzvahs, and even little ones can hold the coins or carry the package to the door.

## Why we dress up

G-d's name does not appear even once in the whole Megillah. The miracle of Purim looked like a string of ordinary events, but G-d was there behind every one. On Purim we "hide" too: behind a mask or a costume, the real person is still inside. Many children dress as Queen Esther or Mordechai; any happy costume is fine. Ideas are in our [Purim costumes](/learn/purim-costumes) guide.

## Making mishloach manot together

- Let each child choose a friend, a teacher or a neighbor to give to.
- Fill a paper bag, a small box or a decorated plate with two kinds of food: a cookie and a clementine, a juice box and pretzels.
- Kids can draw on the bags, add stickers, or write a short "Happy Purim!" note.
- Make an extra package for someone who might be lonely, like an older neighbor.
- Deliver on Purim day, in costume, and let the children knock on the door.

## Baking hamantaschen

Hamantaschen are three-cornered cookies filled with jam, chocolate or poppy seed. Children can roll the dough, cut circles with a cup, add a spoon of filling and pinch three corners together. Bake a big batch the day before Purim, so there is plenty for the mishloach manot and the feast.

## Common questions

### Can young children come to the Megillah reading?
Yes. Children are part of the mitzvah and the fun, and groggers are made for them. If a little one gets restless, step out for a moment and come back for the end.

### Does a child need to give their own gifts to the poor?
It is a beautiful habit to let children give with their own hands, even a few coins, so they learn that joy means sharing. For exact amounts and details, ask the Rabbi.

### Are there costumes we should avoid?
Choose costumes that are happy and kind. Many families avoid scary or violent costumes, and the custom is for boys and girls each to dress in their own style of clothing. Ask the Rabbi if you are unsure.
