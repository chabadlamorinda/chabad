The East Bay is not one community but many neighborhoods and towns, each with its own Jewish flavor. Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, together called Lamorinda, sit in the hills between Oakland and Walnut Creek. Chabad of Lamorinda, in Lafayette, is the Jewish home for families in all three towns, with no membership and no dues.

## The big picture

A 2018 study by the Jewish Community Federation counted about 350,000 Jews across the ten-county Bay Area. It found that the East Bay's Jewish adult population had grown by about a third since 2011, while San Francisco's share was about 17 percent of the total. In other words, more and more Bay Area Jewish families live on this side of the bridge. You can read how it began in [Jewish history of the Bay Area](/learn/jewish-bay-area-history).

## How the East Bay fits together

Think of it as a string of communities along the hills and freeways:

- **Berkeley and Oakland** to the west, with a long Jewish history. Berkeley is home to the Magnes Collection of Jewish Art and Life, part of UC Berkeley. Women in Lamorinda generally use the Chabad mikvah in Berkeley, arranged with the Rebbetzin. See [mikvah](/learn/mikvah).
- **Lamorinda** in the middle: Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda. Moraga has no synagogue of its own, so Moraga families come to Lafayette, about ten minutes away. Orinda is a few minutes east on Highway 24, or one stop on BART.
- **Pleasant Hill and Martinez** just north and northeast, about 15 and 20 minutes from us. See [Jewish Pleasant Hill](/jewish-pleasant-hill) and [Jewish Martinez](/jewish-martinez).
- **Walnut Creek and the Tri-Valley** farther east and south, with their own Jewish communities and congregations. We do not list specific organizations here. If you would like a recommendation, ask the Rabbi.

## What this means for your family

Because the East Bay is spread out, many families belong to more than one community. Your child may go to school in one town, your parents may live in another, and a friend may invite you to a Shabbat dinner over the hill. That is wonderful. Judaism has always been about connection, and a Jew is welcome wherever they walk in.

If you live in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda, the community closest to you is a few minutes away: Shabbat services and a hot Kiddush lunch, [Lamorinda Jewish Preschool](/programs/jewish-preschool), [Hebrew School](/programs/hebrew-school), [adult classes](/programs/adult-education) and holiday celebrations. See [Jewish Lafayette](/jewish-lafayette), [Jewish Moraga](/jewish-moraga) and [Jewish Orinda](/jewish-orinda).

## Common questions

### How many Jews live in the East Bay?
The 2018 Bay Area study found that the East Bay's Jewish adult population had grown by about 33 percent since 2011. For the exact current number, see the Jewish Community Federation's community studies.

### Where can Jews in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda go for Shabbat?
Chabad of Lamorinda, at 3477 Golden Gate Way in Lafayette, welcomes every Jew for Shabbat. See [Shabbat in Lamorinda](/learn/shabbat-in-lamorinda) or call 925-384-7242.

### Is there a synagogue in Moraga?
No. Moraga has no synagogue of its own, so Moraga families come to Chabad of Lamorinda in Lafayette, about ten minutes away.

### Where do women in Lamorinda go to the mikvah?
Women in Lamorinda generally use the Chabad mikvah in Berkeley, arranged with the Rebbetzin. Call or text Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski privately at 925-384-7241.

### Is there a Jewish museum in the East Bay?
Yes. The Magnes Collection of Jewish Art and Life is in Berkeley and is part of UC Berkeley.
