Light Up Lafayette is Chabad of Lamorinda's Grand Chanukah Menorah Lighting, held this year on Sunday, December 6, 2026 at 4:00 PM at Lafayette Plaza Park (the corner of Mount Diablo Blvd and Moraga Rd in Downtown Lafayette). It is free and open to everyone, and you do not need to be a member of anything or know any Hebrew to join in.

## What is happening at Light Up Lafayette?

Families from Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, and from all around the East Bay, gather in the park for an afternoon built around one big menorah and a lot of fun. This year's program includes:

- **A Grand Chanukah Gelt Drop**
- **Decorate Your Own Donut**
- **A Giant Dreidel Game**
- **LED light wands for the kids**
- **Free hot cocoa, donuts and gelt**
- **Local dignitaries**, who take part in the celebration

Please [sign up](/events/chanukah-grand-menorah-lighting-2026) so we can plan enough donuts and cocoa for everyone. It takes about a minute. If you would like to help welcome every family, you can [sponsor or give](/donate?fund=holidays#give).

## What is a public menorah lighting, and why do we do it?

Chanukah is about light winning over darkness, and the sages taught that the menorah should be seen, so that the miracle is made public. A public lighting takes that idea out of our living rooms and into the middle of town. Chabad.org describes the usual pattern: an oversized menorah, short blessings before the lighting, songs afterward and refreshments, often with crafts and activities for children. Chabad.org also notes that attending is simple: you need to bring nothing, and outdoor events call for warm clothing.

Chabad has been putting up public menorahs since the 1970s, when the Rebbe launched a campaign to bring Chanukah out of the home and into public life. Today there are public menorahs in cities all over the world. To read more, see [Public menorah lightings](/learn/public-menorah-lighting).

## Why come? Four good reasons

Chabad.org lists several reasons to attend a public lighting. Here are the ones that matter most to us in Lamorinda:

1. **Standing together.** A crowd of Jews and friends celebrating openly is a quiet, strong answer to anything that tries to frighten us.
2. **Meeting neighbors.** You may meet Jewish families who live a few streets away, and a friendship may start over a donut.
3. **Pride for our children.** Kids see a whole park celebrating a Jewish holiday, proudly and in the open.
4. **Unplugging.** It is an hour or two of real conversation, music and cocoa, with no screens.

## A good plan for the day

1. **Sign up** on the [event page](/events/chanukah-grand-menorah-lighting-2026).
2. **Dress warmly.** It is outdoors, in the afternoon in December.
3. **Bring a friend,** especially someone who has never been to a Chanukah event.
4. **Light at home too.** A public lighting is wonderful, but it does not replace lighting your own menorah at home. Chabad.org says that those who attend should still light at home. [How to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah) walks you through it, and [tonight's candles](/menorah) shows you the order.
5. **Need a menorah, candles, oil or donuts for your own home?** [Bay Kosher](/programs/bay-kosher), a project of Chabad of Lamorinda, is always kosher, and the [Chanukah shopping guide](/learn/chanukah-shopping-in-lamorinda) lists what to get.

## Common questions

### When and where is the Chanukah menorah lighting in Lafayette?
Chabad of Lamorinda's Grand Chanukah Menorah Lighting is on Sunday, December 6, 2026 at 4:00 PM at Lafayette Plaza Park, at the corner of Mount Diablo Blvd and Moraga Rd in Downtown Lafayette.

### Is Light Up Lafayette free?
Yes. It is free and open to everyone, with free hot cocoa, donuts and gelt. We ask you to sign up so we can plan enough for everyone.

### Do I have to be Jewish or a member to come?
No. The event is open to everyone. No membership, no tickets, and no background is needed. Jewish families from Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda and their neighbors and friends are all welcome.

### What will the children do?
There is a Giant Dreidel Game, a Gelt Drop, Decorate Your Own Donut and LED light wands for the kids, as well as free donuts, cocoa and gelt.

### Are blessings said at a public menorah lighting?
Often, yes. Chabad.org notes that blessings may be left out at a public lighting that does not follow Jewish law, for example if it is electric or at the wrong hour. Either way, say the blessings when you light at home. Ask the Rabbi if you would like to know more.

### Can I light my own menorah at home if I went to the public lighting?
Yes, and you should. A public lighting adds to the home lighting; it does not replace it.
