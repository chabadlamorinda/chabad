A kippah, also called a yarmulke, is the small cap worn by Jewish men and boys as a sign of reverence for G-d. It reminds the wearer, and everyone around him, that there is always Someone above.

## What it is

Kippah is Hebrew for "dome" or "covering." Yarmulke is the Yiddish word, often explained as coming from the Aramaic phrase yarei malka, "awe of the King." Kippot come in many styles: black velvet, knitted, suede, leather and printed. In some communities the style reflects a particular custom or group, but every kippah fulfills the same purpose.

## Why Jewish men cover their heads

Covering the head is not one of the 613 commandments written in the Torah. It is an ancient Jewish custom that became an accepted practice with the force of law.

The Talmud tells of a mother who was told by astrologers that her son would become a thief. She made sure he always kept his head covered, telling him, "Cover your head, so that the fear of Heaven will be upon you." He grew up to be the great sage Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak.

The Talmud also records that great sages would not walk even a short distance with their heads uncovered, saying that the Divine Presence is above.

## When to wear a kippah

- **At all times.** The traditional practice, followed in Chabad, is for men and boys to keep their heads covered all day.
- **At minimum:** during prayer, Torah study, when saying blessings, and when eating.
- **In synagogue,** cemeteries and Jewish ceremonies.

Many families put a kippah on boys from a very young age, often at the [upsherin](/learn/upsherin) at age three, along with tzitzit.

## What it means

- **Humility.** There is a Higher Power above me, and I am not the center of the world.
- **Awareness.** A constant reminder that G-d sees and cares about what we do.
- **Identity.** It is a visible, proud sign of being Jewish. See our guide to [Jewish pride](/learn/jewish-pride).

## Wearing a kippah in public

Some feel hesitant to wear a kippah at work or in public. Every person's situation is different, and the Rabbi is happy to talk through it with you. Many find that wearing a kippah brings respect and opens conversations, and it reminds them of who they are wherever they go. Some wear a baseball cap or other hat over the kippah.

## Common questions

### Do women wear a kippah?
Traditionally, no. Married Jewish women cover their hair, which is a separate mitzvah with its own meaning. Unmarried girls and women do not need a head covering.

### Should a non-Jewish guest wear a kippah in synagogue?
It is a common courtesy for all men, Jewish or not, to cover their heads in synagogue or at a Jewish ceremony, much as one removes a hat elsewhere. Kippot are usually available at the door.

### Does a kippah need to be a certain size?
It should cover a meaningful part of the head, and it should be visible from most sides. A tiny kippah barely seen may not fulfill the custom well.

### What about sleeping?
Many have the custom to keep a kippah on while sleeping. If it falls off at night, there is no need to worry.
