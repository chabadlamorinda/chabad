Elijah the Prophet (Eliyahu HaNavi, אליהו הנביא) was a Biblical prophet who, tradition says, never died but rose to heaven, and who will return to announce the coming of Moshiach. Jewish families remember him with Elijah's Cup and the open door at the Passover Seder, the chair at a bris, and a song after Shabbat.

## His story

Elijah's story is told in the Book of Kings (I Kings 17 to II Kings 2). He came from Gilead and warned King Achav of a drought, then hid by a stream where ravens brought him food. He stayed with a poor widow whose food never ran out, and when her son died, he prayed and the boy lived. At Mount Carmel he challenged 450 priests of Baal in front of the people. Their offering brought no fire, but when Elijah prayed, fire came down from heaven, and the people cried out that G-d alone is G-d. When Queen Jezebel threatened him, he fled forty days to Mount Sinai, where G-d spoke to him not in wind, earthquake or fire, but in a still, small voice. He ended his life on earth when a chariot of fire took him up to heaven.

## Elijah and the bris

At a bris (circumcision), tradition says, Elijah is present. The Midrash says that at Mount Sinai Elijah complained that the people had stopped circumcising their sons, and G-d promised he would be present at every bris to see that the covenant is kept. For this reason a chair is set aside for Elijah, "the angel of the covenant." Some families leave it empty, and in some places, including the widespread Chabad custom, the sandek who holds the baby sits on it. Ask the Rabbi or the mohel about the custom for your family. See [brit milah](/learn/brit-milah).

## Elijah at the Seder

At the [Passover Seder](/learn/passover-seder), after the Grace After Meals, we pour a special cup of wine and open the front door. Chabad.org gives several reasons.

- **A guarded night.** Passover night is called "leil shimurim," a night of protection, when G-d protected the Jewish people. Opening the door shows our trust.
- **The bris connection.** Only the circumcised could eat the Passover offering. Elijah witnesses every bris, so his presence confirms everyone at the table is part of the covenant.
- **Why a fifth cup.** The Talmud left open whether there should be four or five cups. We drink four, matching four promises of redemption, and pour a fifth that we do not drink, matching the promise "I will bring you into the Land." Elijah will one day settle every unresolved question, so it is "Elijah's Cup."
- **Moshiach.** We pour it just before the second half of Hallel, which looks to the final redemption, and we express hope for Moshiach, whom Elijah will announce.

## Elijah after Shabbat

Many people sing "Eliyahu HaNavi" after Shabbat ends, at [Havdalah](/learn/havdalah). The reason is the belief that Elijah will come to announce the redemption, and the week begins with hope for it. Customs on how to say or sing it differ, so ask the Rabbi what we do.

## The herald of Moshiach

The prophet Malachi promised that Elijah would come before "the great and awesome day of G-d." Jewish tradition says he will announce [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach) and bring peace and unity to families. That is why his name is on the lips of Jews at the happiest and most hopeful moments.

## Common questions

### Who is Elijah the Prophet in Judaism?
Elijah is a Biblical prophet who is said to be present at every bris and who will announce Moshiach.

### Why do we open the door for Elijah at the Seder?
We open the door to show trust in G-d's protection on the "guarded night" of Passover, and to welcome Elijah, who will announce the final redemption.

### What is Elijah's Cup?
Elijah's Cup is the fifth cup of wine poured at the Seder but not drunk. It represents a question left open by the Talmud, and the final redemption.

### Why is there a chair of Elijah at a bris?
Because Elijah is "the angel of the covenant," present at every bris to witness that the covenant is kept. Ask the Rabbi or the mohel about family custom.

### Did Elijah die?
The Bible says he rose to heaven in a chariot of fire. Jewish tradition says he still appears to help people, and will return before Moshiach.

### Do I need to use a special cup?
Many families use a beautiful cup, but any cup will do. Ask the Rabbi about how to do it in your home.
