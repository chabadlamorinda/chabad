When a Jewish baby is born, say "Mazal tov!" (מַזָּל טוֹב, "good luck" or "congratulations") to the parents. A traditional blessing to add is that they raise the child to Torah, chuppah and good deeds, meaning a life of Jewish learning, marriage and kindness.

If a friend in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda has just had a baby, you do not need a speech. Here are simple words, customs and gifts that fit Jewish tradition.

## Mazal tov, and what it means

"Mazal tov" is the standard greeting for any joy, and for a birth it is perfect. You can say it in person, by text, or on a card. The custom for guests at a bris is the same: "Mazal tov" or "Congratulations" is all that is needed, and compliments on the baby or the name are welcome.

## The traditional blessing

Jewish tradition notes that it is customary to wish new parents that they raise their child to Torah, chuppah and maasim tovim, which means learning, marriage and good deeds. This echoes the blessing said at a bris and at a baby naming, wishing that just as the baby entered the covenant, so may he enter Torah, marriage and good deeds. For a girl, the wish is the same: a life of Torah, a Jewish home and good deeds. If you like, add the wish for Jewish "nachas" (נַחַת), the quiet joy a parent has from a child.

## When to say it

- **After the birth.** Call or text as soon as you hear. Hold off on visiting until they invite you.
- **At the bris or naming.** See [attending a bris](/learn/attending-a-bris) and [baby naming](/learn/baby-naming).
- **At Shabbat.** If your synagogue has a Shabbat blessing for the family, say "Mazal tov" at the Kiddush after the service. See [Shalom Zachar](/learn/shalom-zachar).
- **Before the birth?** Many Jewish families wait to buy baby gifts until the baby is born. See [Jewish baby shower](/learn/jewish-baby-shower) for more.

## Gift ideas that mean something

Gifts are a kindness, never an obligation. Ideas:

1. **A meal.** Often the most appreciated gift. See [bringing meals to new parents](/learn/meals-for-new-parents).
2. **A children's book of Jewish stories or Psalms,** or a first aleph-bet book.
3. **A tzedakah box** for the baby's room. See [the tzedakah box](/learn/pushka).
4. **A donation in the baby's honor** to a cause the parents love.
5. **A letter in a children's Torah scroll.** See [a letter in a Torah](/learn/letter-in-a-torah).
6. **A soft blanket or outfit.** A small gift is always welcome.

More ideas are in [Jewish gift ideas](/learn/jewish-gift-ideas) and [a Jewish nursery](/learn/jewish-nursery).

## What not to say

- Avoid asking about the name before the ceremony; many families keep it quiet until the bris or naming.
- Avoid advice on how to parent unless it is asked for.
- If the birth was difficult or the baby is in the hospital, simply say you are thinking of them and offer to help.

## Common questions

### What do you say to new Jewish parents?
Say "Mazal tov!" Many add a wish that the parents raise the baby to Torah, marriage and good deeds.

### What does "Mazal tov" mean?
It means "good fortune" and is used to say congratulations on a happy occasion. It is the standard greeting for a birth.

### Should I bring a gift for a Jewish baby?
A gift is welcome but not required. A meal, a book, a tzedakah box or a small outfit are all good choices.

### Do Jews give baby gifts before the baby is born?
Many families wait until after the birth. See [Jewish baby shower](/learn/jewish-baby-shower), and ask the family what they prefer.

### Is it okay to ask a baby's name?
You can ask, but many families share it only at the bris or naming. Respect the family's wish.

### How do I say congratulations in Hebrew and Yiddish?
In Hebrew, "Mazal tov" (מזל טוב), and in Yiddish, "Mazel tov." Both are understood.
