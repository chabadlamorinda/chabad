Judaism teaches us to notice. Besides blessings over food, there are short blessings for moments of wonder: a flash of lightning, a rainbow after the rain, the first sight of the ocean. Each one takes a few seconds and turns "Wow!" into a moment of connection with the Creator.

Every blessing begins: **Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu Melech ha-olam,** "Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe," and ends with the words below.

## In the sky

- **Lightning**, and also shooting stars and striking natural sights: **oseh ma'aseh vereishit**, "who makes the work of creation."
- **Thunder**: **shekocho ugvurato malei olam**, "whose strength and power fill the world."
- **A rainbow**: **zocher habrit, vene'eman bivrito, vekayam bema'amaro**, "who remembers the covenant, is faithful to His covenant and keeps His word." The rainbow recalls G-d's promise to Noah. It is customary not to stare at it for long.

The blessing for lightning or thunder is said once per storm; if the clouds clear completely and a new storm comes, say it again.

## On the earth

- **The ocean**, seen after not having seen it for 30 days: **she'asah et hayam hagadol**, "who made the great sea." Opinions differ about which seas this applies to, so many say **oseh ma'aseh vereishit** instead. Ask the Rabbi before your next trip to the Pacific coast.
- **Majestic mountains, great deserts or other awe-inspiring landscapes**: **oseh ma'aseh vereishit**.
- **Fruit trees in blossom**, once a year in the spring month of Nisan: **shelo chisar be'olamo kelum, uvara vo beriyot tovot ve'ilanot tovot, leihanot bahem bnei adam**, "who has withheld nothing from His world, and created in it good creatures and good trees for people to enjoy."

## Moments with people

- **Seeing a dear friend after 30 days**, with real joy: [Shehecheyanu](/learn/shehecheyanu). After more than a year: **mechayeh hametim**, "who revives the dead."
- **Seeing a great Torah scholar**: **shechalak mechochmato lirei'av**, "who has shared His wisdom with those who revere Him."

## Why bless the weather?

Because nothing is "just nature." The Baal Shem Tov taught that G-d creates the world anew at every moment. A blessing is a way of saying: I see You in this.

## Common questions

### Do I need to know Hebrew?
The Hebrew is best, but a blessing said in your own language with G-d's name and kingship is valid. Start with the one you will use most, and say it slowly.

### I saw the lightning but missed the blessing. Can I say it later?
These blessings are said right away, within a few seconds of seeing or hearing. If you missed it, wait for the next flash or thunderclap.

### Is there a blessing for an earthquake?
Yes: the same blessing as for thunder, **shekocho ugvurato malei olam**, or **oseh ma'aseh vereishit**.
