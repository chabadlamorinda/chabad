Every October, costumes, candy and pumpkins fill the stores and the neighborhood. Many Jewish parents wonder what to do about Halloween. Here is the Jewish view, and practical, friendly ideas for your family.

## Where Halloween comes from

Halloween grew out of an ancient pagan festival marking the start of winter, later joined to the Christian holy day of All Saints, "All Hallows." Today most people celebrate it simply as a fun night, but its roots and many of its symbols come from other religions' beliefs about spirits and the dead.

## The Jewish view

Judaism asks us not to adopt the religious customs of other nations (the Torah calls them "chukot hagoyim"). For this reason, Jewish tradition does not celebrate Halloween, and Chabad encourages families not to make it part of their Jewish home.

This is not about being unfriendly or making children feel left out. It is about giving them something better: a Jewish identity with its own joy, its own costumes and its own celebrations. Every family's situation is different; if you have questions, talk with the Rabbi.

## Ideas for your family

- **Dress up on Purim.** Judaism has its own costume day, full of joy, gifts of food and charity. Remind children it is coming: see [Purim costumes](/learn/purim-costumes).
- **Plan a family night.** On October 31, make it a cozy evening: a favorite dinner, a game or a movie at home.
- **Be a good neighbor.** If children come to your door, it is perfectly kind to greet them warmly. Many Jewish families give out treats simply as a neighborly gesture, without decorations or celebrating. Others leave the porch light off. Do what feels right for your home.
- **At school.** If the class has a Halloween party, talk with the teacher ahead of time. Many teachers are happy to offer another activity, and your child can bring a kosher treat to share.
- **Point to what is coming.** Shabbat every week, then Chanukah, with lights, gelt and doughnuts. See [Jewish in December](/learn/jewish-in-december).

## Common questions

### Is Halloween a religious holiday?
For most people today it is a secular night of fun, but it began as a pagan festival and is connected to a Christian holy day. That history is why Jewish tradition does not celebrate it.

### Can my kids go trick-or-treating?
Jewish tradition does not encourage it. Many parents plan a special family activity that night instead. For your family's situation, ask the Rabbi.

### Is Halloween candy kosher?
Some is and some is not. Check for a reliable kosher symbol; see our [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols) guide.

### What if my child feels left out?
Give them a Jewish celebration of their own to look forward to, and lots of warmth. Purim, Chanukah and Shabbat are full of the fun children want.
