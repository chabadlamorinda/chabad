When the last child leaves home, many parents in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda feel proud, lonely and a little lost at once. Judaism sees this as a new chapter, not an ending: a time to grow in your own learning, to reconnect with your spouse and to build a community of friends, while staying close to your grown children.

## It is normal to feel it

A quiet house can feel strange after years of carpools, homework and noise. Allow yourself to feel both the pride and the sadness. A woman writing on Chabad.org describes how so much of our sense of self rests on roles such as parent, and when a role grows less defined we can ask, "Who am I now?" Her answer was to lean on something that is always there, her Jewish heritage and the community around it. You are still a parent, and you are also someone with your own soul, mind and mission.

## Rediscover your spouse

Jewish tradition calls a peaceful home *shalom bayit* (שְׁלוֹם בַּיִת), "peace in the home." With the children gone, there is room to listen again. Some ideas: a Friday night dinner just for two, with candles and a blessing; a weekly walk; learning something together. Read about [shalom bayit](/learn/shalom-bayit) and [Shabbat for Beginners](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners). If it feels hard, you are not alone, and the Rabbi and Rebbetzin will talk with you, privately: see [talk to the Rabbi](/help/talk-to-the-rabbi).

## A new Jewish chapter

Many people find that this is the first time in decades with an evening free. Some ways to use it:

- **Learn.** Chabad of Lamorinda has a weekly Torah class in English on Sunday evenings, a Tanya class on Wednesdays at 1:00 PM, and a Torah class in Hebrew with a meal on Tuesdays. See [adult education](/programs/adult-education) and [Torah Classes in Lamorinda](/learn/torah-classes-in-lamorinda). Questions are welcome and no background is needed. Read about [Tanya](/learn/tanya).
- **Join a circle.** The [Women's Circle](/programs/womens-circle) and the [Men's Club](/programs/mens-club) bring friends together for good company, learning and fun.
- **Host Shabbat.** A table with extra seats is a gift to a student or a new neighbor. See [Shabbat in Lamorinda](/learn/shabbat-in-lamorinda).
- **Give your time.** Volunteering is a Jewish way of caring for others. See [Volunteering in Lamorinda](/learn/volunteering-in-lamorinda) and the [volunteer form](/forms/volunteer).
- **Take on one mitzvah.** Pick a single commitment, such as lighting Shabbat candles, putting up a mezuzah or giving tzedakah each week, and build from there.

## Staying close to grown children

- **A call before Shabbat.** Many families make it a habit to call or video chat on Friday, even if the child is far away.
- **Be a safe harbor.** A grown child who knows home is warm and without judgment will want to come back.
- **Holiday visits.** Invite your children to the High Holidays or Passover, and let them see you enjoy your Jewish life.
- **Share what you learn.** Send a thought from class or a recipe for challah.

## Becoming grandparents

If grandchildren arrive, you are in a good position to pass on a Jewish heritage with love and little pressure. Read [Jewish Grandparents](/learn/jewish-grandparents). A grandparent's blessing on Friday night, a story, or a shared Chanukah candle lasts a lifetime.

## Common questions

### How do I cope when my last child leaves home?
Allow yourself to feel both the pride and the sadness, and fill the new time with learning, friends and your spouse. Talk to someone you trust, and the Rabbi is glad to listen.

### What does Judaism say about the empty nest stage?
Judaism sees every stage of life as a chance to grow, and values learning and giving at every age. Your role as a parent continues in a new form.

### Are there Torah classes for adults in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda?
Yes. Chabad of Lamorinda has weekly classes in English and Hebrew, including Tanya. Call 925-384-7242 to confirm the schedule.

### Is there a group for women or men to meet and learn?
Chabad of Lamorinda has a Women's Circle and a Men's Club. Call 925-384-7242 to hear what is planned.

### How can my spouse and I reconnect Jewishly?
Start with a Friday night dinner with candles, and learn something together. Read the guide on shalom bayit and ask the Rabbi for ideas.

### Do I need to be observant to join a class or come to Shabbat?
No. Every Jew is welcome, with no membership or required background.
