College is a time of independence, new ideas and big decisions, and it can be a wonderful time to discover your Jewish identity for yourself. With a little planning, Jewish students can keep Shabbat, eat kosher, celebrate holidays and find a warm Jewish community wherever they study.

## Why college matters for Jewish life

For many young adults, college is the first time Jewish life is a personal choice, not just something their family does. Questions about meaning, identity and values come up naturally. Many students find that this is when Judaism starts to feel truly their own.

## Finding a Jewish home near campus

Having a Jewish community nearby makes all the difference: a place for a home-cooked Shabbat dinner, holiday celebrations, a listening ear and friends who share your background.

**Studying near Lamorinda?** Students at Saint Mary's College in Moraga and Diablo Valley College in Pleasant Hill are warmly welcome for Shabbat at Chabad of Lamorinda. You do not need to know anything or bring anything. Just [contact us](/contact) and come.

Wherever you study, Chabad centers serve students at many campuses around the world.

## Practical tips for Jewish life on campus

### Shabbat
- **Plan ahead:** finish assignments before Friday afternoon when you can.
- **Light [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles)** in your room, if dorm rules allow, or at a Shabbat meal.
- **Join a Shabbat dinner** at a local Jewish community. See our [Shabbat page](/shabbat).

### Kosher food
- Many campuses have limited kosher options. Learn the [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols) so you can find packaged kosher food in any store.
- Keep a small set of your own dishes and utensils if you cook.
- Ask the Rabbi for tips on keeping kosher in a shared kitchen.

### Your room
- Put up a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) on your dorm or apartment door. It is a mitzvah, and a proud sign of Jewish identity.
- Keep a small Jewish library: a prayer book, a Chumash, Tehillim.

### Holidays and exams
- Check the [holidays page](/holidays) at the start of each semester.
- Talk to professors early if an exam or class falls on Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Passover or Shavuot. Many schools have policies for religious observance.

## Facing antisemitism

Sadly, some Jewish students face hostility or hurtful comments on campus. If this happens to you:

1. **You are not alone.** Reach out to Jewish friends, a local rabbi or family.
2. **Document what happened** and report it to the school if appropriate.
3. **Respond with light.** The Lubavitcher Rebbe taught that the best answer to darkness is more light: more Jewish pride, learning and mitzvot. Read our guide to [Jewish pride](/learn/jewish-pride).

## Common questions

### I did not grow up religious. Will I fit in at Chabad?
Absolutely. Chabad welcomes every Jew, whatever their background or knowledge. Many students come for the first time knowing very little.

### Can I bring a friend to Shabbat dinner?
Yes. Friends are welcome. Just let us know in advance so we can plan.

### How do I keep kosher if my school has no kosher dining?
Start with what you can: packaged kosher foods, fresh fruits and vegetables, and simple meals you prepare yourself. Call the Rabbi for practical advice.

### How can parents help their college student stay connected?
Encourage them to find a local Jewish community, send them Shabbat candles or holiday treats, and keep Jewish conversations going during calls home.
