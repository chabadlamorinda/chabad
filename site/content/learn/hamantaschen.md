Hamantaschen (אוזני המן, *oznei Haman*) are triangular, filled pastries eaten on Purim. The name comes from the Yiddish *mohn* (poppy seed) and *tash* (pocket), so hamantaschen are "poppy-seed-filled pockets," and the three-cornered shape recalls Haman, the villain of the Purim story.

## Why do we eat hamantaschen?

Chabad.org explains that the oldest name for the pastry is *oznei Haman*, "Haman's ears." That name appears in a 16th-century Italian Purim skit, and it led to a myth that the pastries commemorate cutting off Haman's ears, but Chabad.org notes there is no record of such a thing. A better explanation lies in the Yiddish name: poppy-seed pockets.

The classic filling was always poppy seeds, which the halachic sources specifically mention. The custom is tied to Esther, who, in the palace of the king, ate beans and seeds, and eating seeds on Purim recalls her. Today, hamantaschen can be filled with almost anything, sweet or savory.

## The hidden meanings

Chabad.org gives several meanings:

- *Tash* also means "weaken." The pastry symbolizes the weakening of Haman and our wish that G-d weaken those who want to harm us.
- Mordechai is said to have sent letters hidden in pastries, so filled pastries recall those messages.
- The filling hidden inside the dough reflects G-d's hidden presence in exile. In the Purim story, G-d's name never appears, yet He is behind every turn.
- The three corners relate to the three forefathers, Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, whose merit weakened Haman.

That hidden miracle is the heart of Purim, and the hamantasch shows it in a form that a child can taste. See [Purim for beginners](/learn/purim-for-beginners) and [the Purim holiday page](/holidays/purim).

## How to bake hamantaschen with your kids

You can use a favorite family dough, or look for the poppy seed hamantaschen recipe on Chabad.org. Here is the basic method:

1. Make a soft dough, let it rest, and roll it out on a floured table.
2. Cut circles with a glass or a cup.
3. Place a spoonful of filling in the center of each circle: poppy seed, jam, chocolate or date.
4. Fold up three sides to form a triangle and pinch the corners firmly, leaving a bit of filling visible.
5. Bake until golden. Let them cool before the children taste them.

Check that the ingredients are kosher. If you want help with the kitchen rules, see [the kosher kitchen](/learn/kosher-kitchen).

## Common questions

### Why do we eat hamantaschen on Purim?
Hamantaschen are poppy-seed pockets, and the custom recalls Haman and the hidden miracle of Purim. They also recall Esther, who ate seeds in the palace.

### What does "hamantaschen" mean?
It comes from the Yiddish *mohn* (poppy seed) and *tash* (pocket): poppy-seed-filled pockets. In Hebrew they are called *oznei Haman*, "Haman's ears."

### What are hamantaschen filled with?
Traditionally poppy seeds. Today they are filled with jam, chocolate, dates, fruit or even savory fillings.

### Why are hamantaschen triangular?
The three corners recall Haman, and they relate to the three forefathers, whose merit weakened him. It is a custom, not a law.

### Do you have to eat hamantaschen on Purim?
It is a beloved custom, not a commandment. The commandments are the Megillah, the festive meal, gifts to friends and gifts to the poor.
