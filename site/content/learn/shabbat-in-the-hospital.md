Nobody plans to spend Shabbat in the hospital. If you or someone you love is there over Shabbat or a holiday, here is what to know. First and most important: **the patient's health comes first.** See [Pikuach Nefesh](/learn/pikuach-nefesh).

## For the patient

- **If there is any danger to life,** everything needed for the patient's care is done on Shabbat: tests, treatment, calling the nurse, signing forms, using the phone to reach the doctor.
- **Follow the doctors.** Take medicine, eat what you are told to eat, and do not refuse treatment because it is Shabbat.
- **If the patient is not in danger** (for example, recovering comfortably), the rules are more detailed. Ask the Rabbi; often the hospital staff can do what is needed.

## For family members

The patient's needs override Shabbat, but a healthy family member keeps Shabbat as usual where possible. Some things to plan:

- **Getting there:** if a life is in danger, drive or ride. Getting home again on Shabbat is a different question; ask the Rabbi.
- **Doors, elevators and lights:** many hospitals have automatic doors and sensors. Ask the Rabbi before Shabbat how to handle them.
- **Phone:** set it aside, but keep it on and reachable if you may be needed for a medical decision.

## Candles

Open flames are usually not allowed in hospitals. Many families use electric candles or simply turn on a light in honor of Shabbat. Whether to say the blessing over an electric light depends on the situation, and opinions differ; ask the Rabbi. See [Shabbat Candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).

## Kiddush and meals

- Bring a small bottle of kosher wine or grape juice and two rolls or matzah for [Kiddush](/learn/kiddush) and [Hamotzi](/learn/hamotzi).
- Ask the hospital whether kosher meals are available, and request them early.
- If the patient cannot eat or drink, do not worry. Shabbat is still Shabbat. A family member can make Kiddush nearby.
- Call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242 for help and guidance with Shabbat food and supplies.

## Visiting

Visiting the sick, [bikur cholim](/learn/bikur-cholim), is a mitzvah on Shabbat too. Keep visits warm and short, and follow the hospital's rules. The Talmud gives a Shabbat greeting for a sick person: "It is Shabbat, when we do not cry out; healing will come soon" (Shabbat 12a).

## Prayer

Ask the Rabbi to say a [Mi Sheberach](/learn/mi-sheberach) for the patient, and add their name to [Tehillim](/tehillim). A few minutes of prayer or a chapter of Tehillim at the bedside can bring great comfort. See also [When someone is sick](/help/someone-is-sick).

## Common questions

### Can I drive to the hospital on Shabbat?
If there is a possible danger to life, yes, without delay. That includes a woman in labor. Do not stop to ask; ask afterwards.

### Can the Rabbi come to the hospital?
Yes. Rabbi Labkowski is glad to visit patients and families, before, during or after Shabbat when possible. Call 925-384-7242.

### What should I pack for Shabbat in the hospital?
Electric candles, grape juice or wine, challah or rolls, a siddur, food that needs no cooking, and a phone charger. Pack before Shabbat begins.

### What about a holiday that lasts two or three days?
The same principles apply. Plan food and supplies for the whole holiday, and call the Rabbi with questions before it starts.
