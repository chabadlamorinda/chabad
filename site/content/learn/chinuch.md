Chinuch (חִנּוּךְ) is the Jewish word for education, and it means more than teaching facts: it means training a child, at the child's own level, toward a lifetime of Jewish living. Parents are the first teachers, and the most powerful lessons are taught by example, with warmth and joy.

## What does chinuch mean?

Chabad.org explains that the word is connected to the idea of shaping something so it fits exactly, like cutting a stone to fit, which suggests that education should be tailored to each child. It also relates to the word for inauguration, the start of a lifelong path. Rashi, the great commentator, explains in Genesis 14:14 that chinuch is a person's entry into the work that he will do all his life. That is why we say "Chanukah," from the same root: the dedication of the Temple. Chinuch is a beginning.

## The source: "Educate a child in his own way"

The verse most often quoted is in Proverbs 22:6: "Educate a child according to his way, so that even when he grows old he will not stray from it." Two lessons stand out.

1. **Each child is different.** A good education suits the child's personality, strengths and interests. Chabad.org tells of the Rebbe teaching that treating every child the same may itself be the problem.
2. **What we plant early lasts.** A child who grows up with Shabbat, blessings and kindness carries them for life.

## Character comes first

Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, the first Chabad Rebbe, taught that there can be no Torah without fine character. The goal of chinuch is not only a child who knows, but a child who is a *mentch* (a good, decent person). Chabad.org emphasizes the educator's task of refining character, together with patience: "A short-tempered person cannot teach" (Ethics of Our Fathers 2:5). Warmth matters too, as the verse says: "The words of the wise are heard when spoken gently." Read about [derech eretz](/learn/derech-eretz) and [raising kind children](/learn/raising-kind-children).

## Practical steps at home

1. **Know your child.** Notice how your child learns and what excites them.
2. **Start the day with Modeh Ani.** This short Hebrew thank-you takes ten seconds. See [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani).
3. **Say blessings before eating.** Children learn that food is a gift.
4. **Make Shabbat the heart of the week.** Candles, challah, a family dinner and a blessing for each child. See [Shabbat with Kids](/learn/shabbat-with-kids).
5. **Give tzedakah together.** A coin in the charity box every Friday builds a giving heart. See [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).
6. **Teach by example.** Children copy what we do. Let them see you pray, learn and be kind.
7. **Stay patient, calm and joyful.** The Rebbe stressed joy and a positive approach for every child.

## At what age does chinuch begin?

Jewish tradition says education begins early, and the specific ages for each mitzvah vary. Customs differ by family and mitzvah, so for your child's situation, ask the Rabbi. A good rule is to start gently now with whatever you can do with love.

## Common questions

### What does chinuch mean in Judaism?
Chinuch means education and training, and also dedication or beginning. It is a parent's and teacher's work of guiding a child toward a lifetime of Jewish living.

### Where does the idea "educate a child according to his way" come from?
It comes from Proverbs 22:6. It teaches that education should fit each child's nature, and that what is planted early lasts into old age.

### Is educating my child Jewishly a parent's responsibility?
Yes. Parents are a child's first and most important teachers, and schools and community are partners. Ask the Rabbi about specific mitzvot.

### When should I start teaching my child about Judaism?
As early as you can, with simple, joyful things like blessings, Shabbat candles and Modeh Ani. For specifics about ages, ask the Rabbi.

### How can I teach my child Jewish values if I did not learn much myself?
Learn together. Start with one small habit, join a class and ask questions. No one is too late, and every Jew is welcome at Chabad of Lamorinda.
