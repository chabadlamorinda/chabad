If you are invited to a Shabbat dinner, the best gift is something sealed and kosher, like a bottle of kosher wine, a box of chocolates with a kosher symbol, or flowers delivered before Shabbat. Plan to arrive before sunset on Friday, and come ready to enjoy a relaxed, festive meal with blessings, singing and good conversation.

## What to bring

Bringing a gift is a kind gesture, though never required. Good choices:

- **Kosher wine or grape juice**, with a reliable kosher symbol on the label. See our guide to [kosher wine](/learn/kosher-wine).
- **Sealed chocolates, cookies or dessert** from a kosher bakery or with a kosher symbol.
- **Flowers**, ideally sent or brought early on Friday, so your hosts can put them in water before Shabbat begins.
- **A book or a small gift for the home**, such as a challah cover or a book of Jewish stories.

## What not to bring

- **Home-cooked food**, unless your hosts asked for it. A kosher kitchen has its own rules, and your hosts may not be able to serve it.
- **Anything that needs to be prepared, cooked or plugged in**, since cooking is not done on Shabbat.

## When to arrive

Shabbat begins at sunset on Friday, about 18 minutes after candle lighting. Check this week's times on our [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times) page. If you drive, plan to arrive before candle lighting, since your hosts will not handle money, phones or car keys once Shabbat begins. If you are walking to services first, ask your hosts when to come.

## What to wear

Most people dress up a bit for Shabbat, in the spirit of honoring the day. In a traditional home, women often wear a skirt or dress with sleeves, and men may be offered a [kippah](/learn/kippah). When in doubt, dress as you would for a nice dinner.

## What happens at the table

1. **Shalom Aleichem:** a song welcoming the Shabbat angels.
2. **[Kiddush](/learn/kiddush):** the host blesses the wine. Listen, answer "Amen," and take a sip.
3. **Washing hands:** guests wash with a cup in the kitchen. There is no talking between washing and the bread.
4. **Hamotzi:** the blessing over two challahs, dipped in salt and shared.
5. **The meal:** several courses, with conversation, songs and words of Torah.
6. **Grace After Meals:** [Birkat Hamazon](/learn/birkat-hamazon), often sung together.

Do not worry about knowing the words. Your hosts will be happy to explain, and just being there is what counts. Read more in our guide to [Shabbat meals](/learn/shabbat-meals).

## A few good-guest tips

- **Put your phone away** for the evening. It is a gift to yourself.
- **Offer to help** carry dishes or clear the table.
- **Do not turn lights on or off** or take photos; ask if you need something.
- **Say thank you** after Shabbat with a call or a note.

## Common questions

### Do I have to be Jewish or observant to come to Shabbat dinner?
No. Every Jew is welcome, whatever their background, and many hosts happily welcome non-Jewish friends and partners too. No one expects you to know what to do.

### Can I bring a bottle of wine without a kosher label?
It is better not to. Wine needs a kosher certification to be served at a kosher table. If you are not sure, chocolates or flowers are a safe choice.

### What if I have food allergies or dietary needs?
Tell your hosts when you accept the invitation. They will be glad to plan around it.

### How long does a Shabbat dinner last?
Usually two to three hours, sometimes longer when the singing and conversation are good. There is no rush on Shabbat, which is part of its beauty.
