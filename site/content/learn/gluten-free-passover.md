People who cannot eat wheat can ask the Rabbi about gluten-free oat matzah for the Passover Seder: oats are one of the five grains from which matzah may be made, although some authorities are strict about oat matzah, so the Rabbi will guide you. Rice, potato and tapioca "matzah" do not fulfill the mitzvah, and spelt matzah contains gluten, so it is not safe for celiac disease.

## What makes matzah, matzah

Matzah (מַצָּה) is unleavened bread, baked quickly from flour and water. The Torah's commandment to eat it at the Seder can be fulfilled only with matzah made from one of the five grains: wheat, spelt, barley, rye or oats. The flour must be mixed with water and baked within 18 minutes, so it never ferments. Read more in [matzah](/learn/matzah).

For most people, the Chabad custom is to use wheat matzah, preferably handmade shmurah matzah ("guarded" from the time of harvest). Oat matzah is for those who truly cannot eat wheat.

## Why rice and potato do not count

Rice, potato, corn, tapioca and almond flours are not among the five grains. A product made from them may be a fine gluten-free food on Passover, but it is not matzah for the mitzvah of the Seder. Gluten-free "matzah" made from these ingredients cannot be used for the Seder, even if it has a Passover label.

## Celiac disease, intolerance and allergy

Doctors separate celiac disease (an autoimmune reaction to gluten), wheat allergy and milder sensitivity. For someone with celiac disease, even a little wheat is harmful, and Judaism asks us to protect our health first (see [pikuach nefesh](/learn/pikuach-nefesh)).

- **Celiac or wheat allergy:** ask the Rabbi about oat matzah that is certified gluten-free and kosher for Passover. Spelt is not an option because it contains gluten.
- **A mild sensitivity:** ask your doctor, then ask the Rabbi what is right for you.
- **Oats and celiac:** some people with celiac disease react to oats, even pure ones. Check with your doctor before the Seder, not at the table.

## Gluten-free shmurah oat matzah

Gluten-free oat shmurah matzah is available from some matzah bakeries. It is usually more expensive and its supply is limited, so order early, well before Passover. Ask the Rabbi what to look for on the package, and which products have a reliable symbol.

## A gluten-free Seder, step by step

1. **Order oat matzah early,** and keep it away from regular matzah and crumbs.
2. **Use a separate plate or a clean cloth** for the matzah on the Seder plate, to avoid cross-contact.
3. **Seder foods are naturally safe:** karpas (vegetable), maror (bitter herb), charoset, eggs and wine are gluten-free, but check the packaging of each. See the [Seder plate](/learn/seder-plate).
4. **Plan the meal around potatoes, rice dishes, meat, fish and vegetables,** not matzah meal. Many "kosher for Passover" cakes use matzah meal, so read the ingredients.
5. **Ask the Rabbi** how much matzah to eat and when, for your situation.

## Common questions

### Is there gluten-free matzah?
Yes. Gluten-free oat matzah, including shmurah oat matzah, is made for people who cannot eat wheat. Order early, since supply is limited.

### Does rice or potato matzah count for the Seder?
No. Matzah for the mitzvah must be made from one of the five grains. Rice and potato products are not matzah, even if they say kosher for Passover.

### Is spelt matzah gluten-free?
No. Spelt contains gluten, so it is not suitable for celiac disease or a wheat allergy.

### Can someone with celiac disease fulfill the mitzvah of matzah?
Often yes, with certified gluten-free oat matzah, if oats are safe for that person; some authorities are stricter about oat matzah. Talk to your doctor and the Rabbi.

### Is oat matzah okay if I just prefer not to eat gluten?
Matzah made of oats is for people who cannot eat wheat for health reasons. Ask the Rabbi about your situation, including a preference or mild sensitivity.
