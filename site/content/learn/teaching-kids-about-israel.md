The best way to teach children about Israel is to begin with the Torah's story of the Land, the holidays that grow out of it and the prayers we say, and to share love before details. Israel is the Land G-d promised to Abraham and his descendants, and a child can feel at home in that story long before he or she understands the news.

## Start with the story

The Land of Israel appears in the Torah from the beginning of the Jewish story. G-d tells Abraham, "I will give this land to your descendants" (Genesis 12:7). Children can hear about Abraham's tent, about the cave he bought for Sarah (see [The Cave of Machpelah](/learn/cave-of-machpelah)) and about Rachel on the road to Bethlehem (see [Rachel's Tomb](/learn/rachels-tomb)). A weekly parsha story keeps the Land present at the Shabbat table (see [Parsha for Kids](/learn/parsha-for-kids)).

The Torah calls it a land "upon which the eyes of G-d your G-d constantly gaze" (Deuteronomy 11:12). For a young child, this is a lovely idea on its own: a special place that G-d looks after.

## Hebrew, the language of the Land

Chabad.org notes that Hebrew is the everyday language of Israel. Children who know the aleph-bet can read the words of their prayers, and every new word is a small bridge. See [Teaching Kids the Aleph-Bet](/learn/teaching-kids-the-aleph-bet) and, for families speaking Hebrew at home, [Raising Hebrew-Speaking Kids in Lamorinda](/learn/raising-hebrew-speaking-kids-in-lamorinda).

## Holidays and food of the Land

- **Tu BiShvat** celebrates the trees and the fruits of the Land (see [Tu BiShvat for Kids](/learn/tu-bishvat-for-kids)).
- **The seven species**: wheat, barley, grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives and dates, for which the Torah praises the Land (see [The Seven Species](/learn/seven-species)). Letting children taste them, one by one, is a way to taste the Land.
- **Pilgrimage festivals**: Passover, Shavuot and Sukkot were the three times a year when Jews went up to Jerusalem.
- **Jerusalem kugel**: Chabad.org tells that this noodle dish with caramelized sugar and pepper began in nineteenth-century Jerusalem. It is an easy way to bring a taste of Jerusalem to Shabbat.

## Places children can picture

Stories give children places to imagine. Jerusalem, whose name Chabad.org links to Abraham's words, "G-d will see," is where the Temple stood and where Jews face when they pray (see [Jerusalem in Judaism](/learn/jerusalem)). The Western Wall is where people place notes with their prayers (see [The Western Wall](/learn/western-wall)). Tzfat and Tiberias have the graves of sages (see [Tiberias and the Galilee](/learn/tiberias-and-the-galilee)). A simple map on the wall, with a few places marked, will begin many conversations.

## Prayer: a child's way to help

Chabad.org says that the prayer of children is especially potent. A child can say a chapter of [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) for the people of Israel, and can give tzedakah, which Chabad.org lists among the ways to help Israel (see [Children, Money and Tzedakah](/learn/children-money-and-tzedakah)).

## Keeping it simple: love before details

Young children need warmth, stories and tastes, not arguments. This guide is about the Land as Jewish faith and heritage, and not about politics or current events. If your child asks hard questions, listen first, answer simply and honestly and, if you are unsure, ask the Rabbi. For sensitive subjects, see [Talking to Kids About Antisemitism](/learn/talking-to-kids-about-antisemitism).

## Common questions

### How do I start teaching my child about Israel?
Begin with the Torah's stories of Abraham, Sarah, Rachel and the Land, then add holidays, Hebrew words and foods. Children love stories more than facts.

### What age is right for teaching about Israel?
Even toddlers can enjoy songs, fruits of the Land and a picture of Jerusalem. Details can grow with age.

### How can I teach about Israel without politics?
Focus on the Torah, holidays, Hebrew, food and prayer. If a political question comes up, answer briefly and kindly, and ask the Rabbi if you need help.

### Can children pray for Israel?
Yes. A child can say a chapter of Tehillim, and Chabad.org says that the prayer of children is especially potent.
