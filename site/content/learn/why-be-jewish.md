Many people grow up knowing they are Jewish without ever being told why it matters. In a busy, modern life, it is a fair question: why be Jewish? Here is how Judaism, and especially Chassidic teaching, answers it.

## A soul that is part of G-d

The Tanya, the classic book of Chabad teaching, explains that every Jew has a G-dly soul, "truly a part of G-d above." Being Jewish is not only something you believe or do; it is something you are. That is why a Jew who has never been to synagogue can still feel moved by a Shabbat candle or a Yizkor prayer: the soul recognizes its home.

## A mission, not a label

Judaism teaches that G-d created the world because He wanted a home in the lowest of places, in ordinary life. Every mitzvah does that: lighting a candle, giving tzedakah, putting up a mezuzah, a kind word. Being Jewish means being given a part in that mission, in your own family, your town, your work. It is not about being better than anyone; every person has a purpose. It is about the particular job we were given at Sinai.

## A family three thousand years long

To be Jewish is to belong to a family that stretches from Abraham and Sarah to your grandparents and your children. Through exile and persecution, Jews held on to Shabbat, the holidays, the Torah and each other. Each of us is a link in that chain; what we do today decides whether it continues.

## A life with meaning in every day

Judaism turns ordinary moments into holy ones: a meal becomes a blessing, a week ends in Shabbat, a year is marked by holidays, every stage of life has its ceremony. Many people discover that a Jewish life gives their days structure, their family closeness and their questions depth.

## You do not have to do everything

Being Jewish is not all or nothing. The Rebbe taught that every single mitzvah is precious on its own, and that one good deed leads to another. Start with one: Shabbat candles, a mezuzah, tefillin, a Shabbat meal with friends. See [starting Jewish observance](/learn/starting-jewish-observance).

## Common questions

### Who is Jewish?
A person born to a Jewish mother, or who converted according to Jewish law. Being Jewish does not depend on how much you know or keep. See [who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew).

### I did not grow up religious. Is it too late?
Never. Every Jew is welcome to begin at any age, one step at a time, and the Rabbi and the Rebbetzin are happy to help. See [not religious? You are welcome](/learn/not-religious-welcome).

### My spouse is not Jewish. Can we still come?
Yes, everyone is welcome at our events, and the Rabbi is glad to talk with your family about any question.
