At the Passover Seder every Jew drinks four cups of wine (arba kosot, אַרְבַּע כּוֹסוֹת): one for each of the four expressions of redemption G-d used to promise freedom from Egypt. The cups are drunk at Kiddush, at the end of the telling of the story, after Grace After Meals and after Hallel, each at least a revi'it (a measure of about three ounces or more; ask the Rabbi), while leaning to the left.

## Why four?

In the Torah (Exodus 6:6-7) G-d tells Moses to promise four things to the Jewish people, in four expressions:

1. "I will take you out" (*vehotzeti*), from the burdens of Egypt.
2. "I will rescue you" (*vehitzalti*), from their slavery.
3. "I will redeem you" (*vega'alti*), with an outstretched arm.
4. "I will take you to Me as a people" (*velakachti*), at Sinai.

A fifth expression follows, "I will bring you to the land," and that is why many have a fifth cup, Elijah's cup, which we will see below. Wine is the drink of joy and freedom, so each stage of the redemption is celebrated with a cup. See [the Seder](/learn/passover-seder) and [the Exodus story](/learn/exodus-story).

## When each cup is drunk

1. **Kadesh:** the first cup, Kiddush, at the start of the Seder.
2. **Maggid:** the second cup, at the end of telling the story, after the blessing of redemption.
3. **Birkat Hamazon:** the third cup, after the meal, with Grace After Meals.
4. **Hallel:** the fourth cup, after the songs of praise at the end.

Your [Haggadah](/learn/haggadah) tells you exactly when. Many pour the wine for others and not for themselves, a sign of freedom: a free person is served.

## How much, and how to drink it

- **The size.** The minimum is a revi'it, a measure of liquid. Opinions differ on how many ounces that is today, so the common practice is to use a cup that holds a revi'it or more. Ask the Rabbi which size to use.
- **Drink the cup.** It is best to drink all or most of the cup, ideally in one go or in two. Children who find it too much can drink less; ask the Rabbi.
- **Lean to the left.** We drink and eat leaning on the left side, like free people at a banquet.
- **Red wine** is preferred if you can, since it recalls the redemption. White is acceptable.
- **Kosher for Passover.** The wine or juice must be kosher for Passover; see [kosher wine](/learn/kosher-wine).

## Grape juice and alcohol-free options

If you cannot drink wine for health or other reasons, you may use kosher-for-Passover grape juice. Some people dilute their wine with grape juice. Those who cannot drink even that, or who are pregnant, ill or recovering from addiction, should ask the Rabbi what is best: Jewish law makes room for each person's situation, and there are other ways to fulfill the mitzvah.

## Elijah's cup

After Grace After Meals a full cup is poured for Elijah the Prophet, who according to tradition will announce the coming of Moshiach. The door is opened, and we say "Pour out Your wrath." Chassidic teaching sees the cup of Elijah as the fifth cup, of the fifth expression, "I will bring you to the land," which will come with the final redemption. Children love opening the door to welcome Elijah. See [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach).

## Common questions

### Why do we drink four cups of wine at the Seder?
The four cups match the four expressions of redemption G-d used in the Torah to promise freedom from Egypt.

### When do you drink the four cups of wine?
At Kiddush, at the end of the Maggid (the telling of the story), after Grace After Meals, and after Hallel.

### How much wine is in each of the four cups?
At least a revi'it, a Talmudic measure of about three ounces or more depending on the opinion. Ask the Rabbi which cup size to use.

### Can I use grape juice for the four cups?
Yes, kosher-for-Passover grape juice may be used, and some mix it with wine. Ask the Rabbi if you have any health reason.

### Why do we lean when we drink the four cups?
Leaning to the left shows that we are free people, as people did at a banquet in ancient times.

### What is the cup of Elijah?
It is an extra cup poured after the meal for Elijah the Prophet, who will announce Moshiach. We open the door for him and say a prayer.
