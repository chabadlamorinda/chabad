Jewish community matters because Judaism was always meant to be lived together. Hillel taught, "Do not separate yourself from the community" (Pirkei Avot 2:4), and Jewish life, from prayer to joy to mourning, is built around people showing up for each other. Whether you live in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda, you belong.

## Hillel's teaching

In Pirkei Avot (Ethics of the Fathers), Hillel gives several short pieces of advice. Chabad.org presents the first one this way: stay part of the community, and do not withdraw from communal life. Then he adds humility, withholding judgment and not postponing Torah study. The teaching in Hebrew is "אל תפרוש מן הציבור". It does not say "join" but "do not separate." It assumes you are already part of the people, and warns only against drifting away.

## One body, one people

Chabad teaches that every Jew is a letter in one Torah scroll: if a single letter is missing, the entire scroll is considered incomplete. Each person matters. This is the idea behind *ahavat Yisrael*, loving every fellow Jew, which the Lubavitcher Rebbe taught as the foundation of Jewish life. See [Ahavat Yisrael](/learn/ahavat-yisrael). Chabad's welcome has no tests: every Jew is welcome, whatever they know or keep.

## Praying together

Some of our holiest prayers, such as Kaddish and Barchu, can be said only with a minyan, a gathering of ten adults. Alone, we can talk to G-d, and that is precious. Together, our prayers ascend as one. When someone is mourning, a minyan is how the community holds them. Read about [minyan](/learn/minyan) and [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish).

## Carrying each other in joy and in grief

A community gives you people to celebrate with, at a bar mitzvah or a wedding, and people to sit with at a shiva. It means a meal when you have a baby, a call when you are sick, and someone who notices when you are missing. Read [feeling lonely](/learn/feeling-lonely) if you need a friend now.

## Honest objections

**"I'm not religious."** Community is not a test. Chabad says every Jew is welcome, and what you keep is between you and G-d. Many people begin with just one mitzvah.

**"I don't know anyone."** Nobody does at first. Everyone in the room was once a stranger. Come once and tell us it is your first time, and someone will sit with you.

**"I can do it alone."** You can do many things alone, and that is beautiful. But some things, like a minyan, a seder or a Kiddush with friends, can only be done together, and many find that being needed by others is the best part.

**"The synagogue isn't for me."** Then try a class, a dinner or a volunteer day first. There are many doors.

## Common questions

### Why does Jewish community matter?
Because Jewish life, including prayer, holidays and mourning, is designed to be lived together, and every person is a part of one people.

### What does "do not separate yourself from the community" mean?
It is Hillel's teaching in Pirkei Avot 2:4: stay part of the Jewish community and do not withdraw from communal life.

### Do I have to be religious to join a Jewish community?
No. Every Jew is welcome at Chabad of Lamorinda, with no membership and no tests of observance.

### Why go to synagogue if I can pray at home?
You can pray anywhere, but some prayers, like Kaddish, need a minyan of ten, and being with others adds meaning and support.

### What is a kehillah?
A kehillah (קהילה) is a Jewish community, a group of people who share life, prayer and responsibility for one another.
