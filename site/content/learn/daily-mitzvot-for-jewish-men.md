A traditional Jewish man's day is built around prayer three times a day (morning, afternoon and evening), putting on tefillin on weekday mornings, wearing tzitzit, saying the Shema and learning Torah. You do not have to do it all at once; Chabad teaching is that every single mitzvah counts, so start with one.

## Morning: blessings, tefillin and prayer

The day begins with the morning blessings. In the laws of prayer (*tefillah*), Shacharit, the morning prayer, follows waking and the morning blessings. Read [morning blessings](/learn/morning-blessings).

**Tefillin** (תפילין) are two black leather boxes with parchment scrolls inside, worn on the arm and the head. A few basic facts about tefillin:

- The arm tefillin and the head tefillin are two separate mitzvot. If you have only one, wear it.
- The scrolls contain four Torah passages, including the Shema.
- They go on the weaker arm: the left arm for a right-handed person, the right arm for a lefty.
- A blessing is said before the arm tefillin are tightened. Putting them on fulfills the mitzvah even if you remove them right away, but the ideal is to wear them through the morning prayer, or at least to say the Shema before removing them.
- They are not worn on Shabbat, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Passover, Shavuot or at night. Chabad, like many communities, also does not wear them on the intermediate days of Sukkot and Passover (*chol hamoed*).
- The arm tefillin rests near the heart, and the head tefillin above the forehead, symbolizing dedicating thought, feeling and action to G-d.

The Lubavitcher Rebbe launched a campaign for men and boys to put on tefillin just before the Six-Day War in 1967. For details, buying and checking a pair, see [tefillin](/learn/tefillin), [buying tefillin](/learn/buying-tefillin) and [checking mezuzahs and tefillin](/learn/checking-mezuzahs-and-tefillin).

## Tzitzit

*Tzitzit* (ציצית) are the fringes on a four-cornered garment worn by men, a reminder of the mitzvot. Boys traditionally begin wearing tzitzit at about age three, according to Jewish tradition. See [tzitzit](/learn/tzitzit).

## Three prayers a day

Chassidic teaching lists the times:

- **Shacharit** (שחרית), morning.
- **Mincha** (מנחה), afternoon, before sunset.
- **Maariv** (מעריב), evening, after three medium stars appear.

The Shema is read in the morning and in the evening, and also before sleep. Communal prayer with a minyan, a quorum of ten men over 13, is preferred; if no minyan is available, one prays alone. It also recommends praying from a siddur, with concentration (*kavanah*) rather than speed. See [daily prayers](/learn/daily-prayers) and [praying alone at home](/learn/praying-alone-at-home).

## Common questions

### What does a Jewish man do every day?
He prays three times a day, puts on tefillin on weekday mornings, wears tzitzit, says the Shema and learns Torah. Start with whatever step you can take today.

### What are the daily mitzvot for Jewish men?
The main ones are prayer, tefillin, tzitzit, the Shema and Torah study. Women have their own daily mitzvot, such as Shabbat candles: see [three mitzvot of Jewish women](/learn/three-mitzvot-for-jewish-women).

### When do men put on tefillin?
On weekday mornings, not on Shabbat or Jewish holidays and not at night. Chabad does not wear them on the intermediate days of Sukkot and Passover either.

### Can I start tefillin if I never have?
Yes. Putting them on once fulfills the mitzvah. Ask the Rabbi, who can show you how, and see the [mezuzah and tefillin form](/forms/mezuzah-tefillin).

### How do I know my tefillin are kosher?
Ask the Rabbi to have them checked by a qualified scribe: [checking mezuzahs and tefillin](/learn/checking-mezuzahs-and-tefillin).
