You do not have to read the Tanya from the beginning. A good way to start is to choose the chapter that speaks to what you need today: for worry, Epistle 11; for sadness, Chapters 26 and 27; for a feeling of not being good enough, Chapters 2 and 27; for loss, Epistles 27 and 28. This page matches each need to a chapter on Chabad.org and to a guide here.

## How to use this plan

The [Tanya](/learn/tanya) was written by Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi, the Alter Rebbe, the founder of Chabad. Its first part, Likkutei Amarim, is divided into chapters, and Chabad.org also offers the Iggeret HaKodesh, a collection of the Alter Rebbe's letters, and the Iggeret HaTeshuvah. Chabad.org describes the Tanya as a "one size fits all" life manual, and offers text in English and Hebrew, audio, video and the easy-to-read Tanya in Plain English. Its Plain English page points beginners to Chapters 26 to 28, 32 and 33.

Each line below gives the need, the place in the Tanya as we read it on Chabad.org, and a guide to read first. The chapter numbers are the ones on those pages. Tanya's guidance is spiritual. For sadness or anxiety that does not lift, professional help is also right, and for your own situation, ask the Rabbi.

## If you feel worried, sad or hurt

- **Worried:** Epistle 11 (Iggeret HaKodesh). Start with [how to stop worrying: Tanya on trust](/learn/tanya-on-worry-and-trust).
- **Life hurts right now:** Epistle 11 and Chapter 26. Start with [Tanya in hard times](/learn/tanya-in-hard-times).
- **Sad or heavy:** Chapters 26 and 27. Start with [Tanya on sadness and joy](/learn/tanya-on-sadness-and-joy) and [sadness versus bitterness](/learn/sadness-versus-bitterness).
- **Not good enough:** Chapters 2 and 27. Start with [what Tanya says about your worth](/learn/tanya-on-self-worth).
- **Grateful and want more of it:** Epistle 11 and Chapter 33. Start with [Tanya on gratitude](/learn/tanya-on-gratitude).
- **You lost someone:** Epistles 27 and 28. Start with [Tanya and grief](/learn/tanya-and-grief).

## If you are fighting with yourself

- **Unwanted thoughts:** Chapters 12, 27 and 28. See [Tanya and unwanted thoughts](/learn/tanya-unwanted-thoughts).
- **Tempted or tired of the struggle:** Chapter 12 on the beinoni. See [the beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni), [Tanya on temptation](/learn/tanya-on-temptation) and [why do I still struggle?](/learn/why-do-i-still-struggle)
- **Angry:** Chapter 12 describes answering anger and resentment with kindness. See [Tanya on anger](/learn/tanya-on-anger).
- **Falling and starting again:** the Iggeret HaTeshuvah. See [Iggeret HaTeshuvah](/learn/iggeret-hateshuvah).

## Common questions

### Which chapter of the Tanya should a beginner read first?
Chabad.org points beginners to Chapters 26 to 28 on anxiety, self-blame and sadness, Chapter 32 on love for every Jew and Chapter 33 on celebrating being Jewish. Many people also start with Chapter 1. See [how to start learning Tanya](/learn/how-to-start-learning-tanya).

### Which Tanya chapter is about sadness and depression?
Chapters 26 and 27 speak about joy, sadness and being crushed by failure. They are spiritual guidance and not medical advice. For ongoing low mood, professional help is also right.

### Which part of the Tanya has letters of comfort?
The Iggeret HaKodesh includes the Alter Rebbe's letters, among them condolence letters, Epistles 27 and 28. See [Tanya and grief](/learn/tanya-and-grief).

### Where can I read or listen to the Tanya online?
