Planning a will is a gift to the people you love. Judaism adds three things to think about: making sure your plan is valid in Jewish law, leaving clear Jewish burial wishes, and passing on your values, not only your assets.

## Jewish law and inheritance

The Torah has its own laws of inheritance (Numbers 27). A modern will that divides an estate differently, for example equally between sons and daughters or to a spouse, can be fully valid in Jewish law too, if it is drafted correctly. Rabbinic authorities have developed a **halachic will** form, signed along with your regular will, that makes your wishes binding in both systems.

Ask the Rabbi for guidance, and have your attorney prepare the legal documents.

## Burial wishes in writing

Families are often unsure what a loved one wanted. Write clearly:

- That you want a **traditional Jewish burial**, with a Chevra Kadisha (taharah, the washing and dressing of the body) and a plain wooden casket.
- **No cremation and no embalming**, which Jewish law does not allow. See our [cremation](/learn/cremation-jewish-view) guide.
- Which **cemetery**, and whether a plot has been reserved.
- Who should be called first: "Call Rabbi Labkowski."

Give a copy to your family and to the executor, not only to a safe deposit box that will be opened later.

## Tzedakah and legacy

Many people leave part of their estate to tzedakah, to the causes and community they loved. Jewish tradition encourages it. A gift to a synagogue, school or Chabad center continues your mitzvahs after 120 years.

## An ethical will

Beyond money, leave words: your values, stories and blessings for your children and grandchildren. See our [ethical will](/learn/ethical-will) guide.

## Common questions

### Do I need a halachic will if I already have a regular will?
If your will divides your estate differently from the Torah's order, a halachic will helps make sure your wishes are valid in Jewish law as well. Ask the Rabbi.

### Can I leave equal shares to my daughters and sons?
Yes. With a properly drafted halachic will, equal shares (or any division you choose) can be valid in Jewish law.

### What about organ donation in my will?
There are important questions in Jewish law about organ donation. See our guide to [organ donation](/learn/organ-donation-jewish-view) and ask the Rabbi.
