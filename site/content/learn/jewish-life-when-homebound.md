You can stay fully connected to Jewish life even when you cannot leave your home: you can learn, pray, light Shabbat candles, give charity and welcome visitors, and none of these depends on being in a synagogue. The Lubavitcher Rebbe taught that physical strength is not the measure of a person, and that the mind and spirit can keep growing at every age.

## Your worth does not depend on how much you can do

The Rebbe observed that our society often measures a person by physical strength, and that this leaves many older people feeling useless. He taught the opposite: the Torah honors wisdom, and while the body may weaken, a person's mind can grow in depth. So being homebound does not take away your purpose. Read more in [aging with purpose](/learn/aging-with-purpose).

## Learning from home

The Rebbe urged older people to keep their minds active and to keep growing spiritually, and he asked that learning groups be set up for them, even in homes for the aged. You can do something similar by yourself, with a book or a few minutes of reading each day. See [daily Torah study](/learn/daily-torah-study), and the weekly Torah portion on [this week's page](/parsha). If you would like to study with someone, call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 to hear what can be arranged.

## Prayer and mitzvot at home

Many mitzvot are done at home. Here are some that you can do wherever you are:

- **Shabbat candles.** Lighting candles brings Shabbat into the house. Read [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles). If you live where flames are not allowed, ask the Rabbi about your situation.
- **Prayer.** Read about the [daily prayers](/learn/daily-prayers) and the Book of [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim), the Psalms, which many people say when they need comfort. For what applies to you, ask the Rabbi.
- **Tzedakah.** Giving charity needs no travel. A [pushka](/learn/pushka) at home is a good way to start.
- **Mezuzah.** If your home has no kosher mezuzah, or you are not sure that yours is kosher, call the Rabbi. See [mezuzah questions](/learn/mezuzah-questions).
- **Blessings.** Saying a blessing over food or on a beautiful day is something to do anywhere. See [blessings](/learn/blessings).

## Visits and company

Loneliness is real, and Jewish tradition counts visiting the sick as a mitzvah (see [bikur cholim](/learn/bikur-cholim)). Rabbi and Rebbetzin Labkowski visit the sick at home, in the hospital and in care facilities. If you or someone you love in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda cannot get out, call 925-384-7242, or use the [need help](/forms/need-help) form. For more ideas, see [help for seniors and families](/help/help-for-seniors-and-families) and [feeling lonely](/learn/feeling-lonely).

If you live in senior living, read [Jewish life for a parent in senior living](/learn/jewish-life-in-senior-living-lamorinda).

## Giving back from home

You can also help others from your chair, with a kind phone call, a prayer for someone who is ill, or a donation. If you would like to sponsor visits and help for others, see the [Chesed Fund](/donate?fund=chesed#give).

## Common questions

### Can I do mitzvot if I cannot leave my house?
Yes. Many mitzvot are done at home, including lighting Shabbat candles, giving tzedakah, saying blessings, learning Torah and praying. Ask the Rabbi what applies in your case.

### How can I hear a Torah class if I cannot attend?
Call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 to hear what is available. You can also read the weekly portion on the [Parsha](/parsha) page.

### Does the Rabbi visit homebound seniors in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda?
The Rabbi and Rebbetzin visit the sick at home, in hospital and in care facilities. Call 925-384-7242 or see [help for seniors and families](/help/help-for-seniors-and-families).

### What if I cannot light candles safely?
Safety comes first. Please ask the Rabbi what is best for your situation.

### Where can I find company if I feel lonely?
Call the Rabbi, or read [feeling lonely](/learn/feeling-lonely). You can also ask a family member to call with you.

### How can I pray if I cannot attend synagogue?
You can pray at home, in your own words or from a prayer book. Read [prayer](/learn/prayer), and ask the Rabbi to guide you.
