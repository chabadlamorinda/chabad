The Chanukah menorah is lit where you live and sleep, so a college student lights in the dorm and a traveler lights in the hotel room. Lighting at the office is a beautiful way to share the miracle, but it usually does not replace lighting at home, and is often done without the blessings.

## The basic rule: light where you live

The Talmud says the mitzvah is "a lamp for a man and his household." The place that counts is your home for the night: where you sleep and usually eat. For most people that is home. For a student, it is the dorm room or apartment. For a traveler, it is the hotel, rental or friend's home where they are staying. For the basics of lighting, see our [Chanukah menorah guide](/learn/chanukah-menorah).

## In a college dorm

- **Light in your room** if the rules allow candles. Place the menorah on a stable, fireproof surface away from curtains and papers, and stay with it while it burns.
- **If open flames are not allowed**, speak with the residence office. Many schools make exceptions for a supervised menorah or a common lounge. Lighting in a shared kitchen or lounge of your building, with the blessings, may work too; ask the Rabbi about your setup.
- **An electric menorah** is a good way to display Chanukah when fire is forbidden, but most authorities hold it does not fulfill the mitzvah and no blessing is said on it.
- **Going home for break?** If you will be home with your parents for Chanukah, you may be included in their lighting. Many students still light their own menorah.

## At the office

A menorah at work brings light to your colleagues and is a wonderful act of Jewish pride. Since you do not live there, the usual practice is to light at the office without the blessings, and then light again at home with the blessings. Choose a safe spot, never leave it unattended, and follow your workplace's fire rules.

## In a hotel or someone else's home

- **Hotel:** light in your room, by the window if possible, and stay with the candles until the required time has passed. Some hotels allow a menorah in the lobby too.
- **A friend's or relative's home:** if you are staying the night, you can light there with the blessings. Married couples are included in each other's lighting; if one spouse is away, the one at home lights and the traveler may light too. Ask the Rabbi about your case.
- **Plane or car:** you cannot light while in transit. Light when you arrive, as long as it is still night.

## Timing when you are busy

The ideal time is shortly after nightfall. If you come home from work or class later, you may still light with the blessings as long as someone is awake in the house, or you are awake, during the night. Make sure there is enough oil or candle to burn at least half an hour. On Friday afternoon, light before the Shabbat candles.

## Common questions

### Can I light Chanukah candles with an electric menorah?
An electric menorah is great for display, but most authorities say it does not fulfill the mitzvah, so no blessing is said. Whenever possible, use oil or wax candles.

### My dorm does not allow candles. What should I do?
Ask the residence office for permission to light a supervised menorah, or look for a shared space where it is allowed. You can also call us at 925-384-7242 and we will help you find a solution.

### Do I say the blessings at an office party menorah lighting?
Usually not, because the office is not your home. Light it without blessings to share the joy, then light at home with the blessings. Public menorah lightings led by a rabbi follow their own custom.

### I am staying at my parents' house for Chanukah. Do I light?
You are included in your parents' lighting if you are a guest in their home, but many have the custom that each person lights their own menorah. Ask the Rabbi what fits your family.
