Av (אָב) is the fifth month of the Jewish year counting from Nissan, usually in July and August, with 30 days. It begins with the Nine Days of mourning for the destruction of the Holy Temple, reaches Tisha B'Av on its ninth day, and then turns toward comfort and joy, which is why Chabad calls it Menachem Av, "Av the comforter."

## What is the month of Av?

Av follows Tammuz and comes before Elul. Its special days are the Nine Days, Tisha B'Av (the ninth of Av), Shabbat Nachamu and Tu B'Av (the fifteenth). See the [Jewish calendar](/learn/jewish-calendar) for how the months work, and [this week](/this-week) for today's Hebrew date.

## Why "Menachem Av"?

Menachem (מְנַחֵם) means "one who comforts." In Chabad the month is named Menachem Av because, though it opens with grief, its message is comfort: the pain of the exile is meant to lead to the rebuilding and the days of joy to come. Chabad teaching looks forward to a time, in the days of [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach), when these days of mourning become days of joy.

## The Nine Days

The Nine Days run from the first of Av until the morning of the tenth, the most intense part of the [Three Weeks](/learn/three-weeks). Customs include:

- Not eating meat or drinking wine, except on Shabbat and at a festive mitzvah meal, such as a brit, a bar mitzvah or a siyum.
- Avoiding laundered clothing and new clothes, and not swimming for pleasure.
- Not holding weddings or celebrations, and avoiding unnecessary risky travel.

Customs differ between communities, so ask the Rabbi about your own case.

## Joy inside the sadness: the siyum

Following the Rebbe's encouragement, people complete a tractate of the Talmud during the Nine Days. A completion, a siyum, is a joyful occasion, which brings permitted celebration into these days. Chabad also encourages more Torah study, tzedakah and acts of kindness to answer the baseless hatred that Jewish tradition says led to the destruction.

## Tisha B'Av and the Shabbat before it

The Shabbat before the ninth is called Shabbat Chazon, "the Shabbat of the Vision." On Tisha B'Av itself we fast, sit low, read Eichah (Lamentations) and Kinot (laments). Find the details in [Tisha B'Av for beginners](/learn/tisha-bav-for-beginners) and [Tisha B'Av](/holidays/tisha-bav).

## Comfort: Shabbat Nachamu and Tu B'Av

The Shabbat after Tisha B'Av is Shabbat Nachamu, named for the haftarah that begins "Comfort, comfort My people." Chabad.org describes it as a time of delight, after the fast. On Tu B'Av, the fifteenth of Av, we celebrate a minor holiday that marks a complete turn from the sadness of Tisha B'Av. Chabad communities do not say the Tachanun prayer on that day. Read about [Tu B'Av](/learn/tu-bav).

## Common questions

### What is the Jewish month of Av?
Av is the fifth month counting from Nissan, usually in July and August, with 30 days. It contains the Nine Days, Tisha B'Av and Tu B'Av.

### Why is it called Menachem Av?
Menachem means "comforter." The month is called Menachem Av because it moves from mourning to comfort and the hope for the rebuilding.

### What are the Nine Days?
They are the first nine days of Av, up to Tisha B'Av, a time of deeper mourning with customs such as no meat or wine, except on Shabbat.

### What is Shabbat Nachamu?
It is the Shabbat after Tisha B'Av, named for the haftarah "Comfort, comfort My people." It marks the turn to consolation.

### What is Tu B'Av?
Tu B'Av is the fifteenth of Av, a minor holiday that marks a complete turn from the sadness of Tisha B'Av to joy.

### Can I eat meat during the Nine Days?
The custom is not to, except on Shabbat and at a mitzvah meal such as a brit or siyum. If you are ill or pregnant, ask the Rabbi.
