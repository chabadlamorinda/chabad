Jewish prayer is a daily conversation with G-d, made up of set prayers from the siddur (prayer book) and our own personal words. Jews traditionally pray three times a day: in the morning, afternoon and evening.

## What prayer is

The Hebrew word for prayer is tefillah (תְּפִלָּה). The Sages call prayer "the service of the heart." It includes praising G-d, asking for our needs and thanking Him. Chassidic teaching adds that tefillah comes from a word meaning "to connect": prayer is how the soul bonds with G-d, every single day.

The Torah does not set a text for prayer. In the early Second Temple era, the Men of the Great Assembly, a council of Sages and prophets led by Ezra, composed the core prayers, including the Amidah, so every Jew would have the right words.

## The three daily prayers

- **Shacharit** (morning): the longest service, including the morning blessings, Psalms of praise, the [Shema](/learn/shema) and its blessings, and the Amidah. Men wear [tallit](/learn/tzitzit) and [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) on weekdays.
- **Mincha** (afternoon): a short service, centered on the Amidah, said from early afternoon until sunset.
- **Maariv** (evening): said after nightfall, including the Shema and the Amidah.

On Shabbat, holidays and Rosh Chodesh, an extra service called Musaf is added.

## The heart of the service: the Amidah

The Amidah ("standing prayer"), also called Shemoneh Esreh, is said silently while standing with feet together, facing Jerusalem. On weekdays it has 19 blessings: praise, requests (for wisdom, health, livelihood, redemption and more) and thanks. On Shabbat and holidays, the middle requests are replaced with blessings about the day.

## How to start praying

You do not need to do everything at once. Here are simple steps:

1. **Say [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani)** as soon as you wake up.
2. **Say the Shema** morning and night. The first verse alone is a great start.
3. **Get a siddur** with English translation. Chabad uses the Siddur Tehillat Hashem, in the Nusach Ari tradition.
4. **Pick one prayer** and say it daily, adding more over time.
5. **Talk to G-d in your own words.** Personal prayer is always welcome, in any language.
6. **Join a minyan.** Praying with a community adds power to our prayers.

## Praying with a minyan

A minyan is a group of ten Jewish men age 13 and older. Some parts of the service, such as Kaddish, the Torah reading and the repetition of the Amidah, are said only with a minyan. The Sages teach that G-d's presence rests among a praying community, and that communal prayer is especially accepted.

## Women and prayer

Women are obligated in prayer too. Many authorities hold that women should say at least the morning blessings and the Amidah of Shacharit, and ideally Mincha as well. At minimum, a heartfelt prayer each day with praise, a request and thanks fulfills the basic mitzvah according to some opinions. Ask the Rabbi for guidance that fits your schedule.

## Common questions

### Can I pray in English?
Yes. Jewish law allows praying in any language you understand, and G-d hears every prayer. Hebrew has special holiness, so many people mix the two, saying what they can in Hebrew and the rest in English.

### Do I need to go to synagogue to pray?
No. You can pray anywhere, at home, at work or on a hike. Praying with a minyan is preferable when possible, but private prayer is fully valid.

### What should I do if I do not understand the Hebrew?
Use a siddur with translation, and read the English if you need to. Over time, learning the meaning of the main prayers makes them much more meaningful.

### How long does daily prayer take?
The full morning service takes about 30 to 45 minutes, while Mincha and Maariv take around 10 to 15 minutes each. A shorter prayer said with feeling is better than a long one said in a rush.
