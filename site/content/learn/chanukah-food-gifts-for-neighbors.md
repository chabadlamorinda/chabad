A good Chanukah food gift is a small, sealed treat with a clear kosher symbol, such as doughnut holes, cookies or gelt, given with a note about the holiday. It shares the warmth and light of Chanukah with the people around you.

## Why give food on Chanukah?

Chanukah's mitzvah is to bring light, and the traditions of the holiday include bringing light to others: visitors bring menorahs, treats and cards to people in hospitals and nursing homes. A plate of treats for a neighbor in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda is a small version of the same idea. Our [Chanukah gifts](/learn/chanukah-gifts) guide has more ways to give, and [being a good neighbor](/learn/being-a-good-neighbor) is about the spirit.

## Gift ideas

Chanukah dessert ideas include gelt fudge, gelt cookies, honey and spice cookies, butter cookie dreidels and chocolate mint coins. Any of these make a nice gift. Other simple ideas:

- A small bag of doughnut holes or cookies, tied with blue and white ribbon.
- A jar of fudge or a bag of gelt.
- A jar of applesauce, with a card about the latkes it goes with.
- A box of treats for a class or an office.
- A card with a line about the holiday, and an invitation to the community lighting.

## Giving to someone who keeps kosher

If the person keeps kosher, the best gift is a sealed package with a reliable kosher symbol. Homemade food from a kitchen that is not kosher may not be something they can eat, and a loving gift should not put them in a hard spot. Choose [pareve](/learn/pareve) treats when you can, since pareve foods fit with meat or dairy meals. Read [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols), and see [hosting a kosher guest](/learn/hosting-a-kosher-guest) for the guest's side. If you are not sure what a friend eats, ask them kindly.

[Bay Kosher](/programs/bay-kosher), a project of Chabad of Lamorinda, is a local source for Chanukah and kosher items and is always kosher.

## Teachers and coworkers

Many schools and offices have gift rules, so check first. Keep gifts modest. Note allergies: peanuts, nuts and eggs can matter. A sealed, labeled treat is easy for everyone. See [Chanukah for friends and coworkers](/learn/chanukah-for-friends-and-coworkers) for how to explain the holiday kindly, and [Chanukah at work and college](/learn/chanukah-at-work-and-college).

## Share a little light with those who need it

Bring a plate to an elderly neighbor, someone who is ill, or a person who is alone. It is a lovely custom to bring treats and cards to hospitals and nursing homes. Chabad of Lamorinda's Rabbi and Rebbetzin visit the sick at home, in the hospital and in care facilities. If you know someone who would welcome a visit or help, see [help for seniors and families](/help/help-for-seniors-and-families) or [ask for help](/forms/need-help). You can [volunteer](/forms/volunteer) or help [sponsor visits and help](/donate?fund=chesed#give).

## A simple wrapping idea

1. Put treats in a clear bag or a small box.
2. Add a card: "Happy Chanukah! A little light for you."
3. List the ingredients if you made it yourself, or leave the original kosher symbol visible.
4. Invite them to the menorah lighting.

## Common questions

### What is a good food gift for Chanukah?
A sealed, kosher-certified treat such as gelt, cookies or doughnut holes, with a short card. Pareve treats are the most flexible.

### What should I bring to a Chanukah party?
Ask the host what they need. For a kosher home, bring a sealed kosher-certified treat, or ask first. See [hosting a Chanukah party](/learn/hosting-a-chanukah-party).

### Can I give homemade treats to a kosher friend?
It depends on the friend, and many cannot eat food from a non-kosher kitchen. Ask kindly, or give a sealed kosher-certified item.

### Is it okay to give Chanukah gifts to teachers?
Check the school's rules first. A small sealed treat and a thank-you note is a safe choice.

### Can I bring food to someone in the hospital?
Ask the family and the hospital, since patients may have diet limits. A card or a visit may be best.

### How can I invite a neighbor to Chanukah?
Invite them to the Grand Chanukah Menorah Lighting on Sunday, December 6 at 4:00 PM at Lafayette Plaza Park. It is free and open to everyone.
