On Shavuot morning, Jewish families bring their children, even the youngest babies, to the synagogue to hear the Ten Commandments read aloud, because the Lubavitcher Rebbe taught that there is special significance in children hearing them. The Midrash says that when G-d offered the Torah, He asked for guarantors, and the Jewish people finally offered their children, so the children are the ones who guarantee that the Torah continues.

## Why the children are the guarantors

Chabad.org retells the Midrash this way. Before giving the Torah, G-d asked the Jewish people for guarantors that they would keep it. They offered the patriarchs, and G-d said it was not enough. They offered the prophets, and that was not enough either. Finally they said, "Our children will be our guarantors," and G-d accepted. That is why we bring "the guarantors" to hear the commandments.

The Rebbe explained that children absorb the words of the Torah deeply, and through them, their parents and grandparents are also moved. Chabad.org notes that the Ten Commandments contain the whole Torah, and that hearing them shapes our daily life during the whole year. The Rebbe began calling for this widely in 1980, asking that boys and girls all over come to hear. See [the Ten Commandments](/learn/ten-commandments).

## What to expect

- The Ten Commandments are read during the morning service of the first day of Shavuot. Check the date on the [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays) page.
- Many synagogues have a children's program with games, treats and often an ice-cream party. Chabad.org says these are now very popular.
- Children and women are not obligated by the strict letter of the law to attend, but the Rebbe strongly encouraged it. Ask the Rabbi if you have questions.
- Babies and toddlers are welcome. Bring a snack and a quiet toy, and do not worry about the noise. See [holidays with toddlers](/learn/holidays-with-toddlers).

## Fun ideas for Shavuot at home

1. **Tell the Sinai story at bedtime.** The Jewish people camped at the foot of the mountain as one person with one heart, and G-d gave us the Torah. Ask your child: what would you have said? See [the month of Sivan](/learn/sivan).
2. **Eat something dairy together.** Ice cream, cheese blintzes and cheesecake are classic Shavuot treats. The reasons are in [Shavuot dairy foods](/learn/shavuot-dairy-foods).
3. **Make a craft.** Paper tablets with the Ten Commandments, or a picture of Mount Sinai, are easy and fun. Ask the Rabbi about the customs of decorating the home.
4. **Read the story of Ruth.** A short retelling of [the Book of Ruth](/learn/book-of-ruth) shows children what kindness and loyalty look like.
5. **Talk about the commandments.** Pick one a day and ask: what does it mean to honor my parents? To be honest? To be thankful?
6. **Stay up a little late.** Many families stay up learning on the first night. Even ten extra minutes of a Torah story makes a child feel part of it. See [Shavuot night learning](/learn/shavuot-night-learning).

## A little goes a long way

You do not need to do everything. Choose one or two things, and do them with joy. What children remember is that Shavuot was the day the whole family went together, listened together, and ate something sweet. That memory is a gift that lasts.

## Common questions

### Why do we bring children to hear the Ten Commandments on Shavuot?
Because the Midrash says the Jewish people's children were the guarantors of the Torah, and the Lubavitcher Rebbe taught that there is special significance in bringing even the youngest children to hear it.

### Should I bring my baby to hear the Ten Commandments?
Yes. The Rebbe encouraged bringing even the smallest infants, as the words of the Torah are absorbed by the children and also move their parents. Babies are welcome.

### When are the Ten Commandments read?
During the morning service on the first day of Shavuot. See the [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays) page for this year's date and call 925-384-7242 for local times.

### Is it required for children to come?
Not by the strict letter of the law, but it is a beloved and strongly encouraged custom. Ask the Rabbi if you have a question about your family.

### What are good Shavuot activities for kids?
Telling the Sinai story, baking or eating dairy treats, making paper tablets, reading the story of Ruth and talking about the commandments are all easy options.
