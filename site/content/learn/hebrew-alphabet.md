The Hebrew alphabet, called the aleph-bet, has 22 letters, all consonants, written and read from right to left. Vowels are shown with small dots and lines, and five letters take a different form at the end of a word.

## The 22 letters

- **א Aleph:** silent, carries a vowel
- **ב Bet / Vet:** b (with a dot), v (without)
- **ג Gimmel:** g
- **ד Dalet:** d
- **ה Hei:** h
- **ו Vav:** v
- **ז Zayin:** z
- **ח Chet:** ch (as in Bach)
- **ט Tet:** t
- **י Yud:** y
- **כ Kaf / Chaf:** k (with a dot), ch (without)
- **ל Lamed:** l
- **מ Mem:** m
- **נ Nun:** n
- **ס Samech:** s
- **ע Ayin:** silent, carries a vowel
- **פ Pei / Fei:** p (with a dot), f (without)
- **צ Tzadi:** tz
- **ק Kuf:** k
- **ר Reish:** r
- **ש Shin / Sin:** sh (dot on right), s (dot on left)
- **ת Tav:** t (Ashkenazim pronounce it s without a dot)

## The five final letters

Five letters change shape at the end of a word:

- כ becomes ך (final chaf)
- מ becomes ם (final mem)
- נ becomes ן (final nun)
- פ becomes ף (final fei)
- צ becomes ץ (final tzadi)

## Vowels (nekudot)

Hebrew vowels are dots and dashes placed below, above or beside the letters. For example, a short line under a letter (patach) makes an "ah" sound, and a dot above (cholam) makes an "oh" sound. Torah scrolls are written without vowels; readers learn the correct pronunciation by heart. Prayer books and learning texts include them.

## Letters with deep meaning

Jewish tradition teaches that the Hebrew letters are much more than symbols. According to Kabbalah, G-d created the world through the letters of the Ten Utterances of creation, and these letters continue to give life to everything. Each letter has its own meaning:

- **Aleph** has the numerical value of one, representing the One G-d.
- **Bet** means "house," and it is the first letter of the Torah, teaching that the world is G-d's home.
- **Shin** appears on the [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) and on the head [tefillin](/learn/tefillin), standing for one of G-d's names.

Every letter also has a number value (gematria). Aleph is 1, Bet is 2, Yud is 10, Kuf is 100, and so on. Gematria reveals hidden connections between words.

## Learning the aleph-bet

Tips for beginners:

1. **Learn a few letters at a time,** practicing similar-looking pairs like ב and כ, or ד and ר.
2. **Add vowels** once you know the letters.
3. **Practice with prayers you hear often,** like the [Shema](/learn/shema).
4. **Use songs and flashcards** for children.

A Jewish child traditionally begins learning the letters at age three, at the [upsherin](/learn/upsherin). Children can continue at our [Hebrew school](/programs/hebrew-school).

## Common questions

### How long does it take to learn to read Hebrew?

Many adults learn to sound out Hebrew in a few weeks of regular practice. Fluency takes longer, but reading prayers comes faster than you might think.

### Is Biblical Hebrew the same as modern Hebrew?

They share the same alphabet and much vocabulary. Modern Israeli Hebrew adds new words and differs in some grammar and pronunciation.

### What is the difference between Ashkenazi and Sephardi pronunciation?

The main differences are a few vowels and the letter ת without a dot, which Ashkenazim pronounce as "s" and Sephardim as "t." Both are authentic traditions.

### Why is Hebrew called the holy tongue?

Hebrew is called lashon hakodesh, the holy language, because G-d gave the Torah in Hebrew and, according to tradition, created the world with it.
