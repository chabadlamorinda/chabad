The 613 mitzvot are the commandments G-d gave in the Torah: 248 positive commandments ("do") and 365 negative commandments ("do not"). They cover every part of life, from prayer and holidays to business ethics, food and how we treat other people.

## What is a mitzvah?

Mitzvah means "commandment." It is related to the Aramaic word tzavta, meaning "connection." Chassidic teaching explains that every mitzvah is a way of connecting with G-d. When a person does a mitzvah, they join their will to G-d's will.

In everyday speech, "a mitzvah" also means a good deed. That is true too, but the deeper meaning is a divine instruction that bonds us with our Creator.

## Where does the number 613 come from?

The Talmud (Makkot 23b) teaches that 613 mitzvot were given to Moses at Sinai. The numbers carry meaning:

- **248 positive mitzvot** correspond to the 248 limbs of the human body, as counted by the sages.
- **365 negative mitzvot** correspond to the 365 days of the solar year.

The message: every part of us and every day of our lives can be devoted to G-d.

## How the mitzvot are counted

The Torah does not list the mitzvot by number, so great scholars compiled lists. The most famous is Maimonides' Sefer HaMitzvot, written in the 12th century. Other authorities, such as Nachmanides, count some mitzvot differently, but all agree on the total of 613.

## Types of mitzvot

Jewish tradition groups the mitzvot in several ways:

- **Between people and G-d** (bein adam laMakom): such as prayer, Shabbat and [tefillin](/learn/tefillin).
- **Between people** (bein adam lachaveiro): such as honesty, giving [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) and honoring parents.
- **Mishpatim:** laws whose logic is clear, like not stealing.
- **Eidut:** "testimonies" that commemorate events, like Passover.
- **Chukim:** laws beyond human understanding, like kosher rules, done purely because G-d asked.

## Which mitzvot apply today?

Not all 613 mitzvot apply to everyone at all times. Many depend on the Holy Temple in Jerusalem, on living in the Land of Israel, or on being a Kohen. Some apply only to men or to women. Today a few hundred mitzvot can be practiced, and many people keep dozens of them every day without realizing it.

The Sages also added rabbinic mitzvot, such as lighting Chanukah candles and reading the Megillah on Purim.

## Do I have to do them all at once?

No. Judaism is not all or nothing. Every single mitzvah has infinite value on its own. [The Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe) encouraged people to add one mitzvah at a time, such as lighting Shabbat candles, putting on tefillin or affixing a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah).

## Common questions

### What are the Ten Commandments?

The Ten Commandments, given at Mount Sinai, are among the 613 mitzvot. Jewish tradition teaches that they contain the roots of all the others.

### Do non-Jews have to keep the 613 mitzvot?

No. The 613 mitzvot are for Jews. Non-Jews are asked to keep the [Seven Noahide Laws](/learn/noahide-laws), a universal moral code.

### What are some easy mitzvot to start with?

Giving tzedakah, saying a blessing before eating, lighting Shabbat candles, and saying the Shema at bedtime are wonderful places to begin.

### Are rabbinic mitzvot as important as Torah mitzvot?

The Torah itself instructs us to follow the sages, so rabbinic mitzvot are part of keeping the Torah. They are taken seriously, though the laws for cases of doubt can differ.
