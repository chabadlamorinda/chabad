Chanukah is a holiday of thanks. Besides lighting the menorah, the Sages added two things to our daily prayers for all eight days: the prayer **Al HaNissim**, "for the miracles," and the joyful psalms of **Hallel**.

## Al HaNissim: "For the miracles"

Al HaNissim thanks G-d "for the miracles, for the redemption, for the mighty deeds and for the victories" that He did for our ancestors "in those days, at this time." On Chanukah it continues with the story of Matityahu and his sons, the Maccabees, who stood up to the Greek-Syrian rulers, rededicated the Holy Temple and kindled its lights.

It is added in two places:

- **In the Amidah**, the standing prayer, in the blessing of thanks (Modim), three times a day.
- **In Grace after Meals** (Birkat Hamazon), in the second blessing, the blessing of thanks for the land.

The same prayer, with the story of Esther and Mordechai, is said on Purim.

## If you forgot it

If you forgot Al HaNissim and already finished that blessing, you do not go back or repeat the prayer. In Grace after Meals, you may add a short request among the "Harachaman" lines near the end: "May the Merciful One do miracles for us as He did for our ancestors in those days, at this time," followed by the words of Al HaNissim. Your siddur shows exactly where.

## Hallel every day of Chanukah

Hallel is a group of psalms of praise (Psalms 113 to 118), sung after the morning Amidah. On Chanukah the **full Hallel** is said on all eight days, with its blessing. This is different from Passover, when the full Hallel is said only on the first days. On Chanukah, each day brought a greater miracle, as the oil lasted another day, so each day is celebrated completely.

## More Chanukah changes in the synagogue

- **Torah reading every day**, from the portion that describes the gifts the tribal leaders brought when the Tabernacle was dedicated.
- **No Tachanun**, the prayer of supplication, because these are days of joy.
- On **Rosh Chodesh Tevet**, which falls during Chanukah, two Torah scrolls are read and Musaf is added.

## Common questions

### Do women say Al HaNissim?
Yes. Women who pray the Amidah or say Grace after Meals add Al HaNissim just as men do.

### Is Al HaNissim said at night when lighting the menorah?
No. At the menorah we say the Chanukah blessings and then Haneirot Halalu. Al HaNissim is part of the Amidah and Grace after Meals.

### Can I say Hallel at home if I cannot come to the synagogue?
Yes. Hallel can be said on your own, preferably in the morning after the Amidah. With a minyan it is said with responsive verses, which is an even greater joy.

### Why is Hallel not said on Purim?
The Talmud gives reasons, among them that reading the Megillah is itself Purim's Hallel. On Chanukah Hallel is said every day.
