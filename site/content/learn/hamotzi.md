Hamotzi is the blessing Jews say before eating bread. It thanks G-d "who brings forth bread from the earth," and it begins every Shabbat and holiday meal, as well as any meal with bread.

## The blessing

**Hebrew:** בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, הַמּוֹצִיא לֶחֶם מִן הָאָרֶץ

**Transliteration:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, hamotzi lechem min ha'aretz.

**Translation:** Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who brings forth bread from the earth.

## Why bread has its own blessing

Bread is the most basic food, and it takes the most partnership between people and G-d: planting, harvesting, grinding, kneading and baking. Bread is so central that its blessing covers the whole meal. Once you say Hamotzi, you do not need separate blessings for most other foods eaten as part of that meal.

## When do you say Hamotzi?

Say Hamotzi over bread made from any of the five grains: wheat, barley, rye, oats or spelt. This includes challah, bagels, pita, rolls and sandwich bread. Matzah also gets Hamotzi.

Cakes, cookies, crackers and pastries get a different blessing, *mezonot*, unless you make a full meal of them. Some foods fall in between, so ask the Rabbi about specific items. See our guide to [blessings](/learn/blessings) for other foods.

## How to say Hamotzi, step by step

1. **Wash your hands** with a cup and say the blessing for washing, *al netilat yadayim*.
2. **Do not speak** between washing and eating the bread.
3. **Uncover the bread** and place your hands on it. On Shabbat and holidays, use two whole loaves (*lechem mishneh*), recalling the double portion of manna.
4. **Say Hamotzi** clearly, so others at the table can answer "Amen."
5. **Cut or tear** a slice.
6. **Dip it in salt.** The Chabad custom is to dip the bread in salt three times.
7. **Eat** a piece right away, then share bread with everyone at the table.

## Salt on the table

The table is compared to the altar of the Holy Temple, where every offering was salted. Salt also stands for an everlasting covenant with G-d. That is why bread is dipped in salt after Hamotzi.

## After the meal

If you ate at least an olive-sized piece of bread (a *kezayit*, about one ounce), you say Grace After Meals, [Birkat Hamazon](/learn/birkat-hamazon), at the end of the meal.

## Common questions

### Does one person say Hamotzi for everyone?
At a Shabbat or family meal, the host often says Hamotzi for the whole table. Everyone listens, answers "Amen," and then eats from the bread he cut. Each person still washes his or her own hands first.

### Do I need to wash my hands before Hamotzi?
Yes, when eating bread, Jewish law calls for washing hands with a cup and a blessing before the meal. If you cannot wash, ask the Rabbi what to do.

### What if I forgot to say Hamotzi?
If you remember while still eating bread, say the blessing then and continue. If you already finished, simply say Birkat Hamazon at the end.

### Why do we cover the challah on Shabbat?
On Friday night, Kiddush over wine comes before Hamotzi. Covering the challah keeps it from being "embarrassed" while it waits its turn, a beautiful lesson in sensitivity to others.
