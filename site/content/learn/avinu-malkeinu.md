Avinu Malkeinu (אָבִינוּ מַלְכֵּנוּ, "Our Father, Our King") is a short, heartfelt prayer in which we call out to G-d as both a loving Father and a mighty King and ask for mercy. It is said during the High Holidays, the Ten Days of Repentance and on most fast days, and its first words come from a prayer of Rabbi Akiva in the Talmud.

## What is Avinu Malkeinu?

Avinu Malkeinu is a list of simple requests, each beginning with the same two words: "Our Father, our King." We ask for a good year, for health, for livelihood, for forgiveness, for peace, and for our prayers to be heard. Many congregations sing the last lines together, and the tune is among the best known of the High Holiday season.

Its power is its directness. There are no long arguments, only a child's cry. For someone with little Hebrew, it is one of the easiest places to feel the prayer: the repeated words are simple, and the melody carries the rest.

## The story of Rabbi Akiva

The Talmud (Taanit 25b) tells of a terrible drought. Rabbi Eliezer led the community in a fast and recited twenty-four blessings, but no rain came. Then Rabbi Akiva stood and said: "Our Father, our King, we have no king but You. Our Father, our King, for Your sake have mercy on us." Rain fell.

Our teachers draw a lesson from it. Rabbi Akiva was known for forgiving those who wronged him, and he was able to ask G-d to forgive us in the same way. That is why this prayer is so closely connected to the days of forgiveness.

## Why both "Father" and "King"?

Chabad.org explains that each name alone is incomplete:

- **A Father** loves us and forgives us as His children. But if we thought of G-d only as a father, we might feel we can ignore His instructions.
- **A King** is all-powerful and sets the rules that direct our lives. But if we thought of G-d only as a king, we might feel He is distant and not personally invested in us.

Together the two names say that G-d wants to help us and is able to help us. When a problem is bigger than we can solve ourselves, we can turn to G-d directly with both love and awe.

## When is Avinu Malkeinu said?

It is said:

1. During the Ten Days of Repentance, from Rosh Hashanah to Yom Kippur, in the morning and afternoon prayers (see [the Ten Days of Repentance](/learn/ten-days-of-repentance)).
2. On Yom Kippur, including the closing service.
3. On most fast days (see [minor fast days](/learn/minor-fast-days)).

It is generally not said on Shabbat, when we do not make requests of this kind; ask the Rabbi about the exact practice when the days fall on Shabbat. The Selichot prayers that begin before Rosh Hashanah are described in [our Selichot guide](/learn/selichot).

## How to say it with meaning

You do not need to say every line. Choose one line that matches your life this year, such as health, peace at home, a child's path or work, and say it slowly in English or in your own words. Children can join in on the repeated words "Avinu Malkeinu."

## Common questions

### What does Avinu Malkeinu mean?
Avinu Malkeinu means "Our Father, Our King." It is a prayer addressing G-d as a loving parent and as a ruler, asking for forgiveness, blessing and a good year.

### When do we say Avinu Malkeinu?
It is said during the Ten Days of Repentance, on Yom Kippur, and on most fast days. It is generally not said on Shabbat; ask the Rabbi for details.

### Who wrote Avinu Malkeinu?
The first lines come from a prayer of Rabbi Akiva in the Talmud, said during a drought, after which rain fell. Later generations added more lines.

### Why do we call G-d both Father and King?
Because each name teaches something different: a Father loves and forgives, a King is powerful and gives direction. Together they say G-d both wants to help us and is able to.

### Do I have to say it in Hebrew?
No. You may say it in English or in your own words; the feeling matters more than the language. Ask the Rabbi if you would like help.
