Children learn about money long before they earn any. They watch how we spend, how we save and how we give. Judaism offers simple, lifelong habits that turn money into a way to do good.

## Tzedakah: justice, not just charity

The Hebrew word *tzedakah* means "justice" or "righteousness." Judaism teaches that what we have is entrusted to us by G-d, and part of it belongs to those in need. Giving is not only kind; it is right. Children understand this easily: we share because that is what good people do. See [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).

## A pushka of their own

The simplest habit is a *pushka*, a tzedakah box, in the child's room and in the kitchen. A coin before Shabbat candle lighting, a coin before morning prayers, a coin when something good happens. The Rebbe encouraged every child to have their own pushka, so that giving becomes part of who they are. See [the pushka](/learn/pushka).

## Giving from their own money

When children get an allowance, gift money or Chanukah gelt, many families teach them to set aside a tenth, *maaser*, for tzedakah. Let them choose where it goes: a food drive, a friend in need, a Jewish cause. Choosing teaches them that their giving matters. See [maaser](/learn/maaser).

## Giving many times

Maimonides teaches that giving a small coin many times shapes a generous heart more than one big gift. For children, frequent small giving is the goal: every day, every Friday, every celebration.

## Honest money

Jewish law is careful about honesty: returning lost things, paying people on time, not taking what is not ours. Children learn from small moments, like returning extra change at the store or giving back a toy that was left behind. See [honesty and truth](/learn/honesty-and-truth).

## Happy with what we have

"Who is rich? The one who is happy with his portion" (Ethics of the Fathers 4:1). In a world of ads and comparison, teach children to say thank you for what they have. A blessing before eating, a thank-you note, a gratitude moment at the Shabbat table all help. See [the Jewish view on wealth](/learn/jewish-view-on-wealth).

## Ideas for your family

- **Count it together.** Empty the pushka with your child and decide together where it goes.
- **Give in person.** Bring food to a food drive or a meal to a new mother; children remember what they do with their hands.
- **Chanukah gelt with a twist.** Give gelt, and ask each child to set some aside for tzedakah. See [Chanukah gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt).
- **Talk about it.** Tell your children when and why you give; let them see that it matters to you.

## Common questions

### At what age can children start giving tzedakah?
As soon as they can hold a coin. Even toddlers love dropping coins into a pushka.

### Should children give their own money or ours?
Both are good. Giving them coins to put in teaches the habit; giving from their own allowance teaches that it is truly theirs to share.

### Where can our family give locally?
Ask the Rabbi for ideas that fit your family, from helping a neighbor to supporting Jewish life in Lamorinda.
