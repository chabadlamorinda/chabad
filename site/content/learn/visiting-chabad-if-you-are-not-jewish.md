Maybe your husband, wife, friend or coworker is Jewish. Maybe you are a student studying world religions, a neighbor curious about Chanukah, or a teacher bringing a class. Can you visit a synagogue or come to a Shabbat dinner if you are not Jewish? Yes. You are welcome as our guest at Chabad of Lamorinda in Lafayette.

## Why you are welcome

The Torah says the Holy Temple in Jerusalem would be "a house of prayer for all nations" (Isaiah 56:7). Judaism does not ask other people to become Jewish, but it teaches that every person is created in the image of G-d, and welcoming guests, hachnasat orchim, is one of its most loved mitzvot. Abraham kept his tent open on all four sides for every traveler.

## What to wear

- **Dress modestly**, as you would for a respectful occasion. Women usually wear a skirt or dress below the knee with sleeves; men wear long pants and a shirt.
- **Men** cover their heads in the synagogue with a [kippah](/learn/kippah). If you do not have one, just ask.
- Nobody expects you to know everything. If you are unsure, just ask.

## What happens at services

Services are mostly in Hebrew, with prayer books that have English to borrow. You may sit quietly, follow along, or simply listen. In a traditional synagogue like ours, men and women sit in separate sections ([why?](/learn/mechitzah)). Stand when the congregation stands, especially when the Torah scroll is carried or the Ark is open. Read more in [first time at synagogue](/learn/first-time-at-synagogue).

Some parts of the service are for Jewish participants: being counted in the minyan (the group of ten), being called up to the Torah, or leading the prayers. Nobody will put you on the spot.

## Shabbat customs to know

From Friday at sunset until Saturday night, Jews keep Shabbat, a day of rest from work and technology. As a guest in the synagogue or at a Shabbat meal:

- Please keep your phone silent and out of sight, and do not take photos.
- Please do not smoke, write or use electronics in the synagogue or at the table on Shabbat.
- Wait for the blessings over wine (Kiddush) and bread (Hamotzi) before eating. See [invited for Shabbat dinner?](/learn/shabbat-guest-guide)

## For classes and groups

Teachers, scouts, church groups and university students are welcome to learn about Judaism. Please call ahead so the Rabbi can plan a visit that fits your group and is not on a busy holiday.

## Judaism's message for every person

Judaism teaches that people of every nation can have a close relationship with G-d by keeping the [Seven Noahide Laws](/learn/noahide-laws): believing in one G-d, respecting His name, and living with justice, honesty and kindness. You do not need to be Jewish to be close to G-d.

## Common questions

### Can a non-Jew go to a synagogue?
Yes. A non-Jewish visitor is welcome to attend services as a guest, sit quietly and follow along. Dress modestly, and men cover their heads with a kippah; just ask if you need one.

### Can I bring my non-Jewish spouse or friend to Shabbat dinner at Chabad?
Yes. Guests of every background are welcome at a Shabbat table. Let us know you are coming through the Shabbat meal form or by calling 925-384-7242.

### Do I have to participate in the prayers?
No. You are welcome to sit, listen and follow along in English. Some honors, like being called to the Torah, are for Jewish participants.

### Can a school or church group visit Chabad of Lamorinda?
Groups are welcome to learn about Judaism. Please call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 ahead of time to arrange a visit.
