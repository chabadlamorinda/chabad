To show you are Jewish, you show that your mother, her mother and so on are Jewish, or that you converted according to Jewish law. A rabbi looks at documents and family testimony, and the Rabbi at Chabad of Lamorinda is glad to help families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda sort this out before a wedding, bar mitzvah or aliyah.

## Why a rabbi asks

Jewish identity follows the mother's line, as explained in [who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew). Before a Jewish wedding, a bar or bat mitzvah, a conversion, or moving to Israel, a rabbi needs to be sure, because these matters affect the whole family and future generations. Please do not take the question as doubt about you. It is care, and for most families it is quick and simple.

## Papers that can help

Different families have different papers. A rabbi may look at:

- **A parent's or grandmother's Jewish marriage certificate** (ketubah).
- **Your mother's own Jewish documents**, such as a synagogue membership record or a Hebrew name.
- **Jewish burial records** of your mother, grandmother or great-grandmother, for example a Jewish cemetery record or a tombstone with Hebrew writing.
- **Birth certificates** that show your mother's and grandmother's names.
- **Soviet-era documents.** In the Soviet Union an internal passport listed "nationality" on a fifth line, and many families have documents where a mother or grandmother is listed as "Jewish" (Evrei). This is often a useful clue, and the Rabbi can tell you how much it shows in your case.
- **Testimony** from relatives who knew the family, written or in person.

You do not need all of these. Bring what you have, even if it is a single photo or a name.

## If you have no papers

Many families lost their papers through war, the Holocaust, emigration or the suppression of Jewish life. See [Judaism behind the Iron Curtain](/learn/soviet-jewry-history). Missing papers do not mean the end. Ask the Rabbi. Often other evidence helps, such as how your family lived, relatives' recollections and community knowledge. What the Rabbi can accept depends on your particular story.

## Common questions

### How can I prove I am Jewish?

Show that your mother and her mother were Jewish, with a ketubah, Jewish burial records, birth certificates, old Soviet papers, or testimony from relatives. The Rabbi will tell you what is enough in your case.

### Is a Soviet passport with "Jewish" nationality proof?

It is a helpful clue, especially for a mother or grandmother, but it is not always enough alone. Bring it to the Rabbi to look at together with your family story.

### What if my family papers were lost?

Ask the Rabbi. Other evidence, such as relatives' accounts and how the family lived, may help, depending on your story.

### Do I need proof to get married by a rabbi?

For a Jewish wedding according to Jewish law, the Rabbi will need to be sure that both the bride and groom are Jewish. Call early, ideally several months ahead.

### Is this needed for aliyah to Israel?

Israeli authorities and the Rabbinate have their own requirements. A rabbi who knows your family can often help gather letters and documents, so ask the Rabbi early.

### Will my question be kept private?

Yes. The Rabbi speaks privately, and no membership is required.
