Mazal tov (מַזָּל טוֹב) is the Hebrew greeting for congratulations, and it literally means "good fortune" or "a good sign." Jewish tradition teaches that mazal is a spiritual channel through which blessing flows from above, but also that a Jew is never trapped by the stars.

## What mazal means

The word mazal is connected both to a "constellation" and to something that "flows down" from above. When we say mazal tov at a birth, a wedding or a Bar Mitzvah, we are wishing that good flows to the person, and also saying "we are happy with you." Jewish phrases like this are collected in [Hebrew phrases](/learn/hebrew-phrases).

## "Israel is above mazal"

The Talmud tells that Abraham, who knew astrology, saw in the stars that he would not have children. G-d took him outside and told him, in effect, that he is above the stars. The Sages derived from this: "Israel has no mazal," meaning a Jew's fate is not decided by the stars (Shabbat 156a). Our deeds, our prayers and the mitzvahs we do can change what happens. This idea also goes together with [free will](/learn/free-will) and [personal Divine providence](/learn/hashgacha-pratit).

## Do Jews believe in astrology?

Jewish sources hold different views. Some great sages thought the stars influence the world. Maimonides, the Rambam, strongly rejected astrology as false and forbade relying on it. The Torah forbids fortune-telling, and tells us to be wholehearted with G-d (Deuteronomy 18:10 to 13). In practice, Jews do not use horoscopes to make decisions. Many Jews are curious, and a common compromise is to treat astrology as entertainment at most. For any particular situation, ask the Rabbi.

Interestingly, the Hebrew months traditionally have signs associated with them, such as the fish for Adar, but they are a part of the calendar's symbolism, not a means of prediction.

## How to "change your mazal"

Jewish tradition teaches that even when a difficult decree is hanging over someone, three things can change it: prayer, charity and good deeds. This is why we say Psalms for those in need and give [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) generously. Another custom is the [evil eye](/learn/ayin-hara), where we avoid boasting and add a blessing. These are about humility and trust in G-d, not superstition.

## Common questions

### What does Mazal Tov mean?
Mazal tov means "good fortune" or "congratulations." It wishes that blessing should flow to the person.

### What is mazal in Judaism?
Mazal is the spiritual flow of blessing and destiny from above. Jews believe our deeds and prayers can influence it.

### Do Jews believe in luck?
Judaism sees events as guided by G-d, not by random luck, and teaches that our actions matter. See [hashgacha pratit](/learn/hashgacha-pratit).

### Is astrology allowed in Judaism?
The Torah forbids fortune-telling, and Maimonides rejected astrology. Jews do not make decisions by horoscope. Ask the Rabbi about specific questions.

### Is there a Jewish zodiac?
The Hebrew months have traditional signs, such as the fish for Adar, but they are calendar symbols and not for predicting the future.
