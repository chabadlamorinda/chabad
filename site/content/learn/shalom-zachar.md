A Shalom Zachar (שָׁלוֹם זָכָר, "welcoming the baby boy") is a festive gathering held at the home of a newborn boy on the Friday night after his birth, before the bris. Friends and family come after the Shabbat meal to wish the family mazel tov, and chickpeas are traditionally served.

If you are new parents in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda, or friends of new parents, this guide will help you know what to expect. Mazel tov!

## What is a Shalom Zachar?

The custom is to hold an open, happy gathering on the first Shabbat eve after a boy is born. The bris takes place on the eighth day, and the Shalom Zachar falls on the Friday night before it, so it is the first chance for the community to celebrate. In Chabad practice it is held on the Shabbat after the birth, even if the bris will be delayed for some reason. Some communities postpone it to the Shabbat before a delayed bris, so ask the Rabbi if this applies to you.

There is nothing formal to prepare. The point is that the community comes to your home to celebrate with you. The father and the family do not need to cook a special feast.

## Why this gathering?

Chabad.org explains several reasons. The main one is gratitude: a thank-you to G-d for the baby's safe arrival. There is also a Talmudic teaching that before birth an angel teaches the baby the whole Torah and then, at birth, causes him to forget it. At the Shalom Zachar, friends visit and comfort the baby's soul, as people visit and comfort a mourner.

Another reason is timing. The bris is on the eighth day, the shortest wait that always includes a Shabbat. Since Shabbat is a day of joy, the celebration begins there.

## Why chickpeas?

Chickpeas (and other beans) are served because of their round shape, which is a sign of the cycle of life, and because round foods without a "mouth" echo the quiet of a mourner. In Yiddish chickpeas are called *arbes*, which some link to G-d's promise to Abraham to make his descendants many. Many families also serve a light drink such as beer, and sweets and other snacks. Chabad.org notes that the reason for beer is not certain.

Keep it simple and kosher. Hosts do not need to cook; guests often bring something.

## What does a typical evening look like?

1. The family has its Shabbat dinner, with [candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) and Kiddush.
2. Friends and neighbors come afterward, sometimes after an announcement at the Friday night service.
3. Guests are served chickpeas, drinks and treats, and say mazel tov.
4. Guests visit, wish the family well and head home.

You do not need to do anything formal. People do not stay long.

## The father's aliyah the next morning

On Shabbat morning, at synagogue, the baby's father is called up to the Torah and a blessing for the mother's health and the baby is said. The baby has not yet been named, so the blessing refers to him as "the newborn." This is an honor in many communities. A father who is in mourning does not take it. See [aliyah](/learn/aliyah).

## What about a baby girl?

Chabad.org does not describe an equivalent gathering for a girl. A baby girl is usually welcomed by a naming in the synagogue; see [baby naming](/learn/baby-naming). Families are warmly encouraged to celebrate in whatever way is natural and joyful, and you can ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### What is a Shalom Zachar?
A Shalom Zachar is a gathering held on the Friday night after a baby boy is born, before his bris, where friends and family come to celebrate and chickpeas are served. It is held at the home of the new parents.

### When is a Shalom Zachar held?
It takes place on the Friday night after the birth, after the Shabbat meal. In Chabad practice that is the first Shabbat after the birth, even if the bris will be later than the eighth day.

### Why are chickpeas served at a Shalom Zachar?
Chickpeas are round, a symbol of the cycle of life and of a mourner who is silent with emotion. They are traditionally served as a light, easy food.

### Do we have a Shalom Zachar for a baby girl?
There is no standard equivalent for a girl; a naming in the synagogue is the usual way to welcome her. Ask the Rabbi if you would like to mark it with friends.

### Do I need to host a Shalom Zachar?
It is a custom, not a requirement, and it is simple. If hosting is hard, ask the Rabbi what works for your family.
