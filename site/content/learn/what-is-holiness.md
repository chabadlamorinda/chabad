In Judaism, holiness (קְדֻשָּׁה, kedushah) is what is sanctified to G-d, and the Torah asks every Jew to sanctify himself or herself. According to Chassidic teaching, the purpose of creation is to make a "dwelling place" for G-d right here in the physical world. So holiness is not about escaping ordinary life. It is about bringing G-d into it.

## Holiness is a command

The Torah tells us to sanctify ourselves (Leviticus 11:44). It does not say this only to special people or only inside a synagogue. Rabbi Isaiah Horowitz, known as the Shelah, describes three kinds of holiness that a person can build.

- **Holiness of the body.** How we behave, what we do with our hands and feet, and how we keep away from what is spiritually harmful.
- **Holiness of space.** The Shelah points to the burning bush, where the Torah calls a place holy. Many Jews bring this into the home: see [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) and the [kosher kitchen](/learn/kosher-kitchen).
- **Holiness of time.** Shabbat and the holidays are holy time. The Shelah notes that the Sages count the weekdays as "first day," "second day" and so on toward Shabbat, so that each weekday carries something of Shabbat. Then ordinary activities, too, take on a sacred character.

## Holy and ordinary: seeing the difference

At the end of Shabbat we say [Havdalah](/learn/havdalah), a blessing that separates between holy and ordinary, between light and darkness and between the seventh day and the six days of work. The point of distinguishing is not to despise ordinary life. It is to know what is holy so that we can bring it into the week.

## A home for G-d in this world

A central idea of Chassidic teaching is called dirah b'tachtonim (דִּירָה בְּתַחְתּוֹנִים), a "dwelling in the lower realms." According to the chassidic masters, this is the purpose of the creation of the world and of human beings: to make a home for G-d in the physical world. This idea is tied to the verse in which G-d asks the Jewish people to build a sanctuary so that He may dwell among them (Exodus 25:8). The Lubavitcher Rebbe's teachings turn this idea into a way to live every day.

The idea is that holiness is not found only in a prayer service or on a mountaintop. It applies to everyday life, including such acts as eating with a blessing. See [eating as a mitzvah](/learn/eating-as-a-mitzvah) and [What is a mitzvah?](/learn/what-is-a-mitzvah).

## Holiness of the heart

The Shelah also teaches that holiness lives in the heart, the soul and the limbs, which carry out the mitzvahs. The heart's work includes avoiding hatred, revenge and grudges, and loving one's neighbor. The heart's part is thought and contemplation.

## Common questions

### What does holy mean in Judaism?
Holy (kadosh, קָדוֹשׁ) is the Torah's word for what is sanctified to G-d. The Torah asks Jews to sanctify themselves, and Jewish teaching sees holiness in the body, in places and in time.

### What is kedushah?
Kedushah (קְדֻשָּׁה) is the Hebrew word for holiness. It is also the name of a passage in the prayer service: see [Kedushah](/learn/kedushah).

### Do you have to be perfect to be holy?
The Torah's command to be holy is addressed to every Jew, not only to the perfect. Ask the Rabbi how to begin.

### Can ordinary things be holy?
Yes. According to Chassidic teaching, making the ordinary world into a home for G-d is the very purpose of creation.

### Is Shabbat holy time?
Yes. Shabbat is holiness of time, and Havdalah marks its end. See [Shabbat for beginners](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners).
