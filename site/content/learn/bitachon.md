Bitachon means trust in G-d: the calm confidence that G-d loves you, is in charge, and will bring about what is truly good. It goes one step beyond faith. Faith believes G-d can help; bitachon relies on Him, and that trust brings peace of mind in uncertain times.

## Emunah and bitachon

- **Emunah (faith)** is believing that G-d exists, created the world and guides it. See [belief in G-d](/learn/belief-in-g-d).
- **Bitachon (trust)** is relying on that belief in real life: being calm about a job, a diagnosis or a child's future, because you know you are in G-d's hands.

The classic book Duties of the Heart compares bitachon to a person who trusts a caring, capable friend completely. G-d is that friend, only infinitely more.

## "Think good and it will be good"

The third Chabad Rebbe, the Tzemach Tzedek, told a worried Chassid: "Tracht gut, vet zein gut," "Think good and it will be good." The Rebbe explained that this is not only advice to be optimistic. Trusting that G-d will bring good, even when we cannot see how, is itself a channel that draws down blessing. Bitachon is not just a reaction to what happens; it helps shape it.

## Trust and effort go together

Bitachon does not mean sitting back and doing nothing. The Torah tells us, "G-d will bless you in all that you do" (Deuteronomy 15:18). We do our part, called hishtadlut:

- Go to the doctor and follow medical advice.
- Look for work, study, plan and save.
- Lock the door and drive carefully.

Then we let go of the outcome, knowing the results come from G-d. Effort is like opening a door; the blessing comes through it from Above.

## When worry takes over

Bitachon is a goal we grow into, not a test we pass or fail. Everyone worries sometimes. Anxiety that will not let go can also be a health condition, and getting professional help is a wise step that fits perfectly with trust in G-d. Talking to someone you trust helps too.

## Practical ways to build trust, step by step

1. **Say Modeh Ani** each morning, starting the day with G-d.
2. **Pray in your own words.** Tell G-d what worries you, then hand it over.
3. **Say Tehillim.** King David's psalms are full of trust, like Psalm 23: "G-d is my shepherd, I shall not lack." See [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) and our [Tehillim page](/tehillim).
4. **Remember past kindnesses.** Recall times things worked out in ways you did not expect.
5. **Learn about Divine Providence,** the teaching that G-d guides every detail of life.
6. **Give tzedakah.** Giving shows that we trust G-d to provide.

## Common questions

### Does bitachon mean everything will turn out the way I want?
Bitachon means trusting that G-d will bring about what is good. The Rebbe taught that sincere trust itself draws down visible, revealed good. At the same time, we trust that G-d sees the whole picture, even when we do not.

### If I trust G-d, why do I need to work or see a doctor?
The Torah asks us to make a normal effort and trust G-d for the result. Seeing a doctor and doing your job are part of how G-d's blessing reaches you.

### How can I stop worrying?
Start small: name the worry, do what you reasonably can, and then say a short prayer handing the rest to G-d. If worry is constant, speak with a professional and with the Rabbi.

### Is it a lack of faith to feel afraid?
No. Fear is human. Bitachon is something we work on throughout life, and turning to G-d when afraid is itself an act of trust.
