The highest form of tzedakah in Jewish teaching is to help a person become self-sufficient, so that he or she no longer needs to depend on others. Maimonides ranks this above every other kind of giving, and says it can be done through a gift, a loan, a business partnership or by helping the person find work.

## What Maimonides taught

Maimonides lists eight levels of giving. At the very top, with none greater above it, is to strengthen a person so that he will not need to rely on others, through a gift or a loan, a partnership, or finding him employment. See [Tzedakah Priorities](/learn/tzedakah-priorities) for the whole ladder, and [Giving Anonymously](/learn/giving-anonymously) for the levels just below.

Maimonides' "Eight Degrees of Giving" shows why this is the highest. It preserves the person's dignity and turns a recipient into a giver. Someone who has a job or a business of their own is no longer someone who is "helped"; he or she becomes someone who can help others. Advising people in a way that builds their confidence in solving their own problems is the same idea.

## Why a job is better than a handout

A handout meets today's need. A job meets tomorrow's, too. With newfound money, giving a tenth of it to the poor is the baseline, but creating jobs and partnerships for the unemployed is just as important, if not more important.

## Gift, loan or partnership

The classical commentators add a fine point:

- **A loan** helps a person keep self-sufficiency, but the borrower may feel embarrassed by relying on a friend's generosity. See [Interest-Free Loans](/learn/gemach-interest-free-loans).
- **A partnership** is greater than a loan, according to the Shach, because the partner also benefits, which preserves dignity.
- **A gift,** the Taz explains, counts at this highest level when it is given before the person falls into poverty, to help them recover.

So the timing matters. The best help comes early, before a crisis becomes a collapse.

## Everyday ways to help someone stand on their own

These are common-sense ways to apply the idea; none of them is a ruling.

1. **Help with a job search.** Make an introduction, give a recommendation if you can honestly do so, or look over a résumé together.
2. **Hire, or tell people about openings.** Creating jobs is as important as giving.
3. **Offer a loan or a partnership** to someone with a sound plan.
4. **Share skills.** Teach a trade, a skill or a way of working.
5. **Be there for someone who has lost work.** See [Losing a Job](/learn/losing-a-job).

For serious financial matters, such as loans and partnerships, consult an attorney or advisor, and ask the Rabbi about questions of Jewish law.

## Giving with dignity

Whatever you do, protect the other person's dignity. No one should have to pay with dignity for another's help. Offer warmly, before being asked if you can, and do not make the person feel small.

## Common questions

### What is the highest form of tzedakah?
Helping a person become self-sufficient, through a gift, a loan, a partnership or finding them a job, according to Maimonides.

### Is helping someone find a job a mitzvah?
Yes. In Maimonides' list, finding someone employment is part of the highest level of giving.

### Is a loan better than a gift?
Maimonides' top level includes both. The commentators add that, according to the Shach, a partnership is greater than a loan, and the Taz explains that a gift is at this level when it helps someone before they fall into poverty.

### What if I cannot give money?
Giving includes time, expertise and attention. You can make an introduction, offer advice or spread the word about openings. See [Giving When Money Is Tight](/learn/giving-when-money-is-tight).

### How do I help without embarrassing someone?
Be warm, respect their dignity, and offer before they have to ask when possible.
