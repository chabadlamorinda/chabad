Jews have lived in Contra Costa County since the Gold Rush era, and the community grew from small groups of pioneers in towns like Martinez and Richmond into the many Jewish families who now live in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda. The story below sticks to what museums, newspapers and archives have recorded, and where we do not know something, we say so.

## Pioneers to the present

In 2019 the Richmond Museum of History opened an exhibit called "Pioneers to the Present: Jews of Richmond and Contra Costa County." As J. Weekly reported, it told stories reaching back to the Gold Rush, with miners, soldiers, Holocaust survivors, artists and business owners. Much of the material came from the archives of Temple Beth Hillel in Richmond.

The exhibit shows how varied the community was. A few of the people it featured:

- **Simon Blum**, who arrived in San Francisco as a teenager, settled in Martinez and helped found a dry goods business there.
- **Frances Blumenfeld**, an immigrant from Austria who helped run a department store in Richmond for more than fifty years, and who gave Temple Beth Hillel its first Torah.
- **Fred Hendeles**, a Holocaust survivor who escaped a Nazi labor camp, reached California, and worked in the Richmond shipyards.
- **Joseph and Clara-Rae Genser**, who moved to Richmond in 1946 and helped organize both the Jewish community center and the temple.

Rabbi Dean Kertesz of Temple Beth Hillel told J. Weekly that the Jewish community in West Contra Costa County was always small, with its institutions "built by hand by a small group of people." That phrase is worth remembering: nearly every Jewish community begins with a few families who decide to build something.

## 1950: women bring a growing community together

The Magnes Collection at UC Berkeley records that in 1950 eighty women formed the League of Jewish Women to help unite the growing Jewish community of Contra Costa County. They organized a religious school and started a campaign for a Jewish community center, which they hoped would be a focal point for "Jewish activities, education and cultural programs and religious observances." In 1964 the League became the sisterhood of a congregation when it dedicated its synagogue.

It is a reminder that Jewish women have always been builders of community life. Our sages teach that the Jewish people were redeemed from Egypt in the merit of righteous women. You can read more about this in our guide to [the Jewish year in Lamorinda](/learn/jewish-year-in-lamorinda).

## The suburbs grow: BART comes to Lamorinda

Lamorinda's own growth is tied to transportation. Lamorinda Weekly reports that BART's Lafayette and Orinda stations opened on May 21, 1973, as part of the line linking eastern Contra Costa County to the rest of the Bay Area. Easier commuting helped families settle in the hills and valleys of Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, and with them came Jewish families looking for a synagogue, a school and a community.

We do not have a verified count of when each Jewish institution in Lamorinda began, and we will not guess. If you know your family's own story of arriving here, it belongs to this history, and we would love to hear it.

## A Chabad center opens in Lafayette

Lamorinda Weekly reported on the grand opening of the Chabad Jewish Center of Lamorinda in the autumn of 2019, led by Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski and Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski, emissaries of the Lubavitcher Rebbe. The paper described a center serving Jewish families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda. You can read more about how Chabad reaches Jews all over the Bay Area in [Chabad in the Bay Area](/learn/chabad-in-the-bay-area).

Chabad's idea is simple: every Jew is part of the story, no matter how much or how little they know, and no membership is required. That is the same spirit as the pioneers who built Jewish life by hand.

## Why local Jewish history matters

Jewish tradition places great weight on remembering. We tell the Exodus story every year, and we say "remember the days of old" (Deuteronomy 32:7). Knowing who built Jewish life in your own county makes it personal. Your neighbor's grandparents may have worked in the shipyards or run a store, and you stand on their shoulders.

For the wider picture, see [Jewish Bay Area history](/learn/jewish-bay-area-history) and [Jewish life in the East Bay](/learn/jewish-east-bay).

## Common questions

### When did Jews first come to Contra Costa County?
The Richmond Museum of History's exhibit traced Jewish stories in the county back to the Gold Rush era. A precise date for the first Jewish resident has not been established in the sources we checked.

### Is there a Jewish history museum exhibit about Richmond and Contra Costa County?
The Richmond Museum of History ran "Pioneers to the Present: Jews of Richmond and Contra Costa County" in 2019, using archives from Temple Beth Hillel. The exhibit has ended, but the museum is a good place to ask about its archives.

### When did the League of Jewish Women form in Contra Costa County?
According to the Magnes Collection at UC Berkeley, eighty women formed the League of Jewish Women in 1950 to help unite the county's growing Jewish community.

### When did BART reach Lafayette and Orinda?
Lamorinda Weekly reports that the Lafayette and Orinda BART stations opened on May 21, 1973.

### Where can I find a Jewish community in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda today?

### Where did my family's Jewish story begin?
Every family has one, and many are worth writing down. Ask your grandparents, collect photos, and share them with your own children. Our guide on [Jewish history](/learn/jewish-history-overview) is a good start.
