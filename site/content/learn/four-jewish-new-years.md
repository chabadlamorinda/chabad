The Jewish calendar has four new years, each for a different purpose: the first of Nissan (for kings and festivals), the first of Elul (for animal tithes), the first of Tishrei (Rosh Hashanah, the new year for years), and the fifteenth of Shevat (Tu BiShvat, the new year for trees). Only Rosh Hashanah is the day the year number changes.

## Why four new years?

Judaism counts time for different purposes, just as a school year, a tax year and a calendar year start on different dates. The Mishnah lists four, and Chabad.org explains each one. See the [Jewish calendar](/learn/jewish-calendar) for the whole system.

## 1 Nissan: the new year for kings and festivals

The first of Nissan is the new year for counting the reign of a Jewish king, and for setting the order of the three pilgrimage festivals, with Passover first. It reminds us that timing matters. A person who makes a promise should keep it on time. It is also the month of freedom: see [Passover](/holidays/passover).

## 1 Elul: the new year for animal tithes

In Temple times, the first of Elul started a new year for the tithe of newborn animals from the flock. Today it is the beginning of the month of preparation for the High Holidays. Giving a regular portion year after year builds a habit of generosity. Chassidic teaching also sees Elul as a time when "the King is in the field," close and welcoming. Read about [Elul](/learn/elul).

## 1 Tishrei: Rosh Hashanah, the new year for years

The first of Tishrei is Rosh Hashanah, the new year we all know. The year number goes up, and the Sabbatical and Jubilee years are counted from it. It also set the year for planting trees and for vegetable tithes. It is the day we crown G-d as King of the world and celebrate its creation. Read [Rosh Hashanah for beginners](/learn/rosh-hashanah-for-beginners) and join us: see [Rosh Hashanah](/holidays/rosh-hashanah).

## 15 Shevat: the new year for trees

Tu BiShvat is the new year for trees, the date from which we count the years for fruit tithes and for the first fruits of a new tree, which may not be eaten for three years. It is still celebrated today with fruits and a seder. A tree takes years before it gives fruit, and sap rises quietly in winter before anyone sees blossoms. Growth is often slow and hidden. See the [Tu BiShvat seder](/learn/tu-bishvat-seder), [Shevat](/learn/shevat) and [Tu BiShvat](/holidays/tu-bishvat).

## What the four teach us: many fresh starts

You do not have to wait one day a year to begin again. Nissan teaches us to keep our commitments, Elul to give steadily, Tishrei to begin again with G-d as our King, and Shevat to be patient as we grow. Choose the one that speaks to you, and begin.

## Common questions

### How many new years are there in Judaism?
There are four: 1 Nissan, 1 Elul, 1 Tishrei (Rosh Hashanah) and 15 Shevat (Tu BiShvat).

### Why are there four Jewish new years?
Each starts a count for a different purpose: kings and festivals, animal tithes, years, and trees.

### What is the new year for trees?
Tu BiShvat, the fifteenth of Shevat, is the new year for trees. It is celebrated with fruit.

### What is the new year for kings?
The first of Nissan is the new year for counting the years of a king's reign and for ordering the festivals.

### Which new year is the one we celebrate with a holiday?
Rosh Hashanah, the first of Tishrei, is the main Jewish new year. Tu BiShvat is also celebrated.

### What is the new year for animal tithes?
The first of Elul. In Temple times it marked a new year for tithing the newborn animals of the flock.
