Kosher for Passover means food that contains no chametz, leavened grain, and was made with equipment free of chametz. During the eight days of [Passover](/holidays/passover) Jews may not eat or even own chametz, so year-round kosher food is not enough.

## What chametz is

Chametz is any of the five grains (wheat, barley, spelt, rye and oats) that came into contact with water and was left for 18 minutes or more, allowing it to rise. Bread, cake, pasta, cereal, beer and whiskey are all chametz. So are many processed foods with grain-based ingredients.

The Torah is very strict about chametz on Passover. Even a tiny amount mixed into food is forbidden. Read more in our guide to [chametz](/learn/chametz).

## Matzah

Matzah is made from flour and water, baked quickly before it can rise. At the Seder we eat [matzah](/learn/matzah) as a mitzvah. Chabad custom is to use only handmade shmurah matzah, "guarded" from the time the wheat is harvested.

## Kitniyot

Ashkenazi Jews, including Chabad, do not eat kitniyot on Passover. These are foods like rice, corn, beans, lentils, peas and soy. They are not chametz, but the custom developed because they can be confused with grain or mixed with it. Many Sephardic communities do eat kitniyot. Follow your family custom.

## Chabad custom: no gebrokts

Chabad custom is to avoid gebrokts, "wetted" matzah, during Passover. This means not using matzah meal or matzah with liquids. On the last day of Passover, matzah is dipped and soaked freely.

## Preparing your kitchen

1. **Clean** the kitchen and home of all chametz.
2. **Kasher** what can be koshered, such as stovetops, sinks and certain metal pots, following the Rabbi's guidance.
3. **Cover** counters and surfaces that cannot be koshered.
4. **Use Passover dishes.** Many families have a separate set of dishes, pots and utensils used only on Passover.
5. **Sell your chametz** that you are keeping, so you do not own it during the holiday.

## Shopping for Passover

- Buy products with a reliable "Kosher for Passover" certification. A regular kosher symbol is not enough.
- Fresh fruits and vegetables need no certification, just a good wash.
- Some products, like plain coffee or sugar, may be fine without special certification, but ask first.
- Check medicines and personal care products with the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### Why does Passover food cost more?
Factories must be thoroughly cleaned and supervised for special production runs, and many products are made in small batches. This adds cost. Buying only what you need helps.

### Is everything at the kosher section kosher for Passover?
No. Look specifically for "Kosher for Passover" on the label. Year-round kosher products often contain chametz or were made on chametz equipment.

### What should I do with chametz I do not want to throw away?
Store it in a closed cabinet and sell it through a Rabbi before Passover. You can do this online at [/forms/sell-chametz](/forms/sell-chametz).

### Can I take medication on Passover?
Never stop taking necessary medication without speaking to your doctor. Many medicines are fine. Ask the Rabbi about specific products.
