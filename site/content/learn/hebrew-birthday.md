Your Hebrew birthday is the date you were born according to the Jewish calendar. Because the Jewish calendar follows the moon, it usually falls on a different day of the secular calendar each year, and it is a special day for blessing, reflection and growth.

## Why your Hebrew birthday matters

The Talmud teaches that on a person's birthday, their *mazal* (spiritual source of blessing) is strong. The Lubavitcher Rebbe taught that a birthday is a personal Rosh Hashanah: a day to look back on the past year, make good resolutions, and step into the new year with new energy.

Just as the Jewish people celebrate holidays on their Hebrew dates, celebrating your own birthday on its Hebrew date connects your personal life to the rhythm of Jewish time.

## How to find your Hebrew birthday

You need your secular date of birth and whether you were born before or after sunset, since the Jewish day begins at nightfall. Enter them into our [Hebrew birthday tool](/tools/hebrew-birthday) and it will show your Hebrew date and when it falls this year.

If you were born in Adar and the year is a Jewish leap year (which has two months of Adar), or on the 30th of a month that sometimes has only 29 days, ask the Rabbi which day to celebrate.

## How to celebrate: customs for the day

The Rebbe encouraged a set of meaningful customs for every Jewish birthday:

1. **Get an aliyah.** On the Shabbat before your birthday, be called up to the Torah. Women and girls can attend services and be mentioned in a blessing.
2. **Give tzedakah.** Give extra [charity](/learn/tzedakah) on the day itself, ideally in the morning. If the birthday falls on Shabbat, give before or after.
3. **Pray with extra feeling.** Spend a few more minutes in prayer, thinking about the year ahead.
4. **Say your Psalm.** Recite the chapter of [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) matching your new year of life (see below).
5. **Learn some extra Torah.** Even a few minutes of added study is meaningful.
6. **Make a good resolution.** Take on a new mitzvah or improve in one you already do.
7. **Taste a new fruit.** Eat a fruit you have not tasted this season and say the Shehecheyanu blessing.
8. **Celebrate with friends.** Gather family and friends and share words of Torah and good wishes.
9. **Reflect.** Set aside quiet time to think about the past year and what you want to improve.

## Your personal Psalm

There is a custom to say each day the chapter of Tehillim that matches the year of life you are now in. That is your age plus one. If you just turned 30, you begin saying chapter 31 and keep saying it every day until your next Hebrew birthday. You can find every chapter on our [Tehillim page](/tehillim).

## Common questions

### Do I have to stop celebrating my regular birthday?
No. Many people enjoy both. The Hebrew birthday is the one with the special spiritual customs, so it is worth marking even simply.

### What if I don't know whether I was born before or after sunset?
Use your best estimate. If you truly do not know, ask the Rabbi. He can help you decide which date to observe.

### Can children celebrate their Hebrew birthdays too?
Yes, and it is a beautiful habit to start early. Children can give tzedakah, say a Psalm, and celebrate with friends in a way that teaches them the day is about growth, not only gifts.

### What do I say to wish someone a happy Hebrew birthday?
A common wish is "Happy birthday" with a blessing for a good and sweet year, or the Yiddish "Mazal tov, biz hundert un tzvantzik," meaning "until 120."
