A bar or bat mitzvah does not need a big party: a Jewish boy becomes bar mitzvah at 13 and a girl becomes bat mitzvah at 12 automatically, whether or not any celebration is held. What matters is the child taking on the mitzvot. Chabad.org puts it directly: the ceremony is much more important than the party.

## What the day really requires

Nothing needs to be bought for the status to begin. It starts on the Hebrew birthday itself. See the [bar mitzvah](/learn/bar-mitzvah) and [bat mitzvah](/learn/bat-mitzvah) guides for the full picture. What Chabad.org describes as the heart of the day for a boy:

- **Tefillin.** He begins putting on tefillin on weekday mornings. His first pair is traditionally a gift from parents or grandparents. See [tefillin](/learn/tefillin).
- **An aliyah.** He is called up to the Torah to say the blessings, usually the first time the Torah is read after his birthday.
- **A talk.** A few words on the weekly Torah portion, linked to his own life, where he can also thank people.
- **A mitzvah project.** Studying and doing one mitzvah in depth, often an act of kindness.

For a girl, Chabad.org suggests preparing through study, a personal mitzvah project, and mitzvot special to women: lighting Shabbat candles, separating challah when baking, and building a personal connection to prayer. It stresses that the day is a beginning, not a graduation. See [bat mitzvah projects](/learn/bat-mitzvah-projects).

## What is optional

Chabad.org says that reading the Torah or haftarah has no clear source as a requirement, and suggests the preparation time may be better spent learning the basics of Jewish life. It also cautions against turning the celebration into a wedding-sized event.

So the following are all choices, not requirements: a large hall, a band, a themed party, a long guest list, printed favors.

## Simple ways to celebrate

1. **A festive meal at home.** Some opinions hold that a meal in honor of a bar mitzvah is itself a mitzvah. Family, grandparents and a few friends are enough.
2. **A Kiddush after Shabbat services.** Sharing a Kiddush with the community is warm and fits the spirit of the day. Chabad of Lamorinda has a [Kiddush sponsorship page](/sponsor-a-kiddush). Call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242 to ask what can be arranged.
3. **A small celebration on the Hebrew birthday itself.** Chabad.org suggests this if the party must be on another date.
4. **A day of giving.** Bake challah, light Shabbat candles for the first time, visit someone who is alone, or give *tzedakah*.
5. **A meaningful gift.** Books, a prayer book or a candlestick matter more than anything expensive. Read about [gifts in multiples of 18](/learn/gifts-in-multiples-of-18).

## Common questions

### Does a bar mitzvah need a party?
No. A child becomes bar or bat mitzvah automatically at the age of Jewish adulthood, with or without a celebration. Chabad.org says the ceremony matters much more than the party.

### What are the essentials of a bar mitzvah?
Starting tefillin, being called to the Torah for an aliyah, a short talk, and a mitzvah project. A festive meal is customary.

### Is reading the whole Torah portion required?
Chabad.org says there is no clear source for it, and suggests spending the time learning Jewish basics. Ask the Rabbi what suits your child.

### What are simple ways to celebrate a bat mitzvah?
A small gathering, a talk, lighting Shabbat candles or baking challah, and a mitzvah project such as helping others.
