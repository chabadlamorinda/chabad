You can keep Shabbat and kosher at Lake Tahoe by planning ahead: look up candle-lighting time for the town where you are staying, bring kosher food with you, and look for the local Chabad center, which welcomes Jewish visitors. Many families from Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda drive up to Tahoe in winter, and a little preparation makes it easy.

## Find Chabad of Lake Tahoe

Chabad has a Jewish center serving the Lake Tahoe area, and it welcomes visitors. Search online for "Chabad of Lake Tahoe" to find its current contact details, schedule and any Shabbat meals or events. We do not list an address or phone number here because they can change; confirm the details on the center's own website or by searching before your trip. ## Shabbat on a ski trip

Shabbat starts at sunset on Friday. Tahoe's candle-lighting time is not the same as Lafayette's, so look up the time for **South Lake Tahoe** (or the town you are in) on a Jewish calendar website, and do not use the Lafayette times on our [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times) page. Candles are lit about 18 minutes before sunset; see [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).

Practical tips:

1. **Arrive before Shabbat** with plenty of daylight left, since winter roads and weather can be slow.
2. **Bring Shabbat essentials**: candles, a candle holder or foil tray, challah or rolls, grape juice or wine for Kiddush, and a prepared meal.
3. **Plan the day.** Shabbat is a day of rest from work and travel, so decide in advance how you will spend it, and ask the Rabbi any questions about skiing, driving or hotel keys. See [traveling on Shabbat](/learn/traveling-on-shabbat).
4. **If you can, join the local Chabad** for a Shabbat meal or services. Contact them well ahead.

## Kosher food to pack

Kosher food may be hard to find in the mountains. The simplest approach is to bring your own:

- Sealed packaged foods with a reliable kosher symbol.
- Fresh fruit and vegetables, which need no certification.
- Cooked meals from home, frozen for the road.
- Disposable plates and utensils if you are cooking in a rental kitchen.

For more, read [kosher when traveling](/learn/kosher-when-traveling).

## Chanukah in the mountains

Winter trips often fall near Chanukah. Bring a menorah and candles or oil, and light them each night after dark, in a window if you can. Hotels and rentals often have fire rules, so ask about a safe place, and check with the Rabbi on any question. See [Chanukah away from home](/learn/chanukah-away-from-home) and the [Chanukah guide](/holidays/chanukah).

## Common questions

### Is there a Chabad in Lake Tahoe?

Yes, Chabad of Lake Tahoe serves the area. Search for it online to confirm its current contact details and schedule before your trip.

### How do I keep Shabbat at Lake Tahoe?

Look up the candle-lighting time for the town where you are staying, bring candles, challah, wine or grape juice and prepared food, and arrive before Shabbat. Ask the Rabbi about specific questions.

### Can I find kosher food in Lake Tahoe?

It may be limited, so bring sealed packaged foods with a reliable kosher symbol, fruit and vegetables, and cooked meals from home.

### Can I light a Chanukah menorah in a hotel or rental cabin?

Yes, in most cases, but check the property's fire rules and choose a safe place. Ask the Rabbi if you have a question.

### Are Lafayette's Shabbat times the same as Tahoe's?

No. Candle-lighting depends on location, so look up the time for the town you are in.
