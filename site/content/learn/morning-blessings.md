Before coffee, before the phone, before the news: Jewish tradition begins the day with a short series of thank-yous called **Birchot HaShachar**, the morning blessings. They take a few minutes and change how the whole day feels.

## Step by step

1. **Modeh Ani.** Still in bed, we thank G-d for giving back our soul. See [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani).
2. **Washing the hands.** We pour water over each hand three times, alternating, to begin the day pure. See [washing the hands](/learn/netilat-yadayim).
3. **Asher Yatzar.** After using the restroom, we thank G-d for a body that works. See [Asher Yatzar](/learn/asher-yatzar).
4. **Elokai Neshamah.** "My G-d, the soul You gave me is pure": we thank G-d for our soul and its return each morning.
5. **The blessings over the Torah.** Before learning anything, we thank G-d for giving us the Torah, and we say a few verses so the day begins with Torah.
6. **The fifteen morning blessings.** Each one thanks G-d for a gift we take for granted: seeing, getting dressed, standing up straight, walking, having what we need, strength when we are tired.

## Why thank G-d for ordinary things?

The sages taught that a person should say one hundred blessings a day. The morning blessings were originally said one by one as each thing happened: opening the eyes, stretching, getting up, putting on shoes. They teach us to notice that every ordinary moment is a gift. Chassidic teaching adds that thanking G-d first thing in the morning sets the tone: a person who begins with gratitude meets the day with joy.

## How to begin

You do not need to say them all at once. Start with Modeh Ani and washing the hands, then add a blessing or two each week. A siddur has all of them with translation; many people keep one by the bed or use an app. See [the siddur](/learn/siddur) and [a Jewish morning routine](/learn/jewish-morning-routine).

## Common questions

### Can I say the morning blessings in English?
Yes. Blessings may be said in any language you understand. Hebrew is ideal, and many people learn it gradually.

### Do women say the morning blessings?
Yes. Women say the morning blessings too. A few have slightly different wording; your siddur shows it.

### What if I forgot to say them in the morning?
Most of them may still be said later in the day. Ask the Rabbi about the details.
