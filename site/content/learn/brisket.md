Brisket is a cut of beef from the chest, slow-cooked until tender, and it is one of the best-loved dishes for Jewish holidays and Shabbat. It is popular because it gets better when cooked ahead, so the cook is free when the holiday begins.

For families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, a brisket is also a nice way to start hosting. Here is the story, a simple recipe and tips for slicing and serving.

## Why brisket is a holiday dish

Brisket needs long, slow cooking, and it tastes best made a day ahead. That suits Shabbat and Yom Tov perfectly, when we do not cook but still want a warm, festive meal. See [Shabbat Meals](/learn/shabbat-meals) and [Jewish Foods](/learn/jewish-foods) for more on traditional dishes.

## A simple brisket

This recipe is retold from Chabad.org's basic brisket, which serves about 8.

**You will need:** a thick-cut brisket of about 2½ pounds, a tablespoon of paprika, a teaspoon each of salt and pepper, ½ teaspoon basil, 3 sliced onions, 2 halved garlic cloves, and 1½ cups each of ketchup, dry red wine and water.

1. Heat the oven to 325°F.
2. Rinse the meat and put it in a roasting pan. Rub in the spices.
3. Scatter the onions and garlic on top.
4. Mix the ketchup, wine and water and pour over the meat.
5. Cover tightly with foil, tented so it does not touch the meat.
6. Bake about 3 hours, until a thermometer in the center reads 190°F.
7. Rest 5 to 10 minutes, then slice diagonally against the grain and serve with the juices.

Another Chabad.org version uses root vegetables: a 2½ pound first-cut brisket seared with a spice rub at high heat, then cooked covered for 4 to 5 hours at a low temperature with carrots, parsnips, celery, turnip, onions, wine and broth.

## Make it ahead and slice it well

- **Chill overnight.** Cold brisket is firmer, so it slices thinly and cleanly.
- **Slice against the grain.** Look for the direction of the muscle fibers and cut across them.
- **Reheat in the sauce.** Return the slices to the pan with the juices so they stay moist.

## For Shabbat and Yom Tov

If you serve brisket on Shabbat, fully cook it before Shabbat begins. Read [Keeping Food Warm on Shabbat](/learn/keeping-food-warm-on-shabbat) for what is permitted, and ask the Rabbi about your oven. Many people enjoy it with [kugel](/learn/kugel) alongside.

## Common questions

### What is brisket?
Brisket is a cut of beef from the chest, cooked slowly until it is tender, and a classic dish for Jewish holidays and Shabbat.

### How long do you cook brisket?
Chabad.org's basic recipe bakes a 2½ pound brisket for about 3 hours at 325°F, until a thermometer reads 190°F. Larger or lower-heat recipes take longer.

### Can I make brisket the day before?
Yes, and it is better that way. Chilling overnight makes it easy to slice thinly, and reheating in the sauce keeps it moist.

### How do I slice brisket?
Slice it thinly and against the grain, after it has rested or been chilled.

### Can I serve brisket on Passover?
Yes, if every ingredient is kosher for Passover. Ask the Rabbi if you are not sure about an item.
