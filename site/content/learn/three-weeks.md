The Three Weeks are the period between the fast of the 17th of Tammuz and the fast of Tisha B'Av, when Jews mourn the destruction of the Holy Temple in Jerusalem. The last nine days are the most intense, with more mourning customs.

## What happened during the Three Weeks?

On the 17th of Tammuz, the Romans broke through the walls of Jerusalem in the year 70 CE, during the siege that ended with the destruction of the Second Temple. Three weeks later, on the 9th of Av, Tisha B'Av, both the First and Second Temples were destroyed. Many other tragedies in Jewish history also took place in this season.

The Prophet Jeremiah called this time "between the straits" (Lamentations 1:3), a narrow and difficult period.

## Why we mourn

We mourn not only for a building, but for what was lost: a world in which G-d's presence was openly felt, and the Jewish people lived united in its land. The Talmud teaches that the Second Temple was destroyed because of baseless hatred among Jews.

So the Three Weeks are also a time for repair: extra kindness, unity, [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) and Torah study. The Lubavitcher Rebbe especially encouraged learning about the Holy Temple during these weeks, and focusing on the hope of rebuilding it.

## Customs of the Three Weeks

From the 17th of Tammuz, Ashkenazi custom includes:

- No weddings.
- No haircuts or shaving.
- No listening to live music, and many avoid recorded music too.
- Avoiding activities that require saying Shehecheyanu, such as eating a new fruit or wearing new clothes. Many permit this on Shabbat.

## The Nine Days

From Rosh Chodesh Av until Tisha B'Av, the mourning increases. Customs include:

- No eating meat or drinking wine, except on Shabbat or at a meal celebrating a mitzvah, such as a siyum (completion of a section of Talmud).
- No bathing or swimming for pleasure. Washing for hygiene is permitted.
- No laundering or wearing freshly laundered clothes. Many prepare by wearing their clothes briefly before the Nine Days.
- No renovations or major home improvement for enjoyment.
- No buying new clothes or major new items.

The Lubavitcher Rebbe encouraged holding or joining a siyum each day of the Nine Days, adding Torah study and the joy of a mitzvah to these days.

## Tisha B'Av

The period ends with Tisha B'Av, a full 25-hour fast, the saddest day on the Jewish calendar. We sit on low seats, read the book of Lamentations, and do not wear leather shoes. Read more in our guide to [fast days](/learn/fast-days).

## Common questions

### Do these customs apply to children?
Many of the customs, such as not cutting hair, are kept for children too. Others are relaxed for young children. Ask the Rabbi for your family.

### Can I go on vacation during the Three Weeks?
Travel itself is not forbidden, but many avoid fun trips and especially swimming during the Nine Days. Ask the Rabbi about specific plans.

### What if I need to shave for work?
In some cases, a person whose job requires a clean-shaven appearance may shave during the Three Weeks. Ask the Rabbi about your circumstances.

### Is there anything joyful in this time?
Yes. Our Sages promise that these days will one day become days of celebration, when [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach) comes and the Temple is rebuilt. Chabad custom is to focus on that hope, and to add in joy on the Shabbatot of the Three Weeks.
