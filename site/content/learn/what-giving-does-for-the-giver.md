Judaism teaches that tzedakah helps the giver at least as much as the receiver. The Sages said, "More than the rich man does for the pauper, the pauper does for the rich man," and Chabad.org explains that giving refines a person's character, brings a sense of purpose and fulfillment, and opens the heart.

## "The pauper does for the rich man"

Chabad.org quotes this saying of the Sages in its article on tzedakah. It turns our usual picture upside down. We tend to think the giver is the strong one and the receiver the weak one. The Sages say the opposite: the person in need gives the giver the chance to do something good. The recipient therefore has no reason to feel ashamed.

Another Chabad.org article adds that when a giver meets someone in need, he or she is humbled. G-d could have supplied that person directly, but chose to give the giver a role. The article calls this being a partner in completing creation, and says the word for a poor person, *ani*, is related to the word for "responding": need is a summons.

## What giving builds in us

A Chabad.org article called "Giving Is Receiving" explains that charity's purpose is to benefit the giver at least as much as the receiver. It lists what the giver gains:

- **Character.** Giving refines a person and builds sensitivity, empathy and humility.
- **Reaching beyond ourselves.** It draws on the soul's ability to move past our limits and connect with another person.
- **Fulfillment and joy.** Generosity brings a sense of inner fulfillment and happiness.
- **Protection from self-absorption.** People who resist giving, even with a good-sounding excuse, risk becoming self-absorbed.

The money given is finite. What the giver gains, the article says, is "the gift of sensitivity," which is priceless and which the giver can then share with the world.

## Giving gives meaning to our work

Chabad.org also teaches that G-d left us the task of bringing spirituality and meaning into the world, and that tzedakah gives hard-earned money a purpose beyond personal need. Another Chabad.org article puts it this way: we also need meaning, not just food, and giving provides it. To see why this makes tzedakah so important, read [why the Talmud calls tzedakah equal to all the mitzvot](/learn/tzedakah-equals-all-the-mitzvot).

## Even those who receive give

Chabad.org notes that, according to the Talmud, everyone is obligated to give charity, even someone who is supported by a communal welfare fund. At first this seems puzzling. The answer is that the purpose is to benefit the giver: a recipient who gives a portion is learning to give and becoming a better person. Everyone deserves the dignity of being a giver.

## How to begin

You do not need a large budget. Chabad.org suggests keeping a [pushka](/learn/pushka) at home and putting coins in it every day. Giving time counts too: cooking for a neighbor, visiting someone who is ill or volunteering. See [chesed](/learn/chesed) and [giving to people who ask](/learn/giving-to-people-who-ask). Many in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda can begin right where they are, with a coin and a kind word.

## Common questions

### What does tzedakah do for the giver?
Chabad.org explains that giving refines the giver's character, builds sensitivity, empathy and humility, and brings fulfillment and joy. The Sages said the poor person does more for the giver than the giver does for the poor.

### What does "the poor person does more for you" mean?
It means that the person in need gives you the opportunity to do a mitzvah and become a partner with G-d. Chabad.org quotes the Sages: "More than the rich man does for the pauper, the pauper does for the rich man."

### Does giving make you happier in Judaism?
Chabad.org says generosity brings a sense of inner fulfillment and joy.

### Should a person who receives help also give?
Yes. Chabad.org says even someone supported by a communal fund should give something, so everyone has the dignity of being a giver.

### Is giving only about money?
No. Chabad.org lists time, skill, attention and a smile along with money. See [chesed](/learn/chesed).

### How can I give in a way that is good for me and for the receiver?
Give with warmth and respect for the receiver's dignity. Read [how to give kindly](/learn/how-to-give-tzedakah-kindly).
