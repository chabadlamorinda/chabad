Psalm 91 is the chapter of Tehillim (Psalms) that begins "He who dwells in the shelter of the Most High," and it is the best-known Jewish psalm of protection. Many Jews recite it together with the bedtime [Shema](/learn/bedtime-shema), a custom that goes back to a sage of the Talmud.

## What Psalm 91 says

The psalm is a song of trust in G-d. In Hebrew it opens:

יֹשֵׁב בְּסֵ֣תֶר עֶלְי֑וֹן בְּצֵ֥ל שַׁ֜דַּ֗י יִתְלוֹנָֽן:

"He who dwells in the covert of the Most High will lodge in the shadow of the Almighty" (Psalms 91:1, Judaica Press translation on Chabad.org). The second verse is the person's own answer: "I shall say of the Lord, my shelter and my fortress, my G-d in Whom I trust."

The rest of the chapter describes what that trust brings. G-d saves from the "snare that traps," covers us "with His wing," and is "an encompassing shield." Later the psalm promises that G-d "will command His angels on your behalf to guard you in all your ways" (verse 11), and that they will carry us so we do not stumble on a stone.

Rashi, the classic commentator, reads the "covert of the Most High" as taking shelter beneath the wings of the Divine Presence. He notes that the psalm speaks to each individual: you, personally, are invited to dwell there.

## Why Jews say it at bedtime

The Talmud (Shavuot 15b) tells that Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi recited Psalm 91 before he went to sleep, as protection. Many people follow this custom today, reciting the psalm along with the bedtime Shema, and it appears in most standard prayer books.

Sleep is a time when we let go of control. Saying a psalm of trust before closing our eyes turns that moment into an act of faith: I did what I could today, and now I place myself in G-d's care. You can read more about the nighttime routine in [Jewish view of sleep](/learn/jewish-view-of-sleep).

It is also customary at bedtime to forgive anyone who may have wronged you during the day and to ask G-d's forgiveness, so that you go to sleep at peace.

## Saying it in times of worry

Many Jews turn to Psalm 91 when they feel anxious, when someone they love is traveling or facing danger, or simply when they need a reminder that they are not alone. This is a personal practice rather than a fixed rule. The psalm does not promise that nothing hard will ever happen; it teaches the deeper security of knowing that G-d is with us in all of it. That is the heart of [bitachon](/learn/bitachon), trust in G-d.

Families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda often tell us that a short psalm said slowly, even for one minute, changes the feeling of a day. If you are saying it for a particular person, you can add their Hebrew name and their mother's name, as with any prayer from [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim).

## How to make it your own prayer

1. **Start small.** Read just the first four verses, in Hebrew or in English.
2. **Choose a moment.** Bedtime works well; so does the morning before leaving the house.
3. **Use a translation you understand.** G-d wants your heart, and understanding the words helps.
4. **Say it with a real request.** After the psalm, speak to G-d in your own words, as described in [talking to G-d in your own words](/learn/talking-to-g-d-in-your-own-words).
5. **Keep a book nearby.** Most prayer books include the psalm near the bedtime Shema.

If you are not sure about the pronunciation or the Hebrew letters, that is fine. A teacher can sit with you for a few minutes and you will be reading it comfortably. Compare it with the gentler [Psalm 23](/learn/psalm-23), another psalm of comfort that many people love.

## Common questions

### What is Psalm 91 in Judaism?
Psalm 91 is a chapter of Tehillim about trusting G-d for protection. Jews recite it at bedtime, along with the Shema, and turn to it when they need reassurance.

### Why do Jews say Psalm 91 before sleep?
The Talmud says that Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi recited it before sleeping as protection. Many people follow his custom and say it with the bedtime Shema.

### What does "He who dwells in the shelter of the Most High" mean?
It describes the person who lives with awareness of G-d, as though sheltered under the Divine Presence. Rashi explains the "covert" as the shelter of G-d's wings.

### Does Psalm 91 guarantee that nothing bad will happen?
No. The psalm expresses trust and G-d's closeness; it is not a magic formula. For a personal or painful situation, speak with the Rabbi.

### Do I need to read it in Hebrew?
No. You may read it in any language you understand, though many enjoy learning the Hebrew over time. A Hebrew or English Tehillim is easy to find.
