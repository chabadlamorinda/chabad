Miriam (מִרְיָם) was the older sister of Moses and Aaron, one of the seven prophetesses of the Jewish people. She watched over baby [Moses](/learn/moses) in the Nile, led the women in song and dance after the splitting of the Red Sea, and, according to our Sages, the miraculous well that gave the Jews water in the desert was in her merit.

## Watching over her baby brother

When Pharaoh decreed that Jewish baby boys be thrown into the Nile, Moses's mother hid him in a basket among the reeds. Miriam stood at a distance to see what would happen. When Pharaoh's daughter found the baby, Miriam offered to bring a Jewish woman to nurse him, and brought her own mother. Moses grew up knowing his family.

## A prophet from childhood

According to Chabad.org, Miriam received prophecy as a child: she foretold that her mother would give birth to a son who would redeem the Jewish people. She is also identified with Puah, the midwife who defied Pharaoh. Our Sages teach that when her father, Amram, separated from his wife in despair, Miriam persuaded him to remarry her, and others followed his example. Her faith helped keep the future alive.

## The song at the sea

After the Jews crossed the Red Sea, "Miriam the prophetess, Aaron's sister, took the tambourine in her hand, and all the women went out after her with tambourines and with dances." The Sages say the women had brought tambourines out of Egypt because they were certain G-d would perform miracles. Read the story every year on [Shabbat Shirah](/learn/shabbat-shirah), the Shabbat of the Song, in the portion of Beshalach.

## Miriam's well

For forty years a rock-well traveled with the people and gave them water. The Sages say it was in Miriam's merit. When she died in Kadesh, the well ran dry, and the people understood why. Chassidic teaching sees in this the way women's spiritual strength nourishes the home and the whole community; see more in [Jewish women](/learn/jewish-women).

## A lesson in humility

The Torah also tells that Miriam spoke critically about Moses, was struck with tzara'at, and was sent outside the camp for seven days. Then the whole people waited for her before moving on. It teaches that words matter, and that the community honors its leaders. She died at 126 or 127 and was buried in Kadesh.

## What Miriam teaches us

- **Hope in dark times.** She believed in a redeemer when no redeemer was in sight.
- **Joy as an act of faith.** The women brought tambourines before the miracle.
- **Nourish others.** Like the well, a person can be a source of life.
- **Guard your words.** Even the great are careful how they speak of others.

## Common questions

### Who was Miriam in the Bible?
Miriam was the older sister of Moses and Aaron and a prophetess who led the women in song at the Red Sea.

### What was Miriam's well?
It was a miraculous source of water that traveled with the Jews in the desert in Miriam's merit and dried up when she died.

### Why did Miriam sing at the sea?
She and the women celebrated the splitting of the Red Sea and the Jews' freedom from Egypt with tambourines and dancing.

### Is Miriam one of the seven prophetesses?
Yes, our Sages count her among the seven prophetesses, together with Sarah, Deborah, Hannah, Abigail, Chuldah and Esther.

### Why did Miriam watch Moses at the Nile?
She wanted to protect him and see what would happen, and she arranged for their mother to nurse him.
