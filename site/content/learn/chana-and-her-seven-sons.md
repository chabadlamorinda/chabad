Chana and her seven sons is a story from the time of the Greek decrees about a Jewish mother and her children who refused to give up the Torah, even when the king himself pressed them. It is one of the most moving stories of Jewish courage, and it is a sad one, so we tell it here carefully, with the main lesson in view.

Many families tell this story around Chanukah. It is not a bedtime story for small children, but older children and grown-ups can learn a great deal from it.

## A note before we begin

Jewish sources tell this story in somewhat different ways. The names, the setting and the details are not the same in every telling. In this guide we follow the account on Chabad.org, in which the events happen in the days of Antiochus (see [Who Was Antiochus?](/learn/who-was-antiochus)). If you would like to know how the different sources compare, ask the Rabbi.

## The story

In the days of the Greek decrees, Jews were forbidden to study Torah, keep Shabbat or perform brit milah (read [The Greek Decrees](/learn/decrees-of-the-greeks)). The High Priest Elazar was arrested, and he chose to give his life rather than give up his faith. Soon after, a woman named Chana and her seven sons were brought in.

The king himself questioned them. He demanded that the sons turn away from the Torah and obey his laws. He tried threats, and he tried to tempt them with rewards. One by one, each of the sons answered with calm and courage. Each said that he would not give up his faith in G-d and the Torah. They spoke of their trust that G-d, who gave them life, would in the end set everything right.

The youngest was a child of about seven. The Talmud adds that the king tried to trick him: he would drop his ring, and the boy need only bend down to pick it up, so that it would look as if he had bowed to the king. The boy would not do it.

## The mother's words

Throughout this terrible time Chana stood beside her sons and encouraged them. At the end, the king asked her to speak to the youngest so that at least one of her children would live. She asked to speak with him privately. She reminded him how she had carried him, nursed him and raised him in the fear of G-d. She told him to look at the heavens and the earth and remember that G-d created everything with His word and holds all life in His hand. And she told him of her hope that they would meet again in G-d's light.

She came back and told the king that she had failed to persuade the boy. Then, before he was taken, she kissed him once more. She told him to tell their ancestor Abraham that although he had once bound one son for G-d, she had bound seven. After this, the story ends in grief.

## What the story teaches

- **Faith can be taught at home.** The courage of those boys came from the home their mother built. This is why Chana is a model for Jewish mothers, and why [women and Chanukah](/learn/women-and-chanukah) belong together.
- **Giving up your life for G-d is called Kiddush Hashem**, "sanctifying G-d's name." It is the highest act of devotion. Most of us will never be asked to do it. Read more in [Kiddush Hashem](/learn/kiddush-hashem).
- **The Maccabees' war was won on this foundation.** The brave stand of ordinary families gave the Jewish people the strength to keep going. Read more in [Who Were the Maccabees?](/learn/the-maccabees).
- **Every one of us can stand up in small ways.** Lighting a candle, keeping Shabbat, giving a child a Jewish name: each of these is a form of loyalty.

## How to tell it to children

For young children, it is enough to say that there was once a wicked king who wanted Jewish children to stop being Jewish, and that a brave mother taught her children to stay faithful. Leave out the details. Older children and teens can read the whole story, and it is good to talk about it together afterward. For more suitable stories, visit our [Kids' Corner](/kids) and the guide [Chanukah for kids](/learn/chanukah-for-kids).

## Common questions

### Who was Chana and her seven sons?
Chana was a Jewish mother who, with her seven sons, stood firm in the days of the Greek decrees and would not give up the Torah. Her story is told at Chanukah time as an example of faith.

### Is the story of Chana and her seven sons part of the Chanukah story?
It comes from the same period of Greek persecution, and many people tell it at Chanukah. Jewish sources tell it with some differences in names and details, so ask the Rabbi if you want to compare them.

### What did Chana tell her youngest son?
She reminded him of the love and care she had given him, told him that G-d created the world and holds all life, and said she hoped they would meet in G-d's light.

### What is Kiddush Hashem?
Kiddush Hashem (קִדּוּשׁ הַשֵּׁם) means "sanctifying G-d's name," and the term is used for giving one's life rather than renounce the Torah. It also means living in a way that brings honor to G-d every day.

### Is this story suitable for children?
Because it is about martyrdom, it is best told in a gentle, shortened way to young children. Older children and adults can read the full version.
