Yirat Shamayim (יראת שמים), usually translated "fear of G-d" or "fear of Heaven," means awe, respect and reverence before G-d, not terror. Chabad.org describes it as a "liberating mitzvah": the one thing we are meant to be in awe of is G-d, which frees us from fear of failure, of other people and even of death.

If you live in Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda or the East Bay and the words "fear of G-d" have always made you uncomfortable, you are in good company. You are welcome to ask Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski about it at 925-384-7242.

## Is "fear" the right word?

The Hebrew word yirah can mean both fear and awe. A Chabad.org writer suggests that "respect" often conveys the meaning better than "fear." Love, he explains, is about me: my feelings and my desire to be close. Respect is about the other: they have a real existence and may ask something of me that I must accept.

Without respect, love can turn self-centered, and G-d becomes a reflection of what we wish Him to be. Awe protects us from shaping G-d into an extension of our own ego.

## Different kinds of awe

Chabad.org describes a range:

- **Basic fear.** Concern that displeasing G-d has consequences.
- **A deeper fear.** Like a small child afraid of being separated from a parent, a fear of being cut off from G-d's love. It belongs to people who feel the love in their own soul.
- **Awe and wonder.** The response to contemplating G-d's greatness and creation.

The Tanya (chapter 41) calls the first one lower fear, the fear of doing wrong, which keeps a person from sinning. Higher fear is a shamefaced awe in G-d's presence, the result of keeping Torah and mitzvot. Read more about the [Tanya](/learn/tanya).

## Love and awe: two wings

Chabad.org puts it this way: love sets your goals, and awe sets your boundaries. The Tanya says love and awe are the two wings that lift Torah and mitzvot upward. A bird with one wing cannot rise. Love alone can lose its limits, and awe alone can become cold and heavy. Together, they give both warmth and discipline.

Awe also frees us from ego. It connects us to something larger than ourselves, which love alone does not always do.

## How awe protects us

The Tanya says that awe is the root of "turn away from evil," while love is the root of "do good." A moment of awareness that G-d is present, that He is close and sees even our thoughts, can stop us from saying or doing something harmful. This is the kind of fear that is good for us. It is closely related to [humility](/learn/humility) and to the question of [why we keep the mitzvot](/learn/why-mitzvot).

## Simple ways to feel it every day

Chabad.org encourages setting aside time each day to reflect on your relationship with G-d. If you do not feel awe, reflection can build a lasting awareness over time. The Tanya suggests meditating on G-d's greatness and closeness before Torah study or a mitzvah. Here are some simple ideas:

1. **Pause before a blessing.** Take one breath before you say it. See [blessings](/learn/blessings).
2. **Look up.** Step outside on a clear night in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda and look at the stars.
3. **Notice a small wonder.** A baby's hand, a tree, a meal.
4. **Say Shema slowly.** One sentence, with full attention.

## Common questions

### What does fear of G-d mean in Judaism?
It means awe, reverence and respect for G-d, not terror. Chabad.org calls it a liberating mitzvah.

### Should we be afraid of G-d?
No. Judaism teaches that we should love G-d and also stand in awe of Him. The fear that matters is the fear of being distant from G-d, not fear of punishment alone.

### What is the difference between love and fear of G-d?
Chabad.org says love sets your goals and awe sets your boundaries. The Tanya calls them two wings that need each other.

### What are lower and higher fear in the Tanya?
Lower fear is the fear of doing wrong. Higher fear is a shamefaced awe in G-d's presence. See Tanya chapter 41.

### What if I do not feel awe?
Chabad.org says to reflect regularly on your connection with G-d, and that awareness will grow in your heart. The Tanya says that even accepting G-d's rule over you counts as real service.
