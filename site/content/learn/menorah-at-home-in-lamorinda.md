The Chanukah menorah is placed where its light can be seen: at a window facing the street, or at the doorway of your home, on a stable, fireproof surface away from anything that can burn. If you are not sure which spot fits your house or apartment, ask the Rabbi.

The purpose of the menorah is pirsumei nisa (פִּרְסוּמֵי נִסָּא), "publicizing the miracle." That is why we light it where others can see it. Dates for Chanukah this year are on the [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays) page; this guide covers placement and safety for homes in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda.

## Window or door?

Chabad.org and the Chabad sources teach two ways to publicize the miracle:

- **A window facing the street.** Chabad.org says this is fine if the window is less than about thirty feet above street level, so passersby can see the lights. Hillside homes in Lamorinda often have street-facing windows high above the road; if yours is, ask the Rabbi.
- **The doorway.** The menorah goes on the left side of the entrance, across from the mezuzah, so you walk between two mitzvahs. Chabad custom, as taught by the Rebbe, is generally to light inside the home, at the doorway of the room, though many also light at a window. Ask the Rabbi what is best for you.

Apartments and townhouses with a shared hallway: use a window onto the street or a courtyard if you have one, or light just inside your own door. The menorah need not be seen by many people; what matters is that it is in a place that is part of your home and visible to others.

## The basics

1. **Kindle after nightfall** (Friday is the exception; see below). Check tonight's time at [the menorah page](/menorah).
2. **Use enough fuel.** Candles or oil must burn for at least half an hour after nightfall. See [menorah, oil and candles](/learn/menorah-oil-and-candles).
3. **Place the candles in a straight line, at the same height,** with the shamash (helper candle) set apart.
4. **Light the shamash, say the blessings,** then light the newest candle first, from left to right on later nights ([how to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah)).
5. **Let it burn.** Do not use the Chanukah lights for reading, work or other benefit; that is the job of the shamash.

On Friday afternoon, the menorah is lit before the Shabbat candles, with enough oil or long candles to burn until well after nightfall; read [Chanukah on Shabbat](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat). On Saturday night, make Havdalah first.

## Fire safety in a dry December

Winters in the East Bay can be dry, windy and cold. Chabad.org advises a sturdy, fireproof surface out of the reach of children, away from curtains and other flammable materials. In addition:

- Use a metal or glass tray under the menorah, and a table that is not near a drafty window or door that opens.
- Move curtains, paper decorations and gift wrap away, and keep a candle burning only where you can see it.
- Do not leave the house or go to sleep while the flames are still burning. If you must go out, ask the Rabbi about using a menorah with long-burning oil or arranging for someone to stay.
- Keep a fire extinguisher or a pot of water near. In dry, windy weather follow the instructions of your fire district.
- If a Red Flag fire-weather warning is in force, ask the Rabbi about an alternative, such as a menorah with a glass shield, and see [wildfire readiness](/learn/wildfire-readiness-for-jewish-families).

Children love to help. Let them light under watchful eyes, and teach them to keep their distance.

## Renters, condos and HOAs

California law protects a mezuzah on the entry door, but a menorah on a balcony or in a window is a different question. Many associations and landlords have rules about open flame on balconies and patios, and some prefer you not to light there. Speak with the property manager early and kindly, and ask the Rabbi about lighting indoors by the window or door. For the law on the mezuzah itself, see [your mezuzah, HOA and landlord](/learn/mezuzah-and-religious-displays-hoa-california).

## Common questions

### Where should I put my menorah in my house?

Place it at a window facing the street or at the doorway of your home, on a stable, fireproof surface. Ask the Rabbi which place fits your home best.

### Can I light a menorah in an apartment?

Yes. Light it at a window or just inside your door, away from curtains and with a metal tray. If your lease or building forbids open flame, speak with the manager and ask the Rabbi about alternatives.

### How high can a menorah be placed in a window?

Chabad.org says a window is suitable if it is less than about thirty feet above ground, so people can see the lights. Hillside homes may be higher; ask the Rabbi.

### How long must the menorah burn?

The candles or oil should burn for at least half an hour after nightfall, so that people can see them. Friday needs longer-burning candles because of Shabbat.

### Is it safe to leave the menorah burning?

Never leave open flames alone. Use a safe tray, keep children and pets away, and ask the Rabbi if you must go out while the candles burn.
