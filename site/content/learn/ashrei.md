Ashrei is Psalm 145 from the Book of Tehillim (Psalms), a short song of praise to G-d. The Talmud says that whoever says it three times a day is assured a share in the World to Come. It is one of the best prayers to learn first, because it is simple, upbeat and easy to say in English.

## What the word means

*Ashrei* means "happy" or "fortunate." The prayer opens with two verses that begin with this word: "Happy are those who dwell in Your house," and "Happy is the people for whom this is so." Then comes Psalm 145, which begins "A praise of David."

King David wrote the psalm in an alphabetical pattern: each verse starts with the next letter of the Hebrew alphabet. One letter, *nun*, is skipped. The Sages explain this in different ways, and the psalm is still complete and beautiful. The prayer ends with a verse from Psalm 115: "And we will bless G-d from now and forever. Halleluyah."

## What it says

- G-d is great, kind and close to everyone who calls.
- G-d is compassionate to all He made, and His kingdom lasts for all generations.
- G-d "opens His hand and satisfies the desire of every living thing." This is the verse the Talmud tells us to say with full concentration, because it speaks of G-d feeding the world.
- G-d is near to all who call upon Him sincerely.

## Why the Sages loved it

The Talmud (Berachot 4b) teaches that saying Ashrei three times each day brings great reward. One Chassidic explanation of why we say it every day: Ashrei thanks G-d for His everyday kindness in the natural world, the food on our table and the sun that rises. Hallel, by contrast, thanks Him for rare, extraordinary miracles, and so it is not said daily. When we say Ashrei with attention, we feel that the world is held in loving hands.

## When it is said

Ashrei is part of the daily prayers:

- **twice in the morning service:** once among the psalms of praise, and again after the Amidah;
- **once at the start of the afternoon service** (Mincha).

It is not said in the evening service. That makes three times every day. See the [morning service](/learn/shacharit-morning-service) for a first-timer's walk-through.

## How to say it

1. Read it once slowly in English.
2. Choose one verse a week to learn in Hebrew.
3. When you reach "You open Your hand," pause, close your eyes, and think of one thing you are grateful for.
4. Customs differ about whether to sit or stand; in shul, follow the congregation, and ask the Rabbi.

For more on the book it comes from, see [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim).

## Common questions

### What is Ashrei?
Ashrei is Psalm 145 with a few verses added before and after. It is a song of praise to G-d that is part of the daily prayers.

### What does Ashrei mean?
*Ashrei* means "happy" or "fortunate." It opens with "Happy are those who dwell in Your house."

### Why do we say Ashrei three times a day?
The Talmud teaches that whoever says it three times a day is assured a place in the World to Come. We say it twice in the morning service and once at Mincha.

### Why is there no "nun" in Ashrei?
Psalm 145 follows the Hebrew alphabet, but the verse for the letter *nun* is missing. The Sages offer several explanations, and the psalm is still whole and beautiful.

### Which verse of Ashrei is most important?
"You open Your hand and satisfy the desire of every living thing." Tradition says to say it with full concentration, because it speaks of G-d providing for all.

### Is Ashrei said at the evening service?
No. It is left out of Maariv.
