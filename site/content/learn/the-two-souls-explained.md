According to the Tanya, every Jew has two souls: a divine soul, "a part of G-d above," that longs for goodness and closeness to G-d, and an animal soul, the natural self, with its drives and desires. The feeling of being torn between them is normal, and the Tanya says the choice of who leads is yours.

This is the central idea of the book. If you have ever wondered why you want to do the right thing and also want something else, this is Tanya's answer.

## Why do I feel torn?

Chapter 1 of the Tanya says that every Jew has two souls (see [Tanya Chapter 1 explained](/learn/tanya-chapter-one-explained)). The first, the animal soul, gives life to the body. The second, described in Chapter 2, is a true part of G-d.

So the inner argument is not a sign that something is wrong with you. A Chabad.org article puts it this way: G-d did not create people to be perfect; striving is the point. For the general Jewish view of the inner struggle, see [the yetzer hara](/learn/yetzer-hara).

## The animal soul

The animal soul is the natural self: it wants comfort, pleasure and recognition. Chapter 1 lists the pulls it can bring: anger and pride, the love of pleasure, frivolity and idle talk, laziness and gloom. Yet the same chapter says the Jewish animal soul also has good natural traits, like compassion and kindness. Read more in [is the animal soul bad?](/learn/animal-soul-not-evil).

## The divine soul

The divine soul is what the Tanya calls a part of G-d. Chapter 4 says that its intellect (chochmah, binah and daat) and its emotions (love and fear of G-d) find expression through [thought, speech and action](/learn/three-garments-of-the-soul). Read more in [a part of G-d above](/learn/part-of-g-d-above).

## Who is in charge?

Chapter 12 explains that in the beinoni, the ordinary person who strives, the divine soul directs thought, speech and action, while the animal soul still stirs desires in the heart. He does not let them rule him, even for a moment. Chabad.org's article on the beinoni says the same: a person copes, even if he does not "triumph," and keeps striving. See [the beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni) and [mind over heart](/learn/mind-over-heart-tanya).

The same article gives an image: climbing a steep hill on a bicycle, you will sometimes falter, and what matters is to keep pedaling.

## What this looks like in an ordinary day

These are our own examples of the two voices, not quotations from the Tanya:

- the alarm rings, and one voice says "five more minutes" while another says "get up and learn";
- an annoying message arrives, and one voice wants to reply at once while another says "wait";
- there is an extra treat on the plate, and you ask what you really want.

In each case, you can choose which voice leads.

## A note

The Tanya's guidance is spiritual. It is not a replacement for medical or psychological help. If your inner struggle feels like more than ordinary temptation, such as deep sadness or anxiety, speak with a professional as well, and [ask the Rabbi](/help/talk-to-the-rabbi).

## Common questions

### What are the two souls in Tanya?
The divine soul (nefesh Elokit) and the animal soul (nefesh habahamit). Both are in every Jew.

### Why do I feel torn between right and wrong in Judaism?
Tanya explains it as the two souls pulling in different directions. Feeling the pull is normal and is not a failure.

### Is the animal soul evil?
No. Chapter 1 says it has good natural traits such as compassion and kindness, and Tanya teaches that it can be raised up through a mitzvah. See [is the animal soul bad?](/learn/animal-soul-not-evil).

### Which soul is the real me?
Both are in you. Chapter 2 says the divine soul is truly a part of G-d, and Chapter 12 describes the beinoni, who lets it lead in thought, speech and action.
