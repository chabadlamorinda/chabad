Rosh Chodesh Tevet, the first day of the Hebrew month of Tevet, always falls during Chanukah. On that day two Torah scrolls are read in the synagogue: the first for Rosh Chodesh (Numbers 28:1 to 15) and the second for that day of Chanukah. It is one of the few days of the year that is both a new month and a festival of light.

## Why does Rosh Chodesh always fall on Chanukah?

Chanukah begins on the 25th of Kislev and lasts eight days. The month of Kislev has either 29 or 30 days, so the new month of Tevet always arrives while Chanukah is still going. A teaching on Chabad.org notes that the eight days of Chanukah always include at least one Shabbat and Rosh Chodesh Tevet.

In years when Kislev has 30 days, Rosh Chodesh is two days: the last day of Kislev and the first of Tevet. Then two days of Chanukah are Rosh Chodesh. Chabad.org's schedule for such a year lists both days with the same reading.

You can read about the new month in general in our guide to [Rosh Chodesh](/learn/rosh-chodesh) and about the month itself in [Tevet](/learn/tevet).

## What is read from the Torah?

On a regular Rosh Chodesh, four people are called to the Torah to hear Numbers 28:1 to 15. This passage describes the daily offerings, the Shabbat offerings and the special offerings for the new month in the Temple.

When Rosh Chodesh Tevet falls during Chanukah, two scrolls are taken from the ark:

- **First scroll:** Numbers 28:1 to 15, the passage of Rosh Chodesh, in three aliyot.
- **Second scroll:** the Chanukah reading of that day, from the gifts of the tribal princes. On the sixth day of Chanukah this is Numbers 7:42 to 47 and on the seventh day Numbers 7:48 to 53.

If Rosh Chodesh falls on Shabbat, the weekly Torah portion is read, with the passage that begins "On the day of the Shabbat" (Numbers 28:9 to 15) as the final reading. To see why the princes' gifts are read at all, see [the Torah reading on Chanukah](/learn/chanukah-torah-reading).

## The prayers of the day

Chanukah and Rosh Chodesh each add something to the prayers, and on this day they come together:

- The full **Hallel** is said on every day of Chanukah, including this one.
- **Al HaNissim** is added to the Amidah and to Grace after Meals. When Rosh Chodesh Tevet falls on Shabbat, Chabad.org notes that it is said in Grace after Meals together with *Retzeh* and *Ya'aleh v'Yavo*.
- On Rosh Chodesh, the Musaf prayer is added. See [Musaf](/learn/musaf).

Our guide on [Al HaNissim and Hallel](/learn/al-hanissim-and-hallel) explains where each is said. If you are not sure about a detail of a particular year, ask the Rabbi.

## Why did the Greeks target Rosh Chodesh?

Rabbi Yosef Caro, in his *Beit Yosef*, asks why Chanukah is eight days. In one explanation recorded on Chabad.org, the Greeks tried to abolish three mitzvot: Shabbat, Rosh Chodesh and circumcision (brit milah). The eight days of Chanukah always include a Shabbat and Rosh Chodesh Tevet, and the eighth day connects to brit milah, which takes place on the eighth day of a baby's life. So the festival itself holds all three mitzvot the Greeks tried to cancel.

Another Chabad.org teaching points out why the three were chosen. They all go beyond nature: Shabbat remembers creation, circumcision perfects the body as nature made it, and the new month is set by the Sages. The Greeks believed everything depends on nature alone.

## Common questions

### Does Rosh Chodesh Tevet always fall on Chanukah?
Yes. Chanukah starts on the 25th of Kislev and runs eight days, so the first of Tevet always falls within it.

### How many Torah scrolls are read on Rosh Chodesh Tevet during Chanukah?
Two. The first is for Rosh Chodesh (Numbers 28:1 to 15) and the second for the day of Chanukah.

### Is Hallel full or half on Rosh Chodesh during Chanukah?
The full Hallel is said on all eight days of Chanukah, according to Chabad.org.

### Which three mitzvot did the Greeks try to ban?
Shabbat, Rosh Chodesh and brit milah, as explained on Chabad.org. Chanukah always contains a Shabbat and Rosh Chodesh Tevet, and its eighth day connects to brit milah.

### Why do we say Al HaNissim in Grace after Meals on Rosh Chodesh Tevet?
Because it is said on every day of Chanukah. When Rosh Chodesh falls on Shabbat, it is said with *Retzeh* and *Ya'aleh v'Yavo*.
