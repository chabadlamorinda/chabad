According to Chapter 36 of the Tanya, G-d created the world because He wanted a dwelling place in the lowest realm, this physical world, where His essence would be revealed without any veil. Our Sages teach that G-d desired an abode in the lower realms, and the Tanya explains that we build it through our practical mitzvot.

This is the Tanya's answer to "why am I here?" For families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, it gives meaning to a Tuesday morning as much as to a holiday. This guide is a simple retelling of Chapter 36 of the Tanya.

## The question Chapter 36 answers

Chapter 36 continues from Chapter 35, which asked why practical mitzvot, performed through action, are the ultimate goal of Torah. The answer: G-d's purpose in creation is to have a dwelling in the lower realms. Just as a home is where a person's essence dwells, this world is meant to be a dwelling for G-d's Essence.

In the human being, the chapter explains, the animal soul and the body are the "lower realms." That is why practical mitzvot, which draw G-d's presence into the body and the animal soul, are central. See [The Power of One Mitzvah](/learn/the-power-of-one-mitzvah).

## "Higher" and "lower"

The chapter says higher and lower do not describe distance from G-d. Before Him all worlds, from the highest to the lowest, are equal. The terms describe how much of G-dliness is revealed. The more concealment, the lower the world, and ours is the lowest, with the most darkness.

It also explains that creation came about through a chain of descending worlds, each with more veils. Without them, created beings could not endure the intensity of the light. The chapter cites "For no man can see Me and live" (Exodus 33:20).

## Why the lowest world?

The chapter's surprising point is that the purpose is the lowest world, not the higher ones. Revelation, not concealment, is the goal, so the purpose of the descent is the physical world, where darkness is transformed into light. Light that comes out of darkness is brighter and more intense than the light of the higher worlds. That is why this world's revelation surpasses theirs.

This is how the Tanya turns ordinary life into purpose. A world of concealment is not a mistake. It is the very place where something new can happen. Chapter 37 describes how a mitzvah lifts physical things into holiness; see [Serving G-d in Everyday Life](/learn/serving-g-d-in-everyday-life).

## Torah gives strength

Chapter 36 calls Torah "might" and "strength," because it lets created beings receive the revealed light without being overwhelmed. See [Why Study Torah?](/learn/tanya-on-torah-study).

## The ultimate purpose: Moshiach

Chapter 36 says the Messianic era is the ultimate purpose and fulfillment of creation, not merely a reward for human effort. It describes Sinai as a foretaste: the Ten Commandments revealed G-d's will, and the people's souls departed at each utterance and were restored by the "dew" of Torah. After Sinai, sin brought physicality back, and the world remains coarse until the end of days, when the body and the world will be refined enough to receive the revealed light. Chapter 37 adds that this revelation depends on our service during exile, and that tzedakah, because it elevates so much of the soul, hastens the redemption. See [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach).

## Common questions

### Why did G-d create the world, according to the Tanya?
Chapter 36 says that G-d desired a dwelling place in the lower realms, meaning the physical world, where His essential light would be revealed without a veil.

### What is "a dwelling place in the lower worlds"?
It is the Chassidic phrase (dirah betachtonim) for G-d's purpose in creation. The Tanya says that through our practical mitzvot, the physical world becomes a place where G-d's presence is revealed.

### Does the Tanya say this world is bad?
No. Chapter 36 says "lower" describes concealment, not distance from G-d, and that the light which emerges from darkness is brighter than the light of the higher worlds.

### What does Tanya say about Moshiach?
Chapter 36 calls the Messianic era the ultimate purpose and fulfillment of creation. See [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach) and ask the Rabbi for a deeper explanation.

### How does my daily life help?
Chapters 36 and 37 teach that practical mitzvot, the use of food, homes and money in G-d's service, build the dwelling. Even small acts count; see [the power of one mitzvah](/learn/the-power-of-one-mitzvah).
