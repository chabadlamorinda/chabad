When a child asks "Why are we Jewish?", the best answer is short, warm and true: we are Jewish because we are part of a family that goes back thousands of years, to Abraham and Sarah, and because our soul is Jewish, a precious gift that we get to live out through mitzvot, Shabbat, holidays and kindness. The answer should make a child feel loved and proud, not pressured.

## Why children ask, and what they really want to know

Children usually ask when they notice something different: a holiday, a classmate's question or a family custom. Behind "Why are we Jewish?" often lies something simpler: "Where do I belong?" or "Is it okay to be different?" So begin by saying you are glad they asked. Then give an answer that fits their age, and keep the door open for more questions.

## Honest answers by age

**Ages 3 to 6: belonging and joy**
Little children understand family, stories and fun. You might say:

*"We are Jewish because our family is part of the Jewish people. We celebrate Shabbat and holidays together, we do kind things, and G-d loves every one of us."*

Then show them: light Shabbat candles, taste honey on Rosh Hashanah, shake a lulav. For young children, real, joyful experiences matter more than explanations.

**Ages 7 to 10: the chain and the stories**
Children this age love stories, and they love to know where they come from. You can say:

*"Our family is part of a very long chain. Abraham and Sarah began it thousands of years ago, and every generation passed it on to the next, all the way to you. Being Jewish means we have special things to do, like Shabbat, tzedakah and kindness, and each of us has a unique job to make the world better."*

Name grandparents and great-grandparents, and share what you know of your family's story. Pictures help.

**Ages 11 to 13: soul and mission**
Preteens can think deeper. You can explain the Chassidic idea that every Jewish person has a soul (*neshamah*, נְשָׁמָה) that is a spark of G-d, and that we are connected to every Jew through that soul. Judaism is inherited, like a family treasure we receive and carry forward: you do not earn it, you are born into it, and you choose how to live it.

Add that mitzvot are how we use that soul: each mitzvah, whether a Shabbat candle or an act of kindness, adds light. Preparing for [Bar or Bat Mitzvah](/learn/bar-mitzvah) is a natural moment for this talk.

**Teens: honesty and choice**
Teens want real answers and respect for their questions. Listen first. Tell the truth, including that you struggle with some things too. See our guide [Jewish identity for teens](/learn/jewish-identity-for-teens) and [why be Jewish](/learn/why-be-jewish).

## How a child is Jewish

Traditionally, a child is Jewish when the mother is Jewish or when a person converts under Jewish law. This is something to explain gently and honestly if a child asks; for situations in your own family, ask the Rabbi in confidence at 925-384-7242.

## What if the question is hard?

Sometimes children ask because of something sad or scary they heard. The advice given to a parent whose seven-year-old was afraid to be Jewish is worth remembering: do not argue or give a speech; build positive memories. Go to a celebration together, enjoy Shabbat meals, join a children's program and help your child make friends, so that "Jewish" is linked with joy, belonging and fun. For more serious topics, see [talking to kids about antisemitism](/learn/talking-to-kids-about-antisemitism) and [talking to kids about G-d](/learn/talking-to-kids-about-g-d).

## Make the answer something they can feel

1. **Say it with a smile.** Children read your face.
2. **Do something Jewish together.** A candle, a blessing, a challah.
3. **Tell family stories.** Where did grandparents come from? Which Jewish customs did they love?
4. **Welcome follow-up questions.** "I don't know, let's find out" is a good answer. Ask the Rabbi.
5. **Connect them with friends.** Children love being Jewish when they have friends who share it. See [Jewish kids in Lamorinda](/learn/jewish-kids-in-lamorinda).

## Common questions

### How do I explain being Jewish to a young child?
Say that our family is part of the Jewish people, that we celebrate Shabbat and holidays and do kind things, and that G-d loves us. Then show it with a candle or a song.

### What if my child says "I don't want to be Jewish"?
Stay calm and curious. Ask why. Often it is about feeling different, so give joyful experiences and friends rather than arguments.

### Why are we Jewish if we are not very religious?
Being Jewish is who we are, from our soul and family, not a score on observance. Every Jew can take the next step. See [why be Jewish](/learn/why-be-jewish).

### What do I say when my child asks about G-d?
Be honest and simple: G-d made the world, loves us and cares what we do. See [talking to kids about G-d](/learn/talking-to-kids-about-g-d).

### How can we help our child feel proud to be Jewish?
Celebrate holidays, keep a family Shabbat, share stories, and connect with other Jewish families. Joy teaches more than lectures.
