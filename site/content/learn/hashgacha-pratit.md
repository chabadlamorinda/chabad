Hashgacha pratit means "Divine Providence," the belief that G-d personally watches over and directs every detail of creation. In Chassidic teaching, nothing is random: every event, encounter and moment in our lives is part of G-d's plan and holds a purpose.

## What it means

Belief that G-d runs the world is a basic Jewish principle. Maimonides taught that G-d's providence over people depends on their connection to Him. The [Baal Shem Tov](/learn/baal-shem-tov), founder of Chassidism, taught something even more far-reaching: G-d's providence reaches every single detail of creation. A leaf blowing in the wind, where it lands and when it falls, is directed by G-d for a purpose.

## The leaf and the worm

The Baal Shem Tov once pointed to a leaf falling from a tree and taught that its exact path was decided from Above, as part of the plan of creation. The leaf fell where it did so that a small creature beneath it could find shade. If G-d cares this much about a leaf, how much more does He care about each person.

## How it changes the way we live

- **Nothing is a coincidence.** Meeting a person, overhearing a word, or ending up in a certain place are all opportunities. The Baal Shem Tov taught that everything we see or hear is a lesson for how to serve G-d.
- **Every place has a mission.** The Rebbe often taught that a Jew who finds himself in a certain city, job or neighborhood is there for a reason: to bring light and do mitzvahs there. Living in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda is part of that mission too.
- **Calm in hard times.** Knowing that G-d is in control brings trust and peace. This is called bitachon, trust in G-d.
- **Gratitude.** When we see G-d's hand in small things, we say thank you more often.

## Providence and free will

If G-d directs everything, are we still free? Jewish teaching holds both: G-d guides the world, and we choose how to act. Our choices between right and wrong are truly our own. Read more in [free will](/learn/free-will).

## Seeing it in your own life, step by step

1. **Notice.** At the end of the day, think of one "coincidence" and ask what it might be teaching you.
2. **Say thank you.** Thank G-d for one small detail that went right.
3. **Ask "what can I do here?"** wherever you find yourself, in line at the store or at a meeting.
4. **Learn the stories.** Chassidic stories are full of hashgacha pratit and make the idea come alive.

## Common questions

### Does hashgacha pratit mean everything is already decided?
No. G-d guides the world, but people have free will in their moral choices. Providence sets the stage and gives us opportunities; how we respond is up to us.

### If G-d controls everything, why do bad things happen?
This is one of the deepest questions in Judaism. We trust that G-d is good and has reasons we cannot always see. It is also okay to ask, cry and pray. Talk to the Rabbi if you are struggling.

### Is it a Chabad idea only?
Belief in Divine Providence is basic to all of Judaism. The Baal Shem Tov's teaching that it reaches every detail of creation is especially central in Chassidic thought, including Chabad.

### How can I see Divine Providence when life feels random?
Start small. Look back at events that led you somewhere good, and you will often see a thread. Many people find that the more they look, the more they notice.
