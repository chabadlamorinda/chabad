Yes, you may work on Chanukah. It is a holiday set by the Sages, not a Yom Tov like Passover or Sukkot, so work, school, driving and using a phone are all allowed. What changes during the eight days is mostly the lighting, the prayers and a few customs.

## Why Chanukah is not a day off

The holidays of the Torah, such as Rosh Hashanah and Passover, have restrictions on work. Chanukah was established later by the Sages as a time of thanks and praise. Chabad.org says work is permitted on Chanukah for this reason. See also [Jewish holidays at school and work](/learn/jewish-holidays-at-school-and-work) and [school calendars in Lamorinda](/learn/jewish-holidays-and-lamorinda-school-calendars).

## What does change

- **Lighting the menorah each night.** This is the heart of the holiday. See [how to light](/learn/chanukah-menorah).
- **The Chanukah lights are not for use.** No reading, working or other uses by their light. See [what the shamash is](/learn/what-is-the-shamash).
- **The women's custom.** There is a longstanding custom for women not to do work while the lights burn. See [women and Chanukah](/learn/women-and-chanukah) for the details. This does not mean women cannot work during the day.
- **Special prayers.** We add Al Hanissim (עַל הַנִּסִּים), "for the miracles," to the Amidah and to Grace After Meals, and we add the full Hallel to the morning prayers. See [Al Hanissim and Hallel](/learn/al-hanissim-and-hallel).
- **Joy.** The days are for gratitude, songs and food, with gifts and gelt for children. See [Chanukah foods](/learn/chanukah-foods).

## Fasting and mourning

Chanukah is a time of joy, and it is the custom not to fast, and not to give eulogies at funerals. Mourners follow their own rules; for questions about mourning during Chanukah, speak to the Rabbi. See also [Tachanun](/learn/tachanun), a prayer of supplication that we do not say on Chanukah.

## Chanukah and your workplace or school

You can light the menorah after work or school. If you will be home late, see [when to light the menorah](/learn/when-to-light-the-menorah) and [Forgot to Light the Menorah?](/learn/missed-lighting-the-menorah). For ideas on sharing the holiday with colleagues or classmates, see [Chanukah at work and college](/learn/chanukah-at-work-and-college) and [Chanukah for friends and coworkers](/learn/chanukah-for-friends-and-coworkers).

## Practical tips for a working week

- Put the lighting time on your phone calendar, and plan to be home shortly after sunset if you can. Tonight's time is on [the menorah page](/menorah).
- On the Friday of Chanukah, the menorah is lit before the Shabbat candles, so plan to leave work or school in good time. See [Chanukah on Shabbat](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat).
- If the household is not all home at the right time, a short wait so that everyone can take part is fine.
- If your office or school asks about the holiday, it is a nice chance to share what the lights mean.

## Common questions

### Do you work on Hanukkah?
Yes. Chanukah is not a Yom Tov, so work and school are allowed, and the main mitzvah is lighting the menorah at home each night.

### Is Hanukkah a day off?
No. It is not a holiday of rest. Families light in the evening and often take time to be together.

### Can I drive or use my phone on Chanukah?
Yes. Only on Shabbat, which falls during Chanukah, are these restricted. See [Chanukah on Shabbat](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat).

### Do women stop working on Chanukah?
There is a custom for women to refrain from work while the lights burn, about half an hour after lighting. Customs vary, so ask the Rabbi.

### Do we fast on Chanukah?
No. It is a time of joy, with songs, food and gifts.

### Do kids go to school on Chanukah?
Yes, school goes on. See your school's calendar, and read [Chanukah for kids](/learn/chanukah-for-kids).
