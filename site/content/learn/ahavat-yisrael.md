Ahavat Yisrael (אהבת ישראל) means love of a fellow Jew: the Torah's command to "love your neighbor as yourself" (Leviticus 19:18), which Chassidic teaching explains as seeing every Jew as part of the same soul. Chabad's welcome to every Jew, whatever their background, grows from this idea.

## "The whole Torah on one foot"

The Talmud tells of a person who asked the great sage Hillel to teach him the whole Torah while standing on one foot. Hillel answered that what is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow; the rest is commentary, now go and learn it. Rabbi Akiva likewise called "love your neighbor as yourself" a great principle of the Torah. Love for others is not a side topic in Judaism; it is at its core.

## The puzzle: can you command love?

Human beings naturally care about themselves first. How can the Torah ask us to love someone else as ourselves, and how can it command a feeling at all? The answer of the Alter Rebbe, Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, in the [Tanya](/learn/tanya) (chapter 32) is that every Jew has a G-dly soul, and all G-dly souls come from one source. Bodies differ; souls are really one. So loving another Jew means looking past the outside to the soul, and seeing it as one with your own. The old image is of a body: a hand that is cut is felt by the whole body.

## The Baal Shem Tov: love first

The Baal Shem Tov, founder of Chassidic teaching (see [Baal Shem Tov](/learn/baal-shem-tov)), taught that you should love a fellow Jew even if you have never met, and even if he or she is far from Jewish life. He called love of a fellow Jew the first gateway into G-d's court. He also taught that a soul may come into this world to do one single favor for another Jew.

## The Rebbe: make it a mitzvah campaign

The Lubavitcher Rebbe added Ahavat Yisrael to his [mitzvah campaigns](/learn/ten-mitzvah-campaigns) in 1976, asking every Jew, whatever their level of observance, to take practical steps. His advice was to start each day by deciding to love your fellow Jew, to say only good about others, to look for a chance to do someone a favor, and to bring people together across divisions. The Rebbe also taught that since you never know which favor is the reason a soul came into the world, treat every favor as if it were the one.

## How to practice Ahavat Yisrael today

1. **Start the day with the decision:** "I will love every Jew today."
2. **Speak well** and avoid gossip ([lashon hara](/learn/lashon-hara)).
3. **Judge favorably.** See the guide to [judging others favorably](/learn/judging-others-favorably).
4. **Do one favor** for another Jew: a ride, a meal, a phone call.
5. **Invite someone** to a Shabbat meal or a class.
6. **Never look down on a Jew who is less observant.** Chassidic teaching says most Jews today were simply not given a Jewish education, and should be drawn near with kindness, never criticism. Questions about how? Ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### What is Ahavat Yisrael?
Ahavat Yisrael is the mitzvah of loving every Jew as yourself, based on the verse "love your neighbor as yourself."

### Does Ahavat Yisrael mean Jews do not care about other people?
No. Judaism teaches kindness toward all people. Chassidic teaching says love begins close to home, with family and community, and grows outward from there.

### How can I love a Jew I disagree with?
Chassidic teaching asks you to look at the soul beneath, which is the same as yours. You can disagree about practice and still love the person.

### Who said "love your neighbor as yourself"?
It comes from the Torah, Leviticus 19:18, and Rabbi Akiva called it a great principle of the Torah.

### What did the Rebbe say about Ahavat Yisrael?
The Rebbe made it one of his mitzvah campaigns, calling every Jew to love and help every other Jew.
