In Judaism, "the Chosen People" means the Jewish people were chosen for a task, not for special privilege: to bring G-d's light into the world through Torah and mitzvot. Chabad.org teaches that being chosen is a responsibility, and it asks more of us, not less.

## Where the idea comes from

Just before the giving of the Torah at Mount Sinai, G-d told Moses to tell the people: "You shall be to Me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation" (Exodus 19:6). The Torah also calls the Jewish people an "am segulah" (עם סגולה), a "treasured people." The question is always: chosen for what? See also [Moses](/learn/moses) and the [Ten Commandments](/learn/ten-commandments).

## A kingdom of priests: a job, not a rank

Chabad.org explains that "priests" here does not mean the Kohanim, since not every Jew descends from Aaron. It means a priestly role: bringing G-d into the world and bringing the world closer to G-d. Every Jew, woman or man, has this job. The prophet Isaiah calls it being "a light to the nations." You become a good ambassador by living the Torah and mitzvot yourself, because the way you live is what other people see.

## It does not make anyone better than anyone else

The Jewish idea of being chosen is about responsibility. A person given a mission has more to do, not more rights. The Torah also teaches that all people are created in G-d's image, and that G-d has a path for everyone: the seven Noahide laws, for all of humanity (see [Noahide laws](/learn/noahide-laws)). Righteous people of every nation have a share in the World to Come. So a Jew's task is not to rule others but to inspire, to set an example and to help the world become a better place, a home for G-d.

## The soul behind it

Chassidic teaching, following the [Tanya](/learn/tanya), says every Jew has a G-dly soul, a spark of G-d, regardless of background or level of observance. This is why chosenness is not earned by being religious and cannot be lost by forgetting. It is who we are, which is also why a Jew who feels far from Jewish life is still a full member of the mission. Read more in [Who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew) and [Why be Jewish](/learn/why-be-jewish).

## Small, but with great power

Many people think, "Who am I to change the world?" Chabad.org answers with a thought from our own age: if a tiny bit of matter can release enormous energy, then the power in each person, used for good, is greater still. Every Jew has special abilities and opportunities, and one mitzvah can change a room. A Jew far from other Jews still represents the whole people. The Jewish people do not succeed "by might nor by power but by My spirit" (Zechariah 4:6): our strength is spiritual.

## What this means for a Jew today

You do not need to be perfect. You can begin with one mitzvah: light Shabbat candles, put up a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah), give charity, say hello to the new neighbor. For a Jew in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda, being "chosen" can mean being a good neighbor, a good friend and a proud Jew, so that those who meet you see something good. If you are not sure where to begin, ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### What does "chosen people" mean in Judaism?
It means the Jewish people were given a mission: to be "a kingdom of priests and a holy nation," bringing G-d's light into the world by living Torah and mitzvot.

### Does being chosen mean Jews are better than others?
No. Chosenness in Judaism is about responsibility and a task, not superiority. The Torah also teaches that all people are made in G-d's image.

### What were the Jewish people chosen to do?
To live by the Torah and mitzvot and, by example, to be a "light to the nations," bringing awareness of G-d to the world.

### Does Judaism say non-Jews must become Jewish?
No. Judaism teaches that non-Jews are guided by the seven Noahide laws, and that righteous people of all nations have a place in the World to Come.

### What is "am segulah"?
Am segulah (עם סגולה) means "a treasured people," a phrase from the Torah describing the Jewish people's closeness to G-d.

### Can a person become part of the Jewish people?
Yes. A sincere convert becomes fully Jewish. Ask the Rabbi in confidence about conversion.
