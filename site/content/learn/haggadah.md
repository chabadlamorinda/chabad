The Haggadah (הַגָּדָה, "telling") is the book we read at the Passover Seder to tell the story of the Exodus from Egypt, in a set order that includes the Four Questions, the four sons, the ten plagues, Dayenu, the blessings over matzah and bitter herbs, and Hallel. It guides the whole night, so the right edition can make the Seder a joy for Jewish families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, whatever their background.

## What is the Haggadah for?

The Torah commands us to tell our children the story of the Exodus on this night: "You shall tell your child on that day." The Haggadah is our script for doing that. Its roots go back to the days of the Mishnah and the Talmud, and its words have been recited by Jews around the world for many centuries. Reading the same words as our ancestors connects us to them.

## What is inside

The Seder has fifteen steps, and the Haggadah follows them. Each has a name, from *Kadesh* (blessing over the first cup) through *Nirtzah* (the conclusion). Learn more in [the Passover Seder](/learn/passover-seder). The heart is Maggid (מַגִּיד, "telling"), the long section where we tell the story. Look for:

- **"This is the bread of affliction"** (Ha Lachma Anya), an invitation to all who are hungry.
- **The Four Questions** asked by the youngest at the table; see [the Four Questions](/learn/four-questions).
- **The four sons**, the wise, the wicked, the simple and the one who does not know how to ask: each gets the answer that suits them.
- **The ten plagues**, with a drop of wine removed for each, because our joy is not complete when others suffered.
- **Dayenu**, the song of gratitude, "it would have been enough."
- **Rabban Gamliel's three things:** Pesach, matzah and maror, with their meaning. See [the Seder plate](/learn/seder-plate).
- **Hallel**, songs of praise, with the [four cups of wine](/learn/four-cups).

## Choosing a Haggadah

You do not need to buy an expensive one. Ask yourself:

1. **Does it have the Hebrew text?** It is the original. A transliteration helps if you do not read Hebrew.
2. **Does it have a translation you understand?** English editions are common. Editions with Hebrew and Russian translation also exist; ask the Rabbi for suggestions.
3. **Does it explain what to do?** Step-by-step instructions help first-time hosts.
4. **Does it have commentary and stories?** Chassidic commentary adds depth for the adults at the table.
5. **Is there something for children?** Illustrated editions and activity books keep them involved. See [Passover with kids](/learn/passover-with-kids).

Give every guest a copy if you can, so everyone can follow and read a paragraph.

## How to read it at the Seder

You do not need to read every word. The sages say that the more we tell about the Exodus, the more praiseworthy it is, but a lively Seder matters more than a long one.

- Read aloud, taking turns around the table; each person reads a section in Hebrew or English.
- Stop to talk. Ask the children what they think; welcome their questions.
- Keep the essentials: the Four Questions, the story, Pesach, matzah and maror, the four cups, Hallel.
- Prepare ahead: skim it once before the night, decide who reads what, and mark the places for songs.
- With guests who are new to the Seder, say a sentence before each step about what it means.

## Common questions

### What is a Haggadah?
A Haggadah is the book read at the Passover Seder, containing the story of the Exodus, the blessings, songs and the order of the evening. The word means "telling."

### What is in the Haggadah?
It includes the fifteen steps of the Seder, the Four Questions, the four sons, the plagues, Dayenu, the blessings over the matzah and bitter herbs, and Hallel.

### Which Haggadah is best for beginners?
One with the Hebrew text, a clear translation and instructions for each step. Ask the Rabbi to suggest one that suits your family.

### Is there a Haggadah in English or Russian?
Yes. Haggadahs with English translation are widely available, and there are editions in Russian. Ask the Rabbi for suggestions.

### Do I have to read the whole Haggadah?
The essentials must be said, but you may shorten what is not required. Ask the Rabbi which parts are essential for your family.
