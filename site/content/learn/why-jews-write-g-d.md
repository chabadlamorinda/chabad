Jews write "G-d" with a dash so that G-d's name is never erased, thrown away or treated disrespectfully if the page is discarded. The practice comes from the Torah's command not to erase G-d's name, and it is a sign of respect, not superstition.

## The short answer

When we write G-d's full name on paper, that paper has a certain holiness. Newspapers, flyers and printed pages end up in the trash, in recycling and on the floor. Writing "G-d" with a dash avoids that problem, since the dash form is not the holy name itself.

## The source: not erasing G-d's name

The Torah (Deuteronomy 12:3-4) tells the Jewish people to destroy idolatry in the Land of Israel, and then says not to do the same to G-d: that is, not to erase or destroy His name. The Talmud (Shavuot 35a) learns from here a prohibition on erasing G-d's name. In Jewish tradition, this applies to holy names that are written in the Hebrew language.

## English or Hebrew?

According to Chabad.org, the prohibition clearly applies to the Hebrew names of G-d. For other languages such as English, opinions differ. Some authorities apply the prohibition to every language. Others limit it to Hebrew, but even they advise avoiding erasing the name in other languages when possible. Everyone agrees that the name of G-d in any language must be treated respectfully and not defaced or used in disrespectful places.

So the dash is a precaution, and a good habit.

## Printed pages and screens

Religious Jewish newspapers in pre-Holocaust Europe began writing G-d's name with a hyphen so that readers would not worry about what happens to a paper after it is read. Today the habit covers articles, magazines and newspapers in Yiddish, English and other languages. Holy books such as a Bible or prayer book usually write the name in full, because they are expected to be treated with respect. Chabad.org itself still uses the hyphen, partly because people print articles to read on Shabbat.

What about a computer screen? Chabad.org notes that most halachic authorities do not consider a screen and its contents to have sanctity: typed text is not permanent, and pixels keep changing. So deleting G-d's name on a screen is permitted. Even so, writing "G-d" with a dash when you type is recommended as a good habit, since text can be printed. For your own case, ask the Rabbi.

## What to do with papers that carry G-d's name

If you have a worn prayer book, a Torah study sheet or other pages with G-d's name, do not throw them in the trash. Jewish custom is to place them in a storage place called a genizah (גְּנִיזָה) for respectful burial. See our guide on [genizah](/learn/genizah). Call Chabad of Lamorinda in Lafayette and ask how to give it.

## Why we say "Hashem"

In everyday speech, Jews say "Hashem" (הַשֵּׁם), which means "the Name," instead of using G-d's proper names. Chabad.org explains that "Hashem" is a placeholder rather than a holy name, so it may be erased. It also explains that G-d's titles are not said aloud without good reason, such as in prayer or while saying a blessing, and that using them casually, for instance in "Oh my G-d!", is considered disrespectful and highly discouraged. In writing, many also write "G-d" and "L-rd." For more, see [the names of G-d](/learn/shemot-names-of-g-d).

## Common questions

### Why do Jews write "G-d" instead of "God"?
To avoid ever writing out G-d's name in full where it might be erased or discarded. The practice reflects the Torah's command not to erase G-d's name and respect for it.

### Is it forbidden to write the word "God"?
No. Writing it is not forbidden, and holy books often write it in full. Writing "G-d" is a precaution for pages that may be thrown away.

### Can I erase G-d's name on a computer?
Chabad.org says most authorities treat a screen as having no holiness, so you may delete it there. Typing "G-d" with a dash is still a recommended habit.

### What does "Hashem" mean?
Hashem means "the Name." It is what Jews say in daily speech in place of G-d's holy names.

### What should I do with an old prayer book or a paper with G-d's name?
Do not discard it. Put it aside for genizah. Chabad of Lamorinda in Lafayette can help.

### Can I say "Oh my G-d" in conversation?
Chabad.org says that using G-d's names in a casual way, such as "Oh my G-d," is considered disrespectful and highly discouraged.
