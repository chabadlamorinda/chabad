You can raise confident Jewish children even when they are the only Jewish kid in their class, by making Judaism warm and joyful at home, giving them Jewish friends, and letting them take pride in who they are. Children who feel Jewish at the family table, with other Jewish children, carry that pride with them anywhere.

## Start at home: make it warm

Chabad teaching holds that a Jewish home is the child's first Jewish school. Chabad.org's Torah commentary on Jacob raising his family in the household of Laban teaches that we must raise Jewish children wherever we are, even in an environment far from Jewish life. Small, steady things matter more than big ones:

- **Shabbat.** Light candles and share a Friday night meal, even a simple one (see [Shabbat with kids](/learn/shabbat-with-kids)).
- **A blessing for each child** on Friday night, which tells them they are loved.
- **Jewish stories and songs** in the car, at bedtime or at dinner (see our [kids' corner](/kids)).
- **A mezuzah and a Jewish bookshelf,** so Jewish life is visible.
- **Hebrew names.** Tell the story of whom your child is named for (see [Hebrew names](/learn/hebrew-names)).

## Feeling proud, not different

When a child is the only one, "different" can feel lonely. Help them see it as special.

1. **Name it with joy.** "We are Jewish, and here is a wonderful thing we do."
2. **Prepare simple answers** for classmates: "We light candles on Friday night," "We eat matzah in the spring." Practice it together.
3. **Invite classmates in.** A challah bake or a Chanukah party shares the joy, and friends often ask to come back.
4. **Celebrate your own holidays richly.** In December, bring the full joy of [Chanukah](/holidays/chanukah) home without comparing it with anyone else's holiday; Chanukah is a story of its own.
5. **Read together** about [Jewish pride](/learn/jewish-pride).

## The grandparents' role

Grandparents hold stories no one else can tell. Ask them to share how they celebrated, to call on Friday, or to light candles by video. Children love hearing "this is how Bubbe did it."

## For parents who feel unsure

You do not need to know it all to begin. Many parents in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda learn alongside their children. Take one step, and ask for help when you need it. For teenagers, see [Jewish identity for teens](/learn/jewish-identity-for-teens). If a classmate says something hurtful, speak with the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### How do I raise Jewish kids in a non-Jewish area?
Make Shabbat and holidays warm at home, build Jewish friendships through a program, and help your child feel proud rather than different.

### What if my child is the only Jewish kid in class?
Prepare simple, cheerful answers to classmates' questions, invite friends to a Jewish celebration, and connect your child with other Jewish children.

### Where can my child meet other Jewish children in Lamorinda?
Chabad of Lamorinda in Lafayette offers a Hebrew School, a Jewish Preschool, afterschool and summer camp. Call 925-384-7242 to hear what is planned.

### How can I help my child feel proud in December?
Celebrate Chanukah at home with joy, and explain what it means to us, without comparisons. See [Chanukah](/holidays/chanukah).

### Do we have to belong to a synagogue to join?
No. Hebrew School is open to all, with no membership required.

### Can grandparents help with Jewish identity?
Yes, greatly. Their stories, calls and blessings give children a connection that lasts.
