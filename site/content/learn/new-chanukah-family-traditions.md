The family traditions children remember are the small ones that return every year: a song at the menorah, a special food, a guest at the table. To start a new Chanukah tradition, pick one simple idea that connects to a mitzvah, do it on the first night and keep doing it every year until it becomes "what we do."

## Begin with the heart of Chanukah

Every family tradition on Chanukah works best when it supports the central mitzvah: lighting the menorah and sharing its light. Chabad.org teaches that the eight nights are for adding light, and its Chanukah companion gives each night a thought, a story, a craft or activity and a lighting calendar. That is a good model for a family: one idea a night, kept simple.

Make sure the foundation is in place. See [how to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah) and [the eight nights of Chanukah](/learn/chanukah-night-by-night), which has a theme for each night. This guide is about the extras that make your family's holiday your own.

## Eight traditions to try

Chabad.org lists several old and new traditions. Here they are, with ways to make them yours.

1. **A family song.** After lighting, many sing Haneirot Halalu ("These lights we kindle") and Maoz Tzur, and lighter songs about the dreidel. Pick one song your family always sings. See [Chanukah songs](/learn/chanukah-songs).
2. **Gelt with a purpose.** Gelt rewards learning and teaches giving. Let each child set aside part for tzedakah. See [Chanukah gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt).
3. **A dreidel tournament.** A nightly game, with a trophy that passes from year to year. See [dreidel](/learn/dreidel) and the [dreidel game](/dreidel) online.
4. **A public lighting.** Chabad.org notes that large public menorah lightings, with music, treats and local leaders, have been held in many cities since the 1970s. Make it your yearly outing. It does not replace lighting at home.
5. **A relaxed time for women.** Chabad tradition is that women refrain from housework while the candles burn, so use that half hour for stories, singing and being together.
6. **Share the light.** Take a menorah, treats and cards to someone who may be alone. See [Chanukah mitzvah projects for kids](/learn/chanukah-mitzvah-projects-for-kids).
7. **A dairy party.** A festive meal is a nice mitzvah, and many serve dairy foods. See [hosting a Chanukah party](/learn/hosting-a-chanukah-party).
8. **Fried foods.** Latkes and sufganiyot remind us of the oil. Let the kids make them. See [Chanukah foods](/learn/chanukah-foods).

## More ideas that build memories

- **Everyone lights their own.** Many families give each child a menorah. Ask the Rabbi about your family's custom; see [Chanukah for kids](/learn/chanukah-for-kids).
- **A story each night.** Take turns reading or telling one, perhaps a family story or one from our [Chanukah bedtime stories](/learn/chanukah-bedtime-stories).
- **A guest each night or two.** A neighbor, friend or someone alone. The menorah is meant to be shared.
- **A window photo.** Take a picture of your menorah each year, lined up on a shelf to show how the children have grown.
- **A blessing for each child.** Put a hand on each child's head and bless them. See [blessing your children](/learn/blessing-your-children).
- **A family tzedakah box.** One box, filled across the eight nights, and the family chooses together where it goes.

## How to make a tradition stick

1. **Start small.** One thing is better than eight.
2. **Do it every year,** even if it is simple. Repetition makes it a tradition.
3. **Give everyone a job.** One child lights the shamash, one chooses the song, one sets the table.
4. **Tell the "why."** A tradition is more meaningful when children know what it means.
5. **Keep it joyful.** Skip the pressure. If it becomes a chore, change it.
6. **Write it down.** A short family note: "What we do on Chanukah." Add a line every year.

## Common questions

### How do we start a new Chanukah tradition?
Choose one simple, joyful idea, such as a special song, a story each night or a guest at the table, and repeat it every year. Keep the menorah lighting at the center.

### What are some Chanukah traditions for families?
Lighting together, singing, dreidel, gelt, fried foods, a dairy meal, public menorah lightings and sharing light with others are the classics.

### Should we give a gift every night?
There is no requirement. Gelt is the traditional gift, and many families give small gifts or experiences. See [Chanukah gifts](/learn/chanukah-gifts).

### How can we make Chanukah meaningful and not just fun?
Explain the reason behind each custom, add a small mitzvah such as tzedakah, and spend the half hour by the lights together, not on screens.

### What if our family has different customs or is mixed?
Choose the traditions that fit your home and ask the Rabbi about your questions. Every Jew is welcome at Chabad of Lamorinda.
