Judaism uses several Hebrew names for G-d, each describing a different way He relates to the world. Because these names are holy, Jewish law forbids erasing or destroying them, which is why many Jews write "G-d" in English and why old prayer books are buried, not thrown away.

## The main names of G-d

- **The Four Letter Name (Havayah).** Spelled yud, hei, vav, hei, it is the holiest name, expressing G-d as infinite and beyond time: He was, is and will be all at once. It is never pronounced as written. In prayer it is read Ado-nai, and in conversation people say Hashem.
- **Ado-nai** means "my Master." It expresses G-d as ruler of the world.
- **Elo-him** expresses G-d as Creator and Judge, the power that sets the limits of nature. Chassidic teaching notes that Elo-him has the same numerical value as hateva, "nature": G-d hides within nature.
- **Sha-dai**, written on the back of the [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah), means "the One who said to His world, enough." It is also read as an acronym for "Guardian of the doors of Israel."
- **Ehe-yeh Asher Ehe-yeh**, "I will be what I will be," the name G-d told Moses at the burning bush.

Other titles include Hakadosh Baruch Hu (the Holy One, blessed be He), Ribono shel Olam (Master of the world), Hamakom (the Place) and Shalom (Peace).

## Why we say Hashem

Hashem simply means "the Name." Out of respect, people do not pronounce the holy names outside of prayer or Torah study. Instead they say Hashem, or change a letter, as in Elokim or Elokeinu. That is why you will see these spellings in our guides and in many siddurim.

## Why write G-d with a dash?

The Torah commands us not to erase G-d's name. Strictly, this applies to the Hebrew names written in holy script. Many authorities hold that the English word for G-d, written out in full, is not a holy name in that sense. Still, the widespread custom, including in Chabad, is to write G-d, out of respect and so the word is not discarded or deleted carelessly, especially on paper that may be thrown away. This guide follows that custom.

## Treating holy pages with respect

- **Do not throw away** a siddur, Chumash, Tehillim or any page with a Hebrew name of G-d. Worn holy books are placed in a genizah and buried.
- **Do not bring** pages with G-d's name into a bathroom.
- **Screens:** deleting a name from a computer screen or turning it off is not considered erasing, but printing it creates a page that needs care.
- **Newsletters and invitations** often avoid printing G-d's name for this reason, writing "B"H" (with G-d's help) at the top instead.

## Common questions

### Is it wrong to spell out G-d in full in English?
Many authorities say the English word is not a holy name, so it is not forbidden. The custom of writing G-d is a sign of respect and helps keep holy words from ending up in the trash.

### What do I do with old siddurim and Jewish books?
Do not throw them away. They are buried in a genizah. Contact us at 925-384-7242 and we will make sure they are handled with respect.

### Why do people say Hashem instead of G-d's name?
Hashem means "the Name." It lets us speak about G-d in everyday conversation without using His holy names casually.

### Can I say G-d's name when I say a blessing?
Yes. In blessings and prayer, the names are said in full as written in the siddur. Outside of prayer, the custom is to say Hashem or Elokim.
