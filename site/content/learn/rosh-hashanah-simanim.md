Simanim (signs) are symbolic foods eaten at the Rosh Hashanah evening meal, each paired with a short prayer for a good year. The best known is a slice of apple dipped in honey, with a prayer for "a good and sweet year."

## What the simanim are

The custom goes back to the Talmud, where the sage Abaye taught that at the start of the year a person should see or eat foods that grow quickly or taste sweet, as a good sign. Over the centuries, Jewish communities added foods whose names in Hebrew or Aramaic sound like a blessing.

The simanim are not magic. They are a way of turning the first meal of the year into a prayer, reminding ourselves of what we hope for and who we are asking.

## Why we eat them

Rosh Hashanah is the Day of Judgment, when G-d decides the coming year for every person. Jewish tradition teaches that the way we begin something shapes what follows. So we start the year with sweetness, optimism and trust that G-d will grant a good year.

## The apple and honey, step by step

On the first night of Rosh Hashanah, after Kiddush and the blessing on the challah:

1. **Dip the challah in honey** instead of salt. Many keep this custom from Rosh Hashanah through Hoshana Rabbah.
2. **Take a slice of sweet apple** and dip it in honey.
3. **Say the blessing on the fruit:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, borei pri ha'etz. ("Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who creates the fruit of the tree.")
4. **Then say the prayer:** Yehi ratzon milfanecha shetechadesh aleinu shanah tovah umetukah. ("May it be Your will to renew for us a good and sweet year.")
5. **Eat the apple.**

## Other common simanim

Many families add more foods, each with its own short "Yehi ratzon" prayer, which is printed in most Rosh Hashanah prayer books:

- **Pomegranate:** that our merits be as many as its seeds.
- **Head of a fish or a ram:** that we be "a head and not a tail," leaders and not followers. A ram's head also recalls the binding of Isaac.
- **Carrots:** in Yiddish, "meren" means both carrots and "to increase," so many serve sweet carrot tzimmes.
- **Dates, beets, leeks and gourd:** their Aramaic names sound like prayers that our enemies be removed and that any harsh decree be torn up.
- **Fish:** a sign that we multiply like fish.

Customs vary from family to family. Use what your family has always used, or start with apple and honey and add from there.

## Foods many avoid

Some have the custom not to eat nuts on Rosh Hashanah, since the Hebrew word for nut, egoz, has the same numerical value as the word for sin, chet. Many also avoid very sour or bitter foods at the holiday meals.

## Common questions

### Do we eat apple and honey on both nights?
The blessing with apple and honey is a custom of the first night. Many still dip challah in honey at every holiday meal through Hoshana Rabbah. A new fruit with Shehecheyanu is eaten on the second night; see our guide to [Shehecheyanu](/learn/shehecheyanu).

### Why do we use round challah?
A round challah reflects the cycle of the year and the hope for a full, unbroken year of blessing. Read more in our [challah](/learn/challah) guide.

### Can I do the simanim if I eat alone or with guests who are new to this?
Absolutely. The simanim are simple and a wonderful way to involve guests and children. Print the short prayers and let everyone take a turn.

### What if Rosh Hashanah starts on Friday night?
The simanim are eaten as usual. Only the order of Kiddush changes for Shabbat. Ask the Rabbi if you have questions about your table.
