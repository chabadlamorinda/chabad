Chai (חַי) is the Hebrew word for "alive" or "life," written with two letters, chet and yud. Because those letters add up to 18, Jews give gifts and tzedakah in multiples of 18 as a blessing for life.

## What chai means

Chai is pronounced with a throaty "ch" sound, like "Bach," so it rhymes with "hi." It means living, alive, or life itself. The Torah tells us to "choose life" (Deuteronomy 30:19), and life is one of the highest values in Judaism. Saving a life, called pikuach nefesh, overrides almost every other mitzvah.

The chai symbol is simply the word written in Hebrew letters, often worn as a necklace or shown on art and jewelry.

## Why chai equals 18

Every [Hebrew letter](/learn/hebrew-alphabet) also has a number value. This system is called gematria.

- **Chet (ח)** = 8
- **Yud (י)** = 10
- **Together** = 18

So the number 18 has come to stand for "life," and multiples of 18 carry the same good wish.

## Giving in multiples of 18

It is a widespread Jewish custom to give gifts and [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) in amounts like 18, 36, 180 or 1,800.

- **Gifts.** At a bar or bat mitzvah, wedding or birth, a gift of "double chai" (36) or "ten chai" (180) is a way of saying "to life!"
- **Tzedakah.** Giving in multiples of 18 adds a blessing of life to the mitzvah.
- **Thirty-six.** Double chai also recalls the Jewish tradition that the world rests on the merit of 36 hidden righteous people.

## L'chaim: "to life"

When Jews raise a glass, they say [L'chaim](/learn/hebrew-phrases), "to life." The custom is heard at every Jewish celebration, from a bris to a wedding. A Chassidic gathering, called a farbrengen, is full of sincere L'chaims, each one followed by a blessing for a friend.

## Life with meaning

Judaism does not just value being alive. It values a life of purpose. In our prayers the Torah is called a "Torah of life," and the Torah says of the mitzvot that we should "live by them" (Leviticus 18:5). The chai symbol is a reminder that every day of life is a gift to fill with good deeds, learning and kindness.

## Common questions

### Is chai a religious object?
No. Like the [Star of David](/learn/star-of-david), it is a meaningful symbol, not a mitzvah item. Wearing it is a nice expression of Jewish identity and of gratitude for life.

### Why do Jews say "18" instead of "chai" when giving money?
They mean the same thing. Someone might give "$18" or "$36" or say "I gave double chai." Either way, the amount carries a blessing for life.

### Is there a special meaning to the 18th of a Hebrew month?
Yes, in Chabad especially. The 18th of Elul, called Chai Elul, is the birthday of the Baal Shem Tov, founder of the Chassidic movement, and of the Alter Rebbe, founder of Chabad. It is seen as a day that brings new life into our service of G-d.

### Is 18 a lucky number?
Judaism does not believe in luck the way some cultures do. Eighteen is not magic; it is a reminder of life and a way of adding a blessing to a gift.
