Chabad.org says that, ideally, a dishwasher is used only for meat or only for dairy. A kitchen with one sink can still be kosher: treat the sink itself as non-kosher, never put dishes or food directly into it, and wash meat and dairy in separate dishpans set on small racks.

## Why the dishwasher and sink matter

Hot water and soap carry the taste of food. In a kosher kitchen, meat and milk must stay apart (see [meat and milk in Jewish law](/learn/meat-and-milk)), so we ask: what touches the dishes next? A dishwasher and a sink are shared by every dish in the house, so each needs a plan.

## The dishwasher

Chabad.org says that the ideal is to designate one dishwasher for meat or for dairy only, and not to use it for both, even if they are never run at the same time. Some kitchens have two dishwashers.

Many factors are involved, so Chabad.org advises asking your rabbi for specific questions. If you have one dishwasher, do not start using it for both on your own. Call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 first.

## The sink: one sink or two

According to Chabad.org:

- **Two sinks** are best. If they sit side by side, there must be an effective barrier so water and food cannot splash from one to the other. A kitchen may have one sink for meat and one for dairy.
- **One sink** may be used once it is thoroughly cleaned, but its interior is treated as non-kosher. Do not put food or dishes directly into it. Use separate meat and dairy dishpans, set on slightly raised racks.
- If a sink serves both meat and dairy, do not place dishes on the sink's surface while washing them.
- A metal sink can be koshered by pouring boiling water over the entire sink, with the water boiling right before it is poured. A porcelain sink cannot be koshered.

A sink used before the kitchen became kosher is considered non-kosher unless it is stainless steel and has been koshered. Ask the Rabbi to help you decide.

## Sponges, racks and towels

A kosher kitchen has two sets of everything that touches food (see [how to make your kitchen kosher](/learn/kosher-kitchen)). That includes separate dish racks and basins for meat and dairy. The page on [pareve foods](/learn/pareve) explains the in-between category.

## If you make a mistake

Put the item aside, do not use it, and ask the Rabbi. Chabad.org's advice for a kosher kitchen is that when in doubt, ask.

## Common questions

### Can I use one dishwasher for meat and dairy?
Ideally, no. Chabad.org says to designate a dishwasher for meat or dairy only. If you have only one, ask the Rabbi what to do before using it for both.

### Can I have a kosher kitchen with just one sink?
Yes. Treat the sink as non-kosher, never put dishes or food directly in it, and use separate dishpans on small racks for meat and dairy.

### Can I kasher my sink?
A metal sink can be koshered by pouring boiling water over all of it, with the water boiling just before pouring. A porcelain sink cannot be koshered. Ask the Rabbi about yours.

### What if hot meat and dairy dishes touch in the sink?
Set the dishes aside and ask the Rabbi. Do not use them in the meantime. The answer depends on the facts.

### Do I need separate dishwashers?
Some kitchens have two. If you have one, Chabad.org says to designate it for meat or dairy. Ask the Rabbi what suits your kitchen.

### Do new dishes need anything before they go into the sink?
New metal and glass dishes usually need immersion in a mikvah before first use. See [immersing new dishes](/learn/tevilat-kelim).
