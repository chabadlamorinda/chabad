Bashert (בַּשֶׁרְט) is a Yiddish word meaning "destined" or "meant to be," and it is most often used for the person one is destined to marry. According to the Talmud, a heavenly voice announces forty days before a child is formed whom that child will marry.

At the same time, Chabad.org teaches that matches are made both in heaven and on earth: G-d designates, and our choices, character and effort matter greatly.

## What does bashert mean?

Chabad.org explains that anything can be called bashert, since everything is orchestrated by G-d, who has our best interests at heart. But the word is used mostly for a spouse. A fiancé is called a *basherter* and a fiancée a *basherte*.

The idea of a destined match goes back to the Talmud and Midrash. It gives comfort: there is someone out there, and the search has meaning.

## Destiny and free choice

If a match is destined, why do we need to search at all? And what if someone makes a wrong choice? Chabad.org says that Jewish sources offer a range of views. One Midrash says a person's mate comes from G-d. Another says G-d "sits and makes matches." The Talmud also speaks of matches according to merit. Teachers explain this in different ways: perhaps the first match is decreed and the second is earned by merit, or perhaps prayer and good deeds can change what was decreed.

The overall message, as Chabad.org presents it, is that G-d designates but human choices can improve or worsen the outcome. This fits the Jewish teaching of [free will](/learn/free-will): we are partners with G-d. Make a real effort (the Hebrew word is *hishtadlut*), and trust.

## "Is my date my soulmate?"

Chabad.org gives a practical answer: don't try to answer it. You cannot see another person's soul, so you cannot test for a soulmate. The article says that the soulmate idea can be useful at other stages of life, but it can be a distraction while dating.

Instead, look at the real person:

- Are they a good person, with good character?
- Do you share values and beliefs, including being Jewish if that matters to you?
- Do you communicate well?
- Are you heading in the same direction, wanting similar things from life?

Chabad.org says to leave aside birthdays, astrology and similar signs. In its words, "Stop looking for signs from heaven. The signs are here on earth." When you have found a real connection, leave the connection of your souls to G-d.

## When the search is long

Waiting is hard. Chabad.org points to Jacob, who searched and labored for twenty years before building his family. Perseverance, prayer and working on yourself are all ways to take part. Being a better person, and praying, are described as part of the matchmaking process. See [being single, a Jewish view](/learn/being-single-jewish-view) for encouragement, and [Jewish dating](/learn/jewish-dating) for practical advice.

## If you have been married before

The question of second marriages is also discussed in the sources: one view is that a second match is earned by merit. Nobody is "too late." Everyone is welcome to grow, learn and find love again. If you have a personal question, please ask the Rabbi.

## Building love after the match

Finding a partner is the beginning. A Jewish marriage is built by [love](/learn/love-jewish-view), kindness, and daily effort. Whether you call your spouse bashert from the first meeting or only years later, you make it so by what you give.

## Common questions

### What does bashert mean?
Bashert means "destined" or "meant to be" in Yiddish, and it usually refers to one's destined spouse. Chabad.org adds that anything that happens is part of G-d's plan.

### Is there one soulmate for everyone?
The Talmud teaches that a heavenly announcement names a person's match forty days before conception. Jewish sources also discuss matches by merit and the power of prayer, so teachers explain this in different ways. Chabad.org says matches are made in heaven and on earth.

### What is the 40 days before birth teaching?
The Talmud says that forty days before a child is formed, a heavenly voice declares whose daughter or son this child will marry. It is a source of the idea of bashert.

### How do I know if my date is my soulmate?
You cannot know directly, says Chabad.org. Look at their character, shared values, communication and shared direction, and leave the soul connection to G-d.

### Can I still find my bashert if I am older or divorced?
Yes. Jewish tradition encourages people to keep searching, praying and growing, whatever their age or past. Ask the Rabbi if you would like personal support.
