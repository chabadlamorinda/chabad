When the clocks go back in November, Shabbat suddenly comes early. In the Bay Area, candle lighting on a December Friday can be as early as about 4:30 PM. With a little planning, short Fridays can be calm and the long Friday nights become one of winter's best gifts.

## Plan the week, not just Friday

- **Check the time early in the week.** Candle lighting is 18 minutes before sunset and changes each week. See [this week's times](/shabbat).
- **Shop on Wednesday or Thursday.** Leave Friday for finishing touches.
- **Cook on Thursday night.** Soups, kugels, chicken and cholent all keep well. Many families make challah dough on Thursday.
- **Set the table and the candles** before Friday afternoon gets busy.

## Leaving work early

Many people working full-time explain to their employer, at the start of the season, that on winter Fridays they need to leave early for Shabbat. Most employers are understanding, especially when you offer to make up the hours another day. Plan meetings and deadlines for earlier in the day.

## Keeping food warm

Cooking is not done on Shabbat, so food that will be served hot is fully cooked before Shabbat begins. Common ways to keep it warm:

- **A hot plate (plata)** that is plugged in before Shabbat and left on.
- **A slow cooker (crockpot)** for cholent, set up before Shabbat.
- **A blech**, a metal sheet covering the stovetop flames or burners, left on through Shabbat.

There are important details about what can be put on or moved, and how. Read our [Shabbat practical questions](/learn/shabbat-practical-questions) guide and ask the Rabbi to walk you through it once.

## With children

- **Bath and pajamas before candle lighting.** Children are ready to enjoy the meal.
- **An early, relaxed dinner.** Friday night meals in winter can begin soon after Kabbalat Shabbat, with time for songs, stories and games afterwards.
- **Long Friday nights** are perfect for board games, reading together and visiting friends who live nearby.

## Shabbat ends early too

On Saturday, Shabbat ends at nightfall, around 5:30 PM in midwinter. Many families invite friends for a Saturday afternoon meal, followed by Havdalah together.

## Common questions

### What time does Shabbat start in winter in Lafayette?
It changes every week. In midwinter candle lighting is around 4:30 PM; the exact time for each Friday is on our [Shabbat page](/shabbat) and [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times).

### I can't get home before candle lighting. What should I do?
Plan for it ahead of time, and ask the Rabbi for advice for your situation. The goal is to stop work and light candles before sunset.

### Can I use a timer for the lights?
Yes. Setting a timer before Shabbat to turn lights on and off is common and permitted; it is set up before Shabbat and not changed on Shabbat.

### Can I come to Kabbalat Shabbat at Chabad in winter?
Yes. Kabbalat Shabbat follows the season and starts close to candle lighting in the winter. This week's time is on the [Shabbat page](/shabbat).
