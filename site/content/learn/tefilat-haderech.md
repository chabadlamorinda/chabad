Tefilat HaDerech, the Traveler's Prayer, is a short prayer asking G-d for a safe and peaceful journey. It is said once you have left your city on a trip of meaningful distance, whether by car, plane, train or boat.

## What the prayer says

Tefilat HaDerech (תְּפִלַּת הַדֶּרֶךְ) comes from the Talmud. In it we ask G-d to:

- Lead us in peace and bring us safely to our destination "for life, joy and peace."
- Save us from every enemy, danger and harm along the way.
- Bless the work of our hands.
- Grant us grace, kindness and compassion in His eyes and in the eyes of all we meet.
- Bring us home again in peace.

It ends with the blessing: **"Baruch atah Ado-nai, shome'a tefilah,"** "Blessed are You, G-d, who hears prayer."

The prayer is phrased in the plural ("lead us," "bring us"), so that we pray for all travelers together with ourselves. It is printed in every [siddur](/learn/siddur) and on small cards that many people keep in the car or in a carry-on bag.

## When to say it

1. **Start your trip.** Wait until you have left the built-up area of your city.
2. **Check the distance.** The full prayer with its closing blessing is said for a journey of at least about a parsah, roughly three miles, beyond the city. For shorter trips, some say it without the closing words.
3. **Say it once a day.** Say it once on each day of travel. If you stop overnight and continue the next day, say it again in the morning.
4. **On a plane,** say it after takeoff, once the plane is in the air.
5. **On a long drive,** if you forget at the start, you may still say it as long as you have a good distance left to go.

Since rules about distance and timing have details, ask the Rabbi if you are unsure about a specific trip.

## How to say it

- Stand if you can do so safely, for example when walking or on a train. If you are driving or seated on a plane, you may say it sitting.
- Many have the custom to say it together with another blessing, such as the one after using the restroom, so it closes a blessing properly.
- Say it with feeling. You are asking G-d to watch over you and everyone on the road.

## Tzedakah for the road

A beautiful Jewish custom is to give someone money before a trip to donate as [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) at the destination. The traveler becomes a "shliach mitzvah," a messenger for a mitzvah, and the Sages teach that a person on a mitzvah mission is protected from harm. The Rebbe often gave travelers a coin or a dollar for this purpose. You can also put a coin in a pushka before leaving home.

## Other travel customs

- Make sure the mezuzah on your door is kosher before a long trip.
- Bring a [siddur](/learn/siddur) or a small book of [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim).
- Many say Psalm 91 for protection.

## Common questions

### Do I say Tefilat HaDerech on my daily commute?
Usually not. A regular commute inside your area or a short drive does not call for the full prayer. If the trip is longer than about three miles past the city, ask the Rabbi about your route.

### Can I say it in English?
Yes. Prayer may be said in any language you understand. Saying it in Hebrew is ideal if you can.

### What if I am the driver?
Keep your eyes on the road. You may say it from memory if you know it, or ask a passenger to say it aloud while you listen and answer "Amen."

### Do I say it again on the way home?
Yes. The return trip is a new journey, so say it again once you have left the city on your way back.
