Amen (אָמֵן) is a Hebrew word that means "true" or "so be it," and Jews say it in response to a blessing or prayer they hear, to affirm it and make it their own. It shares a root with emunah (אמונה), faith, so saying Amen is a quiet declaration: "I believe, and I agree."

## What Amen means

The Talmud (Shavuot 36a), as Chabad.org explains, gives three intentions in the word: an oath, accepting a statement, and confirming that it is faithful and true. After a blessing of praise, Amen says, "That is true." After a request, Amen says, "May it be affirmed and speedily fulfilled." Ideally, we have both thoughts in mind together.

The letters of Amen share a root with emunah, belief or trust. Saying it is an act of faith, in one word.

## Amen as a hidden prayer

Amen is also read as an acronym: El Melech Ne'eman (אל מלך נאמן), "G-d, faithful King." The Talmud (Shabbat 119b) teaches that one should draw out the word for as long as it takes to say those three words. That is why a good Amen is not rushed.

## When do we say Amen?

You answer Amen after hearing a blessing or prayer said by someone else, including requests addressed to G-d. A few examples:

- After someone makes a blessing on food, or the blessing over the wine at Kiddush. See [blessings](/learn/blessings).
- After the prayer leader's blessings in the synagogue, including the repetition of the Amidah. This is why the prayer leader repeats the [Amidah](/learn/amidah) aloud with a minyan: so that the congregation can answer Amen.
- After a blessing for a person who is sick, or a request on behalf of someone.
- In the [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish), where the congregation answers "Amen, yehei shmei rabbah," "Amen, may His great Name be blessed."

In ancient courts, a person who answered Amen to an oath was considered to have sworn it himself. That shows how serious the word is.

## Why Amen matters so much

The Talmud (Berachot 53b) teaches that the one who answers Amen is in some ways greater than the one who made the blessing. By listening and answering, you take a full part in someone else's prayer.

Chabad.org, quoting the Talmud, also says that answering Amen with proper concentration opens the gates of Gan Eden and nullifies harsh decrees. Some explain that answering "Amen, yehei shmei rabbah" in Kaddish is connected with nullifying negative decrees. Few words are given such power.

## Teaching children to say Amen

Children can answer Amen long before they can read. Many parents in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda teach it first:

1. Say a blessing slowly at the Shabbat table, and let your child answer Amen.
2. Let children answer Amen after the candle-lighting blessing and after Kiddush.
3. Praise the child warmly: "You made my blessing complete."

Do not worry about perfect pronunciation. A child's Amen is a gift to everyone listening. For more kids' ideas, visit the [kids' corner](/kids).

## Common questions

### What does Amen mean in Hebrew?
Amen means "true" or "so be it." It affirms a blessing or prayer that someone else has said, and it shares a root with emunah, faith.

### Why do Jews say Amen after a blessing?
Saying Amen means "I believe this is true" and joins you to the prayer. The Talmud teaches that the one who answers is in some ways greater than the one who made the blessing.

### What does Amen stand for?
It is read as an acronym for El Melech Ne'eman, "G-d, faithful King." The Talmud teaches drawing out the Amen for as long as it takes to say those words.

### Do you say Amen to your own blessing?
Amen is a response to what you hear from someone else. Whether to say it after your own blessing is a question for the Rabbi, since Chabad.org does not address it.

### What is "Amen, yehei shmei rabbah"?
It is the congregation's answer in the Kaddish: "Amen, may His great Name be blessed." Chabad.org notes that answering it with focus is connected with canceling harsh decrees.

### Can children say Amen?
Yes. Children can learn to answer Amen very young, and it is a lovely way to include them in blessings.
