Giving a little often is, in Jewish teaching, a deeper way to give than a single large gift, because each act of giving shapes the giver's heart. The Alter Rebbe, founder of Chabad, advises dividing one's yearly tzedakah into weekly or monthly payments instead of giving everything at the end of the year.

## The Rambam's thousand coins

The daily Tanya study quotes Maimonides' commentary on the Mishnah, which says that everything is judged according to the multiplicity of action. It gives this example: giving one thousand gold coins in a single gift is praiseworthy. But giving those same thousand coins one at a time builds the quality of generosity much more deeply into the giver's character. The purpose is to refine the soul through repeated action.

A single big gift can be a moment of inspiration. A regular gift is a habit, and a habit becomes who we are.

## The Alter Rebbe: two more reasons

In the Tanya, Rabbi Schneur Zalman teaches that tzedakah pledged for the year should be given in weekly or monthly installments. Until that point, his reason is that giving promptly is a virtue in itself. He then adds two more reasons: each separate act of charity refines the giver's soul, and each act brings about a higher spiritual union above. He also says that giving the same total in many installments is a form of divine service, because it takes continuing effort.

The Rebbe's notes add a caution that spreading out gifts must never delay help to someone who needs it now. If a needy person or a charity needs the money today, promptness comes first.

## Regularity beats size

Two sages are often quoted about the pushka. Maimonides held that regularity matters more than the amount, and the Baal Shem Tov taught not to let a day pass without an act of giving. A few coins every day, a pushka on the wall and an automatic monthly gift all say the same thing: giving is part of who we are, not an occasional event. See [The Pushka](/learn/pushka) and [Giving as a Family](/learn/giving-tzedakah-as-a-family).

Jewish tradition also teaches that how you give matters more than how much. So a monthly gift, given warmly, is complete as it is.

## Maaser: a tenth, month by month

Many people give maaser, a tenth of their income, and it fits well with monthly giving, since income arrives month by month. The Torah invites us to test G-d with the tithe (Malachi 3:10), and the Talmud (Taanit 9a) teaches: give a tenth so that you will become rich. The Torah and Hashem promise success for all giving; maaser is unique because the promise is stated clearly and we are invited to test it. It is never meant to make anyone feel guilty, and if you are going through a hard time, please call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242. ## Common questions

### Is it better to give a lot once or a little often?
According to Maimonides, as the Alter Rebbe quotes him, giving a sum in many small gifts builds generosity into the giver more deeply than one large gift. Both are good.

### Why did the Alter Rebbe say to give weekly or monthly?
He teaches that each act of giving refines the giver's soul, so dividing one's yearly tzedakah into regular payments adds to the benefit of the mitzvah.

### Should I wait to give until year end?
The daily Tanya study advises not to put all the yearly giving into one gift at the end. Never delay, however, if someone needs help now. See [Year-End Tzedakah](/learn/year-end-tzedakah).

### What is the Chai Club?
It is Chabad of Lamorinda's monthly partners. See [the Chai Club page](/chai-club).

### Does maaser come out monthly?
Many people set aside a tenth of each month's income. Ask the Rabbi how to calculate it for your situation.

### Is maaser a promise of success?
Yes, and so is all giving: the Torah and Hashem promise success for all giving. Maaser is unique because the promise is stated clearly and we are invited to test it: test G-d with the tithe (Malachi 3:10), and the Talmud teaches to give a tenth so that you will become rich. Call the Rabbi for personal questions.
