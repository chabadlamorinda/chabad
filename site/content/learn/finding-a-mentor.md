A mentor in Judaism is a teacher or guide you choose to learn from and talk to honestly. Pirkei Avot (Ethics of the Fathers 1:6) says, "Make for yourself a teacher, acquire for yourself a friend, and judge every person favorably." In Chabad, a spiritual mentor is called a mashpia (מַשְׁפִּיעַ), "one who influences."

Everyone needs someone who knows them and will say what they see. That is true for a student, a parent, a busy professional and even a rabbi.

## "Make for yourself a teacher"

The mishnah in [Pirkei Avot](/learn/pirkei-avot) uses two different verbs. A teacher you "make," meaning you decide and commit. A friend you "acquire," meaning you invest in the relationship. Both are choices that take effort.

Chabad commentary on this mishnah, drawn from the Lubavitcher Rebbe's teachings and published by Sichos In English, notes that even someone who is himself a teacher should find a teacher for himself. However wise a person is, he progresses further when his conduct is seen by an objective person. We are poor judges of ourselves.

## What is a mashpia?

According to Chabad.org, a mashpia is a spiritual mentor. The word means "one who influences." The Rebbe spoke of reviving an old custom, from the days of the Alter Rebbe, of having a mashpia in each community: a man for the men and a woman for the women. Their task is to gather people, teach Torah, and inspire awe of Heaven and love for every Jew. The Rebbe said they should be well versed in [ahavat Yisrael](/learn/ahavat-yisrael), love of a fellow Jew.

Chabad.org also notes that a person is appointed as a mashpia when at least three people appoint him. In other words, a mentor is someone others look to. You do not need to know this terminology to benefit from a mentor. The idea is simple: someone wiser, who cares about you, with whom you can be honest.

## Why not just read a book?

Books and websites teach information. A mentor knows you. They can tell you when you are making excuses, encourage you when you are doing well, and help with a real decision: how to raise a child, how to balance work and Shabbat, how to heal from a hurt. Judaism treats learning as something passed from person to person.

## How to choose a mentor

There is no formula, but here are questions that help:

1. Do they live what they teach?
2. Do they listen before they speak?
3. Do you feel respected, not judged or pressured?
4. Do they push you to grow, in your own pace?
5. Can you disagree with them honestly?

Take your time. Start with a conversation or a class. It is fine to ask them about your personal situation, and it is fine not to "sign up" at once.

## Friends and study partners

The same mishnah says to acquire a friend. A good friend is a mirror and a support. Many people learn Torah with a study partner, called a chavruta (חַבְרוּתָא), where two people read a text together and discuss it. A friend who studies with you holds you steady. ## Common questions

### What does "make for yourself a teacher" mean?
It is from Pirkei Avot 1:6 and means you should actively choose a teacher to learn from. Chabad commentary adds that even a teacher should have one, because we cannot judge ourselves objectively.

### What is a mashpia?
A mashpia is a spiritual mentor in Chassidic tradition. The word means "one who influences." According to Chabad.org, it is an old custom to have one in each community.

### Do I need a rabbi to be Jewish?
No. But a rabbi or teacher you trust can help you grow and answer questions. Everyone is welcome at Chabad of Lamorinda, and no membership is required.

### How do I choose a spiritual mentor?
Look for someone who listens, practices what they teach, respects you and encourages gradual growth. Start with a conversation or a class. Ask the Rabbi if you would like help.

### What is a chavruta?
A chavruta is a study partner with whom you learn Torah by reading and discussing a text together. It is a traditional way of learning.
