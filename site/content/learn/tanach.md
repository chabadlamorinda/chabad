Tanach is the Hebrew name for the Jewish Bible. The word is an acronym of its three parts: **T**orah, **N**evi'im (Prophets) and **K**etuvim (Writings). It is the book the whole world calls "the Bible," but for Jews it is our family story, our law and our song, read and studied every week for over three thousand years.

## The three parts

### Torah: the Five Books of Moses
The heart of Tanach, given by G-d to Moses: Genesis (Bereishit), Exodus (Shemot), Leviticus (Vayikra), Numbers (Bamidbar) and Deuteronomy (Devarim). It tells the story from Creation to the eve of entering the Land of Israel, and contains the 613 mitzvot. Learn more in our guide to the [Torah](/learn/torah).

### Nevi'im: the Prophets
Eight books:
- **Early Prophets:** Joshua, Judges, Samuel and Kings, the history of the Jewish people in the Land of Israel.
- **Later Prophets:** Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and the Twelve (the twelve shorter prophets, from Hosea to Malachi, counted as one book).

The prophets call the people back to G-d, demand justice and kindness, and promise the final redemption.

### Ketuvim: the Writings
Eleven books: Psalms ([Tehillim](/learn/tehillim)), Proverbs, Job, Song of Songs, Ruth, Lamentations, Ecclesiastes, Esther, Daniel, Ezra-Nehemiah (one book) and Chronicles. They include poetry, wisdom and the later history of the Jewish people.

Five of the Writings are known as the five Megillot (scrolls): Song of Songs, Ruth, Lamentations, Ecclesiastes and Esther.

## Why 24 books?

Christian Bibles split some books in two (Samuel, Kings, Chronicles, Ezra and Nehemiah) and count the Twelve Prophets separately, so they list 39 books. The text is the same; the Jewish count is 5 + 8 + 11 = 24. The order of the books differs too.

## How Tanach is read in synagogue

- **The Torah** is read from a handwritten scroll on Shabbat, Mondays, Thursdays, holidays and Rosh Chodesh. One portion is read each week, finishing the whole Torah every year on Simchat Torah. See [Torah reading](/learn/torah-reading) and the [weekly parsha](/learn/weekly-parsha).
- **The Prophets** are heard in the [Haftarah](/learn/haftarah), a portion read after the Torah on Shabbat and holidays.
- **The Megillot:** Esther is read on Purim ([Megillah](/learn/megillah)) and Lamentations on Tisha B'Av. In many communities, Song of Songs is read on Passover, Ruth on Shavuot and Ecclesiastes on Sukkot.
- **Psalms** fill the daily prayers. Many people say a portion of Tehillim every day.

## The Oral Torah alongside it

Tanach was never meant to be read alone. When G-d gave Moses the Written Torah, He also explained how to understand and live it. That explanation, the Oral Torah, was passed down by teachers and later written in the [Mishnah](/learn/mishnah) and [Talmud](/learn/talmud). Without it, we would not know how to put on tefillin or what "work" is forbidden on Shabbat. See [what is halacha](/learn/what-is-halacha).

## Common questions

### Where should a beginner start?
With the weekly Torah portion, in English with Rashi's commentary. A few minutes a week connects you with Jews everywhere reading the same portion.

### What is Chumash?
A Chumash is the Five Books of Moses printed as a book, usually with the Haftarot and commentaries. Chabad's daily study, called Chitas, includes a portion of Chumash with Rashi, Tehillim and [Tanya](/learn/tanya) every day.

### Is Tanach written in Hebrew?
Almost all of it. A few passages in Daniel and Ezra, one verse in Jeremiah and two words in Genesis are in Aramaic.

### Which translation should I use?
A Jewish translation with traditional commentary is best, since it follows how the Sages understood the text. Ask the Rabbi for a recommendation.
