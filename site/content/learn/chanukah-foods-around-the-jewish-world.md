Around the Jewish world, Chanukah foods are almost all fried in oil: latkes in Ashkenazi homes, sufganiyot in Israel and bimuelos (fried fritters) in many Sephardi families. They share one idea, remembering the oil that burned for eight days.

## One idea, many dishes

Jewish tradition explains that Jews have eaten oily foods on Chanukah for at least a thousand years, and that the main dishes are potato latkes and fried doughnuts. Another custom, eating dairy, honors Yehudit (Judith). So every community took its own local ingredients and made them into a Chanukah food. See [Chanukah foods](/learn/chanukah-foods) for the reasons.

## Israel: sufganiyot

Jelly-filled doughnuts, [sufganiyot](/learn/sufganiyot) (סופגניות), are very popular in Israel. Families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda with roots in Israel will know them well. The word is Hebrew, but Yiddish speakers call them *ponchikes*, close to what many Russian speakers call doughnuts.

## Ashkenazi homes: latkes

The tradition describes potato latkes as the traditional fried food in Ashkenazi communities, served with applesauce or sour cream. Cheese latkes combine the two customs, oil and dairy. Potatoes came late to Europe, so earlier latkes were made with other ingredients (see [latkes](/learn/latkes)). For the difference between communities, see [Ashkenazi and Sephardi](/learn/ashkenazi-and-sephardi).

## Sephardi and Mediterranean homes: bimuelos

Among Chanukah foods are fried doughnuts called *bumuelos*, or bimuelos, among Chanukah foods. These are fried fritters, often sweet. Many Sephardi families have their own fried treat, and each has a local name and style. If you have one in your family, it is a custom worth keeping.

## A new twist: a themed menu

A Mexican-style Chanukah menu has with tortilla chips, salsa, fish cakes, cheese quesadillas and cheese and black bean chimichangas. This is not an old community custom. It is an invitation to keep the oil and dairy themes while trying something new, especially after several nights of latkes. It is a reminder that the holiday grows with the families who keep it.

## Russian-speaking families

Many Russian-speaking families will find the word *ponchikes* familiar. If you remember a Chanukah or holiday dish from your grandmother's home, share it with us. See [Russian-speaking Jews in Lamorinda](/learn/russian-speaking-jews-in-lamorinda).

## What every dish has in common

1. **Oil.** Fried or oil-cooked foods recall the Temple oil. See [olive oil in Jewish tradition](/learn/olive-oil-in-jewish-tradition).
2. **Family.** The foods are shared at a table with the menorah in the window.
3. **Kosher care.** Whatever the dish, use ingredients with a kosher symbol and keep meat and dairy apart. For help, see [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols). [Bay Kosher](/programs/bay-kosher), a project of Chabad of Lamorinda, is always kosher.

## Make an international Chanukah table

Cook one dish from each tradition: latkes, sufganiyot and a family favorite. Tell the story of where it came from. Ask your children to name a country on a map for each dish. See [a Chanukah dinner menu](/learn/chanukah-dinner-menu) for how to plan.

## Why the differences matter

The differences between communities are not a problem to solve. They show how one Jewish holiday took root in many lands, each community keeping the same message with its own flavors. If your family's table looks different from your neighbor's, both are Chanukah. Ask older relatives what they remember, write the dish down, and cook it this year.

## Common questions

### What are traditional Chanukah foods around the world?
Fried foods: potato latkes among Ashkenazi Jews, sufganiyot in Israel, and bimuelos among many Sephardi families. Dairy dishes are also customary.

### What do Israelis eat on Chanukah?
Jelly-filled doughnuts, sufganiyot, are very popular in Israel.

### What do Sephardi Jews eat on Chanukah?
Many eat fried fritters or doughnuts, called bimuelos or bumuelos. Customs vary, so ask your family or the Rabbi.

### Why do all these foods use oil?
They recall the miracle of the Temple oil that burned for eight days.

### Is it a mitzvah to eat these foods?
No. They are customs. The mitzvah of Chanukah is lighting the menorah. See [the Chanukah festive meal](/learn/seudat-chanukah).

### Can I bring a family dish to a Chanukah gathering?
Yes, with a kosher kitchen and kosher-certified ingredients if the host keeps kosher. Ask the host first.
