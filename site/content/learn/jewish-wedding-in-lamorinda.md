## What does a Jewish wedding need?

According to Jewish law, a wedding has these essentials:

- **A chuppah** (חֻפָּה): the canopy under which the couple stands. It symbolizes the home they will build. See Chabad.org on the chuppah.
- **A ketubah** (כְּתֻבָּה): the Jewish marriage contract, signed before the ceremony. Read [the ketubah](/learn/ketubah).
- **A ring:** given by the groom to the bride under the chuppah, as part of the ceremony.
- **Two kosher witnesses** (עֵדִים, *edim*): they watch the ring being given and sign the ketubah. Who qualifies is a matter of Jewish law, so ask the Rabbi; Chabad.org explains the rules for witnesses.
- **The seven blessings** (שֶׁבַע בְּרָכוֹת) and the breaking of the glass, which recalls the destruction of the Temple even at a moment of joy.

For the full ceremony step by step, read [the Jewish wedding](/learn/jewish-wedding) and [Jewish wedding customs](/learn/jewish-wedding-customs).

## Planning step by step

1. **Call the Rabbi first.** Dial 925-384-7242 several months ahead, before you book a venue, so the date and the Jewish calendar work together. Read [choosing a wedding date](/learn/choosing-a-wedding-date), because weddings are not held on Shabbat or on Jewish holidays.
2. **Meet the Rabbi.** He will walk you through the ceremony and any questions about Jewish status or background, kindly and privately. A Jewish wedding is between two Jews; ask in confidence if you have questions.
3. **Prepare the bride.** The Rebbetzin helps the bride prepare for the mikvah, the ritual immersion before the wedding. See [mikvah](/learn/mikvah).
4. **Arrange the ketubah and chuppah.** The Rabbi helps with both; ask what you need to bring.
5. **Choose your venue.** We do not recommend venues. Weddings can happen in many places around Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda and the East Bay; ask the Rabbi about any space you are considering.
6. **Enjoy the day, and the first week.** The week after the wedding is celebrated with festive meals and the seven blessings.

If you are newly engaged, start with [Jewish engagement](/learn/jewish-engagement). On a budget? See [Jewish wedding on a budget](/learn/jewish-wedding-on-a-budget).

## Why work with a Chabad rabbi?

Chabad of Lamorinda welcomes every Jew, with no membership required. Chabad teaches that marriage unites two halves of one soul, and the wedding is a holy day of new beginnings. We aim for a ceremony that is warm, personal and faithful to Jewish tradition, whatever your background.

## Common questions

### Can a rabbi do a Jewish wedding in Lamorinda?
Yes. Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski officiates at Jewish weddings according to Jewish law and helps with the date, the ketubah and the chuppah. Call 925-384-7242, ideally several months ahead.

### What do you need for a Jewish wedding?
A chuppah, a ketubah, a ring, two kosher witnesses, the seven blessings and an officiating rabbi. The Rabbi guides you through each.

### Who can be witnesses at a Jewish wedding?
The witnesses must meet requirements under Jewish law, so ask Rabbi Labkowski in advance. They sign the ketubah and watch the ring given.

### How far ahead should I call the Rabbi?
Several months ahead if you can, before booking a venue, so the date works with the Jewish calendar.

### Does the bride go to the mikvah before the wedding?
Traditionally yes. The Rebbetzin helps the bride prepare. See [mikvah](/learn/mikvah) or call 925-384-7242.

### Can we get married on Shabbat or a Jewish holiday?
No. Jewish weddings are not held on Shabbat or holidays. See [choosing a wedding date](/learn/choosing-a-wedding-date).
