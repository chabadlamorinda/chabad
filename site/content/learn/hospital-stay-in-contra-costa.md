If you or a loved one is in the hospital in Contra Costa County, tell the staff you are Jewish, and call Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski at 925-384-7242. He visits at home, in hospitals, care facilities and hospice across the area, including Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda, Pleasant Hill and Martinez, and we will add the patient's name to our [Tehillim prayer list](/tehillim).

## What to do in the first hours

1. **Call the Rabbi.** Dial 925-384-7242. He can visit, pray with the patient and help the family.
2. **Tell the hospital you are Jewish.** Say so at admission, and ask that it be noted in the chart.
3. **Ask about spiritual care.** Hospitals such as John Muir Medical Center in Walnut Creek have chaplains, called Pastoral Care Services, who support patients according to their needs and beliefs. A chaplain can help with the patient's needs, and you can also ask the Rabbi to visit.
4. **Ask for kosher meals.** Hospitals vary in what they offer, so ask the nurse or the dietary department at admission. Ask Rabbi Labkowski what to do if kosher food is not available; he can advise based on the patient's situation.
5. **Add the name to the prayer list.** The Rabbi says a Mi Sheberach for the patient at the Torah reading in Lafayette. Use the [Tehillim list](/tehillim). Give the Hebrew name and the mother's Hebrew name if you know them.

## Visiting the sick

Visiting the sick, *bikur cholim* (בִּקּוּר חוֹלִים), is a mitzvah, and Chabad.org teaches that a visit gives the patient strength. Come with a warm word, listen, and don't stay too long. Read [bikur cholim](/learn/bikur-cholim) and [Mi Sheberach](/learn/mi-sheberach).

## Shabbat and holidays in the hospital

Shabbat in a hospital raises real questions: electronic call buttons, doors, devices, food and medication. The rule is that **safety and health come first**: when there is a medical need, Jewish law permits what the patient requires. Do not hesitate to ask for medical care on Shabbat. Call the Rabbi before Shabbat if you can, and read [Shabbat in the hospital](/learn/shabbat-in-the-hospital). For holidays, call 925-384-7242 and ask.

## Before surgery

Many patients find comfort in prayer before an operation. See [prayer before surgery](/learn/prayer-before-surgery). The Rabbi is glad to say a blessing with you.

## When the news is hard

If the illness is serious, you do not need to carry it alone. Read [when a loved one is dying](/learn/when-a-loved-one-is-dying), and call the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin (925-384-7241). Our [help page](/help) lists more places to start.

## Common questions

### Can a rabbi visit someone in the hospital near Walnut Creek or Martinez?
Yes. Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski visits at home, in hospitals, care facilities and hospice across our area. Call 925-384-7242.

### Can I get kosher food in a Contra Costa hospital?
Hospitals differ, so ask at admission whether kosher meals are available. If not, call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 to ask what is best for the patient.

### How do I add someone to a Jewish prayer list for the sick?
Add the name to Chabad of Lamorinda's [Tehillim prayer list](/tehillim); the Rabbi says a Mi Sheberach for them at the Torah reading in Lafayette.

### What if the patient is in the hospital on Shabbat?
Health comes first: ask for any medical care needed, and call the Rabbi for guidance. See [Shabbat in the hospital](/learn/shabbat-in-the-hospital).

### Do I need to be a member to ask for a visit?
No. Every Jew is welcome, with no membership required.

### Does the hospital have a chaplain?
Many do; John Muir Medical Center in Walnut Creek has Pastoral Care Services. Ask the nurse for the chaplain, and also call the Rabbi.
