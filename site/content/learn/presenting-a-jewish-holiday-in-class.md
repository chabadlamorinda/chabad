To present a Jewish holiday in your child's class, plan about 20 minutes: tell the whole story simply, give the children something to do with their hands, and end with one clear message. Chanukah is about light and the courage to be different, and Passover is about freedom, and both fit easily into a classroom in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda.

## Before you go: talk to the teacher

Ask your child's teacher how long you have, how many children there will be, and whether the school has rules about food, candles or visitors. Public and private schools differ, so follow the teacher's and school's guidelines, which you can ask about at the school office. Each school sets its own rules, so check rather than assume. Your goal is to share, not to persuade: you are explaining what your family celebrates, and children of every background are welcome to listen and enjoy.

Ask your child what they want you to say. For many kids, being proud of their holiday starts when a parent comes to class and the other children are curious.

## A 20-minute plan for Chanukah

**1. The story (5 minutes).** Tell all of it, not only the oil. A long time ago the land of Israel was ruled by a king named Antiochus who tried to stop the Jewish people from keeping the Torah. A small band of Jews led by the Maccabees stood up, with courage, against a much larger army. When they won, they returned to the Holy Temple in Jerusalem, and they found only one jug of pure oil for the Menorah, enough for one day. Miraculously it lasted eight days. That is why we light candles for eight nights. See [the Chanukah story](/learn/chanukah-story) for more.

**2. The dreidel game (7 minutes).** The dreidel is a spinning top with four Hebrew letters: Nun, Gimmel, Hey and Shin, which stand for "A great miracle happened there." Explain the letters, then let the children spin it. Chocolate or paper coins can be replaced with stickers or raisins if the school avoids candy and for allergies. The rules are in our guide to the [dreidel](/learn/dreidel).

**3. A craft with no flame (5 minutes).** Chabad.org's list of children's Chanukah activities includes making cards, shaping clay dreidels, decorating a menorah from blocks, and a hunt for the "oil." A paper menorah with nine cut-out flames works well. Show a real menorah, but do not light it in class unless the school allows it.

**4. The message (3 minutes).** One small light pushes away a lot of darkness. Ask: what is a little light you can add this week? A kind word, a share, a hello to a new child.

## A 20-minute plan for Passover

1. **The story:** The Jewish people were slaves in Egypt, and G-d freed them with Moses as their leader. Every spring we tell it again at the Seder.
2. **Taste matzah:** Matzah is flat unleavened bread, made in a hurry because our ancestors had to leave quickly. Check allergies first, because matzah contains wheat. Let children touch, break and taste it. See [matzah](/learn/matzah) and [Passover with Kids](/learn/passover-with-kids).
3. **The Four Questions** or a simple "Why is this night different?" game.
4. **The message:** Freedom means we care about everyone being free, and about telling our children the story.

## Be sensitive and inclusive

- Do not ask other children to take part in a prayer or blessing. Explain what Jews do, and keep the tone informational.
- Ask about food allergies and dietary rules, and wash hands.
- Skip flames, hot wax or anything unsafe. Follow the teacher.
- Invite questions, and answer "I don't know, I'll ask the Rabbi" if needed.
- Let your child take the lead, and never push a child who feels shy.

## Common questions

### How long should a Chanukah presentation for a class be?
About 20 minutes is plenty for elementary students: five for the story, a few for the dreidel and a craft, and a short ending message. Shorter is better than longer.

### What is a good Chanukah activity for a classroom?
Playing dreidel, making a paper or block menorah and acting out the story are good choices. They need no flame and no special food.

### Can I light a menorah in my child's classroom?
Only if the school allows it. Many schools do not allow open flames, so ask the teacher or school office. A picture or an electric menorah can work instead.

### What should I say about Passover to young children?
Say that Passover is the holiday of freedom when Jews remember leaving slavery in Egypt, and let them taste matzah. Keep it simple, and warm.

### How do I handle food allergies when I bring dreidel treats or matzah?
Ask the teacher for the class allergy list first, and choose a food that is safe for everyone. Matzah has wheat, so offer a stamp or sticker for children who cannot have it.
