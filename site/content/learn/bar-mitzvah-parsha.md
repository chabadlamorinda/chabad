A boy's bar mitzvah date is his 13th Hebrew birthday, and a girl's bat mitzvah date is her 12th Hebrew birthday, counted on the Jewish calendar and not the secular one. His or her Torah portion (*parsha*, פָּרָשָׁה) is the one read in the Shabbat of that week. Because the Jewish calendar is based on the moon, the date moves around the secular calendar, so you need to look it up.

If your family lives in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda, we are glad to help you check it, and it takes just a few minutes.

## Step 1: Start with the Hebrew birthday

The date of a bar or bat mitzvah is the Hebrew birthday, which falls on the same Hebrew date as the birth. A child becomes obligated in the mitzvahs on that day. Read more in [Hebrew birthday](/learn/hebrew-birthday).

Boys are 13 and girls are 12. A girl becomes responsible a year earlier. The 12 and 13 are counted in Hebrew years, not secular years.

## Step 2: Check whether the birth was before or after sunset

A Jewish day begins at nightfall, not at midnight. A baby born after sunset belongs to the next Hebrew date. So a baby born at 8 PM may have a Hebrew birthday that is one day later than the date on the secular birth certificate.

This one detail can change the date. If the birth was close to sunset, the exact time and place matter. Chabad.org's date calculator asks for both. If you are not sure, ask the Rabbi, because he can look at it with you.

## Step 3: Find the Torah portion

The portion the child will read or speak about is generally the weekly Torah portion of the Shabbat on or just after the Hebrew birthday. Traditionally the boy is called to the Torah on the first Torah-reading day after his birthday, and many choose the Shabbat after. See the [Torah reading](/learn/torah-reading) and the [bar mitzvah timeline](/learn/bar-bat-mitzvah-timeline).

Why does your portion sometimes differ from what a friend with a nearby date reads?

- **Israel and outside Israel.** On some years, because a holiday lasts one day in Israel and two days outside it, the weekly portions can be out of step for several weeks. A date calculator asks which you want.
- **Holiday Shabbats.** When Shabbat falls on a festival, a special reading replaces the regular portion, and families usually ask the Rabbi what to do.
- **Double portions.** Sometimes two portions are read together on one Shabbat.

## Step 4: Check leap years and Adar

In a Jewish leap year there are two months of Adar. If a birthday was in Adar and the bar mitzvah year is different from the birth year, there are different rulings on when the date falls. Do not guess. Ask the Rabbi and we will tell you the date in your case. See [the Jewish leap year](/learn/jewish-leap-year-and-calendar).

## Common questions

### How do I find my bar mitzvah date?
Your bar mitzvah date is your 13th Hebrew birthday, so convert your birth date (and whether it was before or after sunset) to the Hebrew calendar and add 13 years. A calculator or the Rabbi can do this for you in a minute.

### How do I find my bat mitzvah date?
A bat mitzvah date is the 12th Hebrew birthday, since a girl is responsible one year earlier than a boy. Use the same method as for a bar mitzvah, just with 12 years.

### What is my bar mitzvah Torah portion?
It is the portion read on the Shabbat of the week of your Hebrew birthday, generally the Shabbat on or after it. Ask the Rabbi if the week falls on a holiday.

### If I was born after sunset, is my birthday the next day?
Yes. In Jewish law the day begins at nightfall, so a birth after sunset counts as the next Hebrew date. If the birth was near sunset, ask the Rabbi.

### Can we hold a bar mitzvah on a different date from the Hebrew birthday?
Yes. The status begins on the birthday no matter what, but a family can plan the celebration, aliyah or party on a nearby Shabbat. Call 925-384-7242 and we will help you pick it.

### What if my child was born in Adar or in a leap year?
It depends on the details of the year, so ask the Rabbi. Do not rely on a calculator alone for these cases.
