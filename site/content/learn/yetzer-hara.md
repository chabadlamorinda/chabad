The yetzer hara (יֵצֶר הָרַע) is the "evil inclination," the inner voice that pulls a person toward selfishness, laziness or wrongdoing. Judaism does not see it as a devil outside us, but as a part of human nature that we are meant to master and even turn to good.

## One inner voice, not a devil

Many people ask whether Jews believe in the devil. In Jewish tradition the Satan is not a rival to G-d but an angel who serves G-d's will, as the Talmud describes (Bava Batra 16a). The Talmud also says the Satan, the evil inclination and the angel of death are in a sense one. The real battleground is inside each of us. The Torah asks us to choose; see [free will](/learn/free-will).

## The yetzer hara is not all bad

Our Sages teach that without the evil inclination no one would build a house, marry or work for a living. It is also why the Torah describes humanity as "very good." The goal is not to destroy these drives but to direct them.

## Two souls: Tanya explained simply

The Alter Rebbe, founder of Chabad, explains in the [Tanya](/learn/tanya) that every Jew has two souls:

- The **G-dly soul** (nefesh Elokit): a true part of G-d above, which wants to give, learn and connect.
- The **animal soul** (nefesh habehamit): the natural self, which wants comfort, pleasure and recognition.

These are two inner voices, and you are the one who chooses. The Tanya describes a spectrum of people. The **beinoni** (בֵּינוֹנִי), the "in-between" person, is someone who never acts on a wrong impulse even though the impulse still speaks. The Tanya teaches that this is a level every person can reach. Read more about [the soul](/learn/the-soul).

## Practical tools from Chassidic teaching

1. **Pause before you act.** An urge is often strongest in the first minute. Give it time.
2. **Add light.** A well-known Chassidic idea says you don't fight darkness with sticks, you switch on a light. Take on one small mitzvah, a moment of learning or giving, and the darkness retreats.
3. **Make it small and steady.** Real change comes from small daily steps. See [changing a bad habit](/learn/changing-a-bad-habit).
4. **Ask for help.** Speak to a teacher, friend or the Rabbi. You don't have to fight alone.
5. **Use the setbacks.** A failure is a chance to begin again, not a verdict.

## Common questions

### What is the yetzer hara?
The yetzer hara is the evil inclination, the inner pull toward selfish or harmful choices. Jewish tradition teaches that we can master it and use its energy for good.

### What is the yetzer tov?
The yetzer tov (יֵצֶר טוֹב) is the good inclination, the inner voice that pushes us to be kind, honest and holy. We are meant to strengthen it every day.

### Do Jews believe in the devil?
Not in the Christian sense. The Satan in Jewish sources is an angel acting under G-d's authority. Our struggle is within us.

### How do you overcome temptation in Judaism?
Pause, add light through mitzvahs and Torah, work in small steps and ask for help from a teacher.

### What are the two souls in Tanya?
The G-dly soul and the animal soul. Both live in every Jew, and each person decides who leads.
