The seven species (שבעת המינים, Shivat HaMinim) are the seven foods with which the Torah praises the land of Israel: wheat, barley, grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives and dates. They appear in the verse in Deuteronomy 8:8, and are traditionally eaten on Tu BiShvat.

## What are the seven species?

The Torah describes Israel as "a land of wheat and barley, of vines, figs and pomegranates, a land of olive oil and honey" (Deuteronomy 8:8). The "honey" is understood to be date honey. In that order, the seven are:

1. Wheat (chitah)
2. Barley (se'orah)
3. Grapes (gefen)
4. Figs (te'enah)
5. Pomegranates (rimon)
6. Olives (zayit)
7. Dates (tamar)

## What do they mean?

Chabad.org notes that the seven correspond to the seven emotional attributes of the soul in Kabbalah: wheat is chesed (kindness), barley gevurah (strength), grapes tiferet (harmony), figs netzach (perseverance), pomegranates hod (humility), olives yesod (foundation) and dates malchut (royalty).

There is a simple lesson too. The two grains are necessary for sustenance, and the five fruits add pleasure to life. A Jewish life needs both: what we need, and what we enjoy, each with a blessing.

## The blessings

Say a blessing before you eat, and the right blessing depends on the food:

- Grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives and dates: *borei peri ha'etz*, the blessing for fruit from a tree. Grape juice and wine have their own blessing, *hagafen*.
- Wheat and barley: bread has *hamotzi*, and cake, pasta or cereal made of grain has *borei minei mezonot*.

You can find the words in [blessings](/learn/blessings), and for blessings on food of the earth see [blessings on nature](/learn/blessings-on-nature).

## The after-blessing

After eating the five fruits, there is a special after-blessing, *Al Ha'etz*. Grain foods that are not bread have *Al HaMichyah*, and bread has *Birkat HaMazon*. Chabad.org notes that this is the special blessing for the fruits of Israel. For the words and how much you must eat, see [blessings after food](/learn/blessings-after-food). If you are not sure which blessing to say, ask the Rabbi.

## A Tu BiShvat table to set at home

Set a table with the seven species and a few other fruits. Here is a simple plan:

1. Put out dates, figs, pomegranate seeds, grapes and olives, with a little bread or a cookie made of wheat or barley.
2. Say the blessing on each fruit in the order of the verse: the one mentioned earlier in the verse is eaten first.
3. Say one word of thanks for a land, a season and a year of growth.
4. Say the after-blessing at the end.

See [the Tu BiShvat seder](/learn/tu-bishvat-seder) for the custom of the four cups and more ideas, and [the month of Shevat](/learn/shevat).

## Common questions

### What are the seven species of Israel?
They are wheat, barley, grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives and dates, the foods that the Torah uses to praise the land of Israel.

### Where do the seven species come from in the Torah?
From Deuteronomy 8:8, which calls Israel "a land of wheat and barley, of vines, figs and pomegranates, a land of olive oil and honey." The honey is understood as date honey.

### Why do we eat the seven species on Tu BiShvat?
Tu BiShvat is the New Year for Trees, and eating the fruits of Israel is how we mark it. It reminds us how good the land is.

### What blessing do I say on figs, dates and pomegranates?
Say *borei peri ha'etz*, the blessing for tree fruit. Grapes use the same blessing, unless it is grape juice or wine.

### What is the after-blessing for the seven species?
After the five fruits, we say *Al Ha'etz*. After grain foods that are not bread, *Al HaMichyah*; after bread, Birkat HaMazon. Ask the Rabbi if you are unsure.
