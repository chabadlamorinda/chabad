An adult bar or bat mitzvah is a celebration, at any age, of becoming responsible for the mitzvahs (commandments) as a Jewish adult. In Jewish law you became bar mitzvah at 13 or bat mitzvah at 12 automatically, with or without a party, so an adult celebration does not "make" you Jewish-adult; it marks the moment you choose to embrace it.

For Jews in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda who never had one, it is never too late. Whether you are 25 or 85, you are welcome here.

## What "bar mitzvah" and "bat mitzvah" actually mean

*Bar mitzvah* (בַּר מִצְוָה) means "son of the commandment," and *bat mitzvah* (בַּת מִצְוָה) means "daughter of the commandment." It is not a ceremony that you pass or fail; it is a status. A boy reaches it at 13 and a girl at 12, and from then on the mitzvahs are his or hers to keep.

This is why Chabad teaches that the celebration is secondary. What matters is the inner step: "I am a Jew, and I want to live like one."

## Who chooses an adult celebration

Many people never had one. Some grew up in families that did not observe. Others, including many Russian-speaking Jews who grew up in the former Soviet Union, were never allowed to. Some had a party at 13 but never really learned what it meant, and want to do it again with understanding. Others are parents who feel it while watching their child prepare. All of these are welcome.

## Ways to celebrate

There is no single required format. Here are some common ones:

- **An aliyah to the Torah.** A man can be called up at a Shabbat or Monday or Thursday morning reading, ideally in the week of his Hebrew birthday. Read more about the [aliyah](/learn/aliyah) and the [Torah reading](/learn/torah-reading).
- **Learning to chant a portion.** Some choose to learn to read from the Torah, starting with [Hebrew reading for adults](/learn/hebrew-reading-for-adults). This is optional, and a beautiful goal, not a requirement.
- **A "re-bar mitzvah" with tefillin.** A man who put on tefillin at 13 and then stopped can recommit to wearing them each weekday. See [tefillin](/learn/tefillin).
- **A talk and a study session.** You can share what the day means to you and learn something together with family and friends.
- **For women:** A woman's celebration often takes the form of a gathering with learning, a few words from the heart, and taking on a mitzvah such as lighting [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles). Ask the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin what fits.

1. **Find your Hebrew birthday and Torah portion.** The guide on [your bar or bat mitzvah date and Torah portion](/learn/bar-mitzvah-parsha) walks you through it.
2. **Choose a mitzvah to take on.** Tefillin, Shabbat candles, a weekly class, a first Shabbat dinner. Small and real beats big and short-lived.
3. **Get a Hebrew name** if you do not have one: [getting a Hebrew name as an adult](/learn/getting-a-hebrew-name-as-an-adult).

## Common questions

### Can an adult have a bar mitzvah?
Yes. An adult can celebrate a bar or bat mitzvah at any age, because the obligation began automatically at 13 for a boy and 12 for a girl and the celebration simply marks it. Call Chabad of Lamorinda in Lafayette at 925-384-7242 to plan one.

### Is there a difference between a bar mitzvah and an adult bar mitzvah?
In Jewish law, no. The status is the same; an adult celebration is a conscious, personal way of embracing it. It can be shorter, simpler and more meaningful than a first one at 13.

### Do I need to read Hebrew to have an adult bar or bat mitzvah?
No. Many adults begin by learning the letters and a few blessings, and some never chant a full portion. Chabad does not require reading Hebrew for the celebration.

### Can a woman have an adult bat mitzvah?
Yes. A woman became responsible for the mitzvahs at 12 and can mark it at any age. Customs differ for how, so ask the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin about the best way for you.

### I am not sure I was ever bar mitzvah, or my Jewish status is unclear. What do I do?
Start with a private conversation. The Rabbi can help you sort out your background gently; see [who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew). Everyone is welcome.

### I never had a bar mitzvah because of the Soviet Union. Can I still do this?
Absolutely. Many Jews in the East Bay and across the Bay Area are doing exactly this. Call 925-384-7242; Russian speakers are especially welcome.
