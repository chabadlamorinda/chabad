Giving gifts is not a required mitzvah of Chanukah, but giving gelt, a little money, is a long-standing Jewish custom, especially as a reward for Torah study. Many families also give presents; the Jewish way is to keep them meaningful and to include giving to others.

## What Chabad.org says about gifts and gelt

Chabad.org explains it simply. There is no commandment to give presents on Chanukah. What there is, is the old custom of *gelt* (Yiddish for "money"), especially a reward for learning Torah. The foil-wrapped chocolate coins came later, from American candy makers.

Children traditionally receive gelt after the menorah is lit. Chabad.org adds that children should be encouraged to give part of their gelt to charity, and that adults can use gelt to reward good behavior such as diligence in study or acts of kindness. It is also customary to give more tzedakah during Chanukah, and to give double on Fridays, because money is not handled on Shabbat. For more on the custom, read our [Chanukah gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt) guide.

So gelt is the heart of the matter, and everything else is an extra that each family decides on for itself.

## Meaningful gift ideas

If your family likes to give something on each night, here are ideas that fit the spirit of the holiday:

- **A Jewish book** for a child or adult: a children's holiday story, a Jewish cookbook or a book of Torah thoughts. See our [Jewish gift ideas](/learn/jewish-gift-ideas).
- **A menorah of their own,** so a child lights beside you each night. Learn about [oil and candles](/learn/menorah-oil-and-candles).
- **A pushka,** a tzedakah box, with a first coin in it. See [the pushka](/learn/pushka).
- **Gelt with a Torah reward.** A coin for each blessing a child says, each Chanukah fact learned, or each kind act.
- **Time together:** a night of games, dreidel, a [song](/learn/chanukah-songs) or a family meal.
- **A gift of giving.** Make a donation to tzedakah in the family's name or in honor of a grandparent.

You do not need to buy something big. Many families choose one small thing per night and one family activity.

## Keeping the focus on light

Parents often ask how to help children enjoy gifts without losing the point of the holiday. A few simple habits help:

1. Light the menorah first, sing, and only then give gelt or a gift.
2. Share the story of the [oil and the Maccabees](/learn/chanukah-story) before opening presents.
3. Let each child choose where part of their gelt goes to tzedakah.
4. Say thank you aloud, and write a note to a faraway relative.

## Gifts for teachers and others

Chanukah is also a lovely time to thank teachers and those who help the community. A card with a few coins or a small treat with a good kosher symbol is a warm gesture. See [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols) if you are unsure what to look for.

## Common questions

### Do Jews give gifts on Chanukah?
Many do, but giving gifts is not a mitzvah. The old custom is giving gelt, a small amount of money, especially as a reward for Torah study.

### Why do we give Chanukah gelt?
Gelt rewards learning and teaches generosity, since children are encouraged to give part to charity. Chabad.org describes it as a long-standing custom, not a commandment.

### How many gifts should I give on Chanukah?
There is no set number. Some families give something small each night, others one gift or only gelt. Do what suits your family and keep the holiday about light and family.

### Can I give gelt on Shabbat?
No. Money is not handled on Shabbat, which is why people give gelt, or double, before Shabbat on Friday.
