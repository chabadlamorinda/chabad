To light a Chanukah menorah safely, set it on a sturdy, fireproof surface, keep it well away from curtains, paper and anything that burns, keep it out of reach of children and pets, and stay nearby while it burns. The mitzvah needs a real flame, so a few simple habits keep a joyful holiday safe.

## The basics every home needs

These simple rules come from Jewish tradition and from public fire-safety guidance:

- **A stable, fireproof surface.** A metal tray, a glass or stone table, or a tray lined with foil under the menorah. Avoid wood, plastic and tablecloths.
- **Space from anything that burns.** U.S. fire-safety guidance (the U.S. Fire Administration, citing NFPA) says to keep candles at least 12 inches from anything that can burn, such as curtains, paper decorations, gift wrap and holiday greenery.
- **Out of reach.** Keep the menorah where children and pets cannot knock it over, and keep matches and lighters locked away.
- **Not near drafts.** An open window or heater vent can blow a flame sideways.
- **Working smoke alarms.** Test them before the holiday starts.
- **A bucket or fire extinguisher nearby,** and everyone knowing where it is.

## Set it up carefully

1. **Level the menorah.** Make sure all eight candles or oil cups are in a straight row at the same height, with the shamash (the helper candle) set slightly apart.
2. **Use sturdy candles.** Fit them tightly in the holders, so they cannot tip. See [menorah oil and candles](/learn/menorah-oil-and-candles).
3. **Consider tea lights.** Jewish law suggests that tea lights, small wax candles in metal cups, are sturdy and low and less likely to tip. They may not fit a menorah, so line them up on a plate or fireproof tray, with the shamash apart.
4. **Mind sleeves and hair.** Long sleeves, scarves and hair should stay away from the flames when lighting.
5. **Light carefully.** Use the shamash, held steady, to light each candle; then place the shamash in its holder.

## While it burns

- **Stay nearby.** The lights should burn for about half an hour after nightfall; keep an adult in the room, especially with young children.
- **Do not move the menorah** during that first half hour. If a flame goes out during that time, Halacha says it is best to relight it.
- **Keep the lights for looking.** Not for reading by or working near.
- **Let it burn out safely.** The lights must burn at least half an hour after nightfall. After that, the law says you may put them out if you need to, for example if you must leave the house, though it is not normally recommended. Never leave a menorah burning in an empty house.
- **Friday afternoon needs extra care.** The menorah is lit before the Shabbat candles and must burn longer than usual (Jewish law says up to about an hour and a half), so use enough oil or large candles. See [Chanukah on Shabbat](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat).

## Windows, apartments and electric menorahs

Chanukah lights are placed where passersby can see them, such as beside a doorway (the Chabad custom is near the doorpost opposite the mezuzah) or a window facing the street. Halacha adds a limit: use a windowsill only if the window is less than thirty feet above the ground, so passersby can see the lights. In an apartment, a stable table by the window is safer than a narrow sill. See [the menorah at home in Lamorinda](/learn/menorah-at-home-in-lamorinda).

Electric menorahs do not fulfill the mitzvah, according to Jewish law: the mitzvah requires a real flame of oil or wax. They are lovely for decoration and to publicize the miracle, and can sit beside the real one.

## When lighting is hard

Some people, such as an elderly person living alone, a person in a hospital or care facility, or a student in a dorm, cannot use a flame. Sturdy tea lights on a fireproof plate are often the easiest choice. Where flames are not allowed, the law says to consult a rabbi, so call Rabbi Labkowski. No blessing is said over an electric or battery light. For those who are elderly or hesitant, see [safety at home](/learn/maakeh-safety-at-home) and our [help for seniors and families](/help/help-for-seniors-and-families).

## Common questions

### How do I light a menorah safely at home?
Use a sturdy, fireproof surface, keep it away from anything that burns, keep it out of children's reach and stay nearby. Light with the shamash and do not move it while it burns.

### How far should a menorah be from curtains?
Fire-safety guidance says at least 12 inches from anything that can burn. A wider margin is better.

### Is it okay to put a menorah on a windowsill?
Jewish law says only if the window is less than thirty feet above the ground, and the sill is stable. Otherwise use a table near the window.

### Can I use an electric menorah?
For decoration, yes, but the mitzvah requires a real oil or wax flame. Ask the Rabbi if you cannot light a flame.

### What if the flame goes out?
If it goes out during the first half hour, Halacha says it is best to relight it. After that, there is no need to relight it.

### Can I blow out the menorah if I am worried?
Not during the first half hour after nightfall. After that, the law says you may put the lights out if you need to, though it is not normally recommended. Tea lights on a fireproof plate can help you feel safer.
