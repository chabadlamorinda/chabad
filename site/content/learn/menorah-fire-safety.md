To light a Chanukah menorah safely, set it on a sturdy, fireproof surface, keep it well away from curtains, paper and anything that burns, keep it out of reach of children and pets, and stay nearby while it burns. The mitzvah needs a real flame, so a few simple habits keep a joyful holiday safe.

## The basics every home needs

These simple rules come from Chabad.org and from public fire-safety guidance:

- **A stable, fireproof surface.** A metal tray, a glass or stone table, or a tray lined with foil under the menorah. Avoid wood, plastic and tablecloths.
- **Space from anything that burns.** U.S. fire-safety guidance (the U.S. Fire Administration, citing NFPA) says to keep candles at least 12 inches from anything that can burn, such as curtains, paper decorations, gift wrap and holiday greenery.
- **Out of reach.** Keep the menorah where children and pets cannot knock it over, and keep matches and lighters locked away.
- **Not near drafts.** An open window or heater vent can blow a flame sideways.
- **Working smoke alarms.** Test them before the holiday starts.
- **A bucket or fire extinguisher nearby,** and everyone knowing where it is.

## Set it up carefully

1. **Level the menorah.** Make sure all eight candles or oil cups are in a straight row at the same height, with the shamash (the helper candle) set slightly apart.
2. **Use sturdy candles.** Fit them tightly in the holders, so they cannot tip. See [menorah oil and candles](/learn/menorah-oil-and-candles).
3. **Consider tea lights.** Chabad.org suggests that tea lights, small wax candles in metal cups, are sturdy and low and less likely to tip. They may not fit a menorah, so line them up on a plate or fireproof tray, with the shamash apart.
4. **Mind sleeves and hair.** Long sleeves, scarves and hair should stay away from the flames when lighting.
5. **Light carefully.** Use the shamash, held steady, to light each candle; then place the shamash in its holder.

## While it burns

- **Stay nearby.** The lights should burn for about half an hour after nightfall; keep an adult in the room, especially with young children.
- **Do not move the menorah** during that first half hour. If a flame goes out, Chabad.org advises relighting it.
- **Keep the lights for looking.** Not for reading by or working near.
- **Let it burn out safely.** Chabad.org says that extinguishing early is not normally recommended. If you have to leave the house or are worried after the required time has passed, ask the Rabbi before putting a flame out. Never leave a menorah burning in an empty house.
- **Friday afternoon needs extra care.** The menorah is lit before the Shabbat candles and must burn long enough, so use enough oil or large candles. See [Chanukah on Shabbat](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat).

## Windows, apartments and electric menorahs

Chanukah lights are placed where passersby can see them, such as a window facing the street or a doorway. Chabad.org adds a safety limit: use a windowsill only if the window is less than thirty feet above the ground. In an apartment, a stable table by the window is safer than a narrow sill. See [the menorah at home in Lamorinda](/learn/menorah-at-home-in-lamorinda).

Electric menorahs do not fulfill the mitzvah, according to Chabad.org: the mitzvah requires a real flame of oil or wax. They are lovely for decoration and to publicize the miracle, and can sit beside the real one.

## When lighting is hard

Some people, such as an elderly person living alone, a person in a hospital or care facility, or a student in a dorm, cannot use a flame. Chabad.org suggests that a person who cannot use an ordinary menorah might use tea lights in a tray, which can be filled with water. If no flame at all is allowed, ask the Rabbi. A battery light is sometimes discussed, and blessings should not be recited over it. For those who are elderly or hesitant, see [safety at home](/learn/maakeh-safety-at-home) and our [help for seniors and families](/help/help-for-seniors-and-families).

## Common questions

### How do I light a menorah safely at home?
Use a sturdy, fireproof surface, keep it away from anything that burns, keep it out of children's reach and stay nearby. Light with the shamash and do not move it while it burns.

### How far should a menorah be from curtains?
Fire-safety guidance says at least 12 inches from anything that can burn. A wider margin is better.

### Is it okay to put a menorah on a windowsill?
Chabad.org says only if the window is less than thirty feet above the ground, and the sill is stable. Otherwise use a table near the window.

### Can I use an electric menorah?
For decoration, yes, but the mitzvah requires a real oil or wax flame. Ask the Rabbi if you cannot light a flame.

### What if the flame goes out?
During the first half hour after lighting, Chabad.org advises relighting it. If it goes out later, ask the Rabbi.

### Can I blow out the menorah if I am worried?
Chabad.org says this is not normally recommended before the time is over. If you feel unsafe, use tea lights and a tray, and ask the Rabbi.
