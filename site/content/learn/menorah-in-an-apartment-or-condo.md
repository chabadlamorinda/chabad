In an apartment, condo or dorm, place the menorah inside the doorway of your home, on the side opposite the mezuzah, or in a window facing the street if it is not too high up. If your building does not allow open flames, speak to the Rabbi before you decide on a substitute.

## The two places for the menorah

The menorah is meant to be seen, so that people remember the miracle. Jewish law describes two ways:

- **At the doorway:** place it on a chair or small table just inside the door, on the left side as you walk in, so that you pass between the menorah and the [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) on the right. This is the Chabad custom, and it works well in an apartment, where the inside door is often the only door.
- **In a window facing the street:** this is also a good custom, as long as the window is not too far from the ground.

## What about upper floors?

The sources note that a menorah should be lower than 20 cubits (amot), roughly 30 feet, so passersby can see it. A window on a high floor is often above that, so a window may not publicize anything. For that reason, many Chabad families use the doorway inside the home. If you are not sure how high your window counts, ask the Rabbi.

## How high off the floor

For a doorway, the lights are ideally between about 12 and 40 inches off the floor. Set the menorah on something sturdy and fireproof, away from curtains and anything that burns, and out of children's reach. See [the placement and lighting steps](/learn/chanukah-menorah).

## Dorm rooms and shared spaces

Students lighting at college should light in their room, or in a shared dining space. Many dorms prohibit open flames, which makes this tricky. Where a flame is not allowed, ask the Rabbi before deciding what to do. See [Chanukah at work and college](/learn/chanukah-at-work-and-college) and [Jewish students in Lamorinda](/learn/jewish-students-in-lamorinda).

## What if flames are not allowed?

An electric menorah is not considered to fulfill the mitzvah, according to Jewish law: the Temple menorah burned fuel with a wick and fire. If you truly cannot have a flame, such as in a care facility or a building with strict rules, ask the Rabbi: there may be another place to light, or another solution for your case. An electric menorah in a window can still publicize the miracle, as long as a real menorah is also lit. Your situation is personal, so please ask the Rabbi first.

## Common questions

### Where should I put the menorah in an apartment?
Inside the doorway on the left, opposite the mezuzah, or in a street-facing window that is not too high up.

### How high can a window menorah be?
The sources give a limit of 20 cubits (amot), roughly 30 feet from the ground. Higher than that, the doorway is the better place.

### Can I light a menorah in a dorm?
Yes, where you live, if the rules allow. If your dorm bans flames, ask the Rabbi what to do.

### Can I use an electric menorah?
It does not fulfill the mitzvah on its own, but it can be used to publicize the holiday along with a real one. Ask the Rabbi if you cannot use flames at all.

### Can I use tea lights?
Tea lights work if each one burns long enough. See [oil or candles](/learn/menorah-oil-and-candles).

### Where do I light if I am in a hospital or care home?
Ask the Rabbi in advance; there are options. See [help for seniors and families](/help/help-for-seniors-and-families).
