Tzfat, also spelled Safed (צפת), is a hill city in northern Israel and one of the four holy cities, along with Jerusalem, Hebron and Tiberias. It is known as the city of Kabbalah because the great mystics of the 1500s lived, taught and are buried there.

## One of the four holy cities

Tzfat sits in the northern hills with views toward Tiberias and the Sea of Galilee. Some identify it with Kartan, a Levite city in the territory of Naftali, and an old tradition counts it among the cities of refuge. A Jewish community lived there since the Second Temple period. After the Spanish Expulsion of 1492, many Jewish scholars settled in Tzfat in the 1500s, and it became a center of Torah and a textile town.

## Who lived in Tzfat

- **Rabbi Yosef Caro** lived there from about 1535 to 1575, led its court and compiled the Shulchan Aruch, the code of Jewish law. He also recorded mystical teachings.
- **Rabbi Yitzchak Luria, the Arizal,** arrived in 1570 and died two years later. His students, especially Rabbi Chaim Vital, recorded his teachings.
- **Rabbi Moshe Cordovero** was the leading kabbalist before the Arizal.
- **Rabbi Shlomo Alkabetz** wrote *Lecha Dodi*, the song we sing to welcome Shabbat. See [Kabbalat Shabbat](/learn/kabbalat-shabbat).
- **Rabbi Moshe Alshich** was a famous preacher and Torah teacher.
- **Rabbi Elazar Azkari** wrote the hymn *Yedid Nefesh*.

In 1577, the first printing press in the Holy Land was set up in Tzfat. In 1777, a group of chassidim from Russia came and some settled in Tzfat, and several Old City synagogues still carry the names of that history. In the 1970s the Rebbe sent emissaries to strengthen Chabad's presence there.

## What visitors see

- **The Safed blue.** Many buildings and graves are painted blue. Reasons range from the practical to the mystical, including a reminder of Heaven and G-d's Throne of Glory.
- **The Old Cemetery,** where many of these sages are buried. People come to pray and give charity.
- **The two Ari synagogues.** The Ashkenazi one is said to stand where the Arizal and his students welcomed Shabbat on Friday afternoons. The Sephardic one near the old cemetery is the older.
- **The Old City lanes,** galleries and views.

Check current opening arrangements before you travel, and ask local Chabad families for guidance.

## Shabbat in Tzfat

Many visitors plan a Shabbat in Tzfat so they can sing *Lecha Dodi* in the city where it was written. Book your accommodations early, and prepare for Shabbat so you are not rushing. Read [Preparing for Shabbat](/learn/preparing-for-shabbat) and [the Shabbat candles guide](/learn/shabbat-candles).

## Why Tzfat still calls to Jews

Rabbi Avraham Azulai wrote that the air of Tzfat makes it easier to grasp the inner depths of Torah, probably meaning a spiritual purity. After plagues and earthquakes, Tzfat has revived over recent decades, with tens of thousands of Jews, many synagogues, schools and art galleries. Families from Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda who visit often come home with a new love for the mystical side of Judaism. For an introduction, read about [Kabbalah](/learn/kabbalah).

## Common questions

### Why is Tzfat called the city of Kabbalah?
Tzfat became the center of Jewish mysticism in the 1500s, when the Arizal, Rabbi Moshe Cordovero and other great kabbalists lived and taught there.

### Why is Tzfat blue?
Many buildings and graves are painted a sky blue. Explanations range from practical ones to a reminder of Heaven and G-d's Throne of Glory.

### Who wrote Lecha Dodi?
Rabbi Shlomo Alkabetz wrote *Lecha Dodi* in Tzfat. We sing it on Friday night to welcome Shabbat.

### Who wrote the Shulchan Aruch?
Rabbi Yosef Caro wrote it, and he lived in Tzfat from about 1535 to 1575.

### Is Tzfat one of the four holy cities?
Yes. Jerusalem, Hebron, Tiberias and Tzfat are the four holy cities of Israel.
