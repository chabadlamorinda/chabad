A good Jewish afterschool program keeps children safe, helps with homework, gives them real activities and friends, and adds Jewish learning and values in a warm, natural way, with kosher food. When you look at programs in Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda or the wider East Bay, use a simple checklist: people, program, food, Jewish content and fit.

## Why choose a Jewish afterschool?

After school, children are tired but curious. A Jewish program can use those hours to build skills and friendships and to let a child experience Jewish life as something fun, not a once-a-week obligation. It also helps working parents. Jewish education (*chinuch*, חִנּוּךְ) is not only in the classroom; see [choosing Jewish education](/learn/choosing-jewish-education) and [chinuch](/learn/chinuch).

## A checklist: what to look for

**1. The people**
- **Warm, caring staff.** Watch how adults speak with children. Look for careful, patient educators who are skilled at what they teach and who keep children included rather than pushing them away.
- **Enough adults.** Ask about the number of children per adult.
- **Background checks and first aid training.** These are fair questions to ask any program.
- **Open communication.** Can you reach someone easily? Do you get updates?

**2. Safety and routine**
- **Safe pickup and drop-off.** Who may pick up your child, and how is that checked?
- **A clear daily schedule.** Snack, homework, activity, free play.
- **Allergy and health plans.** Tell the director about allergies in writing.

**3. Homework and learning**
- **Homework help.** A quiet time and a helper for questions.
- **Enrichment.** Sports, science, art and drama keep afternoons rich.

**4. Kosher food**
- If your family keeps kosher, ask whether all food is kosher and under whose supervision. See [kosher in Lamorinda](/learn/kosher-in-lamorinda) and the local program [Bay Kosher](/programs/bay-kosher).
- Ask what happens with food that families send. For halachic questions, ask the Rabbi.

**5. Jewish content**
- **Natural, not forced.** Look for a program where Jewish songs, holidays, stories, Hebrew and Shabbat are part of the day.
- **Welcoming to every family.** Children come with different backgrounds. A good program welcomes them all and does not make anyone feel behind.
- **Values.** Kindness, respect and responsibility, taught by example.

**6. Fit for your child**
- **Visit first.** Watch an afternoon if you can, and let your child see it.
- **Ask your child what they enjoy.** Some children love sports, others art and quiet.
- **Check the hours and the age range** against your family's schedule.

## Questions to ask any program

1. What does a typical afternoon look like?
2. How many children are there for each adult?
3. How do you handle homework, behavior and conflict between children?
4. What is the food policy, and who supervises kosher food?
5. How is Jewish learning part of the day?
6. How do you communicate with parents?
7. What are the hours, ages and registration process?

## Common questions

### What should I look for in a Jewish afterschool program?
Look for caring staff, safety routines, homework help, enrichment, kosher food and Jewish learning that feels natural. Visit before you decide.

### What ages does CKids Lamorinda Afterschool serve?
Ages 5 to 13, according to the program page. Hours are weekdays from 3:00 to 6:00 PM.

### Does a Jewish afterschool serve kosher food?
Ask every program. CKids Lamorinda lists snacks and lunch or dinner; call 925-384-7242 to ask about the kosher details for your child.

### Do we need to be observant or members to join?
Chabad of Lamorinda welcomes every family and does not require membership. Ask about registration when you call.

### How is a Jewish afterschool different from Hebrew school?
Afterschool is a daily care and enrichment program with Jewish content, while Hebrew school is focused learning once a week. Many families use both.

### What if my child has allergies or special needs?
Tell the director in writing before enrolling and ask what support is possible. For halachic or food questions, ask the Rabbi.
