Jewish holidays follow the Hebrew calendar, so they land on a different date of the regular calendar every year, and some fall on school days in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda. The simple plan is to look up this year's holidays, compare them with your school district's calendar in early fall, and write to the school in advance.

## Why the dates move every year

The Jewish calendar follows the moon and is adjusted to the seasons, so Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot and Passover shift against the calendar on the wall. Your child's school calendar is set by the school district, and it may or may not mark the holiday. Do not assume the school knows. See this year's dates on the [Jewish holidays page](/jewish-holidays) and the week's times on [this week](/this-week).

## Which days matter most

Jewish holidays are not all the same. On some days, Jewish tradition asks us to stop ordinary work and school, and on others daily life goes on.

- **Rosh Hashanah** (the Jewish New Year, two days) and **Yom Kippur** (the Day of Atonement, one day) are the most widely observed. Read [Rosh Hashanah for Beginners](/learn/rosh-hashanah-for-beginners).
- **The first two days of Sukkot**, followed by **Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah**, are festival days. See [Sukkot](/holidays/sukkot) and [Simchat Torah](/holidays/simchat-torah).
- **The first two and last two days of Passover**: see [Passover](/holidays/passover).
- **Shavuot**, in late spring, often close to finals and graduation season: see [Shavuot](/holidays/shavuot).
- **Shabbat**, every Friday at sundown to Saturday night, matters for Saturday games and events. Read [Kids' Sports on Shabbat](/learn/shabbat-and-youth-sports).

Chanukah and Purim are joyful holidays that do not require staying home from school. For other days and for how strictly your family keeps them, ask the Rabbi.

## Check your district's calendar

Public schools in our area are run by separate districts: the Lafayette elementary district, the Moraga School District, the Orinda elementary district, and the Acalanes Union High School District, which serves high schoolers from all three towns (Acalanes, Campolindo, Las Lomas and Miramonte high schools, as listed on the district's website). Each district publishes its own academic calendar for the year on its website, and the Acalanes Union and Moraga districts list their calendars for the coming years. Look at the calendar for your child's schools, and for siblings in more than one district, check each one.

We do not print the districts' dates here, because they change. Take this year's Jewish dates and put them next to the district calendar. Mark any conflicts, including **tests, finals, sports games, rehearsals and picture day**.

## California law on absences for religious holidays

This is general information, not legal advice. California's Education Code section 48205 lists "justifiable personal reasons" for an excused absence, and one of them is "observance of a holiday or ceremony of his or her religion" (read the current text on the state's official legislative site). The law asks that the absence be requested in writing by a parent or guardian and approved by the principal or a designated representative. The same section says a student who is excused may make up the assignments and tests that were missed, and receives full credit for work completed satisfactorily within a reasonable time. Details of how a school handles these requests vary, so call the school office if you are unsure.

## A friendly note to teachers

A short, early, positive note works best. Here is a model:

> Dear Ms. ___, our family observes the Jewish holiday of ___ on ___, and ___ will be absent. We would be glad to make up any work. Please let us know if a test or project is planned that week, and we can plan together. Thank you!

Send it a few weeks ahead. Mention the days of the holiday in the note, since many Jewish holidays begin the evening before. If your child is nervous, offer to let them share a little about the holiday with the class. See [Presenting a Jewish Holiday in Class](/learn/presenting-a-jewish-holiday-in-class).

## High school and college

Teens often have the hardest conflicts: sports, clubs, AP exams and finals. Talk to coaches and teachers early, before the season, not the night before. College students, including those at Saint Mary's College in Moraga, can speak to their professors and dean in the same way. For work, see [Jewish Holidays at School and Work](/learn/jewish-holidays-at-school-and-work).

## Common questions

### Can my child miss school for Rosh Hashanah or Yom Kippur in California?
Yes. California's Education Code lists observance of a holiday or ceremony of the pupil's religion as a justifiable reason for an excused absence, with a written request from a parent and approval by the school. This is not legal advice, so check with your school office.

### Do Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda schools close for Jewish holidays?
Each district sets its own calendar, so check the official calendar of your child's district. Do not assume that a holiday is marked, and plan a note to the school either way.

### How far in advance should I tell the teacher?
A few weeks ahead is ideal, and as soon as you see a conflict on the calendar. Teachers appreciate knowing before a test or a project is planned.

### Which Jewish holidays do most families keep their children home for?
Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, the first days of Sukkot, and the first and last days of Passover are the most common. Practice differs among families, so ask the Rabbi what is right for yours.

### What if my child has a test or a game on a Jewish holiday?
Ask the teacher or coach for a make-up date as early as you can. The law allows make-up work for an excused absence. If you need help finding the right words, call Rabbi Labkowski.

### Where can my family spend the holidays in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda?
Chabad of Lamorinda in Lafayette welcomes every Jew, with no membership required. Call 925-384-7242 to hear what is planned, or sign up on the [High Holidays form](/forms/high-holidays).
