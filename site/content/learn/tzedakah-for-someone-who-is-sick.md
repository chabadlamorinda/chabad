When someone is sick, Jewish tradition encourages us to give tzedakah, charity, in their honor. Giving charity in honor of a sick person is a strong merit, which tradition says can annul harsh decrees and even save from death. It is one of several things a person can do alongside visiting, Tehillim and prayer.

## What Jewish tradition teaches

Among the ways to help a sick person, tzedakah is listed first: giving charity in the person's honor is a strong merit. No details are given on how much to give, to whom or how, so there is no set amount and no formula. Any amount given with a full heart is a gift.

Tzedakah (צְדָקָה) means "justice." Giving is not a deal with G-d, and no one should ever be told or feel that someone's illness is connected to what they gave. This is a tradition of love and hope. If you have a personal question, [talk to the Rabbi](/help/talk-to-the-rabbi).

## How to give in someone's merit

You can simply set aside a gift and say, in your own words, that it is in the merit of the person's recovery. Ask the Rabbi if you would like guidance on how and where to give. What the tradition does explain is how to name the person:

1. **Use the person's Hebrew name and their mother's Hebrew name,** for example "[name], son/daughter of [mother's name]." If the mother's name is unknown, use the father's. A parent praying for a child says "my son" or "my daughter."
2. **If you do not know the Hebrew name,** read about [adding a name for healing](/learn/adding-a-name-for-healing) and [Hebrew names](/learn/hebrew-names).
3. **For where to direct your gift,** see [giving priorities](/learn/tzedakah-priorities).

## Alongside tzedakah: Tehillim and Mi Sheberach

Any prayers and psalms work, but many people say a short set of Psalms (20, 30, 121, 130 and 142), or a longer set when there is more time. Some say the psalm for the year of life the patient is in (for a 75-year-old, Psalm 76). See [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) for how to begin.

It is also customary to have a *Mi Sheberach*, a prayer for healing, said during the Torah reading in synagogue. Read more about [Mi Sheberach](/learn/mi-sheberach). ## Common questions

### Can I give charity for a sick person?
Yes. Giving charity in honor of a sick person is a strong merit, which tradition says can annul harsh decrees and even save from death. It is never a promise, so also pray, visit and ask the Rabbi for guidance.

### How much tzedakah should I give for someone who is ill?
There is no set amount, and no details are given on how much, to whom or how. Give what you can, with love.

### Which name do I use?
Use the person's Hebrew name and their mother's Hebrew name. If the mother's name is unknown, use the father's. Titles such as "Rabbi" are skipped.

### Should I also say Tehillim?
Any prayers and psalms work, and there are common sets of Psalms. Read the guide on [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim).

### Does giving tzedakah guarantee a recovery?
No. Judaism teaches a strong merit and hope, but no one can promise an outcome, and no one is to blame for illness. For personal support, call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242 or the Rebbetzin at 925-384-7241.
