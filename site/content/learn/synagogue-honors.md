Synagogue honors, called *kibbudim* (כִּבּוּדִים), are small roles given to people during the service: opening the ark, being called up to the Torah (an aliyah), lifting the scroll (hagbah), dressing it (gelilah) and leading prayers. The gabbai, the person who runs the service, decides who receives each one, and visitors are often glad to be asked.

## What an honor is, and why we give them

In a synagogue, taking part in the Torah service is a privilege. Chabad.org explains that the *gabbai* (גבאי), the warden, keeps the synagogue organized, calls people up to the bimah for aliyot, and gives out the other honors. Leading the prayers is also considered an honor. Many are handed out to people marking something special: a birthday, a yahrzeit, a new baby, an upcoming wedding or bar mitzvah. If you would like one, you can simply tell the gabbai. ## The honors, one by one

**Opening the ark (pesichah, פְּתִיחָה)**
Someone opens the *aron kodesh*, the ark, so the Torah scroll can be taken out or, later, returned. This is a good first honor, because it requires no knowledge at all. Everyone stands while the ark is open.

**Carrying the Torah**
The scroll is carried from the ark to the reading table (the bimah) and back, often with the congregation singing as it passes. Many people touch it with a siddur or the corner of a tallit and kiss that.

**An aliyah (עֲלִיָּה)**
This means being called up by name to say the blessings before and after a section of the reading. It is the best-known honor. For the details of how it works, read [Aliyah](/learn/aliyah).

**Hagbah (הַגְבָּהָה): lifting the Torah**
After the reading, the scroll is raised so that the congregation can see the writing, and everyone looks at it and says a verse. The scroll is usually opened at least three columns wide. Customs vary on the details (how to hold it, when to roll it closed), so if you are offered this honor, ask the gabbai or the Rabbi to show you beforehand.

**Lifting safely:**
1. Practice with the Rabbi or gabbai before the service if you can.
2. Keep a firm, balanced stance, and lift with your legs and a straight back.
3. Slide the scroll toward you so the weight is centered, then lift with both hands.
4. If it feels too heavy, it is fine to say so and decline, or to ask a stronger person to help. The scroll is heavy.

**Gelilah (גְּלִילָה): dressing the Torah**
The person who does gelilah rolls the scroll closed and dresses it with its binder, cover and often a pointer and crown, while the person who lifted it holds it. Ask the gabbai which duties go together in your community.

**Leading prayers**
The prayer leader, the *chazzan* or *shliach tzibbur*, leads some of the prayers aloud. Often this is a member of the congregation. See [Leading the Prayers](/learn/leading-the-prayers).

## Who is given them

The gabbai decides. Chabad customs about who is called for specific honors (for example, who may be called to the Torah and when) have details, such as the special places given to a Kohen and a Levi, and these can differ by community and by occasion. If you are new, just tell the gabbai your Hebrew name if you know it, or ask the Rabbi. See [Getting a Hebrew Name as an Adult](/learn/getting-a-hebrew-name-as-an-adult).

## Common questions

### What is hagbah?
Hagbah is the honor of lifting the open Torah scroll after the reading so the congregation can see its writing.

### What is gelilah?
Gelilah is the honor of rolling the Torah closed and dressing it with its cover and ornaments.

### How do I get an honor in synagogue?
Tell the gabbai or the Rabbi that you would like one, and let them know the occasion, such as a birthday or a yahrzeit. At Chabad of Lamorinda visitors are warmly offered honors such as an aliyah or opening the ark when they would like one: just ask, or call 925-384-7242.

### Who decides who gets the honors?
The gabbai decides. Chabad.org describes him as the one who gives out the honors and calls people up.

### Is it an honor to be asked to open the ark?
Yes. It is a simple and meaningful honor, and a good first one for a newcomer.

### Do I need to know Hebrew to take part?
Not for opening the ark or carrying the Torah. For an aliyah, you say a short blessing that you can read from a card.
