The most meaningful Jewish gifts are ones that help someone do a mitzvah: a kosher mezuzah for a new home, Shabbat candlesticks for a bride, a siddur for a bar mitzvah, or a pushka for a new baby. A gift like this is used again and again, and every time it is used, the giver shares in the mitzvah.

## Why a mitzvah gift?

In Jewish tradition, objects used for a mitzvah become special. Making a mitzvah beautiful, called *hiddur mitzvah*, is itself a mitzvah. A silver Kiddush cup or a handsome Chanukah menorah honors G-d and makes the mitzvah more joyful. A gift that brings someone closer to Jewish life is a gift to the soul.

## Gifts by occasion

**For a new home (chanukat habayit)**
- A kosher [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) for the front door. Always buy a case **with a kosher scroll** from a reliable source; a case alone is just decoration.
- A pushka (charity box) for the kitchen.
- Bread and salt, a traditional blessing for a new home.
- See our guide to [chanukat habayit](/learn/chanukat-habayit).

**For a new baby**
- A [pushka](/learn/pushka) in the baby's name, with a coin to start.
- A children's book of Jewish stories or prayers, or a Modeh Ani poster.
- A tzedakah donation in the baby's honor.

**For a bar mitzvah**
- A siddur, Chumash or book of Tehillim, inscribed with his Hebrew name.
- A tallit bag, or a contribution toward tefillin. Tefillin must be kosher and fitted correctly, so coordinate with the family and the Rabbi.
- Tzedakah in multiples of 18, which equals *chai*, "life."

**For a bat mitzvah**
- Shabbat candlesticks, so she can light her own candles.
- A beautiful siddur or a book about great Jewish women.
- A personal book of Tehillim.

**For a wedding**
- Shabbat candlesticks, a Kiddush cup, a challah board and cover, or a hand-washing cup.
- A set of Jewish books for their new [home library](/learn/jewish-home-library).

**For holidays**
- **Rosh Hashanah:** a honey dish and kosher honey.
- **Chanukah:** a menorah, dreidels, or [Chanukah gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt).
- **Purim:** [mishloach manot](/learn/mishloach-manot), a gift of food.
- **Passover:** a seder plate or a Haggadah.

## Tips for giving to observant friends

- **Food:** give sealed food with a reliable kosher symbol. Home-baked goods may not be eaten in a kosher home.
- **Wine:** choose kosher wine. See our guide to [kosher wine](/learn/kosher-wine).
- **Kitchen items:** new dishes and pots may need immersion in a mikvah before use, so it is a nice touch to let the recipient know they are brand new.
- **Shabbat:** do not deliver a gift on Shabbat or a holiday; give it before or after.

## Common questions

### Why do Jews give gifts in multiples of 18?
The Hebrew letters of the word *chai*, "life," add up to 18. Giving $18, $36 or $180 is a way of wishing the recipient a good and long life.

### What is a good Jewish gift for a non-Jewish friend?
A box of kosher treats, a book about Jewish holidays, or a beautiful Chanukah menorah to admire are all lovely. Jewish tradition values kindness and friendship with all people.

### Is a mezuzah a good housewarming gift?
Yes, one of the best, as long as it includes a kosher scroll. Offer to help put it up or to bring someone who can, and the gift becomes a mitzvah together.

### Can I give a gift of tzedakah instead?
Yes. A donation in someone's honor is a beautiful gift, especially for someone who has everything. Let the person know which cause their gift supported.
