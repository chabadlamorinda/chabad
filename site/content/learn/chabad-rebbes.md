Chabad-Lubavitch has been led by seven Rebbes over more than two hundred years. Each one built on the teachings of the [Baal Shem Tov](/learn/baal-shem-tov) and his predecessors, guiding Chassidim through persecution, war and emigration, and spreading Torah and love for every Jew.

## What is a Rebbe?

A Rebbe is a Chassidic leader and teacher, a guide for the soul. Chassidim turn to the Rebbe for Torah teaching, advice and blessings. In Chabad, the Rebbes also wrote and taught the deep Chassidic philosophy known as [Chassidus](/learn/chassidus).

The name *Chabad* is an acronym for three Hebrew words: *Chochmah* (wisdom), *Binah* (understanding) and *Daat* (knowledge). *Lubavitch* is the name of the small town in Russia that was the movement's home for over a century.

## The seven Rebbes

### 1. Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi (1745 to 1812)
Known as the Alter Rebbe, he founded Chabad. A student of the Maggid of Mezritch, he wrote the [Tanya](/learn/tanya), the foundational book of Chabad thought, and the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, a code of Jewish law. He was imprisoned by the Russian government on false charges and freed on the 19th of Kislev in 1798, a day Chassidim celebrate to this day.

### 2. Rabbi Dovber of Lubavitch (1773 to 1827)
Known as the Mitteler Rebbe, the "middle Rebbe," he was the son of the Alter Rebbe. He moved the movement's center to the town of Lubavitch and wrote many works explaining Chassidic ideas in depth.

### 3. Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Lubavitch (1789 to 1866)
Known as the Tzemach Tzedek, after his books of Jewish law, he was the grandson of the Alter Rebbe. He was both a great legal authority and a master of Chassidus, and he defended Jewish communities against harsh government decrees.

### 4. Rabbi Shmuel of Lubavitch (1834 to 1882)
Known as the Rebbe Maharash, he was the youngest son of the Tzemach Tzedek. He traveled widely to protect Jews and is famous for the motto *L'chatchilah ariber*: when facing an obstacle, do not try to go under it; go over it from the start.

### 5. Rabbi Shalom DovBer of Lubavitch (1860 to 1920)
Known as the Rebbe Rashab, he founded the Tomchei Temimim yeshiva, training a generation of devoted scholars and teachers. His systematic discourses are central to Chabad study.

### 6. Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (1880 to 1950)
Known as the Previous Rebbe, he fought to keep Judaism alive under Soviet persecution. He was arrested and sentenced, and his release in 1927 is celebrated on the 12th and 13th of Tammuz. He came to the United States in 1940 and began building Jewish life in America.

### 7. Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson (1902 to 1994)
Known simply as the Rebbe, he led Chabad from 1950, after the passing of his father-in-law, the Previous Rebbe. He sent thousands of emissary couples around the world to bring Torah and kindness to every Jew, wherever they are. Read more about [the Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe).

## The legacy today

Today there are Chabad centers in many countries around the world, each run by emissaries, called *shluchim*, carrying forward the Rebbes' mission. Chabad of Lamorinda is part of this family.

## Common questions

### Are the Chabad Rebbes related to each other?
Yes. Each Rebbe was a son, son-in-law or grandson of a previous Rebbe, so the leadership stayed within the Schneersohn family.

### Why are there special days for the Rebbes?
Chassidim mark the birthdays, yahrzeits and days of liberation of the Rebbes with gatherings called farbrengens, sharing their teachings and taking on good resolutions.

### Where can I learn the Rebbes' teachings?
Start with the [Tanya](/learn/tanya) and Chassidic discourses, many available in English. Ask the Rabbi to recommend a good place to begin.
