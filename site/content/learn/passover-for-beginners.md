Passover (Pesach) is the eight-day spring holiday celebrating the Jewish people's freedom from slavery in Egypt. For a week we do not eat bread or other leavened grain, we eat matzah, and on the first nights (the first two nights outside Israel) we gather for the Seder to retell the story. If this is your first Passover, you can begin with three simple steps.

## What the holiday is

Our ancestors were slaves in Egypt until G-d brought them out with great miracles. The name Passover recalls that G-d "passed over" the Jewish homes during the last plague. Every year we do not only remember this: our sages say each of us must see ourselves as though we ourselves left Egypt. Chassidic teaching adds that Egypt (in Hebrew, "Mitzrayim") also means the limits we face in our own lives, and Passover is a chance to go free. Read the full [Exodus story](/learn/exodus-story).

## Step one: chametz

Chametz is any food made from wheat, barley, rye, oats or spelt that has risen. During Passover we may not eat it, or own or benefit from it. So before the holiday we clean our homes, kitchens and cars of chametz, and we hold a ceremonial search the night before the Seder. Leftover chametz is burned in the morning. Chametz we do not want to throw away, such as unopened food, is sold to a non-Jew for the holiday, which the Rabbi arranges for you. [Sell your chametz online](/forms/sell-chametz), and see [chametz](/learn/chametz), [selling your chametz](/learn/selling-your-chametz) and [kosher for Passover](/learn/kosher-for-passover).

## Step two: matzah

Matzah is flat bread made of only flour and water, baked in under eighteen minutes so it cannot rise. It is the "bread of affliction" and also the bread of redemption. Chabad families use shmurah matzah, watched from the time of harvest, for the Seder. [Order your matzah](/forms/matzah) and read [about matzah](/learn/matzah).

## Step three: the Seder

The Seder (meaning "order") has fifteen steps: four cups of wine or grape juice, matzah, bitter herbs, the Seder plate, and the telling of the Exodus from a book called the Haggadah. A child asks the Four Questions, and the whole evening is built so children stay curious. You do not need to be an expert. You only need a Haggadah, matzah, wine or grape juice, and people around the table. See [the Seder](/learn/passover-seder), [the Seder plate](/learn/seder-plate), [the Four Questions](/learn/four-questions) and [Passover with kids](/learn/passover-with-kids).

## The rest of the holiday

The first two days and last two days (outside Israel) are festival days with candle lighting, meals and services. In between, we keep eating matzah and no chametz. On the last day, Yizkor is said, and we count the Omer each night until Shavuot. See [counting the Omer](/learn/counting-the-omer). Chabad custom is not to eat matzah that has become wet (gebrokts, such as matzah balls) until the last day of the holiday; ask the Rabbi what to do in your household.

## Common questions

### What is Passover?
Passover is an eight-day spring Jewish holiday celebrating the Exodus from slavery in Egypt, with matzah and the Seder.

### What do you eat on Passover?
Matzah, vegetables, fruit, meat, fish, eggs, dairy and wine, as long as they are kosher for Passover. We avoid chametz: bread, pasta, cereal and anything leavened.

### What is chametz?
Chametz is grain (wheat, barley, rye, oats, spelt) that has been in contact with water and risen. It may not be eaten or owned during Passover.

### How do I get ready for Passover?
Clean your home of chametz, sell what is left through the Rabbi, buy kosher for Passover food and matzah, and plan your Seder. Start a few weeks ahead.

### What is a Seder?
The Seder is the festive meal on the first nights of Passover with fifteen steps, including four cups of wine, matzah, bitter herbs and the story of the Exodus.

### Why do we eat matzah on Passover?
Matzah recalls that the Jews left Egypt so quickly that their dough had no time to rise. It is also called the bread of affliction.
