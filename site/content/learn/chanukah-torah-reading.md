On each day of Chanukah, a second passage from the Book of Numbers (Bamidbar), chapter 7, is read from the Torah. It describes the gifts brought by one of the twelve tribal princes when the Mishkan, the Sanctuary in the desert, was dedicated, and the reading on the eighth day ends with the lighting of the Menorah.

## What do we read each day of Chanukah?

The reading is taken from one long passage, Numbers 7:1 to 8:4, divided over the eight days. According to the schedule on Chabad.org:

- **Day 1:** Numbers 7:1 to 17
- **Day 2:** 7:18 to 29
- **Day 3:** 7:24 to 35
- **Day 4:** 7:30 to 41
- **Day 5:** 7:36 to 47
- **Day 6 (Rosh Chodesh):** 7:42 to 47
- **Day 7:** 7:48 to 53 when it is Rosh Chodesh (in years when it is not, the reading is a little longer)
- **Day 8:** 7:54 to 8:4

The passages overlap a little, so that each day's reading includes the prince of that day. On Rosh Chodesh the Chanukah passage is shorter, because the Rosh Chodesh reading comes first.

## Why do we read about the Mishkan on Chanukah?

The word Chanukah means "dedication." The Torah reading is about the first great dedication in Jewish history, and Chanukah celebrates the rededication of the Holy Temple. Chabad.org gives a beautiful explanation: the Mishkan was finished on the 25th of Kislev, but G-d told Moshe to wait until Rosh Chodesh Nissan to dedicate it. The 25th of Kislev was, so to speak, left waiting, and it was given the rededication of the Temple by the Hasmoneans as its own day of dedication.

You can read more about the Mishkan in our guide to [the Mishkan](/learn/the-mishkan), and about the Temple in [the Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple).

## How does the reading end?

The eighth day opens with the gift of the last prince and then adds up all the gifts. That summary begins with the words "Zot chanukat hamizbe'ach," "This was the dedication of the altar." This is why the last day is called [Zot Chanukah](/learn/zot-chanukah). The reading then continues into chapter 8, which begins with the commandment to Aharon to light the Menorah. It is a fitting finish for a holiday of lights.

## When Chanukah falls on Shabbat or Rosh Chodesh

When a day of Chanukah falls on Shabbat, the weekly Torah portion is read from the first scroll and that day's Chanukah passage from a second scroll. The Shabbat of Chanukah also has its own haftarah, the prophetic reading, which is from the Book of Zechariah, and Chabad.org lists a reading from the First Book of Kings, about the vessels of the Temple, for a second Shabbat. When Rosh Chodesh Tevet falls on Chanukah, a different arrangement is used: see [Rosh Chodesh Tevet during Chanukah](/learn/rosh-chodesh-tevet-on-chanukah). For how the Torah is read in general, see [the Torah reading](/learn/torah-reading).

Each year the dates and the exact readings differ, so look at the calendar for this year. To find which day is Shabbat, see [this week](/this-week) and the [Jewish holidays calendar](/jewish-holidays).

## Do not confuse it with the reading in Nissan

Chabad has a custom of reading the passage of the princes' gifts, one prince each day, during the first twelve days of the month of Nissan, followed by a short prayer of thanks. That is a separate custom. On Chanukah the reading is part of the regular Torah reading in the synagogue.

## Common questions

### What is the Torah reading on Hanukkah?
It is Numbers 7:1 to 8:4, read in parts over the eight days. Each day features the gift of one of the twelve tribal princes at the dedication of the Mishkan.

### Why do we read about the princes on Chanukah?
Chanukah means "dedication," and this passage tells of the dedication of the Mishkan. According to a teaching on Chabad.org, the 25th of Kislev, the day the Mishkan was finished, had to wait for its dedication, and it received the rededication of the Temple as its own day of dedication.

### Is the Torah read on Chanukah every day?
Yes. Chabad.org lists a Chanukah Torah reading for each of the eight days. Call the Rabbi to hear the times of services in Lafayette.

### Why is the eighth day called Zot Chanukah?
The reading on the eighth day sums up all the princes' gifts with the words "Zot chanukat hamizbe'ach," "This was the dedication of the altar."

### What is the haftarah for Shabbat Chanukah?
It is from the Book of Zechariah, which speaks of the Menorah and of light. Chabad.org lists a reading from the First Book of Kings, about the Temple vessels, for a second Shabbat of Chanukah.
