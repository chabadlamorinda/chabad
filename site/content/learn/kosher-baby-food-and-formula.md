Baby food and formula are made in factories that often share equipment between different products, so a kosher symbol on the package is the best guide. Your baby's health always comes first: follow your pediatrician on what your baby needs, and ask the Rabbi about the kosher side.

## Why baby food is complicated

The Orthodox Union (OU) explains in an article on kosher baby food that plants often share production lines between meat, dairy and pareve products. A line that made one product one day may make another the next. Plants deal with this in different ways, such as dedicated pareve lines or careful scheduling and cleaning, and sometimes a rabbi must supervise special koshering.

Ingredients matter too. The OU notes that supplements added for brain and eye development (DHA and ARA) are often made from non-kosher fish oils, and that white grape juice, a common sweetener, is highly sensitive. That article is from 2010, so check the current package and the agency's website for today's products.

## What to look for

Look for a reliable kosher symbol (see [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols)). A "pareve" mark means no milk or meat ingredients, and a "D" or "dairy" mark means dairy. Whether a particular formula is acceptable for your family, and what kind of dairy supervision to look for, is a question for the Rabbi. Call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242.

## When your baby's health comes first

Jewish law gives great weight to a child's needs. The OU notes that Jewish law has leniencies for children and the infirm. If your pediatrician says your baby needs a specific formula, tell the Rabbi, and he will guide you. When a life is in danger, the Torah's principle is pikuach nefesh: saving a life comes first. See [pikuach nefesh](/learn/pikuach-nefesh).

## Homemade baby food

Fresh fruits and vegetables are kosher by nature, though some need checking for insects (see [checking for insects](/learn/checking-for-insects)). If you cook, use your kosher pots and keep to meat and dairy rules (see [meat and milk](/learn/meat-and-milk)).

## Meat and milk for the very young

Very young children do not need to wait between meat and milk, but it is best to feed them something else in between. Ask the Rabbi about your child.

## Passover

Baby food and formula on Passover have their own rules. The OU notes that its rulings change each year. Ask the Rabbi well in advance. See [kosher for Passover](/learn/kosher-for-passover).

## Common questions

### Is baby formula kosher?
It depends on the product and its certification. Look for a reliable symbol, and ask the Rabbi about the formula you use.

### Can I feed my baby a formula without a kosher symbol?
Your baby's health comes first. If your pediatrician says your baby needs a particular formula, ask the Rabbi; Jewish law has important leniencies for children and the sick.

### Is jarred baby food kosher?
Many jarred foods are fruit and vegetable purees, but equipment can be shared. Look for a reliable symbol.

### Does my baby have to wait between meat and milk?
Very young children need not wait but it is best to give something in between.

### Is homemade baby food kosher?
Fresh produce is kosher by nature. Check for insects and use your kosher kitchen setup.
