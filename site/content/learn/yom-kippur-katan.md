Yom Kippur Katan (יוֹם כִּפּוּר קָטָן), the "Minor Yom Kippur," is the day before Rosh Chodesh, the new month. According to Chabad.org, it recalls the sin offering brought in the Temple on Rosh Chodesh, and the most pious observe it with extra prayers, fasting and charity.

Once a month it gives anyone, in Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda or anywhere, a chance to pause, take stock and start the new month fresh. You do not have to fast to benefit from it.

## Why a small Yom Kippur?

Yom Kippur is the great day of forgiveness once a year. The Sages saw Rosh Chodesh as a time of renewal, with a special offering for atonement. So the day before, the thought arose: let us clear our hearts before the new month begins. Many customs grew around it over the centuries, especially in Chassidic and Kabbalistic circles. Read about [Rosh Chodesh](/learn/rosh-chodesh).

## What people do

Customs vary, and no one is required to keep all of them:

- **Extra prayer and Psalms.** In Chabad circles, many recite Psalms and special prayers, and study Chassidic texts. The Rebbe customarily visited the Ohel, his predecessor's resting place, on this day (Chabad.org).
- **Charity.** Giving extra tzedakah is a simple custom for everyone; see [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).
- **Fasting.** The most pious fast until the afternoon or evening. Fasting is not for everyone; if you are ill, pregnant, nursing, elderly or have any health question, do not fast, and ask the Rabbi.
- **Reflection (cheshbon hanefesh).** Take a few minutes to review the past month. See [cheshbon hanefesh](/learn/cheshbon-hanefesh).
- **Teshuvah (return).** Decide on one thing to mend or improve; see [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah).
- **Forgiveness.** Reach out to someone you may have hurt.

## A five-minute monthly check-in

Anyone can try this, at the kitchen table, on the commute or in a quiet corner:

1. **Pause.** Take a breath and thank G-d for the month that passed.
2. **Look back.** Where did I act well? Where did I fall short with family, friends, work or myself?
3. **Say it.** A simple, honest sentence: "I am sorry, and I want to do better."
4. **Repair.** Choose one person to call or one habit to adjust.
5. **Give.** Put a few coins or a donation into the tzedakah box, or give [online](/donate).
6. **Look ahead.** Pick one good deed for the new month.

## When is it?

The date comes from the Jewish calendar. See [this week](/this-week) for upcoming Jewish dates. The day is not observed in every month: it is customarily omitted in months where a festive day or special period falls right before Rosh Chodesh, for example before Rosh Hashanah, in Nissan. Ask the Rabbi for the exact calendar, including when Rosh Chodesh falls near Shabbat.

## Common questions

### What is Yom Kippur Katan?

It is the day before Rosh Chodesh, a "small Yom Kippur" of prayer, charity and repentance. It recalls the sin offering of Rosh Chodesh.

### Do I have to fast on Yom Kippur Katan?

No. Only the most pious fast, and many do not. Health comes first; ask the Rabbi.

### When is Yom Kippur Katan?

The day before each Rosh Chodesh, except in certain months. See [this week](/this-week) and ask the Rabbi for the calendar.

### What do Chabad Chassidim do on Yom Kippur Katan?

Many say extra Psalms, give charity, study Chassidic teachings and reflect. The Rebbe visited the Ohel on this day.

### Can I observe it without fasting?

Yes. Give charity, say a few Psalms, review the month and make one resolution.

### Is it a holiday with restrictions?

No. It is not a holiday, and ordinary work and meals continue.
