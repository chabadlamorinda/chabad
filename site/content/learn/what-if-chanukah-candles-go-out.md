If a Chanukah candle goes out while it should still be burning, it is the custom to relight it, without saying the blessings again. The mitzvah is fulfilled by the lighting itself, so you have not lost anything, but relighting is the proper way to honor it.

## The rule: lighting is what counts

The Talmud teaches that the mitzvah of Chanukah is the kindling. If a light goes out after it was lit properly, you are not required to light it again. Even so, the summary of the laws notes that it is customary to relight any light that goes out during the time it should be burning.

## Do you say the blessings again?

No. The blessings were said at the original lighting, so when you relight there are no new blessings. If the candles went out before you finished lighting, or something else went wrong at the start, ask the Rabbi.

## How to relight

1. Use the shamash, the helper candle, which stays lit beside the menorah for exactly this kind of need. See [what the shamash is](/learn/what-is-the-shamash). If it is out too, use a match.
2. Relight in the same place and way, and stay near the lights.

## Prevent it in the first place

- Keep the menorah away from drafts, open doors and fans. Indoors, a doorway menorah is often protected.
- Use enough fuel: the lights must burn for at least half an hour after nightfall. Short or thin candles can go out too early. See [oil or candles](/learn/menorah-oil-and-candles).
- Make sure the candles sit straight in the holders and the wicks are trimmed.
- Keep a safe distance from curtains, paper and decorations, and never leave a flame near children or pets.

## On Friday and Shabbat

On Shabbat itself we do not light or relight fire. On Friday, the lights are lit early, so they need extra fuel to last into the night, as explained in [Chanukah on Shabbat](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat). If a Friday light goes out after Shabbat has begun, do not relight it. Ask the Rabbi beforehand how much fuel to use.

## Putting the lights out

After the lights have burned for the required half hour after nightfall, the mitzvah has been fulfilled. Because the fuel is holy, it should not be wasted; see [leftover oil and wicks](/learn/leftover-chanukah-oil-and-wicks). If you need to go out earlier or are worried about safety, ask the Rabbi what to do, because safety always comes first.

## Common questions

### What if my Chanukah candles go out?
Relight them without a blessing, using the shamash, while the time for the mitzvah is still on. You do not lose the mitzvah.

### Can you relight Chanukah candles?
Yes. It is the custom to relight, with no new blessings. On Shabbat, you do not relight.

### Do I say the blessing again when I relight?
No. The blessings were said at the original lighting.

### Can I blow out the Chanukah candles after half an hour?
After the lights have burned the required time, the mitzvah is complete. It is best to let the fuel burn out safely, but if you need to put them out, ask the Rabbi about the leftover fuel.

### What if the wind blew out the menorah in the window?
Relight it, from inside the house, and make sure it is sheltered. Consider the doorway placement in [apartments](/learn/menorah-in-an-apartment-or-condo).

### What if a candle goes out because it was too short?
A light must have enough fuel for the required time from the start, so replace it with a candle that will last long enough and light it, without new blessings, and ask the Rabbi if you are unsure. If it happened after Shabbat began, do not relight.
