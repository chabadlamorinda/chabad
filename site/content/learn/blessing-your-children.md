One of the most beautiful moments of the Jewish week takes less than a minute. On Friday night, before Kiddush, parents place their hands on each child's head and bless them. Children who grow up with it remember it for the rest of their lives, and many keep calling home for a blessing even as adults.

## When

Most families bless their children on **Friday night**, after coming home from synagogue or after singing [Shalom Aleichem](/learn/shalom-aleichem), right before Kiddush. Many also do it before Yom Kippur, before a journey, or on a birthday. Any loving moment is a good time.

## The words

Place your hands (or one hand) gently on the child's head.

**For a son:**
> **Yesimcha Elo-him k'Efrayim v'chi-Menasheh.**
> May G-d make you like Ephraim and Menasheh.

**For a daughter:**
> **Yesimech Elo-him k'Sarah, Rivkah, Rachel v'Leah.**
> May G-d make you like Sarah, Rebecca, Rachel and Leah.

**Then, for both**, the [Priestly Blessing](/learn/birkat-kohanim):
> **Yevarechecha Ado-nai v'yishmerecha.** May G-d bless you and protect you.
> **Ya'er Ado-nai panav elecha vichuneka.** May G-d shine His face on you and be gracious to you.
> **Yisa Ado-nai panav elecha v'yasem lecha shalom.** May G-d turn His face to you and give you peace.

## Why Ephraim and Menasheh?

When our forefather Jacob blessed his grandsons, he said that Jews would forever bless their children to be like them. They grew up in Egypt, far from home and surrounded by a foreign culture, and stayed proudly Jewish. And unlike many brothers in the Torah, they never competed; they loved each other. Daughters are blessed to be like our four Mothers, the women who built the Jewish people with faith and kindness.

## Make it your own

After the words, many parents whisper a personal blessing, a word of praise for something the child did that week, or a kiss. Grandparents bless grandchildren; teachers bless students. If a child is away, some parents give the blessing by phone.

## Common questions

### Can a mother bless the children?
Yes. Both parents can bless, together or one after the other.

### My children are teenagers. Is it too late to start?
Never. Tell them you would like to start a new tradition. Teenagers often say little but treasure it.

### Do I need to say it in Hebrew?
Hebrew is traditional, but a blessing said in English or Russian, with love, is a real blessing. Many families say the Hebrew and then the translation.
