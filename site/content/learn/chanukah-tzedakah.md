Yes, Jewish tradition encourages us to give more tzedakah (צדקה, charity) during the eight days of Chanukah. The Lubavitcher Rebbe, quoting the halachic authorities, taught that on Chanukah one should increase in giving tzedakah, because the lights of Chanukah and the giving of charity both bring light into the world and hasten the redemption.

## Where does the custom come from?

In a talk on the second night of Chanukah, the Rebbe quoted the Jewish legal authorities that on Chanukah a person should increase his giving of tzedakah. He connected the two ideas: the Chanukah lights are eternal, and tzedakah, our Sages say, brings the redemption closer. So giving during these days is a way of adding to the light of the menorah.

The Rebbe also explained that on the second day we add something else: the study of Torah. Charity and learning are two sides of the same light. See [Chanukah and Torah study](/learn/chanukah-and-torah-study).

## Gelt: the children's lesson in giving

The best-known giving of Chanukah is *gelt* (Yiddish for "money"), the coins given to children. Here is the thought behind it:

- **A way to teach tzedakah.** Gelt gives children a chance to give. A child can set aside about a tenth of the gelt for charity and use the rest for good, wholesome things.
- **A reward for learning.** Chanukah shares a root with *chinuch*, education, and gelt is given to children as a reward for Torah study.
- **Wealth for holy purposes.** The Greeks tried to turn Jewish possessions toward ungodly ends. Gelt reminds us that money can serve a holy purpose.
- **Helping those in need.** The Magen Avraham, a classic legal authority, notes that money given to the poor lets them buy Chanukah lights without embarrassment.

Chabad custom is to give a little gelt every night, with a larger gift on the fourth or fifth night. The Talmud teaches that the Chanukah lights are not to be used for ordinary tasks such as counting money by their light, so we avoid counting money near the menorah. Our guide to [Chanukah gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt) goes into more detail, and [children, money and tzedakah](/learn/children-money-and-tzedakah) helps parents teach the habit.

## A spiritual gift from the Rebbe

In 1987 the Rebbe spoke to children and asked them to request a special "spiritual Chanukah gelt" from their parents: to make their rooms, and the whole home, a place of prayer, learning and charity. He suggested that a child keep a *pushkah*, a charity box, in his or her room.

## Common questions

### Should I give more tzedakah on Hanukkah?
Yes. Jewish legal authorities, quoted by the Rebbe, say that on Chanukah a person should increase in giving tzedakah.

### How much of the Chanukah gelt should children give to charity?
A common suggestion is about ten percent, with the rest used for good purposes.

### Can I give tzedakah on Shabbat Chanukah?
We do not handle money on Shabbat. The Rebbe suggested giving twice on Friday, once for Friday and once for Shabbat, and on Shabbat itself giving food or drink. Ask the Rabbi about your situation.

### Why does gelt go with tzedakah?
Gelt teaches children to share, rewards learning and reminds us that wealth is meant for holy purposes.

### What is a pushkah?
A *pushkah* (פושקע) is a small charity box. The Rebbe encouraged every child to keep one in his or her room.
