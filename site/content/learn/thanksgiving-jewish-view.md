Thanksgiving is an American holiday, not a Jewish one, yet its central idea, gratitude, is at the very heart of Judaism. In fact, the word "Jew" comes from Yehudah, which means "thanks."

## Thanking G-d every day

Judaism does not set aside one day a year for thanks. Gratitude is built into every day:

- The first words on waking are **[Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani)**, "I thank You," for returning our soul.
- A **[blessing](/learn/blessings)** is said before eating or drinking, and **[Grace after Meals](/learn/birkat-hamazon)** after a meal with bread.
- The Amidah, said three times a day, includes **Modim**, a blessing of thanks for "Your miracles that are with us every day."

The sages call this **[hakarat hatov](/learn/hakarat-hatov)**, "recognizing the good," toward G-d and toward people.

## Gratitude to America

Jews have found in the United States a country of freedom and kindness. The Lubavitcher Rebbe often spoke of America as a "nation of kindness" that allows Jews to live and practice openly, and encouraged gratitude for it. A family gathering to appreciate these blessings fits naturally with Jewish values.

Many Jewish families have a Thanksgiving meal; others prefer not to mark the day. It is not a religious holiday and has no Jewish rituals. If you have a question about your family's practice, ask the Rabbi.

## A kosher Thanksgiving dinner

- **Turkey** is a kosher bird when it is slaughtered and prepared under kosher supervision. Look for a kosher symbol.
- **Keep it meat or dairy.** A turkey dinner is a meat meal: use non-dairy margarine or oil in the stuffing, pies and mashed potatoes, and pareve whipped topping on dessert.
- **Check the label** on stuffing mix, broth, gravy and canned cranberry sauce.
- **Say the blessings**, and bentch (say Grace) after the meal. It is a beautiful moment of thanks at the table.

See our guides to the [kosher kitchen](/learn/kosher-kitchen) and [meat and milk](/learn/meat-and-milk).

## From Thursday to Shabbat

Thanksgiving is always on a Thursday, and Shabbat comes the next evening. With family already together, it is a wonderful weekend to light Shabbat candles, make Kiddush and enjoy a Shabbat meal.

## Common questions

### Is it okay for a Jewish family to celebrate Thanksgiving?
Many do, as a secular day of family and gratitude to the country. It has no religious content to add or avoid. For specific questions, ask the Rabbi.

### What blessing do I say over turkey?
Shehakol, the blessing for meat, fish and eggs. If you eat bread at the meal, the Hamotzi over the bread covers the whole meal.

### Can we have pumpkin pie with whipped cream after turkey?
Not with dairy cream. After meat, Jewish law requires a wait before dairy. Use a pareve topping instead.

### What is the Jewish way to say thank you?
Say it to G-d with a blessing, and to people with words and kindness. Many families go around the table and each person names something they are thankful for.
