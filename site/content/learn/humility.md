"Be very, very humble" (Ethics of the Fathers 4:4). Humility, **anavah** in Hebrew, is one of the most valued traits in Judaism. But it is often misunderstood. Being humble does not mean thinking you are worthless. It means seeing yourself, and everyone else, truthfully.

## Moses, the humblest of men

The Torah says, "Moses was very humble, more than any person on the face of the earth" (Numbers 12:3). This is the same Moses who led the Jewish people out of Egypt, spoke with G-d and gave us the Torah.

How could the greatest prophet be the humblest? Moses knew his gifts. But he knew they were gifts from G-d, and he felt that if someone else had been given the same gifts, perhaps they would have done even more. Humility is not ignoring your strengths; it is knowing where they come from and what they are for.

## Not low self-esteem

Low self-esteem says, "I can't." Humility says, "With G-d's help I can, and it is not about me."

A well-known Chassidic saying, from Rabbi Simcha Bunim of Peshischa, advises keeping two notes in your pockets. One says, "The world was created for my sake" (Talmud, Sanhedrin 37a). The other says, "I am dust and ashes" (Genesis 18:27, Abraham's words). Use each when you need it.

The Midrash teaches that G-d gave the Torah on Mount Sinai, a low mountain, to show that the Torah rests on the humble. But He gave it on a mountain, not a valley: humility includes standing tall for what is right.

## Bittul: the Chassidic view

Chassidus speaks of **bittul**, "self-nullification." This does not mean erasing yourself. It means making room for G-d, the way a glass must be empty to be filled. A person full of himself has no space for anything else. A person with bittul is open to learning, to other people and to G-d's purpose. Paradoxically, this makes a person stronger, not weaker. See [Chassidus](/learn/chassidus).

## Why it matters

- **Learning.** "Who is wise? One who learns from every person" (Ethics of the Fathers 4:1).
- **Peace.** The Talmud says the law follows the school of Hillel because they were gentle and humble, and taught the views of the school of Shammai before their own.
- **Calm.** Much of our [anger](/learn/anger-jewish-view) comes from feeling we were not given our due. Humility lets that go.

## Practical ways to grow in humility

1. **Say thank you,** to G-d and to people. See [hakarat hatov](/learn/hakarat-hatov).
2. **Listen more than you speak.**
3. **Learn from everyone,** including people younger than you or with less education.
4. **Give credit away.**
5. **Do kindnesses quietly,** without needing anyone to know.
6. **Begin the day with [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani),** thanking G-d for life before thinking of anything else.

## Common questions

### Is it wrong to feel proud of an accomplishment?
Feeling joy at what you achieved is healthy. Humility simply remembers that the talent and the opportunity came from G-d.

### Does humility mean letting people walk all over me?
No. Moses stood up to Pharaoh. Humility is honest; it does not mean accepting mistreatment.

### How can a leader be humble?
By serving, not ruling. Moses called himself G-d's servant, and that is how the Torah remembers him.

### I struggle with low self-worth. Is that humility?
No. Judaism teaches that you have a G-dly [soul](/learn/the-soul) and a unique mission. If these feelings are heavy, please talk to someone, and call the Rabbi.
