The Jewish sites children love most in Israel are the ones they can touch and explore: the Western Wall and its underground tunnels, a water walk through an ancient tunnel, digging for ancient pottery, and animals and caves along the way. Families who have gone describe these places from their own trips.

## Start at the Western Wall

Walking up to the Kotel is a moment many children remember. One mother on Jewish tradition describes how a soldier on the way to the Kotel gave her young sons a handful of little Magen David pins. Children can write a note with a prayer and place it in the cracks of the Wall, as many visitors do (see [The Western Wall](/learn/western-wall)). The Wall is the last remaining structure of the Temple Mount complex, and it is a good place to tell children about the Holy Temple (see [The Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple)).

## Underground Jerusalem

Family travel accounts recommend several places in the Old City and nearby:

- **The Western Wall Tunnels.** On a guided tour, you see the huge stones along the Wall, which continues underground. The article describes one stone about 45 feet long and about 550 tons.
- **The Davidson Center.** Archaeological finds from the Second Temple period onward, with a computer presentation that helps children, and coins that interest young collectors.
- **Hezekiah's Tunnel.** A narrow, cold, underground water walk in the City of David, which the author's children remembered most. Bring flashlights and towels, and note that the water can be about three feet deep and the footing slippery.

## Dig for a Day

At Beit Guvrin National Park, families can dig in an ancient cave for under three hours. The author's son found a piece of pottery that an archaeologist dated to about 2,200 years old, possibly from the time of the Maccabees. Wear clothes you don't mind getting dirty. It's a way to bring [Chanukah](/holidays/chanukah) history alive.

## Camels, caves and animals

- **Eretz Bereishit** near Ma'aleh Adumim: a camel caravan, a tent of "Abraham," and baking pita over a fire, a highlight for the author's children.
- **Soreq Cave** near Beit Shemesh: one of the world's most active stalactite caves, with about an hour's walk along the path.
- **The safari near Tel Aviv:** a drive past animals, including ostriches that may approach the car. Don't feed the animals.
- **A jeep tour in the Judean Desert:** bumpy and exciting. The article says it is not recommended for pregnant women or people with heart problems.

Lists of points of interest in Israel also name other places that older children may enjoy, such as Masada, the Dead Sea and Tzfat (see [Tzfat](/learn/tzfat) and [Tiberias and the Galilee](/learn/tiberias-and-the-galilee)).

## Israel is a place where kids can be kids

Another parent, traveling with young sons, notes that Israelis warmly welcome children, even noisy ones. She describes a driver who just laughed when her sons bickered all the way, and a soldier who gave her sons small gifts.

## Before you go

These are accounts from individual families, and details such as hours, tours and conditions change. ## Common questions

### What Jewish sites in Israel are good for children?
The Western Wall, the Western Wall Tunnels, Hezekiah's Tunnel, Beit Guvrin's dig, Eretz Bereishit, Soreq Cave and the Galilee are some that families mention.

### Is the Western Wall appropriate for young children?
Yes. Children can say a prayer and place a note in its cracks, and it is a good place to tell them about the Holy Temple.

### What should we bring to Hezekiah's Tunnel?
One family brought flashlights and towels. The water is cold, and it can be about three feet deep.

### How do I explain the Temple to my child?
Tell the story of the Holy Temple and of Jerusalem at the Wall. Read [Jerusalem in Judaism](/learn/jerusalem) first.

### Is travel to Israel with kids safe right now?
Check your government's current travel advice and ask a trusted local guide. This guide cannot judge it.
