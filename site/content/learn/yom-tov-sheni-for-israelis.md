Outside Israel, Jews keep two days of most festivals, a custom called Yom Tov Sheni shel Galuyot (יום טוב שני של גלויות), "the second festival day of the Diaspora." An Israeli family visiting or living in the Bay Area generally keeps two days like the local community, and for personal cases, such as a move or a trip, the right step is to ask the Rabbi.

## Why does the Diaspora keep two days?

In ancient times, the Jewish calendar was set by the Sanhedrin in Jerusalem, which declared each new month after witnesses saw the new moon. Messengers took time to reach distant communities, and those far away could not be sure which day the month began. So they kept two days to be safe. Even when the calendar was later fixed, the Sages taught that the custom of the second day remains in force. Chabad.org explains this at length for those who want the sources.

## Which holidays have two days?

- **Passover**: the first two days and last two days (so two Seders outside Israel).
- **Shavuot**: two days.
- **Sukkot**: the first two days, and Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah are two days.
- **Rosh Hashanah** is two days for everyone, including in Israel.
- **Yom Kippur** is one day everywhere.

## An Israeli in the Bay Area

If you are visiting from Israel for a holiday, the usual ruling, including in Chabad, is that you keep two days while you are outside Israel, as the local community does. That means a second Seder, a second day of Yom Tov with its restrictions, and a second day of prayers. If you are a guest in someone's home, you keep the second day with them.

If you have moved here, or are here for a year or more, you are generally a local resident for this purpose. If you plan to return to Israel, or your situation is mixed, the details can vary, so please **ask the Rabbi** instead of guessing.

## And an American visiting Israel?

Visitors to Israel have their own rules, and customs differ among communities. Ask the Rabbi before you travel.

## The parsha gets out of sync

When the last day of a holiday outside Israel falls on Shabbat, Israel has already finished the holiday, so Israel reads the next Torah portion while the Diaspora reads the holiday portion. A week or two later the two calendars meet again. See [this week's parsha](/parsha).

## Common questions

### Why do Jews outside Israel keep two days of Yom Tov?

The second day began when far-away communities could not be sure which day the new month started, and the custom was kept after the calendar was fixed. It still applies outside Israel.

### I am Israeli and visiting the Bay Area for Passover. Do I keep two days?

The usual ruling, including in Chabad, is yes, you keep two days while you are outside Israel. Ask the Rabbi if your travel plans are unusual.

### Do I have a second Seder?

Outside Israel, yes. Most families here hold two Seders. See [the Seder guide](/learn/passover-seder).

### Is Rosh Hashanah two days in Israel too?

Yes. Rosh Hashanah is two days everywhere.

### Why is the Torah reading different in Israel sometimes?
