Maakeh (מַעֲקֶה) is the Torah's commandment to put a railing, a parapet, around your roof so that no one falls: "When you build a new house, you shall make a railing for your roof, so that you do not bring blood upon your house if someone falls from it" (Deuteronomy 22:8). Jewish tradition extends it to every danger in the home, which makes safety a mitzvah.

For families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, with hillside decks, balconies and backyard pools, this ancient mitzvah is very practical.

## What the Torah commands

The verse is in the Torah portion Ki Teitzei. In Biblical times roofs were flat and people used them as living space, so a railing prevented a fatal fall. As Chabad.org explains, the obligation applies not only when you build a home but also when you buy or take over one without a railing. The Sifri says it begins as soon as the house becomes yours, even before you move in. The mitzvah is about responsibility: the people who live in a home are accountable for removing the danger of serious accidents.

The Torah calls the person who falls "the one who falls," even before anyone has fallen. The Sages explain that someone may have been destined to fall, but it is a merit to bring good through a person, and a debt to avoid bringing harm through one. We do not blame fate; we do what we can.

## From a roof to a whole home

The Sages teach that the principle goes beyond roofs. Maimonides, in his Code, writes that anything that may cause danger to life must be removed, such as a wobbly ladder, a dangerous animal, a well without a cover or a sloping wall, and that it is a positive commandment to remove hazards and be careful. So the mitzvah is a way of thinking: look at your home and ask, "who could be hurt here?"

Practical examples that families in Lamorinda can consider:

- **Decks, balconies and stairs:** a sturdy railing, with gaps small enough for children.
- **Pools and spas:** a fence and gate with a latch, covers and close supervision.
- **Hillsides and retaining walls:** safe steps and lighting near drops.
- **Ladders and tools:** stable ladders, tools stored safely.
- **Smoke and carbon monoxide detectors,** with fresh batteries, and an escape plan; see [wildfire readiness](/learn/wildfire-readiness-for-jewish-families).
- **Medicines, cleaning products and sharp objects** out of children's reach.
- **Dogs and other animals** that may bite.
- **Car seats and seat belts,** in every ride.

This is general information, not a safety code. Follow local building and safety rules.

## "A new house" and a new marriage

The Rebbe teaches that the verse speaks of more than wood and stone. The Sages say "his house" means "his wife," so a "new house" is the beginning of a married life. When we take on new responsibilities in the material world, we need new safeguards, railings, in thought, speech and action. We should not withdraw from the world; we should engage with it, set healthy limits and make our home a dwelling place for G-d. A new home is a good time to think about the kind of home you want. Read [a Jewish home](/learn/setting-up-a-jewish-home) and [dedicating a home](/learn/chanukat-habayit).

## When does it apply?

Unlike the mezuzah, which has a time frame, the mitzvah of maakeh applies once the home is yours, as described above. If you rent, the landlord is responsible for the building, but you are responsible for what you control. Ask the Rabbi about your situation, including a rented home. See [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah).

## Common questions

### What is the mitzvah of maakeh?

It is the Torah's commandment to build a railing around a roof so that no one falls (Deuteronomy 22:8).

### Does maakeh apply to modern homes?

Yes. Jewish tradition applies it to any hazard in the home that could cause serious injury, such as balconies, stairs, pools and ladders. Ask the Rabbi for your home.

### Does maakeh apply when I buy a house?

Yes. Chabad.org explains that it applies when you take possession, even if the house was already built, and even before you move in.

### What does Jewish law say about swimming pools?

Jewish law teaches us to avoid and remove dangers. A fence, gate and supervision are in the spirit of maakeh. Ask the Rabbi.

### Do I need a railing if I rent?

Speak to your landlord about a dangerous deck or stairs, and take care of what you control. Ask the Rabbi about your case.

### Is safety really a Jewish value?

Yes. The Torah tells us to "guard your souls very carefully" (Deuteronomy 4:15), and saving life is among the highest values. See [pikuach nefesh](/learn/pikuach-nefesh) and [Jewish health and body](/learn/jewish-health-and-body).
