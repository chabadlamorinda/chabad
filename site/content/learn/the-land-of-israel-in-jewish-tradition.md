The Land of Israel (Eretz Yisrael, אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל) is holy to Jews because G-d promised it to Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and their descendants, and because many mitzvot can be fulfilled only there. Jewish tradition teaches that the land itself has a special holiness (kedushah), which is why Jews everywhere pray toward Jerusalem and long for the Land.

## G-d's promise to Abraham

The Torah tells us that G-d appeared to Abraham and said, "I will give this land to your descendants" (Genesis 12:7). The promise was repeated to Isaac and to Jacob, and it became the heart of the story of the Jewish people. Abraham's first purchase of land there, a burial place for Sarah, is told in the Torah in detail (see [The Cave of Machpelah](/learn/cave-of-machpelah)).

Our people lived through exile and return, but the promise was never forgotten. The Land is described in the Torah as one "upon which the eyes of G-d your G-d constantly gaze" (Deuteronomy 11:12), a place that G-d watches over throughout the year.

## A land with its own mitzvot

Many of the 613 mitzvot are connected to the Land of Israel. Chabad.org gives examples:

- **Tithes.** Portions of the harvest are set aside for the Kohen, the Levite and the needy (see [Maaser](/learn/maaser)).
- **Shmita.** Every seventh year the land rests (see [Shmita](/learn/shmita)).
- **The Holy Temple.** Worship and offerings took place in Jerusalem.
- **The seven species.** The Torah praises the Land for wheat, barley, grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives and dates (see [The Seven Species](/learn/seven-species)).

Because of this special holiness, tradition says a person living there should leave only for a weighty reason, such as marriage or Torah study. Living in the Land is itself a mitzvah, and moving there is called aliyah (see [Making Aliyah](/learn/making-aliyah)). For Jews who live elsewhere, the teaching is to support those in need in the Land and to keep the connection alive.

## Places that hold our story

The whole Land is holy, yet Jewish tradition singles out certain places:

- **The Western Wall** in Jerusalem, where Jews pray for the rebuilding of the Temple, for peace and for Moshiach (see [The Western Wall](/learn/western-wall)).
- **The resting places of the Patriarchs and Matriarchs** in Hebron, and Rachel's Tomb on the road nearby.
- **The graves of the sages** in the Galilee: Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai in Meron, the Rambam and Rabbi Meir Baal Haness in Tiberias, the Arizal and Rabbi Yosef Karo in Tzfat (see [Tzfat](/learn/tzfat)).

Many great Jews have kissed the ground when arriving in the Land. It is a sign of love for a place that Jews have prayed for over thousands of years.

## How Jews connect to the Land from far away

Three times a day, Jews pray for G-d to return to Jerusalem in mercy, and Jews face Jerusalem when they pray. The Torah once required a pilgrimage to Jerusalem three times a year, on Passover, Shavuot and Sukkot. Today, the connection continues in prayer, in the holidays tied to the seasons of the Land, in tzedakah, in study and in visiting.

The Torah's promise of the Land is part of Jewish faith and history, not a political position. If you have a question about how a mitzvah of the Land applies to you, ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### Why is the Land of Israel holy to Jews?
G-d promised it to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob and their children, and the Torah gives it a unique holiness. Many mitzvot, such as tithes and the sabbatical year, apply only there.

### What does "Eretz Yisrael" mean?
It means "the Land of Israel." Israel is the name given to our forefather Jacob, and the Land is the home promised to his descendants, the Jewish people.

### Is living in Israel a mitzvah?
Yes, Jewish tradition teaches that living in the Land of Israel is a mitzvah. If you are considering a move, speak with the Rabbi and read about [making aliyah](/learn/making-aliyah).

### Why do Jews pray facing Jerusalem?
Jerusalem is where the Holy Temple stood, and Jews around the world turn toward it in prayer. Three times each day, we ask G-d to return to Jerusalem in mercy.

### Can I connect with Israel if I have never been there?
Yes. You can learn about the Land, keep its holidays, give tzedakah and say [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim). A visit is a wonderful step too (see [Your First Trip to Israel](/learn/first-trip-to-israel)).
