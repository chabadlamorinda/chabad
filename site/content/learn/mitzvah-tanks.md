Mitzvah Tanks are mobile mitzvah centers, often large vans or RVs, that Chabad uses to bring Judaism to Jews wherever they are. Inspired by the Lubavitcher Rebbe, volunteers invite passersby to do a mitzvah right then and there, such as putting on tefillin or taking Shabbat candles.

## How it started

In the late 1960s, the Rebbe launched a worldwide campaign encouraging every Jewish man to put on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin). Over the following years, he added more "mivtzoim," or mitzvah campaigns. In the 1970s, young Chabad students in New York began driving vans through the city as "Mitzvah Tanks," a name chosen to describe a peaceful army of goodness. Today they can be found in cities around the world.

## The Rebbe's ten mitzvah campaigns

The Rebbe highlighted ten mitzvahs that any Jew can do, each one a doorway into Jewish life:

1. **Ahavat Yisrael:** loving every Jew.
2. **Jewish education:** for every Jewish child.
3. **Torah study:** setting regular times to learn.
4. **[Tefillin](/learn/tefillin):** for men over bar mitzvah age, every weekday.
5. **[Mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah):** on every doorway of the home.
6. **[Tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah):** giving charity, with a pushka in the home.
7. **Jewish books:** a home filled with holy books.
8. **[Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles):** lit by women and girls every Friday.
9. **[Kosher](/learn/kosher):** keeping a kosher home and diet.
10. **Family purity:** the laws of [mikvah](/learn/mikvah) in marriage.

## Why one mitzvah matters

The Rebbe taught that every mitzvah has infinite value, and that a single mitzvah can tip the balance of the whole world toward good. He also taught that a mitzvah is never "all or nothing." One Jew putting on tefillin once, or a girl lighting a Shabbat candle for the first time, is a real and eternal connection to G-d.

This is the spirit of the Mitzvah Tank: no judgment, no pressure, just an open invitation. "Are you Jewish? Would you like to do a mitzvah?"

## Through the year

Mitzvah Tanks and Chabad volunteers bring each holiday to the street:

- **Rosh Hashanah:** hearing the [shofar](/learn/shofar).
- **Sukkot:** shaking the [lulav and etrog](/learn/four-kinds), sometimes in a sukkah on wheels.
- **Chanukah:** handing out menorahs and lighting public menorahs.
- **Purim:** reading the [Megillah](/learn/megillah) and sharing mishloach manot.
- **Passover:** giving out handmade shmurah matzah.

## Common questions

### Do I have to be observant to put on tefillin at a Mitzvah Tank?
No. Any Jewish man over 13 may put on tefillin, whatever his background. Volunteers will help you step by step. It takes just a few minutes.

### Why are they called "tanks"?
The Rebbe described the mitzvah campaigns as a spiritual effort to spread light and goodness. The name "tank" was a playful image of a peaceful "army" bringing mitzvahs, not conflict.

### Why do they ask "Are you Jewish?"
Certain mitzvahs, like tefillin, are specific to Jews, so volunteers ask in order to offer the right mitzvah. Non-Jews are warmly welcomed too, and may learn about the [Noahide laws](/learn/noahide-laws).

### Can women do a mitzvah at a Mitzvah Tank?
Yes. Women and girls are offered Shabbat candles and information about lighting them, along with other mitzvahs and holiday items.
