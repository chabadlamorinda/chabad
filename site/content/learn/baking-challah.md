Challah (חַלָּה) is the braided egg bread Jews eat on Shabbat and holidays, and you can make it at home with flour, yeast, water, oil, sugar, eggs and salt. Mix the dough, let it rise twice, braid it, brush it with egg and bake it until golden, ideally on Thursday or Friday.

## A simple challah dough

This is a home-size version, scaled down by us from the large Chabad.org recipe (which uses 5 pounds of flour). Use it as a starting point and adjust to your oven and flour.

**Makes two medium loaves**

- 6 cups flour (plus extra for kneading)
- 1 packet (2 1/4 teaspoons) dry yeast
- 1 1/2 cups warm water
- 1/4 cup oil
- 1/2 cup sugar
- 2 egg yolks (and 1 whole egg beaten, for brushing)
- 2 teaspoons salt
- Poppy or sesame seeds, optional

## Step by step

1. **Wake the yeast.** Dissolve the yeast in a little of the warm water with a spoonful of the sugar, and wait until it bubbles.
2. **Mix.** Combine the flour, salt, remaining sugar, oil, egg yolks and water, then add the yeast. Knead about 10 minutes, until smooth and elastic.
3. **Let it rise.** Put the dough in a greased bowl, cover, and leave in a warm place for 2 to 3 hours, until doubled.
4. **Separate challah.** This is the moment for the blessing, if your dough is large enough. See the section below.
5. **Shape and braid.** See the next section.
6. **Rise again** for about 20 minutes, then brush with the beaten egg and add seeds.
7. **Bake** at 400 degrees F for 15 minutes, then lower to 350 and bake until deep golden. Chabad.org's large loaves take another 30 to 45 minutes; smaller loaves need less, so watch them.

Use kosher-certified ingredients, and keep dairy out of the dough so that the challah stays pareve and can be eaten with a meat meal.

## How to braid challah

**Three strands (the easy one).** Divide a piece of dough into three equal parts and roll each into a strand about 12 inches long. Pinch the tops together, then braid like a ponytail: right over the middle, left over the middle, until the end. Pinch and tuck the ends under.

**Six strands.** Roll six strands, join them at the top, and arrange two on the left, two in the middle and two on the right. Chabad.org describes it like this: cross the outer strands under the inner ones in an alternating pattern, repeating "down and up and under," until you reach the end, then tuck them in. It looks hard, but the rhythm becomes easy after one loaf.

**Round challah.** On Rosh Hashanah the challah is round, a custom many explain as the cycle of the year, and some do not braid it at all. See [Rosh Hashanah for beginners](/learn/rosh-hashanah-for-beginners).

## Taking challah and the blessing

When a large enough dough is made, a small piece is separated and a blessing is said. This is its own mitzvah, covered in [hafrashat challah](/learn/hafrashat-challah), with the blessing and the amount of flour that requires it. Many women take challah in a special moment of prayer. See [challah](/learn/challah) for what the loaves mean on the Shabbat table.

## Baking ahead and freezing

- Bake on Thursday or early Friday, so that the challah is cool before it is stored.
- Wrap cooled loaves tightly and freeze them for up to a few weeks. Defrost on Friday morning.
- Unbaked braided dough can also be frozen, shaped, on a tray. Let it thaw and rise before baking.

Fresh challah is wonderful, but a frozen challah that is on the table beats no challah at all. See [preparing for Shabbat](/learn/preparing-for-shabbat) for a full Friday plan.

## Common questions

### How do you make challah?
Mix flour, yeast, water, oil, sugar, eggs and salt into a dough, let it rise, braid it, let it rise again and bake it at 400 degrees F, then 350, until golden.

### How do you braid challah with six strands?
Join six strands at the top, two on each side and two in the middle, and braid by crossing the outer strands under and over the inner ones, repeating until the end.

### Do I have to make the blessing every time I bake?
No. The blessing and taking challah apply when the dough is large enough. See [hafrashat challah](/learn/hafrashat-challah) or ask the Rabbi.

### Why is challah round on Rosh Hashanah?
Round challah is a Rosh Hashanah custom, often explained as the cycle of the year. See [Rosh Hashanah for beginners](/learn/rosh-hashanah-for-beginners).

### Can I make challah with dairy or butter?
For a meat meal, no, because dairy in the bread makes it dairy. Use oil, so the challah is pareve.
