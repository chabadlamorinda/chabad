Just as we say a blessing before we eat, we thank G-d after we eat. There are three after-blessings: the full Grace After Meals for bread, Al Hamichya for certain special foods, and Borei Nefashot for everything else. Knowing which one to say is easier than it sounds.

## Bread: Grace After Meals

After a meal with bread, we say [Birkat Hamazon](/learn/birkat-hamazon), the Grace After Meals. This is a mitzvah from the Torah: "You shall eat and be satisfied, and bless the L-rd your G-d" (Deuteronomy 8:10).

## Al Hamichya: the special foods

Al Hamichya is a shorter blessing, sometimes called "the three-in-one blessing" (bracha me'ein shalosh) because it summarizes the Grace After Meals. It is said after foods connected to the Land of Israel, and has three versions:

- **"Al hamichya"**, after cake, cookies, crackers, pasta and other foods made from the **five grains**: wheat, barley, spelt, oats and rye.
- **"Al hagefen"**, after **wine or grape juice**.
- **"Al ha'etz"**, after the **fruits of the seven species**: grapes (and raisins), figs, pomegranates, olives and dates.

If you ate more than one of these, you combine them into one blessing. The wording for every combination is in the siddur.

## Borei Nefashot: everything else

After all other foods and drinks, such as fruit, vegetables, meat, fish, eggs, dairy, water and juice, we say the short blessing Borei Nefashot: "Blessed are You... who creates many living beings and their needs, for all that You have created to sustain every living being. Blessed is He who is the life of the worlds."

A note on rice: before eating rice we say "mezonot," but afterward we say Borei Nefashot, because rice is not one of the five grains.

## How much requires an after-blessing

An after-blessing is said only after eating or drinking a minimum amount:

- **Food:** a **kezayit**, literally "the size of an olive," eaten within a short time. Opinions differ about the exact size.
- **Drink:** a **revi'it**, roughly 3 fluid ounces or a bit more, depending on the opinion, drunk without long pauses.

If you ate less, you say the blessing before eating but no blessing afterward. Exact sizes and timing are detailed; ask the Rabbi for practical measures.

## When to say it

Say the after-blessing soon after you finish, in the place where you ate. If you forgot, it can still be said for a while afterward, as long as you have not yet digested the food; ask the Rabbi about your case.

## Making it a habit

- **Keep a blessings card** on the kitchen table or in your bag.
- **Teach children** Borei Nefashot first; it is short and covers most snacks.
- **Say it slowly**, thinking about the food you just enjoyed. See our guide to [blessings](/learn/blessings).

## Common questions

### What blessing do I say after a cookie?
Al Hamichya, if you ate at least a kezayit. Cookies are made from wheat, one of the five grains.

### What do I say after grapes and a glass of water?
For grapes, Al Ha'etz (if you ate a kezayit); for the water, Borei Nefashot if you drank a revi'it. You say both blessings.

### Do I say Al Hamichya after a pasta meal?
Yes, if there was no bread. If you also ate bread, Birkat Hamazon covers everything.

### What if I am not sure how much I ate?
If you are in doubt whether you ate the minimum, ask the Rabbi; in general we avoid saying a blessing that may not be needed. A good tip is to eat a little more so the blessing is clearly required.
