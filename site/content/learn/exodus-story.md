The Exodus is the story of how G-d freed the Jewish people from slavery in Egypt, led by Moses, more than 3,300 years ago. It is told in the Book of Exodus, retold every year at the Passover seder, and remembered in Jewish prayer every single day.

## How the Jews came to Egypt

Jacob, the grandson of Abraham, had twelve sons. His son Joseph was sold by his brothers and ended up in Egypt, where he rose to become viceroy. During a famine, Jacob and his family, about seventy people, moved to Egypt to join him.

After Joseph and his brothers passed away, a new Pharaoh came to power. He feared the growing Jewish population and enslaved them, forcing them to build cities with bricks and mortar. He even ordered that Jewish baby boys be thrown into the Nile.

## Moses

A Jewish mother, Yocheved, hid her baby son and placed him in a basket in the Nile. Pharaoh's daughter found him, named him Moses, and raised him in the palace. As an adult, Moses saw an Egyptian beating a Jewish slave and defended him, then fled to Midian.

Years later, while shepherding, Moses saw a bush that burned but was not consumed. G-d spoke to him from the bush and sent him back to Egypt, with his brother Aaron, to tell Pharaoh: "Let My people go."

## The Ten Plagues

Pharaoh refused, and G-d brought ten plagues on Egypt:

1. Blood
2. Frogs
3. Lice
4. Wild animals
5. Pestilence
6. Boils
7. Hail
8. Locusts
9. Darkness
10. The death of the firstborn

Before the last plague, each Jewish family brought the Passover lamb and put its blood on their doorposts. G-d "passed over" their homes, which is where the name Passover comes from.

## Leaving Egypt

That night Pharaoh finally let the people go. They left in such a hurry that their dough had no time to rise, so they baked flat bread. That is why we eat [matzah](/learn/matzah) on Passover.

## The splitting of the sea

Pharaoh changed his mind and chased the Jews with his army. Trapped between the sea and the Egyptians, the people cried out. G-d told Moses to raise his staff, the Red Sea split, and the Jews walked through on dry land. When the Egyptians followed, the waters returned. Moses and the people sang the Song at the Sea, which we still say each morning.

## From Egypt to Sinai

Seven weeks later, at Mount Sinai, G-d gave the Torah and the [Ten Commandments](/learn/ten-commandments). Freedom from Egypt was not just escape from slavery; it was freedom to serve G-d.

## Why we remember it every day

The Torah tells us to remember leaving Egypt "all the days of your life." We do this in the third paragraph of the [Shema](/learn/shema), in [Kiddush](/learn/kiddush) on Shabbat and holidays, and above all at the [Passover seder](/learn/passover-seder).

Chassidic teaching adds a personal meaning. The Hebrew for Egypt, Mitzrayim, is related to meitzarim, "limitations." Every day we can leave our own "Egypt" by rising above habits and fears that hold us back.

## Common questions

### When did the Exodus happen?
According to the traditional Jewish count, the Exodus took place in the Hebrew year 2448, about 1313 BCE, on the 15th of Nissan. That is the first night of Passover.

### Why did Pharaoh keep refusing?
At first Pharaoh refused on his own. After several plagues, the Torah says G-d "hardened his heart," which commentators explain as letting Pharaoh face the full results of his cruelty.

### Was the Red Sea really split?
Jewish tradition teaches that the splitting of the sea was a true miracle, seen by the entire nation. It is described in the Torah and celebrated on the seventh day of Passover, the anniversary of the crossing.

### How long were the Jews in Egypt?
Jewish tradition counts 210 years from when Jacob arrived until the Exodus, with the harshest slavery in the final decades.
