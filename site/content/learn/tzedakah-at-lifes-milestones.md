Judaism teaches that happy moments are a good time to give tzedakah: a birthday, the months of pregnancy and the day of a wedding all have customs of giving or helping others. Chabad.org explains that giving in gratitude for joy turns the celebration into a blessing for others.

## A birthday: one more gift to charity

The Rebbe's letter on three Jewish birthday customs lists an aliyah to the Torah on the Shabbat before the birthday, extra Torah study on the day, and an additional charitable donation. If the birthday falls on Shabbat, when money is not handled, the donation can be given before or after. The Rebbe explains that charity joins body and soul: the Torah study and aliyah are spiritual, while tzedakah is the material side, linked to the "sweat and toil" of earning money, and money given to a holy cause becomes spiritual.

Chabad.org's history of the Rebbe's Birthday Campaign says the Rebbe introduced it in 1988. Its list of customs includes increasing your usual giving on your birthday, before the morning and afternoon prayers. See [Jewish Birthday Customs](/learn/jewish-birthday-customs) and [Hebrew Birthday](/learn/hebrew-birthday).

## Before a baby is born

Chabad.org's laws and customs of childbirth say that a pregnant woman should be particular to give charity every day, including Fridays and the afternoons before Jewish holidays, before lighting candles. A woman who already gives regularly should increase her usual amount, ideally by a multiple of eighteen. See [Jewish Pregnancy](/learn/jewish-pregnancy) and [Baby Naming](/learn/baby-naming).

After a birth, families often want to give thanks. Ask the Rabbi about how you can mark the naming or the brit. Chabad.org does not describe a fixed tzedakah custom at the birth itself, so this is a personal conversation.

## Around a wedding

On the wedding day, bride and groom are forgiven for their past mistakes, which is why it is customary to fast until the chuppah, and they recite Psalms and special prayers. See [Fasting on Your Wedding Day](/learn/fasting-on-your-wedding-day).

Chabad.org's explanation of wedding customs also says: "When Hashem blesses a person with financial success, it is proper to give a portion to tzedakah." In the blessing for the bride, she is wished the wealth and the good sense to inspire her husband to support Torah institutions.

Another form of wedding giving is *hakhnassat kallah* (הַכְנָסַת כַּלָּה, "attending the bride"), helping a bride who cannot afford a wedding. Chabad.org says that there is no greater charity than this for a poor girl, and that it should be done discreetly. If you would like to help a couple, ask the Rabbi quietly. See [A Jewish Wedding](/learn/jewish-wedding) and [Jewish Wedding on a Budget](/learn/jewish-wedding-on-a-budget).

## An anniversary

We did not find a fixed Chabad custom of tzedakah on a wedding anniversary. But the spirit of gratitude fits: many couples give a gift in a multiple of 18, which Chabad.org explains blesses both the giver and the receiver with a good, long life. See [A Jewish Wedding Anniversary](/learn/jewish-wedding-anniversary) and [Gifts in Multiples of 18](/learn/gifts-in-multiples-of-18).

## Common questions

### Is there a custom to give tzedakah on a Jewish birthday?
Yes. The Rebbe encouraged an additional donation on one's birthday, before the morning and afternoon prayers, together with an aliyah and extra Torah study.

### What if my birthday is on Shabbat?
Money is not handled on Shabbat, so the donation is given before or after the birthday.

### Should a pregnant woman give charity?
Chabad.org says a pregnant woman should be particular to give charity every day, and especially before lighting candles on Fridays and before holidays. Ask the Rabbi about your own situation.

### Do bride and groom give tzedakah at their wedding?
Chabad.org says that when a person is blessed with financial success, it is proper to give a portion to tzedakah, and it encourages helping a bride in need. It does not describe a fixed amount.

### Is there a custom for a wedding anniversary?
We did not find a fixed custom. Many couples give in gratitude, often in multiples of 18. Ask the Rabbi if you would like to dedicate something.

### Why multiples of 18?
Because the Hebrew word *chai* ("life") has the numerical value 18, so giving in multiples of 18 is a blessing of life for both the giver and the receiver.
