Addiction to alcohol, drugs, gambling, or anything else, touches Jewish families too, more than most people realize. If it is touching yours, you are not alone, and there is real hope.

**In an emergency or overdose, call 911. For a crisis, call or text 988. The SAMHSA National Helpline (1-800-662-4357) is free, confidential and open 24 hours, and can refer you to local treatment.**

## No shame in asking for help

Judaism sees every person as a G-dly soul with infinite worth. An addiction is something a person is struggling with, not who they are. Seeking treatment is a mitzvah: the Torah tells us to guard our health and our lives. Getting help early is a sign of strength.

## Faith and recovery

Many people in recovery find that the spiritual side is essential:

- **[Teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah)**, returning, teaches that a person can always come back, at any moment, no matter how far they have gone.
- **Admitting we need a Higher Power** fits naturally with Jewish faith: we are not alone, and G-d gives strength each day.
- **Daily structure:** morning prayer, a few minutes of Torah study, Shabbat as a weekly reset.
- **Community:** being surrounded by people who care is one of the strongest protections.

Twelve-step groups and Jewish life go well together, and many Jews in recovery find their Jewish practice deepens their recovery.

## For families

- **Take care of yourself.** Support groups for families (such as Al-Anon and Nar-Anon) help family members cope.
- **Set loving boundaries.** Caring for someone does not mean covering for them.
- **Keep the door open.** Never give up on a family member. Say "I love you, and I want you to get help."

## Common questions

### Can someone in recovery make Kiddush?
Yes. Kiddush and Havdalah can be made over grape juice. Talk with the Rabbi about holidays like Purim and the Seder; there are ways to keep every mitzvah safely.

### Will people at shul judge me?
Everyone is welcome at Chabad as they are. What you share with the Rabbi is treated with discretion.

### Where do I start?
Call your doctor, the SAMHSA Helpline (1-800-662-4357), or the Rabbi at 925-384-7242. One call is enough to begin.

### Is there a Jewish prayer for strength?
Many people say Psalm 121 ("I lift my eyes to the mountains") or speak to G-d in their own words. See our [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) guide.
