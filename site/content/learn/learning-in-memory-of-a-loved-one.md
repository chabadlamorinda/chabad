Jewish tradition teaches that Torah learning, tzedakah and good deeds done in a loved one's name bring merit to their soul. The most common custom is to study Mishnah, because the Hebrew word Mishnah (מִשְׁנָה) is made of the same letters as neshamah (נְשָׁמָה), "soul."

According to Chabad.org, there is nothing greater we can do for the soul of someone who has passed away. This is a way for children, relatives and friends to keep giving to someone they love.

## Why does learning help the soul?

The Hebrew term for this is *l'ilui nishmat* (לעילוי נשמת), "for the elevation of the soul," or *lizchut* (לזכות), "in the merit of." Chabad.org explains that when relatives and friends take on additional Torah study, give charity and increase their good deeds, these acts bring merit to the one who has passed on. The soul, no longer in the body, cannot do mitzvot, so what we do here counts in its name.

Chabad.org teaches that this is especially so at three times: during the first year of mourning, at the [yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit) (the anniversary of the passing by the Jewish calendar), and any time a family feels moved.

## Studying Mishnah

The [Mishnah](/learn/mishnah) is the foundation of the Oral Torah, organized in short chapters that can be learned in minutes. Chabad.org says that on the yahrzeit, it is customary to study Mishnah for the merit of the soul, and it is especially fitting to learn chapters of Mishnah whose first letters spell the Hebrew name of the person who passed away.

How to start:

1. Find the person's Hebrew name (and the letters of it). If you are not sure, ask the Rabbi.
2. Choose chapters that begin with those letters. Chabad.org's Mishnah for Yahrtzeit page can help.
3. Learn with a clear intention: "This is in memory of ..."
4. Do it with a Hebrew-English edition if you are new to Mishnah. Understanding matters more than speed.

You do not need to know Hebrew. Any Torah learning in any language is valuable.

## Sharing the learning

Families often divide the work. For the shiva, [sheloshim](/learn/sheloshim) (the first thirty days) or the yahrzeit, each relative and friend takes a chapter or a tractate, and together they complete a whole portion. It is a gentle way for friends who do not know what to say to take part. If you are the one grieving, you can ask friends to learn a chapter in your loved one's memory.

## Charity and good deeds

Chabad.org also recommends giving [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) and taking on a mitzvah in a person's merit. This might be Shabbat candles, a regular kindness, or a commitment to attend a class. It is also very meaningful to establish a charitable fund or a lasting project in their name. Chabad of Lamorinda has a quiet way to give in memory of someone; see the [donate](/donate) page.

## Kaddish

[Kaddish](/learn/kaddish) is primarily a duty for children of the deceased. It is recited daily during the eleven months of mourning and then on each yahrzeit. If there are no children, another relative takes it on, or someone can be asked. Learning and charity are for everyone who loved the person, not only for those who say Kaddish. Ask the Rabbi about your family's case.

## Common questions

### How do I learn Mishnah in memory of someone?
Choose chapters of Mishnah and study them with the intention that the merit goes to your loved one's soul. According to Chabad.org, it is customary to learn chapters whose first letters spell the person's Hebrew name. Ask the Rabbi if you would like help choosing.

### Why Mishnah and not other Torah study?
Mishnah has the same letters as neshamah, "soul." Any Torah learning brings merit, but Mishnah is the traditional choice for remembering a soul.

### Can I learn in someone's memory if I do not know Hebrew?
Yes. Use an English translation. Chabad.org encourages relatives and friends to add Torah study, and the intention is what matters.

### Can friends and non-relatives take part?
Yes. Chabad.org says that children, relatives and friends can take on extra learning, charity and good deeds. Dividing the Mishnah among many people is a common custom.

### What else can I do for the soul of a loved one?
Give tzedakah, light a yahrzeit candle, say Kaddish if it applies, and take on a mitzvah in their name. Ask the Rabbi for ideas for your situation.

### When should I do this?
Any time, and especially during the first year and on the yahrzeit. For the exact yahrzeit date, use the website's yahrzeit calculator or ask the Rabbi.
