To get ready for Chanukah you need a menorah (one per household, or one per person), enough candles or oil for all eight nights, a safe place to light it, and a plan for Friday afternoon. Everything else, from latkes to gelt, is a bonus. Here is a checklist for the week before.

## 1. The menorah and its place

- **A menorah for each person who will light,** or one for the household. Jewish tradition describes eight lights in a row at the same height, with a ninth, the helper (shamash), set apart. See [How to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah).
- **Where it will stand.** The tradition recommends a doorway opposite the mezuzah, or a window facing the street, on a sturdy, fireproof surface, away from curtains and out of children's reach.
- **A tray under it,** to catch wax and oil.

## 2. Candles or oil

Olive oil with cotton wicks is the ideal, and wax candles are also fine. Jewish tradition says gas and electric lights do not count. The lights should burn for about half an hour after nightfall, so choose candles that last.

To count the candles: one on the first night, two on the second, up to eight, which is 36 for the eight nights, plus a helper candle each night, for a total of 44. Buy a few extras. For [Friday](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat), the lights are lit before Shabbat begins, so standard half-hour candles will not last. You need longer ones, or more oil. The tradition notes that on Friday the fuel must last until about half an hour after nightfall, which is much longer than usual.

[Oil and candles](/learn/menorah-oil-and-candles) explains the options, and the [Chanukah shopping guide](/learn/chanukah-shopping-in-lamorinda) lists what to get. [Bay Kosher](/programs/bay-kosher), a project of Chabad of Lamorinda, is always kosher and is the local source for Chanukah and kosher items.

## 3. The calendar

- **Check the dates** at [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays), and see which nights fall on Friday and Saturday night. The site shows each year's dates, and [tonight's candles](/menorah) shows what to light.
- **Check the time.** On a regular night, light shortly after sunset (Chabad custom) or after nightfall, as other communities do. See [this week](/this-week) and [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times).
- **Friday plan.** Jewish tradition describes lighting the menorah before the Shabbat candles, so plan to be home early on Friday afternoon. On Saturday night, light after Shabbat ends.
- **Block the half hour.** Put the lighting in everyone's calendar, so no one is out.

## 4. Learn or review the blessings

Three blessings on the first night, two on the following nights. Say them once as a family before the first night, so that everyone feels ready. [Shehecheyanu](/learn/shehecheyanu) is said on the first night. If you are new to this, read [How to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah) with your family, or ask the Rabbi.

## 5. Food, gelt and fun

- **Foods fried in oil,** such as latkes and donuts, remind us of the miracle of the oil. Dairy foods are also traditional. See [Chanukah foods](/learn/chanukah-foods).
- **Gelt and dreidels.** The tradition describes giving children gelt after lighting, and encouraging them to give part to tzedakah. See [gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt) and [Chanukah for kids](/learn/chanukah-for-kids).
- **Tzedakah.** Jewish tradition encourages us to give more during Chanukah, and double on Friday. See [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).
- **Songs.** [Chanukah songs](/learn/chanukah-songs).
- **A night-by-night plan.** [Chanukah night by night](/learn/chanukah-night-by-night) gives each night a theme.

## 6. People

- **Invite someone.** A neighbor, a friend who is alone, a colleague. See [hosting a Chanukah party](/learn/hosting-a-chanukah-party).
- **Check on someone.** A call to a homebound friend. See [Bringing Chanukah to someone homebound](/learn/bringing-chanukah-to-the-homebound).
- **Sign up for Light Up Lafayette.** Our Grand Chanukah Menorah Lighting is on Sunday, December 6 at 4:00 PM at Lafayette Plaza Park, free and open to everyone. [Sign up here](/events/chanukah-grand-menorah-lighting-2026). Read [what to expect](/learn/light-up-lafayette).

## Common questions

### What do I need for Chanukah?
A menorah, candles or oil, a helper candle, a safe place to light it, and matches. Latkes and gelt are traditional extras. Call 925-384-7242 if you need help finding a menorah.

### How many candles do I need for Chanukah?
For all eight nights you need 36 candles for the menorah plus a helper candle each night, which is 44 in total. Buy extra, and longer ones for Friday. Check the [Chanukah shopping guide](/learn/chanukah-shopping-in-lamorinda).

### Where do I buy a menorah and candles in Lamorinda?
[Bay Kosher](/programs/bay-kosher), a project of Chabad of Lamorinda, is always kosher and is the local source for Chanukah and kosher items. You can also call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242.

### What is different about Friday night?
You light before Shabbat begins, and the candles must last longer. See [Chanukah on Shabbat](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat).

### When should I light?
Chabad custom is shortly after sunset. Other communities light after nightfall. See [this week](/this-week) for times.

### Can I light if I have never done it before?
Yes. Everyone is welcome, and it is a mitzvah for Jews of every background. Read [How to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah), or ask the Rabbi.
