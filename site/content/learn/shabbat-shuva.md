Shabbat Shuva, the "Shabbat of Return," is the Shabbat that falls between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur. It is named for the opening words of its special haftarah, "Shuvah Yisrael," "Return, O Israel." It is a gentle, hopeful day, one of the best times of the year to come to synagogue and think about teshuvah.

## Why it is called Shuva

The haftarah, a reading from the Prophets, is from Hosea, and begins "Shuvah Yisrael ad Hashem Elokecha," "Return, O Israel, to the L-rd your G-d." That word shuvah, "return," gives the Shabbat its name. It is also called Shabbat Teshuvah. See [the haftarah](/learn/haftarah).

It falls during the Ten Days of Repentance, between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur; see [the Ten Days](/learn/ten-days-of-repentance). Because Rosh Hashanah can fall on different days of the week, the Torah portion changes from year to year: sometimes it is Vayelech and sometimes Haazinu.

## What is different in the service

The service follows an ordinary Shabbat, with a few changes. The Ten Days additions are put into the Amidah, and Avinu Malkeinu, the prayer we say on weekdays of these days, is not said on Shabbat. The haftarah is the heart of the day.

## The Rabbi's talk

In many communities, the rabbi gives one of the main sermons of the year on this Shabbat, speaking about teshuvah, its meaning and how to start. It is a moment to reflect on the past year and to prepare for Yom Kippur. Come listen: you do not need to know a thing.

## A quieter way into Yom Kippur

Rosh Hashanah is a day of crowds and shofar blasts, and Yom Kippur is a day of fasting. Shabbat Shuva sits between them like a pause to breathe. There is no fast, no rush, just a Shabbat table, a good talk and time to think. For families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda with little ones, it is also the easiest of the High Holiday days to come to: short, warm and welcoming.

## Teshuvah rooted in love

Teshuvah does not mean guilt. The word means "return," like a child coming home. Chassidic teaching stresses that returning to G-d out of love is the highest kind of teshuvah, and the Rebbe taught that this Shabbat carries special power for it. Read more in [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah). Our sages also say that the Shabbat of each week is a taste of the world to come, so a Shabbat during these days is a perfect time for fresh resolutions.

## How to make it meaningful

- Come to synagogue, even for an hour, and hear the haftarah and the talk.
- Light candles and share a Shabbat meal; see [Shabbat](/learn/shabbat) and [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times).
- Decide on one good resolution to carry into the new year.
- Call or write someone you need to apologize to.
- Give charity before Yom Kippur; see [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).

## Common questions

### What is Shabbat Shuva?
It is the Shabbat between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, named for its special haftarah, which begins "Return, O Israel."

### Why is it called Shabbat Shuva?
"Shuva" means "return." The haftarah from Hosea calls the Jewish people to return to G-d.

### What is the haftarah for Shabbat Shuva?
A reading from the prophet Hosea beginning "Shuvah Yisrael," about returning to G-d. See the [haftarah guide](/learn/haftarah).

### What is the Torah reading on Shabbat Shuva?
Vayelech or Haazinu, depending on the year, because Rosh Hashanah falls on different days.

### Is Avinu Malkeinu said on Shabbat Shuva?
No. It is not said on Shabbat, including Shabbat Shuva.

### What do you do on Shabbat Shuva?
Pray, hear the haftarah and the rabbi's talk about teshuvah, enjoy Shabbat meals, and prepare for Yom Kippur.
