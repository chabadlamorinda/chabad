This Chanukah glossary explains the Hebrew and Yiddish words you will hear during the holiday, from menorah and shamash to gelt and Al Hanissim. Each entry has the Hebrew spelling, a one-sentence definition and a link to the guide that explains it in full.

The definitions follow the traditional understanding of each word.

## The holiday and its heroes

- **Chanukah (חנוכה).** The eight-day festival beginning on 25 Kislev, which commemorates the Maccabees' recapture and rededication of the Holy Temple and is marked by lighting the menorah. The word means "dedication." See [what Chanukah means](/learn/what-does-chanukah-mean).
- **Kislev (כסלו).** The Jewish month, roughly November to December, in which Chanukah occurs. See [Kislev](/learn/kislev).
- **Beit Hamikdash (בית המקדש).** The Holy Temple in Jerusalem.
- **Antiochus (אנטיוכוס).** Antiochus IV Epiphanes, the Syrian-Greek emperor of the second century BCE who desecrated the Holy Temple and banned Jewish practices.
- **Maccabees (מכבים).** The Jewish army that revolted against the Syrian-Greeks. The name is traditionally explained as an acronym formed from the verse "Mi kamocha ba'eilim Ado-nai," "Who is like You among the mighty, Ado-nai." See [the Maccabees](/learn/the-maccabees).
- **Matityahu (מתתיהו).** Father of the Maccabees, who led the revolt against the Syrian-Greeks.
- **Yehuda Maccabee (יהודה המכבי).** A member of the family who led the Maccabees to victory.
- **Chashmonaim (חשמונאים).** The Hasmoneans, the priestly family who led the Maccabee armies and later became kings of Judea.
- **Yehudit (יהודית).** A woman of the Hasmonean family who ended the siege of the city of Bethulia, saving its people; she is the reason for the custom of dairy foods.

## The lights

- **Menorah (מנורה).** The seven-branched gold candelabrum of the Temple. The word is also used for the nine-branched Chanukah candelabrum. See [the Temple menorah and the Chanukah menorah](/learn/temple-menorah-and-chanukah-menorah).
- **Chanukiah or hanukkiah (חנוכייה).** The nine-branched candelabrum kindled on Chanukah. See [the Chanukah menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah).
- **Shamash (שמש).** The "attendant" candle, used to light the other lights. See [what is the shamash](/learn/what-is-the-shamash).
- **Shemen zayit zach (שמן זית זך).** Pure, pressed olive oil, the ideal fuel for the menorah. See [menorah oil and candles](/learn/menorah-oil-and-candles).
- **Zot Chanukah (זאת חנוכה).** The final day of Chanukah. See [Zot Chanukah](/learn/zot-chanukah).

## Prayers and songs

- **Hallel (הלל).** Psalms 113 to 118, recited in the prayer service on festivals, in full on every morning of Chanukah. See [Hallel](/learn/hallel).
- **V'al Hanissim (ועל הנסים), also called Al Hanissim.** A passage added to the daily prayers and to Grace After Meals on Chanukah and Purim, thanking G-d for the miracles of those days. See [Al Hanissim and Hallel](/learn/al-hanissim-and-hallel).
- **Haneirot Halalu (הנרות הללו).** A traditional prayer sung after lighting the menorah.
- **Maoz Tzur (מעוז צור).** A traditional song sung after lighting. Its name means "mighty stronghold." See [Chanukah songs](/learn/chanukah-songs).

## Games, gifts and foods

- **Dreidel (סביבון).** A spinning top marked with the letters nun, gimmel, hei and shin, for "A great miracle happened there." In Hebrew it is a *sevivon*. See [dreidel](/learn/dreidel).
- **Gelt, or dmei Chanukah (דמי חנוכה).** Money given on Chanukah, especially to children. See [Chanukah gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt).
- **Latke (לביבה, *levivah* in Hebrew).** A fried potato pancake eaten on Chanukah. See [latkes](/learn/latkes).
- **Sufganiyah, plural sufganiyot (סופגניה).** A fried doughnut, a traditional Chanukah food. See [Chanukah foods](/learn/chanukah-foods).

## Greetings

- **Chanukah sameach (חנוכה שמח).** A traditional holiday greeting meaning "happy Chanukah."
- **Freilichen Chanukah.** The Yiddish version of the same greeting. Learn more in [holiday greetings](/learn/holiday-greetings).

## Common questions

### What does Chanukah mean?
Chanukah means "dedication," from the rededication of the Holy Temple after the Maccabees recaptured it.

### What is the difference between a menorah and a chanukiah?
Menorah originally means the Temple's seven-branched candelabrum, and it is also used for the Chanukah lamp. Chanukiah (hanukkiah) names the nine-branched Chanukah lamp.

### What is a shamash?
The shamash, "attendant," is the extra candle used to light the others on the menorah. It stands apart from the row of lights.

### What does Al Hanissim mean?
Al Hanissim, or V'al Hanissim, means "and for the miracles." It is a prayer added to the daily prayers and Grace After Meals on Chanukah and Purim, thanking G-d for the miracles of those days.

### What is gelt?
Gelt is money given on Chanukah, especially to children. It is also called *dmei Chanukah*, and chocolate coins are a modern treat.
