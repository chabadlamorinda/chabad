Teshuvah means "return" in Hebrew. It is the Jewish path of repentance: recognizing a mistake, regretting it, and resolving to do better, returning to G-d and to our truest self. Judaism teaches that teshuvah is always possible, for anyone, at any time.

## Return, not repentance

The usual English translation, "repentance," suggests becoming a new person. Teshuvah means something deeper: returning to who you really are. Chassidic teaching explains that every Jew has a G-dly soul that is always pure and always connected to G-d. Mistakes cover it up, but they never touch its core. Teshuvah removes the covering.

That is why the Talmud says that in the place where a baal teshuvah (a person who returns) stands, even the completely righteous cannot stand.

## The steps of teshuvah

Maimonides describes three main steps:

1. **Regret (charata):** sincerely feeling sorry for what you did.
2. **Stopping and resolving (azivat hachet and kabbalah l'atid):** leaving the behavior and committing not to repeat it.
3. **Confession (vidui):** saying the words to G-d, privately, out loud. Judaism does not require confessing to another person; you speak directly to G-d.

Maimonides adds that complete teshuvah is when a person faces the same situation again and chooses differently.

## When you have hurt another person

Teshuvah between people and G-d is enough for mistakes that affect only our relationship with G-d. If we have hurt another person, we must also:

- **Make it right,** for example, returning money or repairing damage.
- **Ask for forgiveness sincerely.** If they refuse, Jewish law says to try up to three times, respectfully.

The person who was hurt is encouraged to forgive, and not to hold a grudge, when the apology is sincere.

## When to do teshuvah

Teshuvah can be done any day. Some times are especially suited to it:

- **Elul:** the month before Rosh Hashanah, when G-d is described as "a king in the field," close and approachable.
- **The Ten Days of Repentance:** from Rosh Hashanah to Yom Kippur.
- **Yom Kippur:** the Day of Atonement.
- **Fast days and bedtime:** many say a short prayer each night, forgiving others and reflecting on the day. See our guide to [fast days](/learn/fast-days).

## Teshuvah with joy

The [Tanya](/learn/tanya) teaches that teshuvah should bring joy, not depression. Feeling bad can be healthy for a short time, but it should lead to positive action. Since G-d is always ready to welcome us back, teshuvah is a source of hope.

## Common questions

### Is it ever too late for teshuvah?

Never. Jewish tradition teaches that even a single moment of sincere return can transform a lifetime. G-d's door is always open.

### Does teshuvah erase the past?

Teshuvah brings forgiveness. The Talmud teaches that teshuvah done out of love can even transform past mistakes into merits, because they become the reason for growth.

### Do I need a Rabbi to do teshuvah?

No. Teshuvah is between you and G-d. A Rabbi can offer guidance and support, especially on how to make things right with others.

### What is a baal teshuvah?

It literally means "master of return." It often refers to someone who was not raised observant and has chosen to embrace Jewish practice. In truth, every Jew is on a journey of teshuvah.
