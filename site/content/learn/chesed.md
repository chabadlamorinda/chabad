Chesed means loving-kindness: going out of your way to do good for others, with your time, effort and heart. The Mishnah teaches that the world stands on three pillars: Torah, prayer and acts of loving-kindness.

## What is chesed?

Chesed is often translated as kindness, but it means more: generous, overflowing love that gives beyond what is required. In Hebrew, acts of kindness are called gemilut chasadim.

Chesed is different from [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah), charity, in a few ways that the Talmud explains:

- Tzedakah is given with money. Chesed can be given with money, time, effort or a kind word.
- Tzedakah is for the poor. Chesed is for everyone, rich or poor.
- Tzedakah is for the living. Chesed can also be done for those who have passed away, by caring for their burial.

## Why kindness matters

**Imitating G-d.** The Torah says, "You shall walk in His ways" (Deuteronomy 28:9). The Talmud explains: just as G-d clothes the needy, visits the sick, comforts mourners and buries the dead, so should we.

**The world is built on kindness.** King David said, "The world is built with kindness" (Psalms 89:3). G-d created the world as an act of pure giving.

**Abraham's legacy.** Abraham, the first Jew, is the model of chesed. His open tent and care for strangers were the start of the Jewish people. See our guide to [hachnasat orchim](/learn/hachnasat-orchim), welcoming guests.

## Classic acts of chesed

1. **Visiting the sick (bikur cholim):** spending time with them, helping with practical needs, and praying for them.
2. **Comforting mourners (nichum aveilim):** visiting a shiva house and offering support. See our guide to [comforting mourners](/learn/comforting-mourners).
3. **Welcoming guests (hachnasat orchim).**
4. **Helping a bride and groom** celebrate and start their home.
5. **Lending money or items** without interest, through a free-loan fund (gemach).
6. **Escorting the dead (levayat hamet):** attending a funeral and caring for burial, a kindness that can never be repaid.
7. **Making peace** between people.

## Simple ways to do chesed every day

- Greet people warmly and smile.
- Call someone who is lonely.
- Bring a meal to a new parent or someone who is ill.
- Give a ride to someone who needs one.
- Help a neighbor with a heavy load.
- Volunteer for a community organization.
- Listen without judging.

Small kindnesses matter. As Maimonides teaches, and the Lubavitcher Rebbe often emphasized, one good deed can tip the balance of the whole world.

## Kindness in the Chassidic view

Chassidic teaching explains that every Jew has a soul that is truly a part of G-d. When we do chesed, we are not just helping a stranger, but caring for a part of ourselves. Love for every Jew, ahavat Yisrael, is the foundation of Chassidus.

## Common questions

### Is chesed only for Jews?
No. Kindness to all people is a Jewish value. The Talmud tells us to support the poor and visit the sick of all communities, for the sake of peace.

### What if I do not have time or money?
Chesed does not require much. A kind word, a phone call or a smile can mean a great deal. Every act counts.

### Should I tell people about my acts of kindness?
Where possible, do chesed quietly and with humility, so the person receiving it is not embarrassed. Encouraging others by example is also good.

### How do I teach children chesed?
Let them see you do it and include them. Bring them along when you visit someone or deliver a meal. See our guide to [Jewish parenting](/learn/jewish-parenting).
