Hachnasat Sefer Torah means "bringing in a Torah scroll." It is the joyous day when a new, handwritten Torah is completed and welcomed into its home with singing and dancing. Few celebrations in Jewish life are as moving.

## The last of the 613 mitzvot

The Torah's final mitzvah is that every Jew should write a Torah scroll: "Now write for yourselves this song" (Deuteronomy 31:19). Because it comes last in the count, it is often called the 613th mitzvah. See [The 613 Mitzvot](/learn/613-mitzvot).

Few people can write a whole Torah themselves, so the Sages teach that a person who has a scribe write one for them, or who even helps correct a single letter, shares in the mitzvah.

## How a Torah is written

A Torah scroll is written by hand by a trained scribe (sofer) on parchment, with a quill and special ink. It holds 304,805 letters, and every one must be perfect. Writing a whole scroll usually takes many months, often a year or more. The scribe leaves the last letters only outlined, so that others can fill them in at the celebration.

## Dedicating a Torah in honor or memory

A family or a whole community may sponsor a new Torah. Many dedicate it:

- **In memory** of a parent, grandparent or other loved one, as a lasting merit for their soul.
- **In honor** of a simcha, such as a wedding, a birth or a [bar](/learn/bar-mitzvah) or [bat mitzvah](/learn/bat-mitzvah).
- **For the community,** so that many people can share in it by sponsoring parts of the scroll.

A Torah is read week after week for generations, so a dedication keeps a name alive among the Jewish people.

## The celebration

Customs vary from community to community, but the day usually includes:

1. **Completing the letters.** Family members and guests are each honored to help the scribe fill in one of the final letters. The scribe holds the quill with them or guides their hand.
2. **The procession.** The finished Torah is carried through the streets under a chuppah, the same kind of canopy used at a wedding, with music, singing and dancing. People take turns carrying it.
3. **Welcoming it home.** At the synagogue, the other Torah scrolls come out to greet the new one, and everyone dances with them, much like the [hakafot](/learn/hakafot) of Simchat Torah.
4. **A festive meal,** because completing a Torah is a true simcha.

Children love this day. It is a beautiful way to show them how much the Torah means to us.

## Common questions

### Do I have to know Hebrew to fill in a letter?
No. The scribe guides you through it. All you need is to want to take part.

### Does my family need to sponsor a whole Torah?
Not necessarily. Some families sponsor a whole scroll, and others share in a community Torah. The Rabbi can talk with you about what fits your family.

### Can children take part?
Yes. Children can help fill in letters, dance and carry the smaller decorations. Many also have their own [letter in a Torah](/learn/letter-in-a-torah).
