If your family includes both Jewish and non-Jewish members, you are welcome here. Every Jew is a precious part of the Jewish people, and every family member is welcome to join us for Shabbat, holidays, classes and celebrations, with no judgment and no pressure.

## Every Jew belongs

Chabad's guiding principle, taught by the Lubavitcher Rebbe, is that every Jew is infinitely valuable, whatever their background, level of observance or family situation. There is no such thing as being "too far" or "not Jewish enough." The Jewish soul within every Jew is whole and pure.

That means a Jewish partner in an interfaith family is just as much a part of the community as anyone else, and is warmly invited to explore, learn and celebrate.

## Who is Jewish?

According to Jewish law, a person is Jewish if their mother is Jewish, or if they have converted according to Jewish law.

- **Children of a Jewish mother are Jewish,** fully and completely, regardless of the father's background or how they were raised. They are welcome in Hebrew school, at a bar or bat mitzvah, and in every part of Jewish life.
- **Children of a Jewish father and a non-Jewish mother** are not Jewish according to Jewish law, though they may feel a deep connection to their Jewish heritage. They are welcome to learn and take part in our community, and the Rabbi is glad to talk with the family privately about their questions and options.

Read more in our guide to [who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew).

## The non-Jewish partner

A non-Jewish spouse or parent is warmly welcome at our events and in our community. Judaism does not seek to convert others, and nobody will pressure you.

Judaism also teaches that every human being has a relationship with G-d. The [Noahide laws](/learn/noahide-laws), seven universal commandments given to all humanity, offer a meaningful spiritual path for non-Jews who want one.

## Raising children with Jewish identity

Many interfaith families want their children to feel connected to Judaism. Some simple ways to start:

1. **Light Shabbat candles** on Friday evening and share a family dinner.
2. **Celebrate holidays** at home and with the community, such as Chanukah, Passover and Purim.
3. **Put up a mezuzah** on your door.
4. **Give children a Hebrew name** if they do not already have one.
5. **Enroll in Jewish education,** such as Hebrew school or preschool, for Jewish children.
6. **Tell family stories** about grandparents and Jewish roots.

## Common questions

### Will we feel judged if we come to Chabad?
No. You are welcome exactly as you are. Our community includes Jews of every background, and many families who are just beginning to explore.

### Is my child Jewish?
If the child's mother is Jewish, or the mother's mother, and so on through the maternal line, the child is Jewish. If you are unsure about your family's history, the Rabbi is happy to help you look into it. See our page on [Jewish identity](/help/jewish-identity).

### Can my non-Jewish spouse come to services and events?
Yes. Your spouse is warmly welcome at services, Shabbat meals, holiday celebrations and classes. Some rituals, such as being called to the Torah, are for Jews, and the Rabbi can gently explain anything you are unsure of.

### Can my child have a bar or bat mitzvah?
A Jewish child, meaning a child of a Jewish mother, celebrates a bar or bat mitzvah like any other Jewish child, and we would be delighted to help. If your situation is different, please talk to the Rabbi.

### What if we are thinking about conversion?
Conversion is a serious, personal decision. Judaism does not pressure anyone, but the Rabbi is happy to talk it through. Read our guide to [converting to Judaism](/learn/converting-to-judaism).
