A chuppah (חופה) is the Jewish wedding canopy: a cloth held up on four poles, under which the bride and groom stand as they are married. The word means a "covering," and the canopy stands for the home the couple is about to build together.

## What exactly is a chuppah?

The chuppah is a simple roof of cloth, fastened to the tops of four poles. It can be a beautiful fabric or even a tallit (prayer shawl), and the poles are often held by four friends or relatives.

It is more than decoration. In Jewish law the chuppah completes the marriage that began with the betrothal and the ring. Originally the "chuppah" was the groom's own home, or a room added to his father's house, where the couple would live. The canopy we know today recalls that home, and the cloth over the couple's heads is their first shared roof.

The Chabad.org article on the chuppah notes that the canopy form is credited to the Rema, Rabbi Moses Isserles, in the 16th century, although the idea itself is far older and is found in the Talmud.

## Why do some couples marry under the open sky?

You may have heard that a chuppah is traditionally held outdoors under the stars. Chabad.org explains that Jews in the east of Europe customarily held the chuppah under the open sky, recalling G-d's promise to Abraham that his children would be as many as the stars of the heavens and the sand of the sea. It is a blessing for many generations of Jewish children.

This custom is not followed for a couple where either has been married before. If you would like an outdoor chuppah in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda, or need an indoor one, ask the Rabbi what is best for your wedding.

## Who stands under the chuppah?

Only the bride and groom are required to stand under it. The rabbi, the witnesses, the cantor and the parents do not have to be under the canopy, although many couples invite their parents and honored guests to stand nearby. A portable chuppah can be raised after the ketubah is read, just before the seven blessings.

## What does the chuppah teach about marriage?

- **Home.** Marriage is the founding of a home, a place of calm and stability.
- **Simplicity.** A simple, even fragile roof shared by two people is the start of everything. Chabad.org favors a plain cloth, such as a tallit on four poles, over an elaborate structure.
- **Togetherness.** The chuppah sets the couple apart from the crowd, so that they are not married "in the marketplace," but in a sheltered, private space.

Flowers on a fabric chuppah are acceptable, though a floral structure is not the preferred choice.

## Common questions

### What is a chuppah in a Jewish wedding?

A chuppah is the wedding canopy under which a Jewish couple is married. It symbolizes the home they will share and, in Jewish law, completes the marriage.

### Why is a chuppah just a cloth on four poles?

The cloth is fastened to the tops of the poles so that the poles act like walls, which makes the space under it the couple's own private "home." Simplicity is also the point: a modest roof shared by two.

### Why do Jews marry under the stars?

Jews in the east of Europe held the chuppah under the open sky to recall G-d's promise to Abraham of descendants like the stars of the heavens. It is a custom, not a requirement.

### Does the chuppah have to be outdoors?

No. An outdoor chuppah is a lovely custom, but a wedding indoors is fully valid. Speak with the Rabbi about your venue.

### Who holds the chuppah poles?

Often four friends or family members, which makes a portable chuppah a way for people close to the couple to take part.

### Can we make our own chuppah?

Yes, a simple cloth or tallit on four poles is ideal. Show the Rabbi your plan before the wedding so everything is arranged correctly.
