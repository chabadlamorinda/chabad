Yes, you can pray on the way to work, but the best way is to pray before you leave home and use the commute for the parts that fit. Chabad.org teaches that if you are traveling in the morning, you should say the morning prayers before you leave, and that you may never pray the Amidah while driving.

For people in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda who ride BART or drive Highway 24 to San Francisco, Oakland or the Peninsula, that usually means a short morning routine, a pocket siddur, and a quiet minute of Mincha in the afternoon. No guilt: do what you can, and grow from there.

## Pray before you leave, if you can

Shacharit, the morning prayer, can be said at home, at the center or at any quiet spot. Chabad of Lamorinda's weekday Shacharit is Monday to Friday at 6:30 AM, and Sunday at 8:00 AM, in Lafayette. The Lafayette BART station is close to us. BART's Yellow Line, which runs between Antioch and SFO, stops at both Lafayette and Orinda, so many people can pray first and still catch an early train. Call 925-384-7242 to confirm what is planned this week.

Putting on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) is part of that morning routine, and it is best done at home or at the minyan, before you walk out the door. If a weekday morning leaves you no time, ask the Rabbi what to do rather than skipping it.

## Praying on BART or the train

If you could not pray before leaving, you can pray on the way. A few simple rules from Chabad.org:

- **Standing is better** for the Amidah, the central silent prayer. If standing is not practical, sitting is allowed. Try at least to stand for the bows and the three steps back, if you can.
- **If you will arrive at your stop before the time for prayer ends**, it is better to wait and pray standing, in a quiet corner at the office or the platform.
- **If standing would stop you from concentrating**, sitting is the better choice.
- **Concentration matters most.** Keep your voice low, and let the noise of the train fade.
- **Never the Amidah while driving.** If you drive, say the prayer before you leave or after you park.

The many other parts of the service, like the Shema and Psalms, can be said anywhere, so a small [siddur](/learn/daily-prayers) or a prayer app on your phone is a good friend on a long ride.  If you are unsure what to say in a crowded car, ask the Rabbi.

## Mincha at the office

The afternoon prayer, [Mincha](/learn/mincha-and-maariv), takes only a few minutes. Many people find a quiet conference room or a corner before the end of the work day. Ask a Jewish coworker to join you, and you may form a small minyan right there. Mincha has a limited time in the afternoon, so look for a quiet moment at work rather than leaving it for the ride home; ask the Rabbi for the times.

## The Traveler's Prayer

[Tefilat Haderech](/learn/tefilat-haderech), the Traveler's Prayer, asks G-d for a safe trip. Chabad.org explains it is said for trips that go more than roughly 2.36 miles (3.8 km) beyond the limits of your city, and many also say it for shorter trips through dangerous areas. For an ordinary commute, ask the Rabbi whether you should say it, and when; the answer depends on your route. If you do say it, ideally you stop and stand, but on a bus or train, Chabad.org says it may be said seated if stopping is inconvenient or unsafe.

## Make it doable

1. Pick one prayer to start with, perhaps the Shema in the morning.
2. Keep a small siddur in your bag.
3. Set a calendar reminder for Mincha.
4. Add something every few weeks.

Small steps add up.

## Common questions

### Can I pray on BART?

Yes. It is better to pray at home first, but you can say the prayers on BART, standing if possible. Ask the Rabbi if you have a specific question about your commute.

### Can I say the Amidah sitting down on the train?

Standing is preferred, and sitting is allowed if standing is not practical. If you can stand for the bows, even better.

### Can I pray the Amidah while driving?

No. Chabad.org teaches that the Amidah may not be prayed while driving under any circumstances. Pray before you leave or after you park.

### Where can I put on tefillin in the morning near Lafayette or Orinda?

The best place is at home or at the weekday Shacharit minyan at Chabad of Lamorinda in Lafayette. Call 925-384-7242 to confirm the schedule.

### What if I only have time to pray on the train?

Do what you can: the Shema and a few psalms still count, and the Amidah can be said sitting if needed. Ask the Rabbi to help you build a routine.

### Should I say the Traveler's Prayer for my commute?

It depends on how far you go and where. Chabad.org gives a distance of about 2.36 miles beyond the city limits, so ask the Rabbi about your particular route.
