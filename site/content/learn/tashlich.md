Tashlich is a short prayer said on Rosh Hashanah afternoon beside a body of water that has fish. It means "you shall cast," and it symbolizes casting away our mistakes and starting the new year fresh.

## What is Tashlich?

The name comes from a verse in the book of Micah (7:19): "And You will cast all their sins into the depths of the sea." At Tashlich we say this verse along with others about G-d's kindness and forgiveness.

Tashlich is a custom, not a commandment from the Torah, but it has been practiced across Jewish communities for many centuries and is a beloved part of the High Holidays.

## Why by the water?

The traditional sources give several reasons:

- **Fish never close their eyes.** This reminds us that G-d's eye is always watching over us with care.
- **Fish can be caught in a net.** Just as fish can be suddenly caught, we recall how uncertain life is, and are inspired to return to G-d.
- **The binding of Isaac.** A Midrash teaches that on the way to the binding, Abraham crossed a river that the Satan placed to stop him. Going to the water recalls Abraham's devotion.
- **Water at a coronation.** Kings of Israel were anointed beside springs of water, and on Rosh Hashanah we crown G-d as King.

## When is Tashlich said?

Tashlich is said on the first afternoon of Rosh Hashanah, after the afternoon (Mincha) prayer and before sunset. When the first day of Rosh Hashanah falls on Shabbat, Tashlich is said on the second day instead.

If you missed it, it may be said any time until Hoshana Rabbah, at the end of Sukkot. Many people do this.

## How to do Tashlich, step by step

1. **Find water.** Ideally a river, lake, pond or ocean with live fish. If none is nearby, any body of water will do, and some even use a well or a container of water.
2. **Bring a prayer book.** The Tashlich prayer is printed in most High Holiday prayer books (machzor).
3. **Say the verses.** The core text is Micah 7:18 to 20, which begins "Who is a G-d like You, who pardons iniquity." Many add Psalms and other prayers.
4. **Shake out your pockets.** Many people shake the corners of their garments as a sign of letting go.
5. **Reflect.** Take a quiet moment to think about what you want to change in the coming year.

## A custom about bread

Some people throw bread crumbs into the water. Chabad custom and many authorities discourage this, especially on Shabbat or the holiday, since feeding fish that are not yours is not permitted on a holiday, and it can turn a sincere prayer into a game. The real "casting away" happens in our hearts.

## Common questions

### Does Tashlich take away my sins?
No. Tashlich is a symbolic act. True forgiveness comes through [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah): regret, resolving to do better, and, between people, asking forgiveness. Tashlich helps us feel and express that commitment.

### Can I carry my prayer book to Tashlich?
On a holiday that falls on a weekday, carrying is permitted when it serves a need of the day. On Shabbat it depends on whether there is an [eruv](/learn/eruv). This is one reason Tashlich moves to the second day when Rosh Hashanah begins on Shabbat.

### What if there is no water near me?
You can say Tashlich from a place where water can be seen, even from a distance. Some fill a basin with water. Ask the Rabbi if you are unsure.

### Can children come to Tashlich?
Absolutely. Tashlich is a wonderful family moment and a great way to teach children about fresh starts and asking forgiveness.
