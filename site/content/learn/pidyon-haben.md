Pidyon haben, "redemption of the son," is a mitzvah in which a father redeems his firstborn son from a Kohen by giving him five silver coins. It takes place when the baby is 31 days old and is celebrated with a festive meal.

## Where it comes from

The Torah commands, "Every firstborn of man among your sons you shall redeem" (Exodus 13:13), and sets the price at five silver shekels (Numbers 18:16).

Originally, the firstborn of every Jewish family were meant to serve in the Holy Temple. They were saved during the plague of the firstborn in Egypt, and so they belonged to G-d in a special way. After the sin of the Golden Calf, the honor of service passed to the Kohanim and Levites, who did not take part in that sin. Since then, each firstborn son is "redeemed" from a Kohen.

## Which babies need a pidyon haben

A pidyon haben is done when all of these are true:

- The baby is a **boy** and the **first child born to his mother**. A previous miscarriage may affect this, so ask the Rabbi.
- He was born by **natural delivery**, not by C-section.
- The **father is not a Kohen or Levi**, and the **mother is not the daughter of a Kohen or Levi**.

Because the details can be complicated, it is best to ask the Rabbi early, even during pregnancy.

## When it takes place

The pidyon haben takes place on the 31st day after birth, counting the day of birth as day one. It is not held on Shabbat or a Jewish holiday. In that case it is postponed to the next day. It is usually held in the afternoon or evening, followed by a meal. Our [Hebrew date tool](/tools/hebrew-date) can help you find the day.

## How the ceremony works

1. **The baby is brought in,** often on a silver tray decorated with jewelry, a custom showing how precious he is.
2. **The father presents the baby** to the Kohen and declares that this is his firstborn son.
3. **The Kohen asks,** in a traditional formula, "Which do you prefer: your firstborn son, or the five silver coins?"
4. **The father answers** that he wants his son, and says two blessings: one on the mitzvah of redeeming the son, and Shehecheyanu, thanking G-d for reaching this time.
5. **The father gives the Kohen five silver coins.**
6. **The Kohen blesses the baby,** often with the Priestly Blessing.
7. **A festive meal** follows, beginning with the Kohen's blessing over wine.

The coins must contain the correct amount of pure silver. Special silver coins are commonly used. Ask the Rabbi how to obtain them.

## The meaning

A firstborn son represents a family's new beginning. The pidyon haben teaches that our children are a gift from G-d, and that the first of everything belongs to Him. It is a joyful celebration, and the Sages teach that attending a pidyon haben meal is a special merit.

## Common questions

### What if a pidyon haben was never done?
The mitzvah remains. If the father did not redeem his son, the son can redeem himself as an adult with his own blessing. Ask the Rabbi.

### Does a firstborn daughter need a pidyon?
No. The mitzvah applies only to firstborn sons. A baby girl is celebrated with a naming in synagogue. See [baby naming](/learn/baby-naming).

### Does the Kohen keep the coins?
The coins are a gift to the Kohen. He may choose to return them as a gift, but the redemption must be a real transfer.

### Is the baby circumcised first?
Yes, the [brit milah](/learn/brit-milah) on the eighth day comes first. If the brit was delayed for health reasons, ask the Rabbi about the timing.
