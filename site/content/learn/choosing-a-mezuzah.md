Many people shop for a mezuzah by looking at the case: glass, silver, ceramic or wood. But the mitzvah is the scroll inside. A beautiful case with the wrong scroll is not a mezuzah at all. Here is how to choose well. For where and how to hang one, see [Mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah).

## The scroll is the mezuzah

A kosher mezuzah scroll is:

- **Handwritten** by a trained, G-d-fearing scribe (sofer).
- **On parchment** made from the hide of a kosher animal.
- **In special black ink,** with a quill.
- **Complete and correct.** It contains the two passages of the [Shema](/learn/shema), every letter formed properly. One missing or broken letter can make it not kosher.

## The gift shop problem

Many cases sold in gift shops, online and in tourist stores come with a "scroll" that is printed on paper or photocopied. It may look real, but it is not kosher, and the doorway is still without a mezuzah. Some cases come with no scroll at all.

Buy the case wherever you like. Buy the scroll from a reliable source.

## Mehudar quality

Kosher scrolls come in different levels of quality. A mehudar (beautiful) scroll is written with extra care, in clear and elegant script, and checked by a second expert. Many sellers also check scrolls by computer for missing or swapped letters. A more carefully written scroll costs more, and many families choose the best they can afford, especially for the front door.

## Sizes

Scrolls come in several sizes, often measured in centimeters, such as 10 cm, 12 cm or 15 cm. A larger scroll is not more kosher, but there is more room for beautiful writing. Choose the scroll first, then a case it fits into without being folded or squeezed. Cases for outdoor doors should keep out rain.

## How many do I need?

Usually one for every doorway of the home except bathrooms, including bedrooms and the kitchen. Walk through your home and count, and ask the Rabbi about unusual doorways. Some closets and small spaces are exempt.

## Where to buy

Buy from a seller you trust, such as a Judaica store with reliable rabbinic supervision, or ask the Rabbi to order for you. A low price alone is not a good sign.

## After you buy

- **Put it up** on the right side of the doorway with the blessing. The Rabbi is glad to come and help, and many families make a small [chanukat habayit](/learn/chanukat-habayit) (housewarming).
- **Have it checked** twice in seven years, and whenever a mezuzah has been exposed to sun or rain.

## Common questions

### How can I tell if my mezuzah is kosher?
Take the scroll out of its case. If it is glossy paper with perfectly uniform letters, it is printed. A real scroll is on parchment with handwritten letters. To know whether a handwritten scroll is fully kosher, it needs to be checked by a scribe.

### Can I use a mezuzah from my parents' home?
Possibly. Have it checked first, since old scrolls can fade or crack.

### Is a more expensive case better?
The case only protects the scroll. A simple case with a kosher scroll is a real mezuzah.
