The Baal Shem Tov was Rabbi Israel ben Eliezer (1698 to 1760), the founder of the Chassidic movement. He brought new life and joy to Jewish communities in Eastern Europe, teaching that every Jew, scholar or simple person, is precious to G-d and can serve Him with love.

## His life

Rabbi Israel was born in 1698 in a small town in the region of Podolia, in what is now Ukraine. Tradition says he was orphaned as a child and grew up poor, spending time in the forests and fields in prayer and study.

For years he hid his greatness, working at simple jobs and studying in secret with a group of hidden righteous people. In his thirties he revealed himself as a teacher and leader. He became known as the *Baal Shem Tov*, meaning "Master of the Good Name," often shortened to the Besht.

He settled in the town of Mezhibuzh, where students and followers gathered around him. He passed away on the holiday of Shavuot in 1760.

## The world he came into

The Jews of Eastern Europe had suffered terrible massacres and poverty. Many felt broken. Torah scholarship was highly valued, and simple Jews who could not study deeply often felt distant from G-d. The Baal Shem Tov brought them hope and dignity.

## His key teachings

- **Love every Jew.** The Baal Shem Tov taught that we must love every Jew, even one we have never met, as we love ourselves. He cherished the sincere prayers of simple people.
- **Serve G-d with joy.** Sadness weakens us; joy opens the heart to G-d.
- **Divine Providence.** Nothing happens by chance. Even a leaf turned over by the wind is guided by G-d for a purpose.
- **G-d is everywhere.** G-d's creative energy fills and sustains every part of the world at every moment.
- **Sincerity matters.** A heartfelt prayer or a simple mitzvah done with love is precious to G-d.
- **Every encounter teaches.** Everything we see and hear can teach us something about serving G-d.

## The meeting with Moshiach

In a famous letter, the Baal Shem Tov described a spiritual ascent on Rosh Hashanah in which he asked [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach), "When will you come?" The answer was: when your teachings spread outward. This became a driving mission for the Chassidic movement and especially for Chabad.

## His legacy

After his passing, his student Rabbi Dovber, the Maggid of Mezritch, led the movement. The Maggid's students spread Chassidism across Eastern Europe. One of them, Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, founded [Chabad](/learn/chabad), which developed the Baal Shem Tov's teachings into a deep, structured philosophy called [Chassidus](/learn/chassidus). Read about the [Chabad Rebbes](/learn/chabad-rebbes) who followed.

## Common questions

### Did the Baal Shem Tov write any books?
He did not write books himself. His teachings were recorded by his students and appear in many early Chassidic works.

### What does "Baal Shem Tov" mean?
It means "Master of the Good Name." A *baal shem* was a person known for holiness and for helping people through prayer and blessings.

### How is the Baal Shem Tov connected to Chabad?
Chabad's founder, Rabbi Schneur Zalman, was a student of the Baal Shem Tov's successor, the Maggid of Mezritch. Chabad sees itself as carrying forward the Baal Shem Tov's mission.

### When is the Baal Shem Tov's birthday?
Chassidic tradition marks his birthday on the 18th of Elul, known as Chai Elul, a joyful day in the Chassidic calendar.
