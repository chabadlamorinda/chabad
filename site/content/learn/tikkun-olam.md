Tikkun olam (תִּקּוּן עוֹלָם) means "repairing the world." In Jewish teaching it is the work of every Jew to make the world a better, holier place through mitzvot and acts of kindness, so that it becomes a home for G-d.

## Where the phrase comes from

Tikkun (תיקון) means repair or fixing, and olam (עולם) means world. The phrase appears in ancient Jewish sources, and it is central to Kabbalah. According to the teachings of the Arizal, the great sixteenth-century Kabbalist, the world holds "sparks" of holiness that were scattered, and every mitzvah helps gather and repair them. Chabad.org explains that the Baal Shem Tov taught that this is not only for mystics: every Jew's mitzvot repair and purify the world.

## What it means in Chabad teaching

Chabad teaching puts it simply. G-d wanted a "dwelling place" in this physical world, and we build it through ordinary deeds: giving charity, keeping Shabbat, kosher food, learning Torah. A mitzvah turns a physical object (a coin, candle, piece of parchment) into something holy.

This means tikkun olam is not only kindness to others, though kindness is a big part. It includes both what we do for people and what we do for G-d, and the two go together.

## One deed can tip the scales

Maimonides writes that a person should see the world as perfectly balanced between good and bad, so that one good deed can tip the entire world to the side of merit. The Rebbe often repeated this idea. Chabad.org explains that it means a single mitzvah that comes your way could change everything, not just add one small piece.

So there is no deed too small. A smile, a phone call to someone lonely, lighting Shabbat candles: any of them may be the one.

## Practical ways to repair the world

1. **Give.** Put a coin in a tzedakah box each day. See [giving](/donate).
2. **Care for people.** Visit the sick, welcome a guest, call a lonely neighbor: see [chesed](/learn/chesed).
3. **Bring light.** Light Shabbat candles, put up a mezuzah, put on tefillin. These are the [ten mitzvah campaigns](/learn/ten-mitzvah-campaigns) the Rebbe encouraged.
4. **Learn.** Torah study fills the world with holiness; start with the [Kabbalah](/learn/kabbalah) guide.
5. **Improve yourself.** Repair starts with your own character.

Chabad.org writes about tikkun olam as a Torah idea, not a political platform. This guide leaves out causes and parties; for how a specific deed fits Jewish law, ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### What does tikkun olam mean?
Tikkun olam means "repairing the world." In Jewish teaching, it is the task of making the world a place where G-d is at home, through mitzvot and kindness.

### Where does tikkun olam come from in Kabbalah?
The Arizal taught that holy sparks are scattered through the world, and each mitzvah gathers and repairs them. The Baal Shem Tov taught that every Jew can take part, not only mystics.

### Is tikkun olam only about social action?
No. In Chabad teaching it includes kindness and justice, and also Shabbat, tefillin, mezuzah and learning. Both what we do for people and what we do for G-d repair the world.

### Can one person really change the world?
Maimonides teaches that one deed can tip the scales for the whole world, and the Rebbe often quoted it. So every mitzvah matters.

### What are examples of tikkun olam?
Giving charity, visiting the sick, hosting guests, lighting Shabbat candles, studying Torah and speaking kindly all count.
