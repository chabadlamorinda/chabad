Every business has disagreements: a partner who sees things differently, a client who won't pay, a customer who is unhappy, an employee who feels wronged. The Torah has a great deal to say about money and work, and its guidance can save both the relationship and the business.

## Prevent disputes before they start

- **Put it in writing.** Partnerships, loans, jobs and big orders deserve a clear written agreement. The Torah's laws of business assume that people are honest, and clear terms keep them that way.
- **Be scrupulously honest.** Accurate weights, honest descriptions, no hidden faults. Overcharging (ona'ah) and misleading (geneivat da'at) are forbidden, even when the other person would never find out.
- **Pay on time.** The Torah commands paying a worker's wages on time ("do not hold back the wages of a worker overnight"). Paying contractors and suppliers when promised is part of the same mitzvah.
- **Keep your word.** A handshake and a promise mean something in Jewish law.

## When a dispute begins

1. **Talk directly, calmly and privately.** Many disputes come from a misunderstanding. Listen first.
2. **Don't spread it.** Complaining to others about the person is lashon hara and makes peace harder. See [lashon hara](/learn/lashon-hara).
3. **Look for compromise.** Jewish law praises compromise (pesharah) as the way that brings peace along with justice.
4. **Bring in a third party.** A rabbi or a trusted mediator can often settle in one meeting what lawyers argue over for a year.

## Din Torah: a Jewish court

When two Jews cannot settle a monetary dispute, Jewish law asks them to bring it to a **Beth Din** (rabbinic court) rather than a secular court, unless the Beth Din gives permission. A Beth Din usually works as a binding arbitration panel that civil courts recognize, and it often costs less and takes less time. See our help page on [disputes](/help/disputes).

## Clients and customers

- **Make it right.** If you made a mistake, fix it generously. A good name is worth more than the money.
- **When the customer is wrong,** stay respectful, document the facts, and offer a fair solution.
- **Unpaid invoices:** remind politely, offer a payment plan, and escalate only step by step.

## Employers and employees

Employers must treat workers with dignity and pay them fully and on time. Employees must give honest work for their wages; the sages praise workers who give their full effort. Disagreements about pay, hours or firing are best settled early, face to face, or with a mediator.

## Common questions

### Can I sue another Jew in civil court?
Jewish law asks that monetary disputes between Jews go first to a Beth Din. If the other side refuses to come, the Beth Din can permit going to a civil court. Ask the Rabbi before filing.

### Is a Beth Din decision legally binding?
When both sides sign an arbitration agreement, the decision is usually enforceable like any arbitration award.

### What if my business partner is not Jewish?
The Torah's standards of honesty apply to everyone. Settle fairly, through mediation or the courts as needed.
