A gap year in Israel is a year, usually between high school and college, that a young Jewish adult spends in Israel studying Torah, volunteering or both, often in a yeshiva or seminary program. For families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, spring of senior year is when the decision becomes real.

## What a gap year can be

There is no single kind of gap year. Programs differ widely in religious level, daily schedule and goals. In general terms:

- **Learning programs.** Young men often attend a yeshiva and young women a seminary, where the day centers on Torah, Talmud, Jewish thought and Jewish law. See [what is a yeshiva](/learn/what-is-a-yeshiva).
- **Mixed programs.** Some combine study with volunteering, travel and living in a community.
- **Chabad programs.** Chabad.org describes a pattern: for young women, a year or two of seminary after high school, often away from home and commonly in Israel; for young men, yeshiva study followed in many cases by a year of shlichus, serving as emissaries. Chabad.org also lists yeshivas and programs, such as Mayanot in Jerusalem and Tzfat, as places for those who want a taste of immersive study.

We do not recommend or rank particular programs. Each has its own character, and the right one depends on your child.

## Why families choose it

Chabad.org gives several reasons to spend time in yeshiva: learning how to use Jewish texts, discussing Torah with confidence, absorbing a heritage, being inspired by a community living a Jewish life, and an investment in a future Jewish home. A gap year in the Land of Israel adds the experience of Shabbat and holidays in the Jewish homeland. See [Jerusalem](/learn/jerusalem) and [first trip to Israel](/learn/first-trip-to-israel).

## Questions to ask any program

Sit down as a family and ask the program directly, and get answers in writing:

1. **Supervision and support.** Who looks after students day and night? Who can a parent call?
2. **Shabbat and holidays.** Where do students spend them? What if a student has nowhere to go?
3. **Safety.** What is the plan if the security situation changes? Check current official travel guidance yourself; we do not make safety claims.
4. **Cost.** What is included: tuition, housing, meals, trips, medical insurance? Are there scholarships?
5. **Learning.** What does a typical day look like? How many hours of study and how many of free time?
6. **Next year.** Does the program help with college plans or deferral? Ask the colleges your child is applying to.
7. **Fit.** Will your child feel challenged, not pushed? Talk with parents of alumni.

## Making the decision together

Some students are drawn to deep learning; others want service or travel. Some parents worry about a child becoming more observant, or less connected to college plans. These are real, honest concerns, and it helps to talk them through with someone you trust. Our Rabbi and Rebbetzin are happy to listen. ## Common questions

### What is a gap year in Israel?
It is a year between high school and college, spent in Israel in a Jewish study, volunteering or mixed program.

### What is the difference between a yeshiva and a seminary gap year?
Yeshiva usually refers to a men's study program and seminary to a women's. Both focus on Jewish learning. Many programs differ in style, so research each one.

### Do Chabad young people do a gap year?
Chabad.org describes seminary for young women, often in Israel, and yeshiva study followed by shlichus for young men. Ask the Rabbi for current information.

### How do I choose a gap year program?
Ask about supervision, Shabbat, safety, cost, daily learning and fit, and speak with alumni families.

### When should we start?
Start in the fall or winter of senior year, because applications and housing fill up in the spring. Ask the program for its dates.
