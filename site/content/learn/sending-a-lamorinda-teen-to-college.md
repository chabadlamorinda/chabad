To help a Jewish teen take Jewish life to college, pack a few meaningful things, find the Jewish community near campus before move-in day, and agree on how you will stay connected to home. Many campuses have a Chabad House or a Hillel, and a student who knows where they are on the first week of school starts the year with friends and a place for Shabbat.

## Before they go: a Jewish packing list

Seniors at Acalanes, Campolindo, Miramonte and Las Lomas high schools are busy with applications and graduation. Take an afternoon with your teen before August to prepare.

- **Shabbat candles and a candlestick.** A small set fits in a drawer. See [how to light Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles). Ask the Rabbi about lighting in a dorm, where open flames are often not allowed by the school, since rules and customs vary.
- **A mezuzah for the door.** A student's dorm room or apartment is their home, so it deserves one. See [Mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) and ask the Rabbi about a dorm room's details.
- **Tefillin,** for young men who have become Bar Mitzvah. Have them checked before they go. See our [mezuzah and tefillin form](/forms/mezuzah-tefillin) for help.
- **Family details written down.** Your teen's Hebrew name, the Hebrew names of parents and grandparents, and the dates of family yahrzeits. See our guide to [yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit).
- **A siddur** (prayer book) and a small book of Psalms, or an app on the phone.

## Find the Chabad House and the Jewish community

Chabad is on many campuses around the world. Chabad.org's campus pages show how Chabad Houses are a home away from home, often with Shabbat dinners, classes, holiday celebrations and help with kosher food. What each center offers differs, so look at the website of the one near your teen's campus, or ask us and we will be happy to help you find it. Do not assume what is offered; check, since it changes between schools and years.

A good way to start is to have your teen look up three things before move-in: the Chabad House, the Hillel, and the nearest place for kosher food, if that matters to your family. See [Kosher on Campus](/learn/kosher-on-campus).

## The High Holidays come early

College often starts in late August or September, close to Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur. Many universities do not close, so students must ask professors for an excused absence. Ask your teen to write down the holiday dates from the [Jewish holidays page](/jewish-holidays) and send their professors a short, polite note in the first week. Most campus Chabad Houses and Hillels offer holiday services, but plan where your teen will go, and call us if you want help connecting.

## Being Jewish on a campus with antisemitism

Some Jewish students face hard moments on campus. Talk about it before they leave. Remind your teen that they can be proud and calm at the same time, that they are never alone, and that they can call home, a Chabad rabbi or the Jewish student center at any hour. Encourage them to report any harassment to the school and to tell you about it. Pride grows from knowing their story, so see [Jewish Pride](/learn/jewish-pride) and the guide for [Jewish College Students](/learn/jewish-college-students).

## Staying close to home

- **A weekly call or text before Shabbat.** Parents can light candles at home while their child lights in the dorm.
- **A shared calendar** of holidays and family dates.
- **A standing invitation** to come home for Shabbat on a long weekend, and for the holidays in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda.
- **Send a challah or a care package** for the High Holidays and Chanukah.

Students from our community who come home are welcome at the Shabbat table. See [Shabbat in Lamorinda](/learn/shabbat-in-lamorinda).

## Common questions

### How can my teen find Jewish life at college?
Start with the websites of the Chabad House and Hillel at the school, and write to them before arriving. They can usually tell you about Shabbat dinners, holiday services and kosher food.

### What Jewish items should a college student bring?
Shabbat candles and candlesticks, a mezuzah for the door, tefillin for a Bar Mitzvah-age young man, a prayer book, and written Hebrew names and family yahrzeit dates.

### Can my teen put a mezuzah on a dorm room door?
Usually yes, but check the school's housing rules, and ask the Rabbi about the details for a dorm room.

### What if the High Holidays fall in the first weeks of the semester?
Find the dates on the Jewish holidays page and write to professors in advance. Campus Chabad Houses and Hillels often hold services, so plan ahead.

### How do we keep Shabbat connected from far away?
Set a time each Friday to call or text, and light candles at the same time. Invite your student home for a Shabbat when they can.
