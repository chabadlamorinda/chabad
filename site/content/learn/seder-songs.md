Dayenu (דַּיֵּנוּ) is the best-loved song of the Passover Seder, and its name means "it would have been enough for us." It lists fifteen kindnesses G-d did for the Jewish people, from the Exodus to the Land of Israel, and after each one we sing "Dayenu": if He had done only this, we would have been grateful. Echad Mi Yodea and Chad Gadya are the songs that close the evening.

## Why the Seder has so much singing

The Seder is meant to be lively, and a song is the easiest way to keep children and tired guests awake and happy. The Haggadah itself includes praise, Hallel, and tunes that families have passed down for generations. Singing also turns the story from something we read into something we feel. If your family has its own tunes, keep them. If not, learn one or two, and invite the room to join. See [what a Haggadah is](/learn/haggadah).

## Dayenu: a song of gratitude

Dayenu appears in the Maggid section, where we tell the story. It asks how many levels of kindness G-d has given us, and then each stanza names one: bringing us out of Egypt, the plagues on the Egyptians, splitting the sea, providing for us in the desert, giving us Shabbat, bringing us to Mount Sinai, giving us the Torah, bringing us into the Land of Israel and building the Holy Temple. After each, we answer: it would have been enough.

Does it really mean that any one of them would have been enough? Not exactly. Chabad.org teaches that the song is about gratitude: every step deserves its own thank you, and we do not take any kindness for granted. This is the Jewish idea of [hakarat hatov](/learn/hakarat-hatov), recognizing the good. Even just reaching Sinai united, "as one person with one heart," as Rashi explains, was a gift worth singing about. Teach children this: before asking for more, say thank you for what you have.

## Echad Mi Yodea: "Who knows one?"

This counting song asks a question and answers it from one to thirteen. One is G-d, two are the tablets of the covenant, three are the Patriarchs, four are the Matriarchs, and so on, up to thirteen, the attributes of G-d. Every verse repeats all the earlier ones, so the song grows longer and faster. It is a fun way to remind children of the basics of Jewish life, and the faster it goes, the more they laugh. Ask the Rabbi if you want the full list of each number.

## Chad Gadya: "One little goat"

Chad Gadya (חַד גַּדְיָא, Aramaic for "one little goat") is a cumulative song at the very end of the Seder. A father buys a kid for two coins; a cat eats it, a dog bites the cat, a stick hits the dog, and so on, until in the end G-d Himself puts an end to it all. Children love the animal noises. Many Jewish teachers have read the song as a parable of history, in which one power after another rises and falls until G-d's justice stands at the end. If you want the traditional interpretations, ask the Rabbi.

## Tips for singing with children

1. **Send the tunes ahead.** If your guests do not know the melodies, send them a recording or link a few days before.
2. **Give kids a job.** One child can be the "rooster," another the "dog," and they get to make the sounds.
3. **Use the transliteration.** Print the words in English letters so that everyone can join in.
4. **Keep it short.** If the children are tired, sing the choruses and skip verses.
5. **Do not rush the afikoman.** The songs come after the meal and the afikoman, so plan to save some energy. See [the afikoman](/learn/afikoman).

## Common questions

### What does Dayenu mean?
Dayenu means "it would have been enough for us." It is a Passover song of gratitude that lists fifteen kindnesses G-d did for the Jewish people, from leaving Egypt to the Holy Temple.

### When do we sing Dayenu at the Seder?
We sing it in the Maggid section, in the middle of telling the Exodus story, before the explanation of the three symbols of the Seder.

### What is Chad Gadya about?
Chad Gadya is an Aramaic song about a little goat that is eaten, which starts a chain of events that ends with G-d. Many teachers read it as a parable of history; ask the Rabbi for the traditional explanations.

### What is Echad Mi Yodea?
It is a counting song that asks "Who knows one?" and answers each number with a basic of Jewish belief, such as one G-d and two tablets. Each verse repeats all the previous numbers.

### Do we have to sing the songs of the Seder?
The songs are a beloved custom rather than the center of the Seder, but they fill the night with joy. Sing what your family knows, and ask the Rabbi if you are unsure of your custom.
