Cheshbon hanefesh (חשבון הנפש) means "an accounting of the soul": a regular, honest review of how you are living, so that you can correct what needs correcting and build on what is good. Chabad.org describes it as evaluating your life honestly and removing self-deception, and it is the heart of the days before Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur.

Whether you live in Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda or anywhere in the East Bay, the end of the secular year is a natural moment for this. You do not need to be an expert to begin, only willing.

## What cheshbon hanefesh is, and what it is not

It is not self-punishment. A good accounting looks at both columns: what you did well and what you want to change. It starts from the idea that you have a soul worth caring for. See [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah), the Jewish word for returning to your best self.

It is also not an excuse to blame yourself endlessly. Chabad.org teaches that the soul-accounting leads to action: sincere regret over what was wrong, and a concrete commitment to change.

## "It all depends on me"

Chabad.org shares the Talmud's story of Elazar ben Durdaya, who lived a life of wrongdoing. At the end he cried out to the mountains, the heavens and the stars to help him, in other words to the things he blamed: his parents, his society, his wealth, his luck. None of them could. Then he said, "Ein hadavar talui ela bi," "It all depends on me." He wept until he died, and a heavenly voice declared him worthy of eternal life.

Notice the lesson. Honest accounting means noticing our excuses and taking responsibility, and it means we can change. Even a sincere moment can transform a person.

## When to do it

Chabad.org notes that the Rebbe taught different accountings throughout the year. Short ones cover the day, the week or the month, and the great, yearly reckoning is made in the month of Elul.

- **Every night.** One Chabad.org article describes the bedtime Shema as the best setting for soul-accounting. The prayer includes a passage where we think of anyone we may have wronged. See [the bedtime Shema](/learn/bedtime-shema).
- **Thursday night and the night before Rosh Chodesh.** The same article names these as especially good times.
- **Monthly.** Take a few minutes at the end of each month.
- **In [Elul](/learn/elul).** The month before Rosh Hashanah, when the whole year is reviewed.
- **At the secular new year.** Many people make resolutions now. See [the Jewish view of the secular new year](/learn/secular-new-year-jewish-view).

## Five simple questions to ask

These questions are a simple template, not an official text. Take five minutes, in quiet, with a pen.

1. **What went well?** Name one or two things you are grateful for, or proud of.
2. **Whom did I hurt or let down?** Is there anyone to apologize to?
3. **Where did I make an excuse?** Where did I blame others, or luck, instead of taking responsibility?
4. **What one thing will I change?** Make it small and concrete. See [changing a bad habit](/learn/changing-a-bad-habit).
5. **What mitzvah will I add?** One new act of kindness, learning or prayer.

## Keep it balanced

An accounting is a healthy practice when it leads to growth and not to despair. If looking at your life makes you feel crushed, that is a sign to speak to someone, a friend, a counselor, or the Rabbi. A trusted friend or mentor can also see what we cannot see in ourselves. And if you notice that you are sinking into sadness, see [mental health: a Jewish view](/learn/mental-health-jewish-view).

## Common questions

### What is cheshbon hanefesh?
It is a Jewish "accounting of the soul," an honest, regular review of your actions, and your plan to improve. Chabad.org calls it the work of the High Holiday season.

### How do I do a cheshbon hanefesh?
Set aside a few quiet minutes. Ask what went well, whom you hurt, where you made excuses, what you will change, and what mitzvah you will add.

### When should I do it?
Chabad.org notes it can be done daily, weekly and monthly, with the big yearly accounting in Elul. The bedtime Shema, especially Thursday night, is a favorite time.

### Is cheshbon hanefesh the same as guilt?
No. Guilt looks backward and can paralyze. Cheshbon hanefesh looks at the truth and then moves you forward.

### What did Elazar ben Durdaya teach?
Not to blame others and to take responsibility for ourselves: "It all depends on me." Chabad.org tells how his sincere return transformed him.
