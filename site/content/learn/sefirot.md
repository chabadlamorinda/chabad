The ten sefirot (ספירות) are the ten attributes through which G-d's infinite light is revealed in the world, according to Kabbalah. In Chassidic teaching they are also a map of the human soul: three powers of the mind and seven emotions that you can recognize in yourself.

## What the sefirot are

Kabbalah teaches that G-d is infinite, and the world is finite. The sefirot are the channels through which the infinite is expressed in the finite world. Chabad.org explains that each sefirah is both a light that reveals and a vessel that gives that light a particular shape. They are not G-d Himself, and Kabbalists warn that we do not pray to them. A classic image is a king's two hands: the king is the one acting, even if he uses a different hand for different tasks. This is a deep subject, so if you want to study it seriously, ask the Rabbi to guide you; start with the guide to [Kabbalah](/learn/kabbalah).

## The three powers of the mind: ChaBaD

The first three sefirot are intellect:

- **Chochmah** (חכמה), wisdom: the first flash of an idea.
- **Binah** (בינה), understanding: developing the idea in depth until you grasp it.
- **Daat** (דעת), knowledge: connecting with the idea so it becomes real to you and can lead to action.

The first letters spell ChaBaD (חב"ד), which is where the Chabad movement gets its name: Chabad's approach is to bring Torah to the heart through the mind. Some count Keter, "crown," in place of Daat.

## The seven emotions

The remaining seven are the emotions:

1. **Chesed** (חסד), kindness: love, giving.
2. **Gevurah** (גבורה), strength: discipline, restraint, awe.
3. **Tiferet** (תפארת), beauty: harmony and compassion, balancing giving and restraint.
4. **Netzach** (נצח), victory: endurance, determination.
5. **Hod** (הוד), splendor: humility, gratitude, yielding.
6. **Yesod** (יסוד), foundation: connection, passing the feeling on into action.
7. **Malchut** (מלכות), kingship: expression, receiving from all the others.

## How the sefirot mirror your soul

Chassidic teaching says a person is a small version of the sefirot. Your mind and heart have the same ten qualities, and each can be used well or badly. Love can be pure kindness or it can be smothering; strength can be discipline or anger. Chassidic teaching says character is refined mainly through the mind: when you really understand why a quality matters, it can change you. Self-work, then, is about balance, with each trait in its right place. Read more in [Tanya](/learn/tanya) and [Chassidus](/learn/chassidus).

## The Omer: seven weeks of self-work

Between Passover and Shavuot, Jews count the days of the Omer, forty-nine days, seven weeks of seven days. Each week and each day is linked to one of the seven emotions, so the count becomes seven weeks of refining your character: the first week is Chesed, the second Gevurah, and so on. ## Common questions

### What are the ten sefirot in Kabbalah?
The ten sefirot are the attributes through which G-d's infinite light is revealed in creation: Chochmah, Binah, Daat, Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, Yesod and Malchut.

### What does ChaBaD stand for?
ChaBaD stands for Chochmah, Binah and Daat: wisdom, understanding and knowledge, the three powers of the mind. It is the root of the name Chabad.

### Are the sefirot G-d?
No. They are attributes through which G-d is revealed, not G-d Himself, and Kabbalists teach that we pray only to G-d.

### How are the sefirot used for personal growth?
Chassidic teaching treats the seven emotions as qualities of your own heart. You can ask which you use too much or too little and work on balance, especially during the Omer.

### Do I need to know Hebrew or Kabbalah to understand them?
No. The basics are simple. For deeper study, learn with a teacher; ask the Rabbi.

### Is Kabbalah only for experts?
Chassidic teaching makes the inner dimension of Torah open to every Jew. Ask the Rabbi for where to begin.
