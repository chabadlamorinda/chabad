Tzitzit are the special knotted fringes tied to the four corners of a garment, as commanded in the Torah. Jewish men wear them on a tallit, the prayer shawl, and many also wear a small four-cornered garment with tzitzit, called a tallit katan, all day.

## What tzitzit are

Tzitzit (צִיצִית) means "fringes." The Torah says: "They shall make for themselves fringes on the corners of their garments... and you shall see it and remember all the commandments of G-d and do them" (Numbers 15:38 to 39). This passage is the third paragraph of the [Shema](/learn/shema).

The mitzvah applies to a garment with four corners. Since most clothes today do not have four corners, we wear special garments to fulfill it:

- **Tallit gadol** ("large tallit"): the prayer shawl worn during morning prayers.
- **Tallit katan** ("small tallit"): a smaller garment worn under or over the shirt all day, often simply called "tzitzit."

On each corner, four strings are looped through a hole and folded to make eight strands, then tied with five double knots and wound in a set pattern.

## The meaning of tzitzit

The Sages note that the numerical value of the word tzitzit is 600. Add the eight strands and five knots, and you get 613, the number of [mitzvot](/learn/613-mitzvot) in the Torah. Seeing the tzitzit reminds us of all of G-d's commandments. Wearing them is like being wrapped in a mitzvah, a constant reminder of who we are and what we stand for.

## Who wears tzitzit

- **Men and boys** wear tzitzit. In Chabad and many communities, boys begin wearing a tallit katan at age three, after their [upsherin](/learn/upsherin).
- **The tallit gadol** is worn during morning prayers. In Chabad and many Ashkenazi communities, men begin wearing it at marriage, while in other communities boys wear it from bar mitzvah.
- Tzitzit are a daytime mitzvah. The tallit is worn at night only on Yom Kippur.

## How to put on a tallit, step by step

1. **Check the strings** to make sure none are torn.
2. **Hold the tallit** open with both hands and say the blessing (below).
3. **Wrap it around your head and shoulders** for a moment, as a sign of being surrounded by holiness.
4. **Let it rest on your shoulders**, with two corners in front and two behind.
5. **During the Shema**, many gather the four fringes and hold them, kissing them at each mention of tzitzit in the third paragraph.

## The blessings

**For the tallit gadol:**
Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu l'hitatef b'tzitzit.
"Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us to wrap ourselves in tzitzit."

**For the tallit katan:**
Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu al mitzvat tzitzit.
"Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us concerning the mitzvah of tzitzit."

Someone who will put on a tallit gadol soon after waking may follow a different practice for the tallit katan blessing. Ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### Should tzitzit be worn tucked in or out?
Customs vary. Many people let the strings hang out so they can be seen, since the Torah says "you shall see it." Others tuck them in for various reasons. Ask the Rabbi about your community's custom.

### What if one of the strings tears?
A torn string may make the tzitzit not kosher, depending on how many strings and how much is left. Do not wear it with a blessing until you ask the Rabbi or have it checked.

### Can I wear tzitzit at night?
Yes, many people wear their tallit katan day and night. The blessing, however, is said only during the day.

### What is the difference between tzitzit and a tallit?
Tzitzit are the fringes themselves. A tallit is the four-cornered garment they are tied to: the large prayer shawl or the small everyday tallit katan.
