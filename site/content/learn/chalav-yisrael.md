Chalav Yisrael, often spelled cholov Yisroel, is milk that was supervised by a Jew from the time of milking, to make sure it comes only from kosher animals. Regular kosher-certified milk in the US is not chalav Yisrael, but many authorities permit it; Chabad and many other communities make an effort to use chalav Yisrael.

## What it means

The Torah forbids milk from non-kosher animals. In Talmudic times, people might mix milk from a non-kosher animal, such as a donkey or camel, into cow's milk. So the Sages decreed that milk must be watched by a Jew during milking. Milk produced this way is called chalav Yisrael, "Jewish milk."

## Chalav stam: regular kosher milk

In the 20th century, some authorities, most famously Rabbi Moshe Feinstein, ruled that in countries like the United States, where government regulations strictly control what is sold as cow's milk, the government supervision can count as reliable knowledge. Milk and dairy produced this way is called chalav stam, "plain milk." Many kosher-certified dairy products in American supermarkets rely on this view.

Even Rabbi Feinstein wrote that those who are careful to use chalav Yisrael are acting properly.

## The Chabad approach

The Rebbe strongly encouraged using chalav Yisrael, especially for children, since what we eat affects the soul and character. Many Chabad families and others keep chalav Yisrael at home and look for it in all dairy foods. If you are thinking about taking it on, talk to the Rabbi about how to begin.

## What products are affected

- **Milk and cream**, the main focus.
- **Butter, yogurt, ice cream and dairy desserts.**
- **Chocolate and baked goods** that contain milk or milk powder.
- **Cheese** has an additional rule, called gevinat Yisrael, requiring Jewish involvement in making it. Most kosher-certified cheese meets this.

## How to identify it

1. **Look for the words** "Cholov Yisroel," "Chalav Yisrael" or "CY" next to the kosher symbol.
2. **A "D" or "Dairy"** next to a symbol means the product is dairy, not that it is chalav Yisrael.
3. **Check every item.** A company may make some products chalav Yisrael and others not.

## Practical tips

- **Buy in larger amounts and freeze** milk, butter and cheese if your local store carries little.
- **Choose pareve alternatives**, like non-dairy creamers, when you cannot find chalav Yisrael.
- **Dishes do not change.** Using chalav stam in your dairy dishes does not make them unkosher for someone who follows that view; ask the Rabbi about your kitchen if you switch.

## Common questions

### Is regular kosher milk kosher?
Milk with a reliable kosher symbol is considered kosher by many authorities, based on government supervision. Those who keep chalav Yisrael use only milk supervised from milking.

### Why does Chabad emphasize chalav Yisrael?
The Rebbe taught that the food we eat becomes part of us and affects our spiritual sensitivity, and urged families, especially with children, to use chalav Yisrael.

### Does chalav Yisrael taste different?
No. It is regular cow's milk. The difference is the supervision during milking.

### Do I need new dishes if I start keeping chalav Yisrael?
Usually not. Most authorities do not require kashering dairy dishes that were used with chalav stam. Ask the Rabbi about your situation.
