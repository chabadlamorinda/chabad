With a toddler, nothing goes exactly as planned. That is fine. Little children do not need a perfect Shabbat or holiday. They need warm, happy memories, repeated often. A few minutes of joy each week builds a Jewish identity that lasts.

## Keep it short and repeat it

- **One or two rituals** are enough: candles, Kiddush, challah, a song.
- **Same order every week.** Toddlers love predictability.
- **Let them do it:** pour the water for [netilat yadayim](/learn/netilat-yadayim), hold the challah cover, drop a coin in the [pushka](/learn/pushka).
- **Sing.** Shabbat and holiday songs work better than explanations.

## Shabbat

- Many Chabad families encourage girls from about age three to light their own small Shabbat candle, with a parent's help. See [Shabbat Candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).
- Give tzedakah together before candle lighting.
- Bless the children on Friday night with a hand on their head.
- Bake or decorate a small challah. See [Shabbat with Kids](/learn/shabbat-with-kids).

## Holiday by holiday

- **Rosh Hashanah:** dip apple in honey, hear the shofar (a short blast is plenty), make a round challah.
- **Yom Kippur:** little children do not fast. Keep their day calm with books and quiet play.
- **Sukkot:** decorate the [sukkah](/learn/sukkah) with paper chains and drawings, eat a snack inside, let them hold the lulav with you.
- **Simchat Torah:** flags, dancing and treats.
- **Chanukah:** many Chabad families have each child light a menorah with a parent's help. Spin the [dreidel](/learn/dreidel), make latkes. See [Chanukah for Kids](/learn/chanukah-for-kids).
- **Tu B'Shevat:** taste new fruits and plant a seed.
- **Purim:** a simple costume, a noisemaker, and delivering [mishloach manot](/learn/mishloach-manot) to a neighbor.
- **Passover:** let them ask whatever questions they can, hunt for the [afikoman](/learn/afikoman), and point at the frogs. See [The Passover Seder](/learn/passover-seder).
- **Shavuot:** the Rebbe encouraged bringing every child, even babies, to synagogue to hear the Ten Commandments. Add dairy treats.

## Senses, not lectures

Toddlers learn by touching, tasting, smelling and moving. Smell the [Havdalah](/learn/havdalah) spices, feel the etrog, taste the honey, dance with the Torah. That is real Jewish education at this age.

## Realistic expectations

- Plan around naps and meals, not the other way around.
- A meltdown during Kiddush is not a failure. Finish later, or tomorrow.
- Do what you can. Adding one new thing a year is wonderful.
- Ask grandparents or friends to join; it is more fun with more people.

## Synagogue with toddlers

- Come for the best part: the shofar, the Megillah, hakafot, or the Ten Commandments.
- Bring a quiet bag: board books, soft toys, a kosher snack.
- Step out when needed, without guilt, and come back.
- Children's programs and family events are listed on our [events page](/events).

## Common questions

### My toddler won't sit for the whole Shabbat meal. Is that okay?
Yes. Let them join for candles, Kiddush and challah, then play nearby. They are still part of Shabbat.

### Is my baby too young for synagogue?
No baby is too young to be welcome. Come for a short time and leave when you need to.

### We are not very observant. Where do we start?
Pick one thing, like Friday night candles and challah, and do it every week. See [Shabbat for Beginners](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners).
