When we come safely through something dangerous, Judaism gives us a way to say thank you out loud, in front of the community: **Birkat HaGomel**, the blessing of thanksgiving. It turns relief into gratitude, and lets friends share in our joy.

## Who says it

The blessing comes from Psalm 107, which describes four kinds of people who should thank G-d after being saved:

- someone who **recovered from a serious illness** (including after major surgery);
- someone who **crossed the sea**;
- someone who **traveled through the desert** or another dangerous journey;
- someone who was **released from captivity**.

From these, Jewish law teaches that anyone who came safely through real danger says it, for example after a serious accident or a dangerous situation. After a **flight over the ocean**, such as to Israel or Europe, the blessing is said. For a woman after childbirth, it depends on the family's custom; ask the Rabbi.

## How and when

The blessing is said in front of a **minyan** (ten men), usually in synagogue right after the Torah reading. Often the person is called up for an aliyah and says it after the closing blessing. It is best said within three days, but if that passed, it can still be said later.

The words:

> **Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu Melech ha-olam, ha-gomel l'chayavim tovot, she-gemalani kol tov.**
> Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who does good to the undeserving, and who has done me every good.

And everyone answers:

> **Amen. Mi she-gemalcha kol tov, hu yigmalcha kol tov, selah.**
> May He who has done you every good continue to do you every good, forever.

## Why say it in public

Gratitude grows when it is shared. Saying the blessing before the community turns a private moment into a celebration, and reminds everyone listening that blessings come from G-d. Many people also give [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) or sponsor a Kiddush in thanks.

## Common questions

### I had surgery. Should I say Birkat HaGomel?
After a serious illness or major surgery, usually yes, once you are well enough to come to synagogue. Ask the Rabbi about your situation.

### Can I say it if I cannot come to synagogue?
It is meant to be said with a minyan. If that is hard, talk to the Rabbi; often a minyan can be gathered where you are.

### Do I say it after a flight?
Yes, after a flight that crossed the ocean, for example to Israel, Europe or Hawaii. Say it at the first Torah reading you can attend after you land.

### Can someone say it for me?
The person who was saved says it themselves. Others can add their own thanks, give tzedakah in your honor, and answer "Amen."
