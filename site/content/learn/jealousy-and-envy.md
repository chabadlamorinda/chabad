A neighbor's new car, a friend's perfect vacation photos, a colleague's promotion. Envy is one of the most human feelings, and social media has made it easier than ever to feel that everyone else has more. The last of the [Ten Commandments](/learn/ten-commandments) speaks directly to it: "You shall not covet... anything that belongs to your neighbor."

## Why the Torah cares about a feeling

Most commandments are about what we do. "Do not covet" is about what we want. Jewish teachers explain that this is because envy quietly does damage: it steals joy from what we have, it spoils friendships, and it can lead to words and actions we regret. The Sages said that "jealousy, desire and the pursuit of honor take a person out of the world" (Pirkei Avot 4:21): they make it impossible to enjoy the life we are actually living.

## How can a commandment control a feeling?

The classic answer: the more deeply a person believes that G-d gives each of us exactly what we need for our own mission, the less sense it makes to want someone else's. The commentator Ibn Ezra gives a classic example: a simple villager does not long to marry the king's daughter, because he knows that life is simply not his. When we truly see our own life as given to us by G-d, with its own purpose, another person's blessings stop being a threat.

## Practical ways to quiet envy

- **Count your blessings, out loud.** Begin the day with [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani), and thank G-d for specific things. [Gratitude](/learn/hakarat-hatov) is the opposite of envy.
- **Bless the other person.** When you notice envy, wish them well, sincerely, in your own words. It changes you more than them.
- **Remember you see only part of the picture.** Every life has hidden struggles; photos show the highlights.
- **Use envy as fuel, the good kind.** The Sages said "the envy of scholars increases wisdom." If someone's kindness or learning inspires you, let it push you to grow, not to resent.
- **Give.** [Tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) teaches the heart that what we have is not only ours, and that we have enough to share.
- **Limit the triggers.** If scrolling leaves you feeling worse, take a break. [Shabbat](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners) is a weekly one built in.

## Common questions

### Is it a sin to feel jealous?
A passing feeling is human. The Torah asks us not to let it grow into wanting what belongs to someone else, and not to act on it. Working on it is itself a mitzvah.

### What about jealousy in a marriage or between siblings?
That is a different, sensitive kind of jealousy. See [shalom bayit](/learn/shalom-bayit) and [sibling rivalry](/learn/sibling-rivalry), or talk privately with the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin.

### Is "holy envy" a real idea?
Yes. Being inspired by someone's goodness to become better yourself is praised in the Talmud, as long as it comes with goodwill toward them.
