The Chanukah menorah is lit each night shortly after sunset, according to Chabad custom, and the lights must burn for at least half an hour after nightfall. Because the time changes every night and by location, check tonight's time for Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda on [the menorah page](/menorah) instead of relying on memory.

## The basic rule

The Talmud says the mitzvah begins at sunset and lasts "until the marketplace empties," meaning until people are no longer out on the street. Today, the menorah is in the home, and the main idea is to light at a time when the family is together and the lights burn long enough to be seen.

The Chabad custom is to light shortly after sunset, before the stars come out. Many other communities light when the stars appear. Both are old, established customs. If your family has a custom, ask the Rabbi what to do.

## How long the lights must burn

The lights must burn for at least half an hour after nightfall. If you light at sunset, there is a gap before nightfall, so have enough fuel for at least 50 minutes in total. Check that your candles or oil really last that long. See [oil or candles](/learn/menorah-oil-and-candles). If a candle is too short, use a different one rather than ignoring the rule.

After lighting, it is a good custom to stay near the lights for about half an hour, to sing, learn or enjoy the glow with family.

## Friday: before Shabbat candles

On Friday the menorah is lit before the Shabbat candles, because once Shabbat starts we no longer kindle a flame. Since Friday's lighting is early, it needs much more fuel: the lights must burn until half an hour after nightfall. Use large candles, or oil cups that hold enough oil. Chabad custom is to light the menorah first and then the Shabbat candles. ## Saturday night: after Shabbat

On Saturday night, light after Shabbat has ended. Many families light after Havdalah, and in some homes the order is reversed, so follow your custom. Do not light until Shabbat is over. See [Chanukah on Shabbat](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat) and [Havdalah](/learn/havdalah).

## What if you cannot light at the usual time?

If the family is not home yet, it is fine to wait a short while so everyone can see and take part. If you come home late and the household is still awake, you can light then. For missed nights and edge cases, read [Forgot to Light the Menorah?](/learn/missed-lighting-the-menorah).

## Common questions

### What time do you light the menorah?
Chabad custom is to light shortly after sunset, and the lights must burn at least half an hour after nightfall. Tonight's exact time for Lafayette is on [the menorah page](/menorah).

### Can you light Chanukah candles before sunset?
Not on a regular weekday: the mitzvah begins at sunset. If you truly cannot light later, ask the Rabbi, since in some cases an earlier time is possible. Friday is the exception: we light before Shabbat, and use extra fuel so they burn until after nightfall.

### How long should the Chanukah candles burn?
At least half an hour after nightfall. If lit at sunset, plan on at least 50 minutes of fuel.

### When do you light the menorah on Friday?
Before the Shabbat candles, with enough oil or long candles to last half an hour past nightfall.

### When do you light the menorah on Saturday night?
After Shabbat ends. Many light after Havdalah; some do it the other way around. Ask the Rabbi about your custom.

### Do I have to light at exactly sunset?
No. Chabad custom is shortly after sunset, but other customs light at nightfall. What matters is that the lights are lit at the right time and burn long enough.
