Jews do not have one language: the holy tongue is Hebrew (Lashon Hakodesh, לשון הקודש), and in each land Jews also built their own everyday languages, such as Yiddish, Ladino, Judeo-Arabic and Bukharian, mixing the local speech with Hebrew and writing it in Hebrew letters. Today Hebrew is the national language of Israel, and English is the most common language of Jewish life in the Bay Area.

## Hebrew, the holy tongue

Hebrew is the language of the Torah. It was spoken in the Land of Israel until about the second century of the common era, and afterward Jews around the world kept it alive in prayer and study. In the late 19th century it was revived as an everyday spoken language, and Modern Hebrew (Ivrit) is the national language of Israel. Many scholars consider it a distinct language from Biblical Hebrew. To begin reading, see [the Hebrew alphabet](/learn/hebrew-alphabet).

## Aramaic

Aramaic was adopted during the Babylonian exile and served as a Jewish common language for over a thousand years. Parts of the Bible, the [Talmud](/learn/talmud), the Zohar and the Kaddish prayer are in Aramaic. Kurdish Jews spoke it until several decades ago.

## Yiddish and Ladino

**Yiddish** developed among Jews in the German lands about a thousand years ago and spread to Poland and Russia. It still thrives in Chassidic communities. See [Yiddish words and phrases](/learn/yiddish-words-and-phrases).

**Ladino** is the language of Sephardic Jews, rooted in Spain before the Expulsion of 1492. Jews carried it around the Mediterranean and to Romania, France, the United States and Latin America. It declined over about 150 years and is now slowly being revived.

## Languages of the Middle East and Central Asia

- **Judeo-Arabic:** Arabic dialects that Jews spoke in the Middle East and North Africa for centuries, written in Hebrew letters.
- **Judeo-Persian:** Persian dialects spoken by Jews. **Juhuri** (Judeo-Tat) is spoken by the Mountain Jews of the Caucasus.
- **Bukharian**, a Jewish language of Central Asia, and **Judeo-Georgian** each have about 80,000 speakers.
- Others, such as Judeo-Provençal, Jewish Malayalam in Cochin, Judeo-Roman and Yevanic, are almost gone.

Many Russian-speaking families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda come from these regions, and we love hearing their stories. Read more in [Russian-speaking Jews in Lamorinda](/learn/russian-speaking-jews-in-lamorinda).

## One pattern, many languages

In every language the pattern repeats: Jews took the local speech, added Hebrew and Aramaic words for Jewish life, and wrote it in Hebrew letters. Hebrew and Yiddish words also entered other languages. English has "mensch" and "chutzpah." And English is now a main language for teaching Torah worldwide, including here in the Bay Area.

## Common questions

### What language do Jews speak?
Jews speak the language of the country where they live. Hebrew is the holy tongue and the national language of Israel, and Jews have also developed languages such as Yiddish and Ladino.

### What is the Jewish language called?
Hebrew is the language of the Jewish people and the Torah. Yiddish is the traditional everyday language of Ashkenazi Jews of Europe.

### What is Ladino?
Ladino is the language of Sephardic Jews, which began in Spain before 1492. It combines old Spanish with Hebrew words and is still spoken by a small group.

### What is Judeo-Arabic?
Judeo-Arabic is the Arabic spoken and written by Jews in Arab lands, written in Hebrew letters.

### What language do Bukharian Jews speak?
Bukharian Jews of Central Asia speak Bukharian, a Jewish language of the region, which has about 80,000 speakers.
