The Ten Lost Tribes are the ten of the twelve tribes of Israel who made up the northern Kingdom of Israel and were exiled by Assyria in the time of the First Temple. They disappeared from history, and Jewish tradition teaches that they will be gathered back when Moshiach comes. No one has conclusively identified where they are.

## Who were the ten tribes?

After the reign of King Solomon, the Jewish people split into two kingdoms. The southern kingdom, Judah, kept the tribes of Judah and Benjamin (with Levi), and the northern kingdom, Israel, held the other ten tribes. Read how it happened in [the kingdoms of Israel and Judah](/learn/kingdoms-of-israel-and-judah) and about the family in [the twelve tribes](/learn/twelve-tribes).

According to Chabad.org, the northern kingdom lasted 241 years, and its kings were all wicked. Prophets such as Elijah, Elisha, Hosea, Amos and Jonah spoke to the people, but they did not return. In the end the king removed the guards that stopped people from going up to the Temple in Jerusalem, and the people still did not go.

## The Assyrian exile

The exile came in stages over about eighteen years. First the Assyrian king Tiglath-Pileser took the northern tribes of Naphtali and Zebulun. Then the Reubenites, Gadites and half of Manasseh were taken to places such as Halah, Habor and the River Gozan. Finally King Hoshea rebelled, and Shalmaneser besieged the capital, Samaria, for three years. When it fell, the Kingdom of Israel ended. Assyria settled the land with other peoples.

Chabad.org notes that more than 80% of the Jewish people were lost, the largest proportional loss in Jewish history. Some people from the ten tribes had already settled in Judah, and the Prophet Jeremiah later brought some back from the east, so not every member of those tribes was lost.

## Where are they? Legends and the Sambatyon

For centuries people have wondered where the ten tribes went. Chabad.org says no one has identified them conclusively. Here are a few well-known accounts, which should be read as traditions, not proven facts:

- **The Sambatyon river.** The Talmud tells of Rabbi Akiva citing a river whose water flows on weekdays and rests on Shabbat. Ancient writers also mention it, but they do not agree on which day it rests. Later stories place the lost tribes behind this river.
- **Eldad HaDani.** In the ninth century a traveler calling himself Eldad of the tribe of Dan appeared in Kairouan (today Tunisia) with stories of the tribes living near the Sambatyon. A leading rabbi of the time said much of his account fit Jewish tradition. Medieval scholars disagreed about him: Rashi and Tosafot cited his writings, while Ibn Ezra and the Maharam of Rothenburg dismissed them.
- **Modern claims.** People have proposed places across Africa, Asia and the Americas. This guide takes no position on any modern group's claims; Chabad.org does not endorse any specific location.

## Will they return?

The sages of the Talmud debated this. Rabbi Akiva said they will not return, comparing their exile to a day that does not come back. Rabbi Eliezer said they will, like a night followed by morning. Rabbi Shimon said it depends on their repentance. Chabad.org explains that Rabbi Yehuda the Prince declared that the ten tribes have a share in the World to Come, and that authorities rule that they will be reunited with the rest of Israel with the coming of Moshiach. The prophets Isaiah and Ezekiel speak of this reunion. Read about [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach).

Citing the Maharal of Prague, Chabad.org adds that the ten tribes will not be found by searching: their exile is a divine decree lasting until the messianic era, and they exist now mainly in potential.

## Why does it matter to us?

The story tells us two things. First, that Jewish unity is precious and fragile: the nation was once split. Second, that no Jew is ever truly lost. The promise is that every Jew will be gathered back. If you wonder about a family tradition or a roots question of your own, ask the Rabbi; see [who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew).

## Common questions

### What are the Ten Lost Tribes?
They are ten of the twelve tribes of Israel who lived in the northern kingdom and were exiled by Assyria. Their descendants merged into other peoples.

### Where are the Ten Lost Tribes today?
No one knows for certain. Chabad.org says they have not been conclusively identified and will not be found by searching.

### What is the Sambatyon?
The Sambatyon is a legendary river that rests on Shabbat. It appears in the Talmud, and later stories place the lost tribes beyond it. Treat it as tradition, not a verified place.

### Will the Ten Lost Tribes return?
Tradition says they will be gathered back with Moshiach. The Talmud records a debate between Rabbi Akiva and Rabbi Eliezer, and authorities rule in favor of the return.

### Who was Eldad HaDani?
He was a ninth-century traveler who claimed to come from the tribe of Dan and told of the lost tribes. Medieval scholars disagreed on how far to trust him.

### Are Jews today descended from the ten tribes?
Some tribal members settled in Judah before the exile, and Jeremiah brought some back. For your own family, ask the Rabbi.
