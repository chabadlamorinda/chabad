Many people think Jewish prayer means reading Hebrew from a prayer book, in synagogue, at set times. That is an important part of it. But Judaism also teaches something simpler and very personal: you can talk to G-d anytime, anywhere, in your own words, and He is listening.

## Prayer began with the heart

The Talmud calls prayer "the service of the heart." The first prayers in the Torah were personal: Abraham praying for the people of Sodom, Hannah weeping quietly for a child, Moses pleading for his sister Miriam with five words: "Please, G-d, heal her, please." The set [daily prayers](/learn/daily-prayers) were arranged later, in part so that everyone would have the right words. But personal prayer never stopped.

## Any language, any time

You do not need Hebrew. G-d understands every language, and a sincere prayer in English or Russian is a real prayer. You do not need a synagogue: you can pray in the car, on a walk, by a hospital bed, at your child's bedside. You can whisper, or say it only in your heart.

## What to say

Anything that is true. Some ideas:

- **Thank Him** for something specific that happened today.
- **Ask** for what you need: health, a livelihood, a good match, peace at home, help with a decision. Judaism encourages asking; it shows we know where blessings come from.
- **Ask for others**: someone sick, a friend who is struggling, the Jewish people.
- **Say what is hard.** Frustration, fear and even confusion can be said to G-d. Many of King David's [Psalms](/learn/tehillim) do exactly that.
- **Say sorry**, and ask for help to do better.

## A simple way to begin

1. Find a quiet minute; it does not have to be long.
2. Begin with a familiar line, like the [Shema](/learn/shema) or "Modeh ani, I thank You."
3. Speak simply, as to a loving parent who knows you completely.
4. End with a small resolution: a good deed in the merit of your prayer.

Giving a coin to [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) before praying is a beautiful old custom; it opens the heart.

## Common questions

### Does G-d really listen to me?
Yes. Jewish teaching says that no sincere prayer is ever wasted. Sometimes the answer is not what we asked for, or comes later, but every prayer is heard.

### Is personal prayer instead of the prayer book?
It is in addition to it. The set prayers connect us to all Jews and to generations before us; personal words add your own voice. Many people add a personal request in the middle of the Amidah, in the blessing "Shema Koleinu."

### What if I don't know what to say?
Start with "Thank you," or read a Psalm slowly. Even sitting quietly and wanting to connect is a beginning.
