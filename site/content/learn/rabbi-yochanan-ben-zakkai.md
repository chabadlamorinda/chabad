Rabbi Yochanan ben Zakkai (רבן יוחנן בן זכאי) was the leading sage of his generation, and when Jerusalem was under Roman siege he asked the Roman commander for "Yavneh and its sages." That request moved the center of Torah learning to a small town, and the Torah survived the destruction of the Temple in the year 70.

## Jerusalem under siege

According to Chabad.org, the city in those years was torn apart from the inside. While the Roman general Vespasian conquered the north, Jewish factions in Jerusalem fought each other. The Zealots wanted all-out war and rejected the Sages' proposal to send a peace delegation. Some groups even burned the city's stores of food, which had been stocked for a long siege, to force the people to fight. They also stopped anyone from leaving.

Rabbi Yochanan, the leading sage of his time, saw that Jerusalem was doomed. He wanted Torah scholarship to live on somewhere else.

## The escape and the request

Because the city was sealed, Rabbi Yochanan pretended to be dead, and his disciples carried his "coffin" out of Jerusalem. He walked straight to Vespasian's tent and greeted him as "Your Majesty." Vespasian answered that he was only a general. Rabbi Yochanan replied that he must become emperor, or the Temple would not be handed to him. A messenger then arrived with news that the emperor Nero had died and Vespasian had been named in his place.

Impressed, Vespasian offered him any reward. Rabbi Yochanan made three requests. The main one was: spare Yavneh and its Torah sages.

Chabad.org adds a thought-provoking note. Rabbi Yochanan did not ask for Jerusalem itself to be spared, apparently because he thought such a request would be refused. The Talmud discusses whether he chose wisely. Ask the Rabbi if you would like to explore that discussion.

## What happened at Yavneh

Yavneh became the new home of the [Sanhedrin](/learn/the-sanhedrin) and a spiritual center for the Jewish people. Chabad.org describes what Rabbi Yochanan and his colleagues did there:

- He founded a major yeshiva and re-established the Sanhedrin, giving it equal standing with the earlier Great Sanhedrin so its rulings would be respected.
- The court recorded the laws of the Temple service and its layout, because eyewitnesses were passing away.
- It organized the prayer service, and revised the [Amidah](/learn/amidah) to pray for the Temple's restoration rather than only its survival.
- It sent delegations to Rome to speak for Jewish needs and paid to redeem Jewish captives.
- It extended customs to keep the Temple's memory alive. For example, the four species on [Sukkot](/holidays/sukkot) are held all seven days everywhere, not only in the Temple.

Rabbi Yochanan trained Rabban Gamliel, the rightful heir of the family of Hillel, to lead as Nasi, and then stepped aside so Rabban Gamliel could lead without being overshadowed.

## Judaism after the Temple

Chabad.org makes an important point. Torah law did not change after the destruction, and observance continued, even without the sacrifices. What changed was where the nation's life was centered: in the study hall, the synagogue and the home. This was not the first time Jews rebuilt after a loss. See [Ezra and Nehemiah](/learn/ezra-and-nehemiah) for the story of the return from Babylon, and [the Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple) for what the Temple meant.

## What it teaches us

Rabbi Yochanan's choice was not about winning the battle. It was about saving what could not be replaced. The same is true today, whether you live in Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda or anywhere else in the Bay Area: Judaism lives wherever people learn Torah, light Shabbat candles, and keep the traditions in their homes. These days of the year are tied to that history: see [the Tenth of Tevet](/learn/tevet), a fast that recalls the siege of Jerusalem and the beginning of the destruction of the First Temple, and [Tisha B'Av](/learn/tisha-bav-for-beginners), the day the Temple fell.

## Common questions

### Who was Rabbi Yochanan ben Zakkai?
He was the leading sage in Jerusalem during the Roman siege and a disciple of Beit Hillel. He founded the academy at Yavneh and re-established the Sanhedrin there.

### What does "Give me Yavneh and its sages" mean?
It was Rabbi Yochanan's request to the Roman commander Vespasian to spare the town of Yavneh and its Torah scholars. It let Torah study continue after Jerusalem fell.

### How did Rabbi Yochanan escape Jerusalem?
Because the Zealots would not let anyone leave, he pretended to die, and his disciples carried him out in a coffin. He then went to Vespasian's tent.

### How did Judaism survive after the Temple was destroyed?
Torah law and observance continued, centered on the study hall, prayer and the home. Yavneh's court also organized prayer and set customs that keep the Temple's memory alive.

### Where was Yavneh?
Yavneh was a town in the Land of Israel that became the seat of the Sanhedrin after the Temple's destruction. Chabad.org tells how the Sanhedrin later moved on to other towns.
