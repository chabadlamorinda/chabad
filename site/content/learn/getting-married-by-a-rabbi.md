Yes, a rabbi can marry you in a Jewish wedding, and in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda you can ask Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski of Chabad of Lamorinda by calling 925-384-7242. A Jewish wedding needs two Jewish partners, a ring from the groom, two kosher witnesses and a ketubah, and a rabbi who knows the laws of marriage guides it so that each part is done correctly.

## What does a Jewish wedding require?

A Jewish marriage has two stages, and both are needed for it to be valid:

1. **Kiddushin (betrothal).** The groom gives the bride a ring in front of two witnesses and says the words of betrothal, and she accepts. The ring is the object by which the betrothal is made. See [The Jewish wedding ring](/learn/jewish-wedding-ring).
2. **Nissuin (the nuptials).** Under the chuppah, seven blessings are said and the couple is joined, and then they spend a few private minutes together in yichud. See [What is a chuppah?](/learn/what-is-a-chuppah) and [Yichud](/learn/yichud).

Between these two stages the ketubah, the marriage contract, is read aloud. It protects the wife. See [The ketubah](/learn/ketubah).

## Why a rabbi?

The details matter in Jewish law, and a rabbi makes sure they are right. For example:

- The **ring** must belong to the groom and be a plain band.
- The **witnesses** must be specifically appointed. They must be Torah-observant adult Jewish men who are not relatives of the couple or of each other.
- The **ketubah** is filled in by the officiating rabbi. If you want a designer ketubah, the rabbi should approve its text first.

The rabbi also brings the warmth and meaning to the day, helping you choose honorees for the blessings and explaining what will happen to your families and guests.

## Do both partners have to be Jewish?

A Jewish wedding according to Jewish law is between two Jews. If you are unsure about your own Jewish background, or your partner's, please speak privately and kindly with the Rabbi; he is glad to help and to listen. A gentle explanation is in [Who is a Jew?](/learn/who-is-a-jew), and [Proving Jewish ancestry](/learn/proving-jewish-ancestry) explains what is usually asked. Each situation is personal, so ask the Rabbi.

## What if we are already married civilly?

The site's guide on [the Jewish wedding](/learn/jewish-wedding) notes that many couples who married civilly choose later to have a chuppah, and that it is a beautiful step at any stage. Ask the Rabbi about the details.

## Common questions

### Can a rabbi marry us in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda?

Yes. Rabbi Labkowski officiates at Jewish weddings according to Jewish law. Call 925-384-7242, ideally several months ahead.

### What are the requirements for a Jewish wedding?

Two Jewish partners, a plain ring owned by the groom, two kosher witnesses, a ketubah, and a chuppah with the seven blessings.

### Who can be a witness at a Jewish wedding?

Torah-observant adult Jewish men who are not relatives of the couple or of each other.

### Do we need to belong to a synagogue?

No. Chabad of Lamorinda welcomes every Jew, and no membership is required.

### Can we write our own vows?

The ceremony has set parts, such as the ring and the blessings. Ask the Rabbi where personal words fit in.

### Can a Jewish wedding be at a venue instead of a synagogue?

Weddings can take place in many places. Ask the Rabbi about the space you are considering.
