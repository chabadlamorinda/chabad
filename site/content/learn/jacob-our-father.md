Jacob (Yaakov, יַעֲקֹב) was the third of the Patriarchs, after Abraham and Isaac, and the father of the twelve sons who became the twelve tribes of Israel. After wrestling with a mysterious angel he was given a second name, Israel (Yisrael, יִשְׂרָאֵל), the name of the whole Jewish people.

## The twins and the blessing

Jacob and his twin Esau were born to Isaac and [Rebecca](/learn/the-matriarchs) after twenty years of marriage. Esau became a hunter; Jacob was a studious, quiet man. Jacob bought Esau's birthright for a bowl of lentil stew, and later, at Rebecca's urging, he received Isaac's blessing in Esau's place. Furious, Esau threatened him, and Jacob fled to his uncle Laban in Haran.

## The ladder at Beth El

On the road, alone, Jacob dreamed of a ladder standing on the earth with its top reaching heaven and angels going up and down. G-d promised him the Land, many descendants and protection wherever he went. Jacob named the place Beth El, "House of G-d." The lesson Chassidic teaching draws: heaven and earth are connected, and even a lonely roadside can become holy.

## Rachel, Leah and twenty years with Laban

Jacob worked seven years for Rachel. Laban gave him Leah first, and Jacob worked seven more years for Rachel, then six more for his flocks, about twenty years in all. Leah bore Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun and a daughter, Dinah. Rachel bore Joseph and Benjamin. Bilhah and Zilpah bore Dan, Naphtali, Gad and Asher. The sons are the subject of [the twelve tribes](/learn/twelve-tribes).

## Wrestling the angel: the name Israel

Returning home, Jacob feared Esau. At the Yabbok river he wrestled through the night with a mysterious figure, which our Sages identify as Esau's guardian angel. His thigh was injured, yet he would not let go until he was blessed. He was told: "You shall no longer be called Jacob but Israel." The name is explained as one who strives with angels and men and prevails. This is why Jews do not eat the sciatic nerve (gid hanasheh). The next morning Esau ran to him, embraced him, and the brothers parted in peace.

## Two names, two ways of serving

The Rebbe teaches that the two names are two modes of serving G-d. Yaakov is service in the hidden: Jacob received the blessing quietly, and we too bring holiness into ordinary things like eating and business without announcing it. Yisrael is service in the open, like the Shabbat meal, where the holiness is plain to see. The Torah keeps calling him Yaakov even after the new name, because every one of us needs both. Read this on the Shabbats of Vayetzei and Vayishlach; see [this week's Torah portion](/parsha).

## Later years

Rachel died giving birth to Benjamin, and Jacob mourned for Joseph, whom his brothers had sold, for twenty-two years. In [Joseph's story](/learn/joseph-and-his-brothers) he moved to Egypt with about seventy members of his family, lived there seventeen years and asked to be buried in the Cave of Machpelah in Hebron. The Talmud says "Jacob our father did not die": his children carry on his life.

## Common questions

### Who was Jacob in the Bible?
Jacob was the third Patriarch, son of Isaac and Rebecca, husband of Leah and Rachel and father of the twelve tribes of Israel.

### Why was Jacob renamed Israel?
After he wrestled through the night with a mysterious angel and would not let go without a blessing, he was told he would now be called Israel, one who strives with angels and men and prevails.

### What is Jacob's ladder?
It is Jacob's dream at Beth El of a ladder from earth to heaven with angels ascending and descending, a promise that G-d is with us wherever we go.

### Why don't Jews eat the sciatic nerve?
Because Jacob's thigh was injured in the fight with the angel, the Torah forbids eating the gid hanasheh. Ask the Rabbi about kosher meat and how it is prepared.

### What is the difference between Yaakov and Yisrael?
In the Rebbe's teaching, Yaakov is serving G-d through hidden, everyday matters and Yisrael is serving G-d openly; a Jew needs both.
