The first yahrzeit (יארצייט) is the first anniversary of a death by the Hebrew calendar: one Hebrew year after the date of death, on the same Hebrew month and day. On that day, Jewish tradition asks us to light a memorial candle, say Kaddish, give charity and learn Torah in the person's memory.

Chabad of Lamorinda can arrange Kaddish for your loved one: please use the form on our [Kaddish page](/kaddish). A memorial plaque can also be added in the synagogue; please consult the Rabbi (925-384-7242).

For someone who has lost a parent, it also marks the end of the twelve months of mourning.

## Finding the date

The yahrzeit follows the Hebrew calendar, so it falls on a different secular date each year. Jewish tradition gives an example: a death on 4 Elul falls, a year later, on 4 Elul.

- **If burial was delayed,** the yahrzeit still follows the date of death. Some authorities use the burial date for the first year if burial was long delayed, so ask the Rabbi.
- **If the death was at dusk,** the following day is considered the yahrzeit.
- **Leap years, and deaths in Adar, Kislev or Tevet** have special rules. The first yahrzeit can fall differently in these cases. Ask the Rabbi to confirm your date.

See our guide to the [yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit) for more on dates. You can also save your family's dates in your free [account](/account), and we will send reminders.

## The night before: the candle

Light a memorial candle at nightfall on the evening before the yahrzeit, and let it burn out by itself unless there is a fire risk. A real candle is preferred over an electric bulb. When the yahrzeit falls on Shabbat or a holiday, the candle is lit before the regular candle lighting. If you forgot, light it the next morning (but not on Shabbat), and if the day has passed, give to charity. See [the yahrzeit candle](/learn/yahrzeit-candle).

## What to do on the day

1. **Say Kaddish** at every service of the day. A psalm is usually added to the morning service so that the mourner can say at least one Kaddish alone. Kaddish requires a minyan. Read about [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish), and see our [Kaddish page](/kaddish) if you cannot be at a minyan.
2. **Lead the services if you can,** or at least learn the short afternoon prayer, Minchah.
3. **Learn Torah.** Mishnah is traditional, and its letters are the same as the word neshamah, "soul." If that is not possible, read a chapter of the Bible. See [learning in memory of a loved one](/learn/learning-in-memory-of-a-loved-one).
4. **Give tzedakah** in the person's memory: to a school, a synagogue, a medical cause or the poor.
5. **Bring refreshments** to the morning service and say "l'chaim." Chassidic teaching says this is customary but should not become a full party.

On the Shabbat before the yahrzeit, a mourner traditionally receives an aliyah, and if possible chants the maftir.

## Fasting

Some mourners fast from dawn to nightfall. Once someone takes this on every year, it becomes an obligation. If you cannot fast, avoid meat, wine and festivities instead. Do not fast on holidays or other joyous days. If you are unsure, ask the Rabbi.

## Visiting the grave

An annual visit is traditional. Recite Psalms and the memorial prayer, in Hebrew or English, and study Mishnah at the graveside if you can. The tradition says it is better to say them yourself than to hire someone. Prayers are directed to G-d, not to the person who died. See [visiting a grave](/learn/visiting-a-grave). If the stone is not yet set, the [unveiling](/learn/unveiling) is customarily held within the first year.

## Common questions

### What is a first yahrzeit?
It is the first anniversary of a person's death according to the Hebrew calendar. It falls one Hebrew year after the date of death.

### How do I find the date of the first yahrzeit?
Convert the date of death to the Hebrew calendar, and use the same Hebrew month and day one year later. In a leap year or for a death in Adar, ask the Rabbi to confirm.

### What do you do on a first yahrzeit?
Light a candle on the evening before, say Kaddish, learn Torah, give tzedakah and visit the grave if possible.

### Do I stop saying Kaddish before the yahrzeit?
For a parent, Kaddish is said for eleven months, and then again at every yahrzeit. The twelve months of mourning end at the first yahrzeit.

### Can I light the candle on Shabbat?
Light it before the day begins, before regular candle lighting, since a flame is not kindled on Shabbat. Ask the Rabbi if you forgot or have questions.

### Do we have to go to the cemetery?
An annual grave visit is traditional, but if you cannot go, you can say Psalms and learn Mishnah elsewhere.
