A yahrzeit is the yearly anniversary of a loved one's passing, observed on the Hebrew date of death. On the yahrzeit, family members light a 24-hour candle, say Kaddish, and do good deeds in the person's memory.

## What does yahrzeit mean?

Yahrzeit is a Yiddish word meaning "year's time." Jewish tradition teaches that on this day each year, the soul of the departed rises to a higher level in heaven. The good deeds we do on earth help bring this elevation about, giving the day a special spiritual power.

## How to find the date

A yahrzeit follows the Hebrew calendar, so it falls on a different English date each year.

- Use our [yahrzeit calculator](/tools/yahrzeit) to find the date.
- Remember that a Hebrew day begins at sunset. If your loved one passed away after sundown, the Hebrew date is the next day.
- The first yahrzeit is usually counted from the date of death. In some cases (such as when burial was delayed more than a few days), ask the Rabbi.
- In a Jewish leap year, if the death occurred in the month of Adar, ask the Rabbi which Adar to observe.

The yahrzeit begins the evening before, at sunset, and continues until nightfall the next day.

## How to observe a yahrzeit

1. **Light a yahrzeit candle.** At sunset on the evening the yahrzeit begins, light a candle that burns for 24 hours. The flame represents the soul, as the verse says: "The soul of man is the candle of G-d" (Proverbs 20:27). There is no special blessing.
2. **Say Kaddish.** Attend services to recite the [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish) at the evening, morning and afternoon prayers.
3. **Have an aliyah.** Many people are called to the Torah on the Shabbat before the yahrzeit, or on the day itself if the Torah is read.
4. **Give tzedakah.** Charity in the person's memory brings merit to their soul.
5. **Learn Torah.** Studying, especially Mishnah, is a traditional way to honor the departed. The Hebrew letters of the word Mishnah are the same as neshamah, soul.
6. **Visit the grave.** Many people visit the cemetery on or near the yahrzeit.
7. **Sponsor a kiddush.** Some families offer refreshments after services, so people can say blessings in their loved one's memory.

## Mood of the day

A yahrzeit is a day of remembrance, not sadness alone. Many people fast, while others share stories and gather family. It is a chance to keep the person's values alive.

## Common questions

### Can I light a yahrzeit candle on Shabbat?

No, a flame cannot be lit on Shabbat. If the yahrzeit falls on Shabbat, light the candle before Shabbat candles on Friday, using a candle that burns long enough. If it falls on a holiday, light from an existing flame. Ask the Rabbi if unsure.

### What if I forgot the yahrzeit?

Do not feel guilty. You can give tzedakah, say a prayer, or light a candle when you remember. Writing the upcoming dates in your calendar each year makes it easier to remember.

### Do I observe the yahrzeit by the English or Hebrew date?

Jewish tradition follows the Hebrew date. Our [yahrzeit calculator](/tools/yahrzeit) will show you the English date for each upcoming year.

### Who observes a yahrzeit?

Children observe it for parents. Spouses and siblings often observe it too. Anyone who wishes to honor a loved one is welcome to light a candle and do a mitzvah.
