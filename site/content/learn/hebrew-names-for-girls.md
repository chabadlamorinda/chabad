Hebrew names for girls come from the Torah's matriarchs and heroines, from nature, from virtues, and from Yiddish. Popular choices include Sarah (שרה), Rivkah (רבקה), Rachel (רחל), Devorah (דבורה), Shira (שירה) and Aviva (אביבה). A Jewish girl is traditionally named at the Torah reading shortly after her birth.

The Hebrew spellings and meanings below follow Chabad.org's list of popular Jewish girl names, retold in our own words. Meanings of ancient names are sometimes debated, so treat them as a guide.

## Matriarchs and women of the Bible

- **Sarah** (שרה): princess or ruler.
- **Rivkah** (רבקה), Rebecca: a woman who takes a heart.
- **Rachel** (רחל): ewe.
- **Devorah** (דבורה), Deborah: bee.
- **Chana** (חנה), Hannah: gracious.
- **Esther** (אסתר): the heroine of Purim.
- **Ruth** (רות): friendship.
- **Naomi** (נעמי): pleasantness.
- **Avigail** (אביגיל), Abigail: father of joy.
- **Tamar** (תמר): palm tree.
- **Yocheved** (יוכבד): honor of G-d.
- **Tzipporah** (ציפורה): bird.
- **Batya** (בתיה): daughter of G-d.
- **Yael** (יעל): doe.
- **Hadassah** (הדסה): myrtle.
- **Chava** (חוה), Eve: giver of life.
- **Miriam** (מרים): the prophetess, sister of Moses.

## Nature and beauty

- **Aviva** (אביבה): spring.
- **Shoshanah** (שושנה): lily or rose.
- **Talya** (טליה): dew of G-d.
- **Shalhevet** (שלהבת): flame.
- **Peninah** (פנינה): pearl.
- **Yaffah** (יפה): beautiful.
- **Tziviah** (ציביה): deer.
- **Shifra** (שפרה): beautiful.

## Virtues and joyful words

- **Bracha** (ברכה): blessing.
- **Simchah** (שמחה): joy.
- **Tikvah** (תקוה): hope.
- **Bina** (בינה): understanding.
- **Nechamah** (נחמה): comfort.
- **Chaya** (חיה): life.
- **Tehilah** (תהילה): praise.
- **Shira** (שירה): song.
- **Menuchah** (מנוחה): peace and rest.
- **Ahava** (אהבה): love.
- **Gila** (גילה): gladness.
- **Tovah** (טובה): good.
- **Chedva** (חדוה): delight.
- **Tiferet** (תפארת): glory and splendor.
- **Malka** (מלכה): queen.

## Yiddish classics

Many Jewish families brought Yiddish names from Europe, and they remain loved:

- **Bluma** (בלומא): flower.
- **Shterna** (שטערנא): star.
- **Golda** (גאלדא): gold.
- **Zissa** (זיסא): sweet one.
- **Zlata** (זלאטא): gold coin.
- **Freida** (פריידא): joy.
- **Zelda** (זעלדה): happiness.

## How to choose

Chabad.org says parents may name a child after a departed loved one, mark a special event, or choose any Jewish name they find beautiful. Many families give the Hebrew name together with a secular name. Customs differ between communities, for example about naming after relatives who are living or departed, so ask the Rabbi. More in [Hebrew names](/learn/hebrew-names) and [Jewish baby names](/learn/jewish-baby-names).

## How a girl is named

Traditionally a girl's name is given at the Torah reading shortly after her birth. Ask the Rabbi how it works and when to schedule it. Families often add a celebration, see [Simchat Bat](/learn/simchat-bat) and [baby naming](/learn/baby-naming). ## Common questions

### What are some Hebrew names for girls?
Sarah, Rivkah, Rachel, Devorah, Chana, Esther, Ruth, Shira and Aviva are popular, with many more listed above.

### What Hebrew girl names mean light or joy?
Simchah (שמחה) and Chedva (חדוה) mean joy and delight. Shalhevet (שלהבת) means flame.

### When is a Jewish girl given her name?
Traditionally at the Torah reading shortly after her birth, according to Chabad.org.

### Can we name a baby after a relative?
Yes. Parents often name after departed loved ones. Customs about living relatives differ, so ask the Rabbi.

### Does my daughter need a Hebrew name?
A Hebrew name is the hallmark of Jewish identity and is used in prayer and at life events. Ask the Rabbi about an adult or older girl who lacks one, see [getting a Hebrew name as an adult](/learn/getting-a-hebrew-name-as-an-adult).
