Tanya calls a heart that feels closed, hard or unmoved, even when you understand that G-d is great, timtum halev (טִמְטוּם הַלֵּב), a "dullness of the heart," and Chapter 29 teaches that the way back is humility and honest self-accounting, not forcing yourself to feel more. Even a sincere person who tries to do right can have such times.

If prayer feels empty or nothing moves you, you are not broken. Tanya describes exactly this experience and says what to do.

## What Tanya describes

According to Chabad.org's presentation of Chapter 29, a person who is trying to live rightly may find that his heart is, at times, as hard as stone. He cannot open it in prayer, which Tanya calls the "service of the heart." This dullness may also make it harder to hold back from permitted pleasures, such as eating whenever the urge comes.

The chapter gives the cause: the arrogance of the animal soul. It rises up and dims the light of the soul, so that even if he understands G-d's greatness with his mind, that understanding does not sink into his heart.

## The remedy is not "try harder to feel"

Tanya uses a picture from the Zohar: a wooden beam that will not catch fire has to be splintered. The point is that the way forward is not to make the light stronger but to break down what blocks it. Since the animal soul is the person's own "self," the chapter says to humble it, to weaken the ego that opposes holiness.

The practices it suggests include:

- Setting fixed times to humble yourself and see yourself as small.
- Taking an honest accounting ("a master of accounts") of your thoughts, words and deeds, asking whether each served holiness or not. See [cheshbon hanefesh](/learn/cheshbon-hanefesh).
- Inwardly facing the animal soul, with firm words, reminding it that G-d is unchanging.
- Reflecting on past sins, since deeper repentance is always possible.

These practices aim at the arrogance of the animal soul, the ego, not at your worth as a person. If you already feel worthless or very low, this is not the practice to start with; talk to the Rabbi, and to a doctor or counselor.

The chapter illustrates this with the spies in the Torah, whose faith returned only once their arrogance was broken, not through a new miracle.

## When there is no feeling

The response, says the chapter, is not despair but humility, and seeking not only to "hear" about G-d but, as it were, to "see": a deeper awareness of Him. For the theme of humility, see [Tanya on humility](/learn/tanya-on-humility). Even a person who feels free of serious sin should do this inner accounting.

The chapter also says that doubts about faith come from the animal soul and not from the Jewish soul, which believes. If you have questions about faith, see [doubts in faith](/learn/doubts-in-faith) and ask the Rabbi.

## Keep acting

Another comfort from Tanya: the level of beinoni does not depend on feeling, but on what you do (see [the beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni) and [why do I still struggle](/learn/why-do-i-still-struggle)). When the heart is closed, you can still pray, give charity and do mitzvot. For [prayer](/learn/prayer) and [kavanah](/learn/kavanah-in-prayer), many find it helpful to start simply.

## A word of care

Tanya's guidance is spiritual. If you feel numb to everything, not only to prayer, or this has lasted a long time, please talk to a doctor or counselor: emotional numbness can be a sign of depression or another condition, and professional help is also right when needed. Ask the Rabbi about a personal situation. See [mental health in Judaism](/learn/mental-health-jewish-view).

## Common questions

### What is timtum halev?
Timtum halev is Tanya's term for a "dullness of the heart": a heart that feels hard, closed or unmoved, even when the mind understands G-d's greatness. It is the subject of Chapter 29.

### Why does my prayer feel empty?
Tanya says a dull heart can come from the arrogance of the animal soul, which dims the soul's light so that understanding does not reach the heart.

### What does Tanya say to do when I feel nothing?
Humble yourself, take an honest accounting of your thoughts, words and deeds, and keep acting. Do not despair.

### Does feeling nothing mean I do not believe?
No. The chapter says doubts come from the animal soul, not from the Jewish soul, which believes. For a personal conversation, ask the Rabbi.

### When should I seek professional help?
If the numbness reaches beyond prayer, lasts a long time, or comes with hopelessness. Tanya's guidance is spiritual and professional help is also right when needed.
