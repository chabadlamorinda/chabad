When a wildfire threatens your home in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda, Jewish law is clear: saving life comes first, and you leave right away, on Shabbat and holidays too. Prepare calmly now, so that on the day you can act without hesitation.

This guide is general information. For fire and evacuation, always follow the instructions of the fire district, the county and the utility.

## Life comes first: pikuach nefesh

Pikuach nefesh (פִּקּוּחַ נֶפֶשׁ) means "saving a life." The Torah says of its commandments, "you shall live by them" (Leviticus 18:5), and the Sages teach that this means live by them, and not die by them. Chabad.org explains that when life is in danger, Shabbat is treated like a weekday for whatever is needed, and that "it is forbidden to hesitate." Acting quickly is praiseworthy; asking questions while danger grows is not.

In practice, if an evacuation order or a real threat comes on Shabbat or a holiday, you may and should drive, use your phone to call for help, switch on lights and gather your family. Do not wait to ask permission. You can speak to the Rabbi afterwards. Read more in [pikuach nefesh](/learn/pikuach-nefesh).

## Ready, Set, Go

Local fire agencies teach three steps, known as "Ready, Set, Go!" As described in Lamorinda Weekly, they mean: be ready by preparing ahead, be set by watching conditions, and go early when fire threatens. Leaving early keeps the roads clear for firefighters and keeps your family safe. The Lamorinda press has urged residents not to wait for a final "go order" before preparing to leave (Lamorinda Weekly).

1. **Sign up for alerts.** Contra Costa County's Community Warning System (CWSAlerts) sends emergency notices to your phone and email. Sign up at cwsalerts.com and look up your evacuation zone there.
2. **Talk it over at home.** Agree on two routes out and a place to meet.
3. **Keep a bag ready** (below), and keep your car's gas tank from running low in fire season.
4. **Listen to the authorities.** Follow your fire district, the county and the utility, not social media.

## A Jewish go-bag

Local guidance suggests medications and spare glasses, chargers, masks, toiletries, a battery radio and copies of important papers. Add what makes your home Jewish:

- Tefillin and a tallit, and a siddur (prayer book) or a prayer app
- Shabbat candles, matches or a lighter, and a small metal tray (a hotel may not allow open flame; ask)
- A kiddush cup and a small challah or matzah, if you have room
- A written list of Hebrew names, birthdays and yahrzeit dates, so these dates are never lost
- Your ketubah, conversion papers and other family documents (keep copies in the cloud as well)
- Medicines, including anything that needs a cold pack
- Small children's comfort items

If you own a Torah scroll or other holy items, ask the Rabbi in advance what to take. Items of sentimental value matter, but people always come first.

## A red-flag Shabbat

On a day of high fire danger, light Shabbat candles with extra care:

- Use a metal or glass tray on a stable, bare surface, away from curtains, paper and the edge of the table.
- Never leave flames unattended with small children or pets. Candles in the dining room, where you sit, are safest.
- Keep a bottle of water and a fire extinguisher where you can reach them, and know that you may use them if there is danger.
- If it is safer, use fewer candles, or ask the Rabbi about an alternative on that Friday. Find this week's time at [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times); Kabbalat Shabbat at the Chabad center is {kabbalat-shabbat} this week.

Chabad.org notes that saving life overrides Shabbat, so you may use a fire extinguisher or call the fire department (Mechabeh). Possessions are different: ask the Rabbi in advance what to do about them.

## Common questions

### Can I drive on Shabbat to escape a wildfire?

Yes. When there is a real danger to life, Jewish law requires you to leave, including by car, and not to hesitate. Call the Rabbi afterwards if you have questions.

### Can I use my phone to get emergency alerts on Shabbat?

If you carry a phone for safety in a time of danger, yes. In ordinary times, it is common to keep the phone off; ask the Rabbi if you rely on one for an alert during fire season.

### How do I sign up for evacuation alerts in Lamorinda?

Register at cwsalerts.com, Contra Costa County's Community Warning System, and check your evacuation zone there. Cities and the Moraga-Orinda Fire District point residents to this system.

### What Jewish items should I take if I have to evacuate?

Take people, pets and medicines first, then tefillin, a siddur, candles, a kiddush cup and a written list of family Hebrew names and yahrzeit dates. Ask the Rabbi about anything larger.

### Should I light Shabbat candles on a very dry, windy day?

Yes, with extra care: a stable tray, no curtains nearby, never unattended. If you are worried, ask the Rabbi.

### Can I put out a small fire on Shabbat?

Yes, if there is danger. Saving life and preventing a spreading fire allows you to use an extinguisher or call 911.
