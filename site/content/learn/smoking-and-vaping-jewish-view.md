Many Jewish authorities rule that smoking is forbidden by Jewish law, because the Torah commands us to guard our health and life. The Lubavitcher Rebbe strongly discouraged smoking, and Chabad.org states that cigarettes in their current form fall under the mitzvah to guard your health.

This guide explains the general view in simple words. It is not medical advice: please talk to your doctor about your health, and ask the Rabbi about your personal situation.

## The mitzvah to guard your health

The Torah says, "Guard yourselves and guard your souls very much" (Deuteronomy 4:9, 4:15). Maimonides and the classic codes of Jewish law understand this as a broad command to protect your life and health. Something that is known to be dangerous is to be avoided, not just discouraged.

Chabad.org notes that the harm of smoking builds up over time. No single cigarette is blamed, but the whole habit is. Medical authorities warn of lung cancer and other illnesses, and Jewish law takes a known health danger very seriously.

## What the Rebbe said

According to Chabad.org, the Rebbe strongly discouraged smoking. He explained that anything dangerous to health is already forbidden, so there is no need for a new, separate decree. He also hesitated to issue a permanent, formal ban, because cigarettes might one day be made safe, and a formal ruling of this kind would be fixed forever. He therefore did not say that smoking can never be changed, but he held that cigarettes as they exist now are forbidden by the command to guard your health.

Some respected rabbis in the past did not forbid smoking, mainly because it was so widespread and its dangers were not yet fully known. Chabad.org points out that many later authorities, once the evidence was clear, do rule that smoking is forbidden. Because there are differences of opinion about how to phrase the law, ask the Rabbi for a personal answer.

## What about vaping?

Chabad.org says that many rabbinic authorities strongly discourage vaping because of its health risks, and that discussing its kosher status is not an endorsement. Vaping is common among teenagers and young adults, and it is often presented as a safer alternative to smoking.

There is also a kosher question. Some authorities treat inhaling the vapor like eating or drinking, so kosher concerns arise. The liquid may contain glycerin, which can come from animal or plant sources, and flavorings may include alcohol from grain, which matters on Passover. So the ideal is not to vape at all. If someone does, many authorities require reliable kosher certification. Ask the Rabbi for guidance.

## Smoke around others

Chabad.org also discusses secondhand smoke. A person may ask a smoker to stop in a shared space such as a workplace or school, and the smoker should comply. Wanting clean air is not rude. It is your right.

## Quitting is a form of teshuvah

Addiction is real, and quitting is hard. The Chofetz Chaim, quoted on Chabad.org, taught that addiction does not remove a person's responsibility, but that does not make the person bad. Making a decision to change is a kind of [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah), return and growth, and Jewish tradition honors every sincere effort, including many tries.

Practical steps that many people find helpful:

1. Talk to your doctor about help to quit. There are programs and medicines.
2. Choose a date and tell someone who will support you.
3. Remember that observant Jews do not smoke on Shabbat, which shows that abstaining is possible. Many find Shabbat a good day to begin.
4. Do not be discouraged by a setback. Start again.

## Common questions

### Is smoking kosher?
Many Jewish authorities say smoking is forbidden because the Torah commands us to guard our health. Chabad.org says the Lubavitcher Rebbe strongly discouraged it and held that cigarettes in their current form are forbidden. Ask the Rabbi for your own situation.

### Did the Rebbe forbid smoking?
He strongly discouraged it and said that anything dangerous to health is already forbidden. According to Chabad.org, he did not issue a formal permanent ban, since cigarettes might someday be made safe.

### Is vaping allowed in Judaism?
Chabad.org says many rabbis strongly discourage vaping because of its health risks. It also raises kosher questions about the ingredients, especially on Passover. The ideal is to avoid it.

### Can I ask a coworker to stop smoking near me?
Yes. According to Chabad.org, a person can ask a smoker in a shared workplace or school to stop, and the smoker should comply. Ask politely.

### Does Judaism look down on someone who smokes?
No. Judaism treats every person with respect and values every effort to change. Quitting is a kind of teshuvah, and the Rabbi is happy to help without judgment.
