El Malei Rachamim (אֵל מָלֵא רַחֲמִים, "G-d, full of compassion") is the Jewish memorial prayer for a soul who has passed away. It is chanted by a rabbi or cantor at funerals, at unveilings and at the Yizkor services on Yom Kippur and the festivals, asking G-d to grant the soul peace.

## What the prayer says

The prayer is short and tender. Here is its meaning in our own words, not a full translation:

1. It calls to G-d as the One "full of compassion," who dwells on high.
2. It asks for the soul to be given proper rest under the wings of the Divine Presence, among the holy and pure souls who shine like the radiance of the sky.
3. It names the person, with his or her Hebrew name and the name of the parent, and says that the person has gone to his or her world.
4. It states that we give tzedakah (charity) in the person's memory, which is why the prayer joins remembering with doing good.
5. It asks that the soul rest in Gan Eden, the Garden of Eden, and be sheltered forever and "bound up in the bond of life."
6. It ends with "may he or she rest in peace," and we answer "Amen."

You may also hear it called Kel Maleh Rachamim, El Maleh Rachamim or El Male. They are the same prayer; the spelling comes from different ways of pronouncing Hebrew.

## When it is said

According to Chabad.org's article on Yizkor, the rabbi or cantor chants it publicly:

- **At a funeral** (see [the Jewish funeral](/learn/jewish-funeral)).
- **At an unveiling,** when the monument is dedicated (see [the unveiling](/learn/unveiling)).
- **At holiday Yizkor services,** which are said on Yom Kippur and the festivals (see [Yizkor](/learn/yizkor)).
- **After the Torah readings** on Monday and Thursday mornings, and on Shabbat afternoons, in many communities.

Details can differ by community and by day, so ask the Rabbi when and where it is said. It is not usually said on joyful days.

## What about the yahrzeit?

Many communities have El Malei Rachamim said at the Torah reading on the yahrzeit, or on the Shabbat before it. Chabad.org states that this is not the Chabad custom. Instead, Chabad families observe the [yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit) by lighting a memorial candle, saying [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish), studying Torah, giving tzedakah and doing a good deed in the person's merit. If your family has a different custom, honor it, and ask the Rabbi if you are unsure.

## Common questions

### What does El Malei Rachamim mean?
It means "G-d, full of compassion." It is the memorial prayer that asks G-d to grant peace to the soul of a person who has passed away.

### When is El Malei Rachamim said?
It is chanted at funerals, unveilings and holiday Yizkor services, and in many communities after Torah readings. Ask the Rabbi about specific occasions.

### Is El Malei Rachamim said on the yahrzeit?
In many communities yes, but Chabad.org states it is not the Chabad custom. Chabad families light a candle, say Kaddish, learn Torah and give tzedakah.

### Do I need the person's Hebrew name?
Yes, the prayer names the person and the parent. See [Hebrew names](/learn/hebrew-names), or ask the Rabbi if the name is unknown.

### Can I say it myself?
It is usually chanted by a rabbi or cantor. For a private moment of prayer, ask the Rabbi what is best for your situation.

### Is El Malei Rachamim the same as Kaddish?
No. [Kaddish](/learn/kaddish) is a praise of G-d said by mourners; El Malei Rachamim is a plea for the soul's peace.
