A bris is postponed when the baby is not fully healthy on the eighth day, for example when he has jaundice, was born early, or is unwell in any way. Jewish law is clear that the baby's health comes first, and the bris takes place only once he has recovered.

Many parents in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda hear "we need to wait" from the pediatrician and feel worried that something has gone wrong. Nothing has. Postponing is itself a mitzvah, and it is what the Torah asks of us.

## Why health comes first

The brit milah (בְּרִית מִילָה, "covenant of circumcision") is a great mitzvah, but Jewish law places the life and health of a child above it. Maimonides, the great medieval sage, put it simply: a bris can always be done later, but a Jewish soul cannot be brought back. Chabad.org explains that the Lubavitcher Rebbe applied this principle to jaundice, following the ruling of the Tzemach Tzedek, the third Chabad Rebbe.

The baby does not lose anything by waiting. The covenant is his from birth, and the bris is simply held on the day he is ready. See [Brit Milah: a simple guide](/learn/brit-milah) for what the ceremony means.

## When a bris is delayed

Chabad.org lists these as reasons to wait:

- **Jaundice**, the yellowing of the skin that is common in newborns.
- **Prematurity or weakness**, including a baby who is still in the hospital.
- **Any illness**, such as an eye infection.
- **Doubt.** If there is even a remote question whether the baby is strong enough, we wait until he is.

The doctors and a qualified mohel decide whether your baby is healthy enough. This guide is not medical advice, so please follow your pediatrician and your mohel. If you are not sure whether to postpone, call the Rabbi right away.

## Setting the new date

Once the baby has recovered, the bris is held without unnecessary delay. How soon exactly depends on the case. Chabad.org notes that in some cases the bris takes place right after recovery, and in others, such as when the skin color had to return to normal, the custom is to wait seven full days after recovery. Because sources differ, ask the mohel and the Rabbi what applies to your son.

Two more points:

1. **A postponed bris does not override Shabbat or a holiday.** A bris on the eighth day pushes aside Shabbat, but a delayed bris is held on a weekday.
2. **Twins** are each circumcised when each one is healthy. If one is ready and the other is not, they do not wait for each other.

## What to do in the meantime

- **Tell your mohel and the Rabbi** as soon as the doctor mentions a delay.
- **Let guests know.** Say that the date is not set yet and you will update them. People understand.
- **Keep your plans flexible.** Hold off on booking a hall or ordering food until the date is clear. See [preparing for a bris](/learn/preparing-for-a-bris) for the checklist.
- **Rest and recover.** After a birth, your first job is to care for the baby and for yourself. See [new parents in Lamorinda](/learn/new-parents-in-lamorinda).
- **Say a few Psalms** for the baby's health if that gives you comfort. Our [Tehillim page](/tehillim) has them.

## Common questions

### Can a bris be postponed for jaundice?
Yes. Jaundice is one of the most common reasons to postpone a bris. The bris waits until the baby has recovered, and the mohel and pediatrician guide the timing.

### Who decides whether to postpone a bris?
The baby's doctors and a qualified mohel assess his health, and the Rabbi guides the Jewish law. If there is any doubt about the baby's strength, the bris is delayed.

### How long after recovery is the bris held?
It depends on the case and on the sources. Some cases allow the bris right after recovery, others wait seven full days. Ask the mohel and Rabbi Labkowski, at 925-384-7242, about your son.

### Can a postponed bris be on Shabbat?
No. A bris on the eighth day may take place on Shabbat, but a delayed bris is held on a weekday, not on Shabbat or a holiday.

### If we postponed the bris, is my son still Jewish?
Yes. He is Jewish from birth if his mother is Jewish. The bris is a mitzvah that enters him into Abraham's covenant, and waiting for his health does not change who he is.

### What if my baby needs a longer hospital stay?
Then the bris waits until the doctors and the mohel agree he is well. Call the Rabbi, who will help set the date with you and the medical team.
