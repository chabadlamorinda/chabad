Besides the holidays every Jew knows, the Chabad year has its own special days: anniversaries of the Rebbes' births, releases from prison and passings. They are not new holidays in Jewish law. They are days to remember, to learn and to be inspired. Like every Jewish date, each begins the evening before.

## Through the year

**6 Tishrei.** The yahrzeit of **Rebbetzin Chana Schneerson**, the Rebbe's mother, who passed away in 1964. She had stood by her husband through his arrest and exile in the Soviet Union.

**10 Kislev.** The release of the second Chabad Rebbe, **Rabbi DovBer, the Mitteler Rebbe**, from imprisonment in 1826, after false charges.

**19 Kislev, [Yud Tes Kislev](/learn/yud-tes-kislev).** The release of the Alter Rebbe, founder of Chabad, from prison in 1798. It is called the "Rosh Hashanah of Chassidus."

**10 Shevat, Yud Shevat.** The yahrzeit of the sixth Rebbe, **Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn**, the Previous Rebbe, in 1950. One year later, on 10 Shevat 1951, **the Rebbe**, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson, formally accepted the leadership of Chabad.

**22 Shevat.** The yahrzeit of **Rebbetzin Chaya Mushka Schneerson**, the Rebbe's wife, in 1988. Many Jewish women and girls mark it with extra mitzvahs, especially [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).

**11 Nissan.** The birthday of [the Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe), born in 1902 in Nikolaev, Ukraine. Many begin saying the chapter of [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim) that matches the Rebbe's new year, as Chassidim do for their own birthdays.

**12 and 13 Tammuz.** The release of the Previous Rebbe in 1927. He had been arrested by the Soviet authorities for spreading Judaism and sentenced to exile. On 12 Tammuz, his birthday, he learned he was free; on 13 Tammuz he was formally released.

**3 Tammuz, Gimmel Tammuz.** The yahrzeit of the Rebbe in 1994. Many thousands visit the Rebbe's resting place, the Ohel, in Queens, New York, and people around the world write letters asking for a blessing.

**20 Av.** The yahrzeit of **Rabbi Levi Yitzchak Schneerson**, the Rebbe's father, a great Kabbalist who passed away in 1944 in exile in Kazakhstan, after being imprisoned for his work for Judaism.

**18 Elul, Chai Elul.** The birthday of the [Baal Shem Tov](/learn/baal-shem-tov), founder of the Chassidic movement, in 1698, and of the Alter Rebbe in 1745. "Chai" means "life," and the day brings new life into the month of [Elul](/learn/elul) before Rosh Hashanah.

## How these days are marked

- **Farbrengens.** Warm gatherings with stories, niggunim, words of Torah and l'chaim.
- **Learning.** Studying the teachings of the Rebbe whose day it is, or beginning a new study, like a daily portion of [Tanya](/learn/tanya).
- **Good deeds.** Extra [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) and an extra mitzvah, such as putting on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) with a friend or lighting Shabbat candles.
- **Good resolutions.** These days are a natural moment to take on something new.
- **On a yahrzeit,** many light a candle, give charity and learn in the person's memory.

The Rebbe taught that a yahrzeit of a tzaddik is not only a day of sadness. Their life's work goes on, and our good deeds are part of it.

## Common questions

### Are these days holidays in Jewish law?
No. They are not holidays like Passover or Sukkot. Some are joyous days on which Chabad communities omit Tachanun. They are times for learning, gathering and good deeds.

### Do I need to be Chabad to take part?
Not at all. A farbrengen is open to everyone, and every Jew is welcome.

### When do these dates fall this year?
Hebrew dates move on the regular calendar. See our [Jewish calendar](/learn/jewish-calendar) guide, or check the [events page](/events) for local gatherings.

### Can I send a letter to the Ohel?
Yes. Many people write a letter asking for a blessing, especially before Gimmel Tammuz. Ask the Rabbi how.
