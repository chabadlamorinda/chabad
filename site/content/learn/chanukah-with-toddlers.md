Chanukah with a toddler or baby works best when it is short, bright and safe: a few minutes at the menorah, a song, a little gelt and an early bedtime. Even a baby in your arms takes part just by watching the lights, and children who feel the warmth of the holiday carry it for life.

## Keep the lighting short and sweet

Little children cannot sit still for half an hour, and they do not need to. A simple rhythm works:

1. **Gather everyone** before you light. Let the toddler stand beside you, or hold the shamash, the helper candle, only with your hand over theirs.
2. **Say the blessings** together. Toddlers love to answer "Amen," and the sound of the words is itself a gift. See [teaching kids the Chanukah blessings](/learn/teaching-kids-the-chanukah-blessings).
3. **Sing a song or two** while the new light is kindled. A short song such as "Haneirot Halalu" or a favorite dreidel song is plenty. More ideas are in [Chanukah songs](/learn/chanukah-songs).
4. **Watch the flames** for a minute and talk: "Look at the light. Where do we put the new candle?"

The lights are meant to burn for about half an hour after nightfall, so adults stay nearby while the child moves on to playing. For the exact steps, see [how to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah).

## Safety comes first with small children

- Put the menorah on a sturdy, fireproof surface, well away from curtains and out of a child's reach, and keep the matches and lighter locked away. Chabad.org also advises keeping the menorah out of children's reach.
- Never leave a toddler alone in a room with burning lights.
- Keep sleeves, hair and dangling toys away from the flames.
- A battery or electric light can be a fun toy for a toddler to "light" while you light the real menorah, but it does not replace it. See [menorah fire safety](/learn/menorah-fire-safety).
- Chocolate coins, wrapped coins and small dreidels can be choking hazards for very young children. Ask your pediatrician what is right for your child's age.

## Chanukah play for little hands

Chabad.org's kids' pages suggest many things a toddler can enjoy with a grown-up:

- **Dreidel play.** A big, chunky [dreidel](/learn/dreidel) to spin and watch is enough; no need to learn the game yet.
- **Freeze dance.** Play Chanukah music, dance, and freeze when the music stops.
- **Cookie shapes.** Press cookie cutters in the shape of a menorah or dreidel into dough, or let little ones decorate a donut with sprinkles.
- **Videos and songs.** JewishKids.org (Chabad.org) has short animated videos, songs and stories about Chanukah. Choose a few minutes, not a whole afternoon.
- **A "gelt hunt."** Hide gold-wrapped gifts or chunky toys for a child old enough to search safely.
- **Simple crafts.** See [Chanukah crafts for kids](/learn/chanukah-crafts-for-kids), and pick the messy-but-safe ones.

## Bedtime, naps and sleep

Families with early bedtimes often wonder what to do. The lights are lit in the early evening, at the time shown on the [menorah page](/menorah), so many families plan a short early dinner and a simple bath routine around it. If your little one's bedtime is a real problem on certain nights, such as Friday or the later Saturday-night lighting, after Shabbat ends, ask the Rabbi about the best time for your family. On Friday, the Chanukah lights are lit before the Shabbat candles; see [Chanukah on Shabbat](/learn/chanukah-on-shabbat).

A calm routine, a lullaby and the glow of the lights make a lovely way to end the day. For more general tips on holidays with little ones, see [holidays with toddlers](/learn/holidays-with-toddlers).

## Common questions

### How do I celebrate Chanukah with a toddler?
Keep it short: light together, say Amen to the blessings, sing a song, watch the flames for a moment and add a simple activity like dreidel play or a donut. Adults stay nearby while the lights burn.

### Is it safe to have a menorah with a baby in the house?
Yes, with care. Use a stable, fireproof surface out of reach, keep matches locked up and never leave a child alone with the lights. Ask the Rabbi if you have a special situation.

### Can my toddler light the menorah?
A child can help by holding the shamash with an adult's hand over theirs, but an adult should handle the flame. Many families let older children light their own once they are ready.

### What if my child's bedtime is before the lights are lit?
Light at the proper time with the children who are awake, and tell the sleepy child about it in the morning. Ask the Rabbi what is best for your family.

### Can toddlers eat Chanukah gelt?
Be careful: coins and small treats can be choking hazards for very young children. Ask your pediatrician about what is safe at your child's age.
