A sefer Torah (סֵפֶר תּוֹרָה) is a handwritten parchment scroll containing the Five Books of Moses, written by a trained scribe in a script and with a precision set by Jewish law. It is the holiest object in a synagogue, kept in the ark and read aloud in public throughout the year.

## What a Torah scroll is

When Jews say "the Torah," they often mean this scroll, the one kept in the ark of the synagogue. According to Chabad.org, it contains the Five Books of Moses: Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers and Deuteronomy. The scroll is one form of Torah. Learn more about the Written and Oral Torah in [What Is the Torah?](/learn/torah).

## How a Torah scroll is written

Chabad.org describes the process this way:

- **By hand, on parchment.** A scribe, called a *sofer* (סוֹפֵר), writes with a feather quill and ink. The work takes many months.
- **In a special script.** The letters are written in *ktav Ashurit* (כְּתָב אַשּׁוּרִית), the traditional Hebrew script, and each letter must follow rules set by Jewish law.
- **Every letter counts.** A scroll contains 304,805 letters. One missing or damaged letter can make a scroll unfit for use until it is fixed.
- **A trained craftsman.** A sofer must study and train intensively before qualifying. Scribes of Torah scrolls, tefillin and mezuzahs have always held a place of honor in the community.

Chabad.org mentions great scribes through history: Moses, who according to tradition wrote 13 Torah scrolls on the day he died; Ezra, whose scroll was kept in the Temple; and Maimonides, who describes writing his own scroll in his *Mishneh Torah*. A printed Torah book is useful for study, but a scroll must be handwritten to be used for the public reading. For a single letter in the scroll, see [A Letter in a Torah](/learn/letter-in-a-torah).

## How it is read

The Torah is read aloud on Shabbat morning and afternoon, on Mondays and Thursdays, on Rosh Chodesh, on fast days and on major holidays. The reading is divided into weekly portions, called *parshiyot*, so that the whole Five Books are completed once a year. The reader chants it with a traditional melody indicated by cantillation marks, which also serve as punctuation. The melody differs among Ashkenazi, Sephardi and Yemenite communities. See [Torah Reading](/learn/torah-reading).

## How we show respect

- **The ark.** Between readings the scroll rests in the *aron kodesh*, the holy ark, which faces Jerusalem.
- **Standing.** Many people stand when the ark is open and as the scroll is carried through the room. Ask the Rabbi about the custom in your community.
- **Kissing.** As the scroll passes, it is customary to touch it with a siddur or the fringes of a tallit and then kiss that. People do not touch the parchment directly with their hands. Ask the Rabbi for details.
- **The pointer.** Readers use a pointer, a *yad* ("hand"), so that fingers never touch the text.
- **Honors.** People are called up to the reading, and the scroll is lifted and dressed; see [Synagogue Honors](/learn/synagogue-honors).

The Chabad.org material does not give a full list of care rules, so if you wonder how to handle a scroll, or what happens if one is damaged, please ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### What is a Torah scroll?
A Torah scroll is a handwritten parchment scroll of the Five Books of Moses, kept in the synagogue ark and read aloud in public.

### How is a Torah scroll written?
A trained scribe writes it by hand, with a quill and ink, in the traditional Hebrew script, over many months. Each of its 304,805 letters must follow Jewish law.

### Why do people kiss the Torah?
It is a way to show love and honor for the Torah. People usually touch the scroll with a siddur or tallit and kiss that, rather than touching the parchment.

### How often is the Torah read?
It is read on Shabbat morning and afternoon, Monday and Thursday, Rosh Chodesh, fast days and holidays, so that the whole Five Books are read each year.

### Can I touch a Torah scroll?
Please ask first. The scroll is handled only in specific ways, so ask the Rabbi what is customary.
