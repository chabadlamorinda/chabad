At a Bar or Bat Mitzvah, grandparents are the living link in the chain from one generation to the next. Their role is not fixed by law, but they are often honored with a place at the Torah, a blessing, a gift that carries meaning and a few words to the child. The most important thing a grandparent can give is simply being there, and telling the child that he or she is part of a story older than all of us.

## Why grandparents matter on this day

Judaism sees the Jewish people as a chain: parents teach children, who teach their children. A Bar or Bat Mitzvah is the moment a link is joined. A common suggestion for the Bar Mitzvah speech is that the child give special thanks to the grandparents, who likely made sacrifices to hold on to their Jewish identity and pass it on to their children and grandchildren. Many grandparents did exactly that, sometimes in difficult times and places. See [Jewish grandparents](/learn/jewish-grandparents).

## Honors at the service

At a Torah reading, honors such as an aliyah, opening the ark, lifting or dressing the Torah are given to family members. Customs and practice differ by synagogue and the day of the week, and who may be called depends on the situation, so please ask the Rabbi in advance. A few possibilities:

- **An aliyah or another honor** for a grandfather. Ask the Rabbi what is possible at your celebration. See [aliyah](/learn/aliyah).
- **A place of honor for a grandmother,** such as a blessing or a few words at the meal. Ask the Rabbi how best to honor her.
- **Standing beside the child** as he or she begins. A grandparent can also hold the child's hand at the Kiddush.

## Giving a blessing in your own words

A grandparent's blessing is a lasting gift, and it does not need a special text. Some ways to share it:

1. **A few short sentences** at the meal. "I am proud of you because ..., and I bless you that ..."
2. **A letter** that the child can keep for life. Share your hope, one memory and one thing you learned.
3. **A family story,** a tradition or a recipe that you want to pass on.
4. **A Hebrew blessing** you know, such as the one parents give their children on Friday night. See [blessing your children](/learn/blessing-your-children).

If you want to leave a deeper written message for the family, read about [the ethical will](/learn/ethical-will).

## A grandparent's speech

If the family invites you to speak, keep it to a minute or two.

- **Start with the child**, not with yourself.
- **Share one memory** and one wish.
- **Mention the family chain,** such as your own parents or grandparents.
- **End with a blessing** and a simple "Mazal tov."

## Meaningful gifts

Popular gifts include books of Jewish learning, ritual items and gifts of money, often in multiples of 18, the numerical value of *chai*, "life." Parents or grandparents often give a girl her first Shabbat candlestick when she starts to light. For a boy, a pair of tefillin is the classic gift; talk to the parents before buying, so there are not two pairs, and read [buying tefillin](/learn/buying-tefillin). See also [gifts in multiples of 18](/learn/gifts-in-multiples-of-18) and [girls lighting Shabbat candles](/learn/girls-lighting-shabbat-candles).

## If you live far away

Many grandparents travel to Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda for the celebration, and some join by video if it is not on Shabbat or a festival, when screens are not used. For a Shabbat celebration, plan to arrive before Shabbat starts and ask the Rabbi about practical details, such as walking distance and meals. ## Common questions

### What is the grandparents' role at a Bar Mitzvah?
There is no fixed role in Jewish law, but grandparents are often honored at the Torah, give a blessing or letter and are thanked by the child. Ask the Rabbi what fits your celebration.

### Can a grandfather get an aliyah at the Bar Mitzvah?
Often yes, depending on the day and the service. Ask the Rabbi early so that the honors are planned.

### What should a grandparent say in a speech?
Speak about the child first, with one memory, one wish and a blessing. A minute or two is plenty.

### What is a good gift from a grandparent?
A book of Jewish learning, candlesticks for a girl, tefillin for a boy after talking to the parents, or money in multiples of 18.

### Can grandparents join if they are far away?
They are welcome in person. If the celebration is not on Shabbat or a festival, joining by video may be possible. Ask the family and the Rabbi.

### Can grandparents help with the Bar Mitzvah preparation?
Yes. Learning with a grandchild, telling the family story and helping with the mitzvah project are all precious. See [Jewish grandparents](/learn/jewish-grandparents).
