The Lubavitcher Rebbe, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson (1902 to 1994), was the seventh leader of the Chabad-Lubavitch movement and one of the most influential Jewish leaders of modern times. From his headquarters in Brooklyn, New York, he inspired a worldwide network of emissaries and a renewal of Jewish life across the globe.

## His early life

The Rebbe was born in 1902 in Nikolaev, in the Russian Empire, to Rabbi Levi Yitzchak Schneerson, a great Kabbalist and communal rabbi, and Rebbetzin Chana. Even as a child he was known for brilliant Torah knowledge.

In 1928 he married Rebbetzin Chaya Mushka, the daughter of the sixth Chabad Rebbe, Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn. The couple lived in Berlin and Paris, where the Rebbe also studied at university. In 1941 they escaped Nazi-occupied Europe and arrived in New York.

## Becoming the Rebbe

After the passing of his father-in-law in 1950, Rabbi Menachem Mendel formally accepted leadership of Chabad in 1951. In the shadow of the Holocaust, when many felt Jewish life could not recover, he set out to rebuild it everywhere.

## His approach

**Every Jew matters.** The Rebbe taught that every Jew, regardless of background or level of observance, has a holy soul and deserves love. He never wrote anyone off.

**Shluchim (emissaries).** He sent young couples to live in communities around the world, to serve local Jews with warmth and without judgment. Today there are thousands of Chabad centers in more than 100 countries.

**One mitzvah at a time.** The Rebbe launched "mitzvah campaigns" encouraging Jews to do one more good deed: put on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin), light Shabbat candles, affix a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah), give [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah), study Torah, keep kosher and more.

**Caring for the whole world.** He championed the [Noahide laws](/learn/noahide-laws) as a moral foundation for all humanity and advocated for education rooted in values. Education Day USA is marked each year on his birthday.

**Moshiach.** He taught that our generation can bring the era of [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach), a world of peace and G-dliness, through acts of goodness and kindness.

## His teachings and correspondence

The Rebbe delivered thousands of talks, often at Chassidic gatherings called farbrengens lasting many hours. He wrote tens of thousands of letters answering questions on everything from faith to health to business. His teachings fill hundreds of volumes.

For decades, he stood for hours on Sundays greeting thousands of people individually and giving each a dollar to give to charity, a practice known as "Sunday dollars."

## His passing

The Rebbe passed away on the 3rd of Tammuz, 5754 (June 12, 1994). The day, known as Gimmel Tammuz, is marked each year with study, prayer and good deeds. Many people visit his resting place in Queens, New York, called the Ohel, to pray.

## Common questions

### Why is he called "the Rebbe"?

Rebbe means "my teacher." In Chassidic communities it refers to a spiritual leader. Many people, Chabad and otherwise, simply say "the Rebbe" when referring to Rabbi Schneerson.

### Did the Rebbe have children?

The Rebbe and Rebbetzin had no children. The Rebbe often said that every Jew was like his child.

### Is there a new Rebbe?

No successor was appointed. Chabad continues to be guided by the Rebbe's teachings and directives.
