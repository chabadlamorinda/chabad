The best Jewish lullabies are simple: the Shema at bedtime, Modeh Ani at waking, a wordless niggun (melody) hummed while rocking, and a Yiddish lullaby about Torah. The Lubavitcher Rebbe spoke of the beautiful custom of Jewish mothers to rock their children to sleep with a Jewish lullaby whose message is that Torah is the most precious treasure.

For parents in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, this is the easiest Jewish practice to start on night one. You do not need a good voice, and a baby does not care.

## Why sing Jewish songs to a baby

Early childhood memories shape a person's character, consciously and subconsciously, and so the songs a mother sings at night have a lasting influence. The Rebbe explained, at a gathering in 1973, that the traditional lullaby carries a message that Torah is the most precious treasure. A baby does not understand the words, but he learns warmth, safety and belonging, and the sound of Hebrew and Yiddish becomes familiar.

## Shema at bedtime

The [Shema](/learn/shema) (שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל, "Hear, O Israel") is the first verse many Jewish children learn, and traditionally it is the last words said before sleep. As soon as a child starts to speak, he is taught key Torah verses, including the Shema. With a baby, you can simply say or sing the verse softly as you lay him down. See [the bedtime Shema](/learn/bedtime-shema) for a short routine.

## Modeh Ani in the morning

[Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani) (מוֹדֶה אֲנִי) is the short prayer of gratitude said on waking, "I thank You, living and eternal King, for returning my soul to me with compassion." Parents can say it for the baby, in a soft voice, when they pick him up in the morning. It is only a few words, and it is a beautiful way to start the day.

## Niggunim: songs without words

A [niggun](/learn/niggun) (נִגּוּן) is a wordless melody, a cherished part of Chassidic life. Humming a niggun while rocking, walking or feeding is easy and calming. You can use any melody you know or one you learn at Chabad. Ask the Rabbi for a favorite.

## A Yiddish lullaby about Torah

"Oyfn Pripetchik" is a Yiddish song that pictures a warm home with a rabbi teaching little children the aleph-bet. Its message is to review what you learn, not to fear it, and that Torah is a source of good. Jews sang it to their children for generations, even in the Soviet Union, when doing so could be dangerous. You can find recordings online, and families who speak Russian or Hebrew at home often have lullabies of their own to add.

## Common questions

### What are Jewish lullabies?
They are songs, usually in Hebrew or Yiddish, that parents sing to babies, such as the Shema, Modeh Ani, wordless niggunim and Yiddish songs about Torah.

### Should I sing the Shema to my baby?
Yes, if you would like to. It is a beautiful custom to say the Shema before sleep. With a baby, a soft, short recitation is enough.

### What is a niggun?
A niggun is a wordless Jewish melody, especially in Chassidic tradition. Humming one is a simple way to soothe a baby.

### What did the Rebbe say about lullabies?
The Rebbe said that Jewish mothers have the custom of rocking their children to sleep with a Jewish lullaby, whose message is that Torah is most precious.

### Do I need to sing in Hebrew or Yiddish?
No. Any language is fine, but a few Hebrew words, like the Shema, make it Jewish. Sing what comes naturally.
