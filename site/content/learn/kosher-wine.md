Kosher wine is wine made from start to finish under Jewish supervision, using only kosher ingredients, and handled by Shabbat-observant Jews from the time the grapes are crushed. Because wine has such an important role in Jewish life, it has special rules beyond those for other foods.

## Why wine is special

Wine is used for many mitzvot: [Kiddush](/learn/kiddush) on Shabbat and holidays, [Havdalah](/learn/havdalah), the four cups at the Passover Seder, and the blessings at a wedding and a bris. It has its own blessing, borei pri hagafen, "who creates the fruit of the vine."

In ancient times wine was also used in idol worship. To keep Jews far from this and to protect Jewish community life, the Sages ruled that wine and grape juice touched or handled by non-Jews, or by Jews who do not keep Shabbat, may not be drunk. This is why kosher wine needs extra care.

## What makes wine kosher

- **Kosher ingredients.** Yeasts, fining agents and other additives must be kosher. Some non-kosher wines are clarified with animal products.
- **Kosher equipment.** All winery equipment is kosher or properly koshered.
- **Shabbat-observant handling.** From crushing until the bottle is sealed, the wine is handled only by Shabbat-observant Jews.
- **Israel-specific laws.** Wine from Israel also needs the agricultural laws of the Land, such as separating tithes, to be observed.

## What mevushal means

Mevushal means "cooked." Mevushal wine has been heated (today usually by flash pasteurization) before bottling. According to Jewish law, cooked wine is no longer subject to the rule about being handled by others. This makes mevushal wine practical for restaurants, caterers and events where non-Jewish waiters pour the wine.

Non-mevushal wine is just as kosher. The difference only matters for who may open and pour it after the bottle is opened. Look for the word "mevushal" on the label.

## Other grape products and drinks

- **Grape juice** follows the same rules as wine and needs certification.
- **Products with grape ingredients**, such as some jams, vinegars, sodas and candies, need reliable certification.
- **Beer and most spirits** have fewer issues, but flavored drinks, liqueurs and whiskies aged in wine casks can be a problem. Look for a [kosher symbol](/learn/kosher-symbols) or ask.

## Choosing kosher wine

Kosher wine today ranges from simple sweet Kiddush wine to award-winning dry wines from Israel, California, France and beyond. Check that the label has a reliable certification. For Kiddush, any kosher wine or grape juice works.

## Common questions

### Is all Israeli wine kosher?
No. Many Israeli wineries are kosher, but not all. Always check for a reliable certification on the label.

### Can a non-Jewish guest pour kosher wine at my table?
If the wine is mevushal, yes. If it is not mevushal, it is better that a Shabbat-observant Jew opens and pours it. Ask the Rabbi about your situation.

### Can I use grape juice for Kiddush?
Yes. Kosher grape juice may be used for Kiddush and the blessing is the same, borei pri hagafen. Many families use it for children or anyone who does not drink alcohol.

### Does mevushal wine taste different?
Modern flash pasteurization is very quick, and many people cannot tell the difference. Wine lovers may notice a slight change in some styles.
