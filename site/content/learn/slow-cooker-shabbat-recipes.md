A slow cooker is one of the easiest ways to have a hot Shabbat lunch: you prepare the food before Shabbat, turn the cooker on before candle lighting, and leave it alone until the next day. Cholent is the classic dish, but chicken, brisket and vegetarian stews work just as well, and the rules for using a crockpot depend on your model, so ask the Rabbi.

For families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, a slow cooker also means a calm Friday: the lunch is already cooking while you set the table.

## Why a slow cooker works on Shabbat

Cooking is one of the [39 categories of work](/learn/thirty-nine-melachot) we rest from on Shabbat. But Jewish homes have long enjoyed a hot meal on Shabbat day by starting the food before Shabbat and leaving it on a heat source that is not touched. Slow-cooking Shabbat lunch overnight in a crockpot or on a covered flame is the traditional way to make cholent. See [cholent](/learn/cholent) for the history.

## The simple rules to ask the Rabbi about

The details depend on the kind of cooker and on custom, which is why we do not give a one-size answer here. In general:

- **Turn it on before Shabbat** and do not turn it off, change the setting or add raw food afterwards.
- **Have the food ready early.** Cook it well before candle lighting rather than at the last minute.
- **Some cookers raise extra questions**, for example models with variable temperature, or with an insert that lifts out. Opinions among rabbis differ, so ask the Rabbi about yours.
- **Taking food out and putting it back** has its own rules. Serve what you need at one time and ask the Rabbi before returning a pot.
- **Stirring or adding liquid** also has rules, so avoid them unless the Rabbi has told you what to do.

See [how to keep food warm on Shabbat](/learn/keeping-food-warm-on-shabbat) and [Shabbat practical questions](/learn/shabbat-practical-questions). Check [this week's candle-lighting time](/shabbat-times) so everything is on in time.

## Five easy slow cooker ideas for Shabbat lunch

These are simple ideas, not fixed recipes. Adjust to your family's taste and keep everything kosher.

1. **Classic cholent.** Onions, meat, beans, barley, potatoes and paprika. Make it the way your family likes.
2. **Chicken and potatoes.** Chicken pieces with potatoes, onions and carrots, seasoned simply.
3. **Brisket or pot roast.** Browned first, then layered with onions and vegetables with a little liquid.
4. **Vegetarian bean stew.** Beans, root vegetables and tomatoes with seasoning, a good choice for guests who do not eat meat.
5. **Chickpea hamin.** Chickpeas, rice and warm spices, inspired by the Sephardi Shabbat stews. See the Sephardi section in [cholent](/learn/cholent).

If your Shabbat meal includes meat, keep dairy out of the pot and out of the meal. Eggs in their shells can be tucked in for a brown, creamy result, a traditional touch.

## A simple Friday plan

1. **Thursday:** soak beans, chop vegetables.
2. **Friday morning:** brown the onions or meat, layer the cooker and cook on high so it is done well before Shabbat.
3. **Before candle lighting:** switch it to the setting you want for the night. Then leave it alone.
4. **Shabbat day:** after services, serve. Ask the Rabbi if you need to take out food and put it back.

## Common questions

### Can you use a slow cooker on Shabbat?
Yes, if it was turned on before Shabbat and you do not adjust it afterwards. Because rabbis differ on certain models, ask the Rabbi about yours.

### What is the best slow cooker recipe for Shabbat?
Cholent is the classic, with meat, beans, barley and potatoes. Chicken with potatoes, brisket and vegetarian bean stews are also popular.

### Can I add water to my cholent on Shabbat?
Not unless the Rabbi tells you how, because adding liquid has rules. Add enough water before Shabbat so it does not dry out.

### Can I put the slow cooker on a timer?
Many families use timers for various appliances, but a timer on a cooker raises questions about when the food is cooked. Ask the Rabbi before relying on one.

### Do I need a blech if I have a slow cooker?
No. A slow cooker is its own heat source. A blech is another way to keep food warm; see [keeping food warm](/learn/keeping-food-warm-on-shabbat).
