Tzniut means modesty, and in Judaism it is a way of living with dignity and inner focus that applies to both men and women. It touches how we dress, speak and act, reflecting the idea that what is most precious is often kept private.

## What tzniut really means

The prophet Micah says, "Walk humbly (hatznea lechet) with your G-d" (Micah 6:8). From that word comes tzniut. It is not about hiding or feeling ashamed. It is about recognizing that every person has a G-dly soul, and that our bodies, words and actions deserve to be treated with respect.

Modesty is often described as the difference between being on display and being present. A modest person does not need to draw attention to be confident, because their value comes from within.

## Modesty for everyone

Tzniut applies to all of us:

- **Speech.** Speaking respectfully, avoiding crude language, gossip and boasting. See our guide to [lashon hara](/learn/lashon-hara).
- **Behavior.** Not showing off wealth or achievements, giving charity quietly, and keeping private matters private.
- **Privacy in marriage.** Affection between husband and wife is precious and personal.
- **Humility.** The Torah praises Moses as the humblest of all men. Humility is the inner side of modesty.

## Modest dress

Jewish law includes guidelines for dress for both men and women. Men traditionally wear a [kippah](/learn/kippah) and dress with dignity, especially in synagogue. For women, the general guidelines followed in traditional communities include:

- Necklines that cover the collarbone area.
- Sleeves that cover the elbows.
- Skirts or dresses that cover the knees, including when sitting.
- Married women covering their hair, with a scarf, hat or wig.

These details are discussed in Jewish law, and communities and families differ in how they practice. For specific questions, ask the Rabbi or Rebbetzin.

## Why it matters

- **Dignity.** Chassidic teaching compares the Jewish woman to a princess: "All the glory of the king's daughter is within" (Psalms 45:14). Modesty honors that inner royalty.
- **Focus on the person.** Modest dress invites others to relate to who you are rather than how you look.
- **Holiness at home.** Tzniut helps create a home where respect and closeness grow; see [shalom bayit](/learn/shalom-bayit).

## Starting step by step

You do not need to change everything overnight. Many people begin with one area:

1. **Choose one modest outfit** for Shabbat or synagogue.
2. **Watch your words** for one week: no gossip, no put downs.
3. **Give one gift of tzedakah** anonymously.
4. **Talk to your children** about privacy and self-respect in an age of social media.

Every step counts, and no one will judge you for where you are.

## Common questions

### Is tzniut only for women?
No. Modesty in speech, behavior and dress applies to men and women alike. Women's dress guidelines are discussed more often, but humility and dignity are for everyone.

### Do I have to dress modestly to come to Chabad?
No. Everyone is welcome at Chabad of Lamorinda just as they are. Many people simply like to know the customs, and we are happy to share them.

### Why do married Jewish women cover their hair?
Jewish law teaches that a married woman's hair is private, shared with her husband. Covering it with a scarf, hat or wig is a mitzvah many women find meaningful. Ask the Rebbetzin if you would like to learn more.

### Does modesty mean I cannot dress nicely?
Not at all. Jewish tradition encourages dressing beautifully, especially for Shabbat and holidays. Modest clothing can be stylish and elegant.
