Chanukah for teens means taking the holiday from "something my parents do" to "something I do": lighting your own menorah, understanding what the lights mean and sharing them with friends. It is a holiday about standing up for who you are, which is exactly what being a teenager is about.

## What the lights are really about

The Greek rulers of the story did not try to wipe the Jews out. According to Chabad teaching, they wanted to cut the Jewish people off from G-d. They did not mind Jews doing some things as a culture or philosophy, as long as it was not rooted in a commandment from G-d. That is why they defiled the oil, and why the miracle of one sealed jar matters: it shows that a part of every Jewish soul can never be cut off.

Chassidic teaching holds that Chanukah has eight lights because eight points beyond nature. When you connect to that deepest part of yourself, you do more than push the darkness away; the darkness itself becomes light. In plain words: you do not have to be perfect. Your Jewish spark is still there.

## Make the menorah yours

1. **Light your own menorah.** Many families let everyone light. Ask your parents or the Rabbi; for how it is done see [how to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah). Learn the blessings so you can say them yourself.
2. **Learn the story, in your own words.** Read the [Chanukah story](/learn/chanukah-story) and decide which part speaks to you.
3. **Use a real flame, safely.** The mitzvah is done with oil or wax flames, not electric lights. See [menorah fire safety](/learn/menorah-fire-safety).
4. **Face it outward.** Chanukah lights are placed by the window or door, to be seen by people passing by. Jewish identity is not meant to be hidden.

## Bring it to your friends

- Invite friends over to light and play dreidel (see [dreidel](/learn/dreidel)), with latkes and donuts.
- Share the story in one minute: "A small group stood up for who they were, and a little oil lasted eight days."
- Wear or give something small: a card, a dreidel or gelt.

The teaching about the shamash, the helper candle, is a good model. The shamash lights the other candles and loses nothing. Sharing your light with someone else does not make yours smaller.

## Show some Maccabee courage today

The Maccabees were not a huge army. They were people who decided they could not stay quiet. Being a Jewish teen today can take its own courage: asking for a day off for a holiday, saying no to something you do not agree with, speaking up kindly when something unfair is said, or lighting your menorah in a window.

If something hurtful happens at school, you are not alone. Talk with a parent, a teacher or the Rabbi. See [Jewish identity for teens](/learn/jewish-identity-for-teens) and [Jewish teens in Lamorinda](/learn/jewish-teens-in-lamorinda).

## Common questions

### How can teens celebrate Chanukah?
Light your own menorah, learn the blessings, invite friends over, do a mitzvah project and share the story. Make at least one part of the holiday your own.

### Can a teen light the menorah?
Yes. Teens, like all Jews, take part in the mitzvah. Many families let every member light their own menorah; ask the Rabbi about your family's custom.

### Can I use an electric menorah in my dorm or room?
An electric menorah is for display; the mitzvah is done with a real flame, which many dorms do not allow. Ask the Rabbi what to do. See [Chanukah at work and college](/learn/chanukah-at-work-and-college).

### Why is Chanukah relevant for teenagers?
It is a story about a small group of people deciding to stay true to their identity under pressure. Chassidic thought teaches that the light can turn darkness itself into light.

### What gifts do teens get on Chanukah?
Gelt, small gifts and time with family are traditional; many families give part of the gelt to tzedakah. See [Chanukah gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt).
