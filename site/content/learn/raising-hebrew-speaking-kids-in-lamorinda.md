Israeli families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda keep their children speaking Hebrew by making it the language of the home, and by giving Hebrew a living place: songs, stories, Shabbat and the holidays. Children who hear Hebrew at the Shabbat table and in Torah learning grow up with more than a language. They keep a connection to their people.

## Make Hebrew the language of home

Children living in America hear English all day at school, and Hebrew can slip away quietly. These habits help.

- **One language at home.** Many families choose Hebrew for the house and English for school and friends. Keep it consistent, even when the child answers in English.
- **Songs and books.** Children's songs, bedtime stories and the Hebrew books you loved as a child. Even a few minutes a day add up.
- **Video calls with grandparents.** Saba and Savta are the best Hebrew teachers. Make a regular time.
- **Hebrew friends.** Playdates with other Israeli families help children see that Hebrew is a living language and that it is fun.
- **Be patient.** A child who answers in English is still learning. Praise every Hebrew word.

## Which Hebrew? Modern and the Holy Tongue

Chabad.org explains that Jewish tradition calls the Hebrew of the Torah and prayers Lashon HaKodesh (לְשׁוֹן הַקֹּדֶשׁ), the Holy Tongue, and that modern Hebrew is usually called Ivrit. Both matter. Everyday Hebrew helps a child talk with family, and the Hebrew of the siddur and the Torah opens the door to Jewish life. Chabad.org also notes that the sages advise teaching children to understand Hebrew, not only to read it. Read more in [Lashon HaKodesh](/learn/lashon-hakodesh) and [Hebrew Phrases](/learn/hebrew-phrases).

## More than language: the Jewish year in a new place

In Israel, the holidays are in the street, in school and on television. In Lamorinda, your home must be the place where the holidays live. Here are some ways to bring Israel's Jewish rhythm to your kitchen.

- **Friday night.** A Shabbat dinner with candles, kiddush and challah, and a blessing for each child. See [Shabbat for Beginners](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners).
- **Tell the stories in Hebrew.** On Chanukah, Purim and Passover, tell the story in Hebrew first. Each holiday has a guide on our site.
- **Teach the child the morning words.** [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani) is a short prayer in Hebrew that children can say when they wake.
- **Torah in Hebrew.** Children see that Torah is not only for school when parents learn too.

## Common questions

### How can I keep my children speaking Hebrew in California?
Speak Hebrew at home, read and sing in Hebrew every day, and arrange regular video calls with grandparents in Israel. Consistency matters more than perfect grammar.

### Is it normal for my child to answer in English?
Yes. Children surrounded by English at school often answer in it. Keep speaking Hebrew to them, and they will understand and gradually speak more.

### Should my child learn Hebrew reading and prayer too?
Yes, the Hebrew of the siddur opens Jewish life. Chabad.org notes that understanding the words is as important as reading them, so teach the meaning together.

### What is the difference between Ivrit and Lashon HaKodesh?
Ivrit is modern spoken Hebrew, and Lashon HaKodesh, "the Holy Tongue," is the classical Hebrew of the Torah and prayers. They are closely related and a child can learn both.
