The Seudat Havraah (סעודת הבראה) is the "meal of condolence" or "meal of recovery," the first meal mourners eat after returning from the funeral. Others prepare it for them, and it traditionally includes round bagels or rolls and peeled hard-boiled eggs.

It is a simple, quiet meal, and one of the first acts of kindness a community offers to a family in mourning.

## What the Seudat Havraah is

Mourners do not eat their own food at this first meal. Relatives, close friends or neighbors provide it, which lets others show they care. If nobody can provide it, the mourners may eat their own food.

According to Chabad.org, this meal is private, for the immediate family, and not a public gathering. The practice is rooted in the prophet Ezekiel (24:17), which the Talmud reads to mean that a mourner eats food that others have brought.

It follows the funeral and the return from the cemetery, when mourners begin [shiva](/learn/shiva). See [what to expect at a funeral](/learn/what-to-wear-to-a-jewish-funeral).

## What is served

- **Bread, preferably round rolls such as bagels.**
- **Peeled hard-boiled eggs.** Some also add lentils.
- After the bread and egg, other foods may be eaten, and wine in moderation.
- On Chol Hamoed (the intermediate days) of Sukkot and Passover, cake or matzah and coffee or juice replace the bread and egg.

Chabad.org notes that the food is listed as "and/or," so other round rolls may replace bagels. The egg is the usual food; lentils are sometimes added.

## Why round foods and eggs?

The custom goes back to Jacob, who cooked lentils after Abraham died; the sages say he did it to console his father Isaac. The Talmud gives two reasons for lentils:

1. A lentil has no "mouth" (no crack), like a mourner whose grief leaves them unable to speak.
2. A lentil is round, like mourning, which comes around to everyone in the cycle of life.

The egg looks like a finished object but is really the start of a new life. It stands for faith and hope in the resurrection, in the time of [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach). Eggs are peeled so that mourners need not struggle with the shell and look as if they are ravenous.

Chabad.org gives other reasons too. The meal consoles mourners by showing they are not alone. It also encourages them to eat, since grief often takes away appetite.

## Timing and a few details

- If the burial was during the day, the meal is served until nightfall. If it was after nightfall, mourners are not required to eat it.
- On a Friday or the eve of a holiday, the meal is not eaten after the ninth halachic hour, so the mourners keep their appetite for the Shabbat or holiday meal.
- In serving, some communities have non-mourners put the bread and egg into the mourners' hands. Customs differ, so ask the Rabbi.
- Mourners cannot join a group of ten for Grace After Meals, but may join a group of three.

## Food during the rest of shiva

After the first meal, mourners may prepare their own food, but many communities continue to bring them meals because the family has other worries. Most Ashkenazic communities, including Chabad, do not put out food for visitors, and Chabad toasts "l'chaim" on the seventh day, right after shiva ends. For ideas on what to bring, see [what to bring to a shiva house](/learn/what-to-bring-to-a-shiva-house) and [setting up a shiva house](/learn/setting-up-a-shiva-house).

## Common questions

### What is the Seudat Havraah?
It is the first meal of mourning, eaten after the funeral, consisting of foods like bagels and hard-boiled eggs provided by others. The name means "meal of recovery" or "condolence."

### Why do Jews eat eggs after a funeral?
Eggs are round, standing for the cycle of life, and they stand for hope in the resurrection, since an egg looks finished but holds the beginning of new life.

### Who provides the meal of condolence?
Relatives, friends or neighbors. Mourners do not eat their own food at this meal unless no one can provide it.

### Can the mourners prepare the meal themselves?
Only if no one else can bring it. After this first meal, they may prepare their own food during shiva.

### Is the Seudat Havraah a public gathering?
No. Chabad.org describes it as private, for the immediate family, not a public condolence event.

### Does the meal apply if the funeral is on a Friday?
It is not eaten after the ninth halachic hour of the day, so the mourners can keep their appetite for Shabbat. Ask the Rabbi in a specific case.
