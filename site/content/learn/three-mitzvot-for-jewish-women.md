The three special mitzvot of Jewish women are lighting Shabbat and holiday candles (*hadlakat neirot*, הדלקת נרות), taking challah (*hafrashat challah*, הפרשת חלה) and the laws of family purity (*taharat hamishpachah*, טהרת המשפחה). Chabad teaching calls them the foundation of Jewish living, and the Lubavitcher Rebbe pointed out that their first letters spell the name Chana.

## Why women, and why "Chana"?

Men and women are both obligated in these mitzvot, but Chassidic teaching explains that the woman is given precedence and carries the authority in making sure they are done. She sets the tone of the home, and these mitzvot are about the home: light, food and family.

The Rebbe's mother was Rebbetzin Chana Schneerson. After she passed away in 1964, the Rebbe noted that three of the mitzvah campaigns he encouraged matched the three letters of her name in Hebrew: **Chet (ח)** for challah, **Nun (נ)** for *niddah*, the family purity laws, and **Hei (ה)** for *hadlakat neirot*, candle lighting. He used this to encourage women and girls to strengthen their commitment to all three.

## 1. Shabbat and holiday candles

A light points to its source, and lighting candles before Shabbat reminds us of G-d, who gave us this day. Once the candles are lit, the home feels different: people dress up, sit down to a festive meal and pray.

- Light at least 18 minutes before sunset. Find this week's time at [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times).
- A girl lights one candle. After marriage, a woman lights at least two.
- Put a few coins in a charity box first.
- Cover your eyes while saying the blessing. The moment right after, with your eyes still covered, is an especially good time for private prayer.

Girls can begin lighting around age three, when they can say the blessing. See [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) and [girls lighting Shabbat candles](/learn/girls-lighting-shabbat-candles).

## 2. Challah

Before baking bread, a small piece of dough is separated and burned, as the Torah says: give "the first of your kneading" (Numbers 15). A piece of about an ounce is separated and said to be challah. Chabad teaching explains that it expresses that everything we have comes from G-d, and that serving Him comes first.

The blessing is said only when the dough is large enough (the a common measure is 59 ounces of flour; for a smaller amount, ask the Rabbi or Rebbetzin). Read the whole procedure in [taking challah](/learn/hafrashat-challah), and see the practical steps in [baking challah](/learn/baking-challah). Chabad teaching also connects this mitzvah to keeping kosher: holiness reaches the kitchen.

## 3. Family purity

The laws of family purity guide a husband and wife through each month: a time of separation and a time when the wife immerses in the mikvah, after which the couple reunites. Chassidic teaching calls them the touchstone of Jewish life and the portal to a Jewish future. 

It is a private subject, best learned one-on-one. Start with [family purity](/learn/family-purity) and [the mikvah near Lafayette](/learn/mikvah-near-lafayette), then speak with the Rebbetzin.

## Common questions

### What are the three mitzvot of Jewish women?
They are lighting Shabbat and holiday candles, separating challah from dough, and keeping the laws of family purity with the mikvah. Together their Hebrew initials spell Chana.

### Why are these mitzvot given to women?
Chabad teaching explains that the woman shapes the spiritual character of the home, and these three mitzvot are about the home. Men are also obligated; the woman has precedence.

### Who was Chana?
Chana Schneerson was the mother of the Lubavitcher Rebbe. After she passed away, the Rebbe connected the three mitzvot to the letters of her name.

### Can a man light Shabbat candles?
Yes, if no adult woman is present to light, a man lights them.

### I never did any of these. Where do I begin?
Pick one. Light candles this Friday, or bake challah for Shabbat. Every mitzvah is a complete step.
