The Fast of the Firstborn (Taanit Bechorot, תַּעֲנִית בְּכוֹרוֹת) is a one-day fast held on the day before Passover (Erev Pesach) by firstborn sons, in thanksgiving that the Jewish firstborn were spared in the tenth plague in Egypt. It is customary to end the fast early by attending a siyum, a celebration of completing a Torah study, which makes the day a joyful beginning of Passover.

## Why firstborns fast

The Torah tells that G-d struck the firstborn of Egypt but passed over the homes of the Israelites. Our sages say that the firstborn were saved by merit of this miracle, and therefore the firstborn are sanctified to G-d (see [the Exodus story](/learn/exodus-story) and [Pidyon HaBen](/learn/pidyon-haben)). Since the firstborn were saved, they fast on the day before Passover: a day of gratitude and humility.

## Who fasts

- **Firstborn men and boys.** A man who is the firstborn of his mother (the first child born to her) is expected to fast.
- **Fathers of young firstborn sons.** A boy who is not yet bar mitzvah does not fast; his father fasts in his place.
- **Firstborn daughters.** The obligation is on firstborn sons. Some women take it on themselves as a custom; ask the Rabbi what is right for you.
- **Pregnant or ill people.** Health comes first. Ask the Rabbi.
- **Unusual cases** (a father's firstborn who is not the mother's firstborn, adopted children and so on): ask the Rabbi.

When the day before Passover falls on Shabbat, the fast is held earlier in the week, on Thursday. See [Shabbat and holiday times](/shabbat-times) and [the Jewish holidays calendar](/jewish-holidays) for this year.

## The siyum: a way to eat on this morning

A siyum (סִיּוּם, "completion") is a celebration when a person finishes a tractate of the Talmud or another section of Torah learning. It is a *seudat mitzvah*, a festive meal of a mitzvah, and those who take part in it need not fast. So the custom grew that after morning services, a siyum is held, with a short Torah lesson and a light meal, and the firstborn eat with a clear conscience.

It is a widespread custom for firstborn to take part in a siyum. If you cannot get to one, call the Rabbi; there is nothing wrong in asking.

## How the day goes

1. Start the day with morning prayers (a minyan, if you can).
2. The siyum and a small meal, if you are going.
3. The day is busy: the search for chametz was done the night before; the chametz is burned in the morning, and the last hour for eating chametz comes. See [selling your chametz](/learn/selling-your-chametz) and [Passover cleaning](/learn/passover-cleaning).
4. Afternoon: finish cooking for the Seder, set the table, light the candles, and get ready for [the Seder](/learn/passover-seder).

## Common questions

### What is the Fast of the Firstborn?
The Fast of the Firstborn is a fast held on the day before Passover by firstborn sons, to remember that the Jewish firstborn were saved in the plague of the firstborn in Egypt.

### Who has to fast on the day before Passover?
Firstborn men fast, and fathers fast for firstborn sons who are not yet bar mitzvah. Ask the Rabbi about unusual cases.

### Do firstborn daughters fast?
The fast is for firstborn sons. Some women choose to keep it as a custom. Ask the Rabbi what is right for you.

### What is a siyum before Passover?
A siyum is a celebration of completing a section of Torah learning. After the morning prayers, a siyum is held so that the firstborn who take part in the festive meal need not fast.

### What if the day before Passover is Shabbat?
The fast is held earlier, on Thursday. Check the calendar for this year or call the Rabbi.
