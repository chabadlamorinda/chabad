On Shabbat, observant Jews set aside phones, computers, television and other electronics for 25 hours, from Friday before sunset until Saturday night. Chabad.org teaches that this is part of resting from creative work, which makes Shabbat a weekly digital detox, long before anyone used the phrase, and a day to be fully present with family, friends and G-d.

For families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, where phones are part of daily life, an unplugged Shabbat can be a refreshing change. Here is why Jewish law sets the screens aside, and how to try it.

## Why electronics rest on Shabbat

Shabbat is described as "an island in time." The Torah asks us to rest from *melachah* (מְלָאכָה), usually translated "work," but meaning creative work, not physical exertion. According to Chabad.org, there are 39 categories of such work, modeled on the building of the Mishkan, the Tabernacle in the desert. They include writing, cooking, building and lighting a fire. You can read about them in [the 39 melachot](/learn/thirty-nine-melachot).

Chabad.org's list of what is avoided on Shabbat includes the radio, television, phones, computers and alarm clocks; no electrical devices are operated. The reason for each device is a detailed matter of Jewish law, which we explain in [electricity on Shabbat](/learn/electricity-on-shabbat). For a wider view of how Judaism sees technology, see [technology and screens, a Jewish view](/learn/technology-and-screens-jewish-view).

## Rest, not only rules

The goal is not only to avoid things. Shabbat is a day of *menuchah* (מְנוּחָה), rest of body and soul. Chabad.org describes it as time to disengage from material concerns, to reconnect with your soul and purpose, and to spend quality time with family. Without the phone, the day has room for a long meal, a walk, prayer and Torah learning, and conversation. We do not make scientific claims here; try it, and see how it feels for you.

## How to unplug, step by step

1. **Choose a window.** If a full day is too much, begin with Friday evening, as in [how to start keeping Shabbat](/learn/how-to-start-keeping-shabbat).
2. **Tell people ahead of time.** Let family, friends and colleagues know you will be offline from Friday afternoon. Chabad.org suggests telling guests in advance that devices are set aside for the meal.
3. **Finish what you need before Shabbat.** Make calls, send messages and charge devices before candle lighting; see [this week's candle-lighting time](/shabbat-times).
4. **Set the house before Shabbat.** Chabad.org notes that lights, heating and air conditioning can be set ahead of time, and timers may be used.
5. **Put the phone away.** Many families use a drawer or a basket by the door. The phone is also muktzeh, an object not handled on Shabbat; see [muktzeh](/learn/muktzeh).
6. **Fill the time.** Plan a meal, a book, a game and a walk; see [Shabbat afternoon ideas](/learn/shabbat-afternoon-ideas).
7. **Make a plan for emergencies.** Chabad.org states that Shabbat laws are set aside when life is at risk; saving a life is a mitzvah. If you are in doubt, or if your health requires a device, ask the Rabbi in advance.

## What if I need my phone?

Everyone's situation is different: doctors, parents of young children, people caring for someone ill. If you have a question about a medical device, an on-call job or a family emergency, ask the Rabbi before Shabbat at 925-384-7242. Never put anyone in danger because of Shabbat.

## Common questions

### Why don't religious Jews use phones on Shabbat?
Chabad.org teaches that operating electrical devices is avoided on Shabbat as part of resting from creative work, so phones, computers and televisions are set aside.

### Is Shabbat a digital detox?
In effect, yes: for 25 hours a week you rest from screens and spend time with people. For Jews, the purpose is not only the break but connecting to G-d and family.

### Can I use my phone on Shabbat in an emergency?
Yes. Chabad.org says that violating Shabbat to save a life is a mitzvah, not a violation, even when the danger is uncertain. Call for help right away, and ask the Rabbi afterwards.

### Can I use a timer on Shabbat?
Chabad.org says timers may be used for lights, heating and air conditioning, if they were set before Shabbat. Ask the Rabbi about other uses.

### What do I do with my kids without screens?
Offer games, books, drawing supplies and outdoor time. See [Shabbat with kids](/learn/shabbat-with-kids).
