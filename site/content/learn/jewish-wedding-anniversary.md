A Jewish wedding anniversary is a day to renew the love and commitment of your wedding, not only to remember it. Many couples mark it on the Hebrew date of the wedding, add a mitzvah or an act of giving, and share the day with family and friends.

## Why an anniversary matters in Judaism

Chabad.org explains that Judaism does not see an anniversary only as a celebration of the past. According to Chassidic teaching, the spiritual energy of a past event is more easily reached on its anniversary. For a marriage, this means the love, loyalty and friendship that you shared under the chuppah (the wedding canopy) can be readily reawakened on that day. The same article adds that we need not wait for an anniversary to feel close: ordinary acts done with intention can make any day meaningful. That is the spirit of the day.

## Your Hebrew date

Jewish dates follow the Hebrew calendar, so your Hebrew anniversary falls on a different day each year on the civil calendar. To find yours, see [the Hebrew birthday guide](/learn/hebrew-birthday), which explains how to convert a date. Marking the Hebrew date is a custom and a lovely one; many couples celebrate on both dates. If the wedding took place after nightfall, ask the Rabbi which date applies.

## Ideas for the day

Chabad.org does not set a ritual for wedding anniversaries, so these are suggestions in the spirit of Jewish tradition rather than rules.

1. **Renew your commitment.** Take a quiet moment together to say what you appreciate in each other and what you promise for the year ahead. See [shalom bayit, peace in the home](/learn/shalom-bayit).
2. **Light the candles together.** Join in [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) when the date falls near Shabbat.
3. **Give tzedakah.** Give a gift in honor of your marriage (see [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah)).
4. **Share a Kiddush.** Mark the day with the community after services (see [sponsoring a Kiddush](/learn/sponsoring-a-kiddush)).
5. **Add one mitzvah as a couple,** such as inviting a guest for Shabbat or studying together.
6. **Gather family and friends** for a meal, and have each guest share a blessing for you.

## Milestone years

Couples often make more of milestone years, such as the 25th and 50th. Chabad.org has long carried blessings for these milestones. A blessing for the next years is a heartfelt way to mark them, and you are welcome to ask the Rabbi or Rebbetzin for one. We will not invent customs, so for questions about a particular tradition, ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### How do Jews celebrate a wedding anniversary?
Many couples mark the day with a renewed commitment, a mitzvah or tzedakah, and a meal with family. There is no required ritual.

### Should we celebrate on the Hebrew or the civil date?
Either, and many couples do both. The Hebrew date is a lovely Jewish custom. Ask the Rabbi if you are unsure which date applies.

### Is there a special blessing for an anniversary?
Chabad.org has no prescribed anniversary blessing, but couples receive blessings from the Rabbi and from friends. Call 925-384-7242 and ask.

### How do I find my Hebrew wedding date?
Convert your civil date with the guide on [the Hebrew birthday](/learn/hebrew-birthday). The Rabbi can help.

### What are good Jewish anniversary gifts?
Meaningful gifts include a gift to tzedakah in your names, Shabbat candlesticks, or time spent learning together.

### Can we sponsor a Kiddush for our anniversary?
Yes, a Kiddush is a lovely way to share the day. See [sponsoring a Kiddush](/learn/sponsoring-a-kiddush) or ask the Rabbi.
