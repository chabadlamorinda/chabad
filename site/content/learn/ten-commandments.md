The Ten Commandments are the ten statements G-d spoke to the entire Jewish people at Mount Sinai, and later engraved on two stone tablets. They include believing in G-d, keeping Shabbat, honoring parents, and not murdering, stealing or lying.

## Where they come from

Seven weeks after the Exodus from Egypt, the Jewish people camped at the foot of Mount Sinai. There, with thunder, lightning and the sound of a shofar, G-d spoke to all of them directly. The event is described in Exodus chapter 20, and Moses repeats it in Deuteronomy chapter 5.

Moses then went up the mountain for 40 days and brought down two stone tablets, with five commandments on each. This revelation is celebrated each year on the holiday of Shavuot.

## The Ten Commandments in plain English

**First tablet: between people and G-d**

1. I am the L-rd your G-d, who took you out of Egypt.
2. Do not have other gods, and do not make or worship idols.
3. Do not take G-d's name in vain.
4. Remember the [Shabbat](/learn/shabbat) day and keep it holy.
5. Honor your father and your mother.

**Second tablet: between people**

6. Do not murder.
7. Do not commit adultery.
8. Do not steal (this refers mainly to kidnapping).
9. Do not bear false witness against your neighbor.
10. Do not covet what belongs to your neighbor.

## What they mean

In Hebrew they are called the Aseret Hadibrot, the "Ten Statements." They are not the only commandments. The Torah contains [613 mitzvot](/learn/613-mitzvot). The Sages explain that all 613 are included in the Ten Commandments, like branches growing from roots.

A few lessons from how they are arranged:

- **The two tablets mirror each other.** The first commandment, belief in G-d, sits across from "do not murder," because harming a person, created in G-d's image, is like harming G-d's image itself.
- **Honoring parents is on the first tablet.** Parents are partners with G-d in creating a child, so honoring them is part of honoring G-d. See our guide to [honoring parents](/learn/honoring-parents).
- **The last one is about thoughts.** "Do not covet" reaches into our hearts, teaching that Judaism is about who we are inside, not only what we do.

## Why they were said to everyone

At Sinai, G-d did not give the Torah to a few leaders. Every man, woman and child heard it, along with the souls of every future generation. This shared experience is the basis of the Jewish people's faith, passed down from parent to child.

## Hearing them on Shavuot

On the first day of Shavuot, the Ten Commandments are read from the Torah in synagogue, and the congregation stands. The Lubavitcher Rebbe encouraged every Jew, including babies and young children, to come to synagogue to hear them, reliving the experience of Sinai.

## Common questions

### Why are there different lists of the Ten Commandments?
Some religions divide the verses differently. In the Jewish counting, "I am the L-rd your G-d" is the first commandment, and "do not have other gods" is the second.

### Do Jews post the Ten Commandments in synagogue?
Many synagogues show the two tablets above the ark as a symbol. In the time of the Talmud, the Sages stopped reciting them daily so no one would think only these ten were important.

### Are the Ten Commandments for non-Jews too?
Many of their moral teachings are universal and are also part of the [Seven Noahide Laws](/learn/noahide-laws), which the Torah gives to all humanity.

### Why do we stand during the reading?
Standing recalls how the Jewish people stood at the foot of Mount Sinai. Many communities, including Chabad, have the custom to stand.
