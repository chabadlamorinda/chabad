When a Jewish person dies, call Rabbi Yisroel Labkowski at 925-384-7242 (or Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski at 925-384-7241) first, as soon as you can, before you call the burial society (Chevra Kadisha) or a funeral home, and before you sign anything. The Rabbi will guide your family through every step and make the arrangements. If the death is sudden, call 911 first. We are so sorry for your loss, and you do not have to do this alone.

**Jewish burial is of the utmost importance, and it can all be arranged even if there is no budget. Financial help is available. Please call at once.** See [help with the cost](/learn/jewish-burial-financial-help).

This guide lists the steps Chabad.org gives for the first hours. It does not repeat the prayers at the bedside (see [when a loved one is dying](/learn/when-a-loved-one-is-dying)) or the whole funeral (see [Jewish funeral customs](/learn/jewish-funeral)).

## The very first moments

Chabad.org teaches that everyone present should say the blessing that acknowledges G-d as the true Judge. The Rabbi can tell you the words by phone. Mourning begins at the moment of death for a father, mother, spouse, son, daughter, brother or sister. For the next steps, Chabad.org advises:

- Do not touch or move the body for at least 15 minutes.
- After that, the eyes are closed and the body is covered with a sheet.
- A candle is lit near the head, except on Shabbat and holidays.
- Mirrors and pictures of people are covered, and any open water in the room is poured out.
- Relatives who are present may ask the person for forgiveness, and offer their own. Those who are not there can do so later, at the funeral or the grave.

Keep the room quiet. Conversation should be about the funeral arrangements and the person's good qualities, or it can be Psalms ([Tehillim](/learn/tehillim)).

## Who to call

Chabad.org says the local **Chevra Kadisha** (the Jewish burial society) should be reached right away for instructions, to make sure the **funeral home**, if you use one, knows what a Jewish burial needs, and to ask a **rabbi** about anything unusual (a death on Shabbat or a holiday, burial far away, organ donation). Here in Lamorinda, the first call is to Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242, before the burial society or a funeral home: that one call starts all of it, and he makes the arrangements. See [Jewish funerals near Lafayette](/learn/jewish-funerals-near-lafayette).

Tell the funeral home that your loved one needs tahara (ritual washing), a *shomer* (a guard), white shrouds, a traditional wooden casket and the care of the Chevra Kadisha. See [tahara and the Chevra Kadisha](/learn/chevra-kadisha-and-tahara).

## If the death is in a hospital, hospice or care home

Chabad.org says to tell the staff that the Chevra Kadisha will care for the body, and to ask them to wait for instructions before they do anything. If the body must be moved to the hospital morgue, a Jewish person should go along and stay in sight until the Chevra Kadisha takes over. Do not remove tubes or lines, and give the Chevra Kadisha any clothing or linens with blood on them, because they are buried with the person. A person who has just died is to be treated with the same dignity as a living person.

Say clearly that you do **not** want an autopsy or embalming. Chabad.org says autopsies are strongly forbidden (if the authorities require one, ask a rabbi who knows this area at once) and embalming is forbidden. See [autopsy: the Jewish view](/learn/autopsy-jewish-view) and [embalming and viewing](/learn/embalming-and-viewing-jewish-view). Organ donation is a complicated question; speak to the Rabbi before deciding ([organ donation](/learn/organ-donation-jewish-view)).

## Do not leave the body alone

A Jewish person should stay with the body until burial. This is called *shemirah*, "guarding." It may be a family member, a friend, a member of the Chevra Kadisha or someone hired, and people may take turns. Chabad.org says those who guard should not eat, drink, smoke or sleep in the room, and should read Psalms instead. If the body is driven anywhere, a Jewish person should ride along.

## If the death is on Shabbat or a holiday

The restrictions of Shabbat and the holidays still apply, including to preparing for burial. Chabad.org says the body is not moved, is covered with a sheet, and is left until Shabbat or the holiday ends. Call the Rabbi, who will guide you through it.

## Common questions

### What do I do when someone dies at home?

If the death is sudden or unexpected, call 911 first. Then call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242. Cover the person with a sheet after about 15 minutes, and a Jewish person should stay with the body until the Chevra Kadisha takes over.

### Who do I call first when a Jewish person dies?

Call Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242 (or the Rebbetzin at 925-384-7241) first, before the Chevra Kadisha (burial society) or a funeral home. He will guide you through every step and make the arrangements with them.

### What if my loved one died in a hospital?

Tell the staff the Chevra Kadisha will care for the body and ask them to wait for instructions. Say you do not want an autopsy or embalming, and call the Rabbi right away.

### How soon must a Jewish burial take place?

As soon as possible, and no later than 24 hours, unless a delay honors the deceased, such as legal requirements, shrouds and a casket, or close family who must travel. See [why Jews bury quickly](/learn/why-jews-bury-quickly).

### Can someone stay with the body overnight?

Yes. In fact, Jewish tradition asks that someone does. The person who stays recites Psalms. If you cannot, the Chevra Kadisha or the funeral home can arrange a *shomer*; ask the Rabbi.

### What if I cannot afford the funeral?

Call the Rabbi anyway. Jewish burial can all be arranged even with no budget, and financial help is available.
