The Torah's first story about brothers is Cain and Abel; later come Isaac and Ishmael, Jacob and Esau, Joseph and his brothers. The Torah does not hide sibling rivalry. It teaches from it.

## The lesson of Joseph's coat

Jacob gave Joseph a special coat, and his brothers' jealousy grew until they sold him. The Talmud learns: "A person should never treat one child differently from the others," because of a little extra wool, a family was torn apart for years. Children notice everything: time, attention, praise and gifts.

## Every child is special in a different way

Treating children equally does not mean treating them identically. Each needs different things. What matters is that each child feels uniquely loved:

- **One-on-one time** with each child, even 10 minutes, regularly.
- **Praise each child for their own strengths,** without comparing ("Why can't you be like your sister?" plants resentment).
- **Celebrate each one's milestones** fully.

## When they fight

- **Stay calm and fair.** Hear both sides before judging.
- **Separate first, solve later** if tempers are high.
- **Teach the words:** "I'm sorry," "I forgive you," "Can we share?"
- **Don't always rescue.** Help them learn to solve small conflicts themselves.
- **Hurting is a line,** with words or hands. Make that clear for everyone.

## Building a lifelong bond

- **Shared mitzvahs:** baking challah together, a family [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) box, visiting a grandparent.
- **Family traditions:** Shabbat songs, holiday roles, a special Chanukah night.
- **Teach respect for older siblings.** Jewish law includes honoring an older brother or sister.
- **Model it.** Children watch how parents treat their own siblings.

## Grown-up siblings

Rivalries don't always end in childhood. Adult siblings often clash over caring for parents or inheritances. See [family feuds and inheritance](/learn/family-feuds-and-inheritance).

## Common questions

### Is fighting between siblings normal?
Some fighting is normal and teaches children to handle conflict. Constant hostility, bullying or violence needs a parent's firm help, and sometimes outside help.

### My kids say I love one more. What do I do?
Take it seriously, without arguing. Ask what makes them feel that way, and add one-on-one time and specific praise for the child who feels left out.

### Should children share everything?
Not everything. Children are allowed some things that are theirs. Teach generosity, but also respect for each other's belongings.
