Modeh Ani is the short prayer Jews say the moment they wake up, thanking G-d for returning their soul and giving them a new day. It is just twelve words in Hebrew, and it is usually the very first prayer children learn.

## The words

**Hebrew:**
מוֹדֶה אֲנִי לְפָנֶיךָ, מֶלֶךְ חַי וְקַיָּם, שֶׁהֶחֱזַרְתָּ בִּי נִשְׁמָתִי בְּחֶמְלָה, רַבָּה אֱמוּנָתֶךָ

**Transliteration:** Modeh ani l'fanecha, melech chai v'kayam, shehechezarta bi nishmati b'chemlah, rabbah emunatecha.

**Translation:** I offer thanks to You, living and eternal King, for You have mercifully restored my soul within me. Great is Your faithfulness.

Women and girls say **Modah ani** instead of Modeh ani.

## What it means

Jewish tradition compares sleep to a small taste of death: the soul partly leaves the body at night. Each morning, G-d returns it to us, refreshed. The last words, "Great is Your faithfulness," express that G-d trusts us with a new day, believing in what we can accomplish.

Starting the day with "thank you" sets the tone for everything that follows. Before checking a phone or thinking about the to-do list, the first thing we do is recognize that life itself is a gift.

## Why it can be said before washing

Normally we wash our hands before saying prayers in the morning. Modeh Ani does not contain any of G-d's names, so it may be said right away, while still in bed, before washing.

## The morning routine, step by step

1. **Wake up and say Modeh Ani** right away, while still in bed. Pause after "Modeh ani l'fanecha" and think about standing before G-d.
2. **Wash your hands (negel vasser).** Keep a cup and a basin of water next to your bed. Pour water from the cup over your right hand, then your left, alternating until each hand has been washed three times.
3. **Get ready for the day.**
4. **Say the blessing for washing hands** and the morning blessings, found at the beginning of the siddur:
   Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav v'tzivanu al n'tilat yadayim.
   "Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments and commanded us concerning washing the hands."
5. **Continue with morning prayers** as you are able. See our guide to [prayer](/learn/prayer).

## Teaching children Modeh Ani

Modeh Ani is easy to learn and beautiful to say with children. Many families put a Modeh Ani poster by the bed, sing it together with a simple tune, and keep a small cup and bowl for washing. It teaches children from the youngest age to start every day with gratitude.

## Common questions

### Do I say Modeh Ani if I wake up in the middle of the night?
Modeh Ani is said once, when you wake up for the day. If you get up briefly at night and go back to sleep, wait until morning.

### What if I forgot to say Modeh Ani right away?
You can still say it later in the morning. It is best said as soon as you wake up, but saying it later still expresses thanks to G-d.

### Can I say Modeh Ani in English?
Yes. Saying it in English is fine, especially if that helps you mean it. Many people learn the Hebrew too, since it is short and easy to memorize.

### Why do we wash our hands in the morning?
Our Sages teach that during sleep a spiritual impurity rests on the hands. Washing removes it and prepares us to serve G-d, like a Kohen washing before serving in the Temple.
