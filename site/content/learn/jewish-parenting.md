Jewish parenting means raising children with love, Torah values and a strong Jewish identity, mainly by living those values yourself. Jewish tradition sees every child as a precious soul entrusted to parents by G-d, and education as a sacred task.

## The Jewish view of parenting

The Torah commands: "You shall teach them diligently to your children" (Deuteronomy 6:7), part of the [Shema](/learn/shema). Parents are the first and most important teachers. King Solomon wrote, "Educate a child according to his way" (Proverbs 22:6), meaning each child should be taught in the way that suits them.

The Hebrew word for education, chinuch, also means "dedication" or "inauguration." It is about starting a child on a lifelong path.

## Teaching by example

Children learn more from what they see than from what they hear. When they see parents:

- Saying blessings before eating,
- Giving tzedakah,
- Treating each other with respect,
- Lighting Shabbat candles and making Kiddush,
- Learning Torah,

they absorb these values naturally.

## Daily mitzvot with children

Simple habits build a Jewish life. Many families include:

1. **[Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani)** when waking up, thanking G-d.
2. **Washing hands** in the morning (negel vasser).
3. **Blessings** before and after eating.
4. **A pushka**, a tzedakah box, where children put a coin each weekday.
5. **The Shema** at bedtime, with a kiss on the mezuzah.
6. **Jewish books and stories** at home. See our guide to the [Jewish home library](/learn/jewish-home-library).

The Lubavitcher Rebbe encouraged hanging a picture or sign with Jewish verses near a baby's crib and giving children their own siddur, Chumash and tzedakah box.

## Shabbat and holidays

Shabbat and the holidays are the heart of Jewish family life:

- Let children help prepare: braid challah, set the table, decorate the sukkah.
- Give each child a role at the Shabbat table, like sharing something from the weekly parsha.
- Make holidays fun and sensory: dreidels, hamantaschen, the Seder's questions.

## Jewish education

Formal Jewish education gives children knowledge, community and pride. A Jewish preschool, Hebrew school, day school and summer camp all play a part. Milestones like the [upsherin](/learn/upsherin) at age three and bar or bat mitzvah mark a child's growth.

## Discipline with love

Jewish teaching guides parents to be firm but warm. The Talmud advises, "The left hand pushes away, while the right hand draws near," meaning love should always be stronger than discipline. Never discipline in anger. Praise effort and good character.

## Each child is unique

Every child has different strengths. Jewish tradition values nurturing each one's personal path, not forcing children into the same mold.

## Common questions

### What if I did not grow up observant?
You can start today, at whatever level is right for your family. Children learn alongside you, and many parents find their own Judaism grows as they teach their children.

### At what age should Jewish education begin?
Right away. Even infants absorb the atmosphere of Jewish songs, blessings and Shabbat. Formal learning, such as the Hebrew alphabet, often starts around age three.

### How do I handle a child who resists?
Stay patient and positive. Make Jewish life joyful and avoid power struggles. Focus on the relationship, and ask the Rabbi or an experienced educator for guidance.

### How do I teach my child to be proud of being Jewish?
Celebrate Jewish life with joy, share stories of Jewish history and heroes, and connect with a warm Jewish community.
