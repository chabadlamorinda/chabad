Tracing your Jewish roots starts with the family: names, stories and documents, especially Hebrew tombstones and marriage contracts, which record each person's Hebrew name and father's name. Jewish tradition values remembering where we come from, and discovering your family's story can deepen your own Jewish life.

## Why roots matter in Judaism

The Torah is full of family trees, from Adam to Abraham to the twelve tribes. Every Jew is part of a chain going back to Sinai. Many people who research their families find not only names and dates but a connection to the mitzvahs and traditions their grandparents kept.

## Step by step: where to start

1. **Talk to older relatives now.** Ask about names, towns, synagogues, occupations and customs. Record the conversations.
2. **Gather documents.** Look for birth and marriage certificates, immigration papers, naturalization records, letters and photos.
3. **Find Hebrew names.** A Hebrew name with the father's name (for example, Moshe ben Yaakov) can unlock a generation. Read about [Hebrew names](/learn/hebrew-names).
4. **Visit or photograph tombstones.** Jewish tombstones usually give the Hebrew name, the father's name, the Hebrew date of death, and sometimes a Kohen or Levi symbol. See [visiting a grave](/learn/visiting-a-grave).
5. **Look at the ketubah.** The Jewish marriage contract lists the bride's and groom's Hebrew names and their fathers' names, and the place and date of the wedding.
6. **Convert Hebrew dates.** Use our [Hebrew date converter](/tools/hebrew-date) to turn dates on documents into regular dates, and the [yahrzeit calculator](/tools/yahrzeit) to find yahrzeits.
7. **Search records.** Archives of immigration, census and community records, and Holocaust memorial databases, can fill in missing branches.

## Clues hidden in Jewish customs

- **Kohen or Levi.** Hands raised in blessing on a tombstone mean a Kohen; a pitcher means a Levi. This status passes from father to son and matters today, for example for the Torah reading and [pidyon haben](/learn/pidyon-haben).
- **Naming patterns.** Ashkenazi Jews usually name children after relatives who have passed away; Sephardi Jews often name after living grandparents. Repeated names can point to earlier generations.
- **Family customs**, like how long to wait between meat and milk or how a family lit Shabbat candles, may show which community your ancestors came from.

## Remembering those lost in the Holocaust

For many families, research leads to relatives who perished. Learning their names and saying Kaddish or lighting a candle on their yahrzeit is a profound way to honor them. If no date of death is known, ask the Rabbi when to observe it. See [Holocaust remembrance](/learn/holocaust-remembrance).

## DNA tests and Jewish identity

DNA tests can be interesting and may show Jewish ancestry, but they do not decide whether someone is Jewish. In Jewish law, a person is Jewish if their mother is Jewish, or through conversion. If research suggests your mother's line is Jewish, the Rabbi can help you look at documents and understand what it means. Read [who is a Jew](/learn/who-is-a-jew) and our [Jewish identity page](/help/jewish-identity).

## Common questions

### How can I find out if I am Jewish?
Look for records about your mother's mother and her line: ketubahs, Jewish burial records, synagogue records or family letters. Bring what you find to the Rabbi, who can help you understand it.

### Can a DNA test prove I am Jewish?
No. DNA can suggest Jewish ancestry but cannot establish Jewish status, which goes through the mother or conversion. Documents and family history are what count.

### How do I read Hebrew on a family tombstone?
Take a clear photo of the whole stone and send it to us. Tombstones usually show the Hebrew name, the father's name and the Hebrew date of passing.

### How do I know if my family are Kohanim or Levites?
Ask older relatives, check tombstones for hands or a pitcher, and look at family names like Cohen or Levi, though names alone are not proof. A tradition passed from father to son is the main source.
