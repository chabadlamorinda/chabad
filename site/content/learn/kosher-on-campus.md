Going to college is a big step, and keeping kosher and Shabbat away from home can feel daunting. With a little planning, many students do it successfully, and often find it becomes an anchor in a busy new life. Here is how to start.

## Before you go

- **Ask about kosher options** when visiting or choosing a school. Some dining halls offer kosher food; many do not.
- **Find the Chabad on campus.** Chabad centers serve many campuses around the world, and a Chabad rabbi and rebbetzin are often a student's home away from home.
- **Ask about housing** with a kitchenette, or a dorm where a small fridge and microwave are allowed.
- **Talk it over with the Rabbi** before you leave, so you know the basics.

## Finding kosher food

- **Learn the [kosher symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols).** Many packaged foods in ordinary supermarkets are certified kosher.
- **Fresh fruit and vegetables** are kosher; check produce for insects as needed. See [Checking for Insects](/learn/checking-for-insects).
- **Kosher meat and chicken** need certification. Some students order frozen kosher meals; others buy at a kosher store when they can.
- **On a budget?** See [Kosher on a Budget](/learn/kosher-on-a-budget).

## Your own microwave and dishes

If you cannot eat in the dining hall, a small kitchen of your own makes kosher possible:

- **Your own microwave,** used only for kosher food.
- **A few dishes, a pot and utensils,** kept separate. Keeping a dairy and meat set apart, or eating mostly dairy and pareve, keeps things simple.
- **Tevilah:** new metal and glass dishes are immersed in a mikvah before use. See [Tevilat Kelim](/learn/tevilat-kelim).

Sharing a kitchen or microwave raises questions; ask the Rabbi.

## Shabbat on campus

Shabbat can be the highlight of the week. Many campus Chabad houses host Friday-night dinners and Shabbat meals, open to every Jewish student. If there is none nearby, you can light candles, make Kiddush and invite friends to a simple meal in your room. Look up candle-lighting times for your campus each week.

## Roommates and professors

- **Roommates:** explain simply and early. "I keep kosher, so please don't use my dishes or microwave." Most people are respectful and curious.
- **Professors:** at the start of the semester, tell them which Shabbat and holiday dates you cannot attend class or take exams. Many schools have policies for religious observance. See [Jewish Holidays at School and Work](/learn/jewish-holidays-at-school-and-work).

## Common questions

### Can I eat in a non-kosher dining hall if I choose carefully?
Food cooked in a non-kosher kitchen is generally not kosher, even if the ingredients are. Sealed, certified packaged items can be fine. Ask the Rabbi for guidance.

### How do I find the Chabad on my campus?
Search for Chabad and your school's name, or ask Rabbi Labkowski, who is glad to connect you.

### What if I have an exam on Shabbat?
Speak to the professor early; many will arrange another time. If it is difficult, call the Rabbi for advice.

### I am not fully observant. Where do I start?
Start with one step, such as kosher meat or a Friday-night meal. Every mitzvah counts. See [Jewish College Students](/learn/jewish-college-students).
