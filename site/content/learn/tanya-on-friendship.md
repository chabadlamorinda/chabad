In Tanya's view, friendship is a meeting of souls: two people with G-dly souls that come from one source, who give each other love that does not depend on what they get. A true friend helps us see the best in ourselves, and Chassidic tradition built a whole gathering, the farbrengen, around friends encouraging one another.

For the Torah's general teaching about friends, see [friendship in Judaism](/learn/jewish-friendship). Here we follow the Tanya's angle.

## Friendship that goes deeper than usefulness

Chapter 32 of the [Tanya](/learn/tanya) teaches that all Jewish souls come from one root, so Jews are truly brothers and sisters. Chabad.org's text points to a difference between two kinds of love. Love that rests on outer things, such as looks, advantages, or shared interests, lasts only as long as the outer thing does. Love that rests on the soul is deeper.

Chabad.org's article on Ahavat Yisrael calls the first kind "dependent" love and the second "unconditional." A friendship that begins with a shared interest can still grow into the second kind. Ask yourself: if the shared interest disappeared, would I still care about this person?

## Every favor might be the reason

The same article tells of the Baal Shem Tov, who taught that a person should love another Jew even if they have never met, and that a soul may come down into this world just to do one favor for another Jew. The Lubavitcher Rebbe added: since we cannot know which favor is the one, treat each as though it were. See [Ahavat Yisrael](/learn/ahavat-yisrael).

That makes friendship concrete. The phone call, the ride, the soup for a sick friend are all more important than they feel at the time. For friendships in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, where many families are busy and far from relatives, this kind of everyday kindness is how a community forms.

## What the farbrengen teaches about friends

A farbrengen (פארברענגען) is Yiddish for "spending time together." Chabad.org describes it as an informal Chassidic gathering where people share words of Torah and sing melodies. The Rebbe, in a letter that Chabad.org quotes, wrote that its purpose is to help participants encourage each other in Torah learning, in doing mitzvot beautifully and in good character, and to live in a way that makes G-d's name beloved to others.

Chabad.org's "What to Expect at a Farbrengen" adds two features of a good friendship. The conversation can be candid but comes from care and unity. And people often make a *hachlatah*, a resolution to improve, advising that it be "one that you can actually follow through with."

So a Chassidic friend is not only company. He or she is someone who, kindly and with warmth, nudges you toward the best of yourself, and lets you do the same. For more, read [what is a farbrengen](/learn/lchaim-and-farbrengen).

Jewish learning is traditionally done with a partner. See [chavruta learning partner](/learn/chavruta-learning-partner). Learning Tanya with a friend is a natural place to start.

## A few questions for friendship

These are ways to apply the teaching, not quotations from it:

1. **Do I see this friend's soul?** Name one quality that you admire.
2. **Is my love dependent on something?** Can it grow beyond that?
3. **Can I be encouraging?** What small resolution might we make together?

## Common questions

### What does Tanya say about friendship?
Tanya does not have a section on friendship, but chapter 32 teaches that Jewish souls share one source, which is the root of deep, unconditional love between people.

### What is Chassidic friendship?
It is friendship that includes helping each other grow, and a farbrengen is a gathering for that.

### What is a farbrengen for?
According to a letter of the Rebbe on Chabad.org, a farbrengen is to encourage each other in Torah learning, mitzvot and good character.
