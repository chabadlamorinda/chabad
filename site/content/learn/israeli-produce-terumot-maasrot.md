Produce grown in the Land of Israel must have its tithes (terumot and maasrot) separated before it is eaten, and this applies to Israeli produce bought outside Israel as well. Produce with reliable kosher certification from Israel has usually been taken care of; produce without certification may need to be tithed, or avoided, and it is best to ask the Rabbi.

## What are terumot and maasrot?

When the Temple stood, farmers in the Land of Israel gave portions of their crop to the Kohanim, the Levites and the poor. These are the tithes: *terumah* (תרומה), the portion for the Kohen, *maaser rishon*, the first tithe for the Levite, and a second tithe, either *maaser sheni* or *maaser ani* (for the poor), depending on the year in the seven-year cycle. Chabad.org explains that maaser sheni is separated in years 1, 2, 4 and 5 of the cycle and maaser ani in years 3 and 6. You can read about tithing in general in [Maaser](/learn/maaser).

Today these gifts are not given in the same way, but the produce is still not permitted to be eaten until the tithes have been separated. This mitzvah belongs to the Land (see [The Land of Israel in Jewish Tradition](/learn/the-land-of-israel-in-jewish-tradition)).

## Why it matters outside Israel

Most fresh produce in stores carries no kosher certification, and Israeli produce is no different. According to Chabad.org, produce from certified Israeli establishments needs no separation, because the certification covers it. Uncertified produce may not have been tithed. That is why many people avoid uncertified Israeli produce altogether.

## Three questions to ask about Israeli fruit and vegetables

1. **Is it certified?** If it has reliable kosher certification, ask the Rabbi if you are unsure about the certificate (see [Kosher Symbols](/learn/kosher-symbols)).
2. **Is it orlah?** The fruit of a tree in its first three years is forbidden (orlah), and the fourth year's fruit has special rules. Outside Israel, only fruit known to be orlah is forbidden, but Israeli fruit that might be orlah is a question for a rabbi. Chabad.org notes that this applies to fruit, not vegetables, and that practices change, so ask a knowledgeable rabbi.
3. **Was it grown in a shmita year?** In the seventh year, the land rests (see [Shmita](/learn/shmita)). Terumah and maaser do not apply to shmita-year produce, but other rules do. Chabad.org says it is generally advisable not to buy Israeli produce during shmita. Check the calendar and ask the Rabbi.

## If you already bought uncertified Israeli produce

Chabad.org gives a method, which may be used when the produce is not orlah and was not grown in a shmita year. In outline:

1. Gather produce of the same kind from the same source.
2. Separate a little more than one percent of it.
3. Designate a coin as the maaser sheni, to be redeemed by the produce.
4. Say the declaration, and wrap and discard the separated portion.
5. Deface the coin and dispose of it.

Normally no blessing is said when separating from store-bought produce, because somewhere along the way the tithes may already have been separated. The details depend on the kind of produce, the value of the produce and the current year, so please ask the Rabbi to walk you through it. The article is clear that it is a general guide, not a substitute for asking a rabbi about your particular case.

## Common questions

### Do I need to separate tithes from Israeli fruit that I bought in California?
If the produce is not certified, it may need to be tithed, or avoided. Ask the Rabbi about the particular fruit, since the rules depend on the kind of produce.

### Do I say a blessing when I separate terumah and maaser from store-bought produce?
Normally no, since someone in the supply chain may already have separated the tithes. A blessing is for the unlikely case that you are certain no tithes were separated. Ask the Rabbi.

### What is orlah?
Orlah is the fruit of a tree in its first three years, which is forbidden. The fourth-year fruit has its own rules.

### Can I buy Israeli produce in a shmita year?
Chabad.org says it is generally advisable not to buy Israeli produce during shmita, because other issues arise. Ask the Rabbi.

### Do I need to separate tithes from certified Israeli produce?
Chabad.org says produce from certified Israeli establishments needs no separation, since the certification covers it.
