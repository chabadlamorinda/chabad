A bat mitzvah celebrates a girl's 12th Hebrew birthday, the day she becomes responsible for the mitzvot. The most memorable celebrations are built around her: what she learned, a mitzvah she takes on, and the people who love her. Here are ideas that families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda can mix and match, simple or grand.

## Start with what she learned

At Chabad of Lamorinda, a bat mitzvah girl joins the **Bat Mitzvah Club** and learns with the Rebbetzin about the mitzvot of a Jewish woman and about Jewish heroines. Let her share some of it: a short talk, a display of what she studied, or a few words about a woman of the Torah she admires, such as Sarah, Rachel, Devorah or Esther. See [Jewish women](/learn/jewish-women).

## Light Shabbat candles together

Many girls begin lighting their own [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) at their bat mitzvah, or continue a custom they began as little girls. Lighting together with her mother, grandmothers and friends on the Friday of her celebration is a moving moment, and her own candlesticks make a lasting gift. See [girls lighting Shabbat candles](/learn/girls-lighting-shabbat-candles).

## A challah bake

Invite her friends and relatives to bake [challah](/learn/challah) together. She can explain the mitzvah of separating challah and say a prayer for someone who needs one. Everyone takes a loaf home.

## A mitzvah project

Give the day a heart: collecting food for families in need, visiting seniors, or raising tzedakah for a cause she cares about. There are more ideas in [bat mitzvah projects](/learn/bat-mitzvah-projects).

## A mother-daughter moment

Some mothers write their daughter a letter of blessing, or pass on a family story or a piece of jewelry from a grandmother. Grandmothers can do the same. A few minutes like this can be the part of the day she remembers most.

## A mitzvah of her own

Encourage her to choose one mitzvah to make her own from now on: giving tzedakah each weekday, a short daily prayer, a kind act each week, or learning something Jewish regularly.

## The celebration itself

Any setting works: a dinner, a lunch, a party at home, an evening with girls and women. In Chabad communities, the bat mitzvah celebration usually takes place outside the synagogue service, where she can speak and shine. Keep it within your means; meaning matters more than size. See [a meaningful celebration](/learn/meaningful-bar-bat-mitzvah-celebration).

## Gifts with meaning

Shabbat candlesticks, a siddur (prayer book) with her name on it, a tzedakah box, Jewish books, or a gift in her honor to a cause she cares about.

## Common questions

### What do you do at a bat mitzvah celebration?
Usually the girl gives a short talk about what she learned, family and friends say a few words or blessings, and everyone enjoys a festive meal. Many add a challah bake, candle lighting or a mitzvah project.

### What is a meaningful bat mitzvah idea?
Build the day around a mitzvah she takes on, such as lighting Shabbat candles or giving tzedakah, and let her share what it means to her.
