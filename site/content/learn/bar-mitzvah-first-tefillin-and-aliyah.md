On his bar mitzvah, a boy does two things for the first time as a Jewish adult: he puts on tefillin as a mitzvah of his own, and he is called up to the Torah for an aliyah. Both are simpler than they look. At Chabad of Lamorinda, the Rabbi practices with him beforehand and stands right beside him. Here is what happens, step by step.

## Before the day: practice

In Chabad custom, a boy begins putting on tefillin about two months before his bar mitzvah, so that by the day it already feels natural (see [starting tefillin before the bar mitzvah](/learn/starting-tefillin-before-bar-mitzvah)). In the weeks before, he also practices the aliyah blessings and, if he is reading, his part of the Torah portion.

## Tefillin on a weekday morning

Tefillin are worn on weekday mornings, not on Shabbat or Jewish holidays. Many families mark the first weekday morning after the 13th Hebrew birthday with tefillin at the morning prayers, surrounded by family. Here is what he does:

1. **The arm tefillin.** He places the box on his left upper arm (a left-handed boy uses his right arm), facing the heart, says the blessing, tightens the strap and wraps it seven times around his forearm.
2. **The head tefillin.** He places the box above his forehead, at the hairline and centered, with the knot at the back of his head.
3. **The hand.** He wraps the rest of the arm strap around his middle finger and hand.
4. **Shema.** He says the Shema, the central Jewish declaration of faith, and whatever prayers he is ready for.

His own pair of kosher tefillin is a highlight of the preparation, and the Rabbi can help you get one. See [buying tefillin](/learn/buying-tefillin).

## The aliyah: called up to the Torah

The Torah is read on Monday and Thursday mornings, on Shabbat morning and on holidays. Once he is bar mitzvah, a boy can have his first aliyah at any of these readings: often on the Shabbat after his Hebrew birthday, or on a Monday or Thursday together with his tefillin. Ask the Rabbi to plan it with you.

1. **He is called up** by his Hebrew name. If you do not know it, the Rabbi will help; see [Hebrew names](/learn/hebrew-names).
2. **He steps up to the bimah,** where the Torah lies open, and the reader shows him the place.
3. **The first blessing.** He touches the Torah with the corner of a tallit or the Torah's sash, kisses it, and says the blessing before the reading.
4. **The reading.** He follows along as the reader chants, or reads the section himself if he has prepared it.
5. **The closing blessing.** He says the blessing after the reading.
6. **His father's blessing.** The father may say the short blessing "Baruch Shepatarani." See [the father's blessing](/learn/parents-blessing-at-bar-mitzvah).
7. **A blessing for him.** The Rabbi says a Mi Sheberach blessing for the bar mitzvah boy and his family.

In many communities, children then toss soft candies at him: a sweet wish for a sweet life.

## A few words of Torah

At the Kiddush or the meal, the boy often shares a short d'var Torah. A few heartfelt minutes are plenty. See [the bar mitzvah speech](/learn/bar-mitzvah-speech).

## The Kiddush and the meal

After services, the family can [sponsor the Kiddush](/sponsor-a-kiddush) and celebrate with the whole community, and then enjoy a festive meal (seudat mitzvah) with family and friends.

## Common questions

### When does a boy put on tefillin for the first time?
In Chabad custom, about two months before his bar mitzvah, first without the blessing and then with it, so he is ready by his 13th Hebrew birthday.

### Can the bar mitzvah aliyah be on a Monday or Thursday?
Yes. The Torah is read on Monday and Thursday mornings too, and a weekday aliyah lets him wear his tefillin at the same service. Ask the Rabbi about the timing.

### Does my son have to read from the Torah himself?
No. Saying the blessings is the aliyah. Reading the portion himself is a beautiful extra if he wishes.

### Why are tefillin not worn on Shabbat?
Tefillin are a sign between G-d and the Jewish people, and Shabbat and the holidays are themselves such a sign, so tefillin are not worn on those days.
