The Star of David, called the Magen David in Hebrew, is the six-pointed star made of two overlapping triangles that has become the best-known symbol of the Jewish people. It is not a mitzvah or a holy object, but over the centuries it has become a proud sign of Jewish identity around the world.

## What the name means

Magen David (מָגֵן דָּוִד) means "Shield of David." It refers to King David, the warrior king and sweet singer of Israel, and to the idea that G-d is the true shield who protected him. In the third blessing after the Haftarah reading on Shabbat, we still praise G-d as "Magen David," the Shield of David.

## Where it comes from

The six-pointed star is not described in the Torah or the Talmud. It appears as a decoration in some ancient and medieval Jewish settings, alongside many other designs. Over time, especially in Central Europe in the late Middle Ages, Jewish communities began using it on flags, prayer books and synagogue buildings. By the 1800s it was widely recognized as the Jewish symbol, in the way the cross became the symbol of Christianity.

During the Holocaust, the Nazis forced Jews to wear a yellow star as a badge of shame. Jews turned it back into a badge of honor, and today it appears on the flag of Israel, on synagogues, on jewelry and on Jewish gravestones.

## What it symbolizes

Because it is a tradition rather than a law, there are many beautiful explanations of its meaning:

- **G-d everywhere.** The six points face up, down and in all four directions, with the center in the middle. Together they suggest G-d's rule over every part of creation.
- **Heaven and earth.** One triangle points up, like our efforts to reach G-d. The other points down, like G-d's blessing reaching us. The two are woven together.
- **Seven.** Six points plus the center make seven, like the six days of creation and Shabbat in the middle that gives them meaning.
- **Kabbalah.** Some Kabbalistic writings connect the star's shape to the spiritual attributes described in [Kabbalah](/learn/kabbalah).

## How Jews use it today

- **Jewelry.** Many people wear a Magen David necklace as a quiet statement of Jewish [pride](/learn/jewish-pride).
- **Synagogues and Jewish homes.** It is used in decoration, on ark curtains and on challah covers.
- **Gravestones.** It often marks a Jewish grave.

A Magen David is a lovely way to show who you are. But the Jewish tradition teaches that the strongest "shield" is doing a mitzvah: lighting [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles), putting on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin), giving [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).

## Common questions

### Is the Star of David mentioned in the Bible?
No. The Torah never describes a six-pointed star as a Jewish symbol. The name "Shield of David" comes from Jewish prayer, and the symbol itself became widely used much later.

### Is it okay for a Jew to wear a Star of David?
Yes. It is a fine way to express Jewish identity. It is not a holy object, so there is no special blessing and no special rule for wearing it, though it is respectful not to bring it into a bathroom when it bears Hebrew words or Torah verses.

### What is the difference between a Star of David and a mezuzah?
A Magen David is a symbol. A [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) is a mitzvah: a handwritten scroll of Torah verses placed on the doorpost. A mezuzah case with a star on it is only kosher if the scroll inside is kosher.

### Can a non-Jew wear a Star of David?
Many people wear it as a sign of friendship and support for the Jewish people. Since it is a symbol of Jewish identity, it is best to wear it with that respect in mind.
