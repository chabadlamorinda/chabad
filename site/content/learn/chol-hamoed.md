Chol HaMoed (חוֹל הַמּוֹעֵד, "the weekday of the festival") are the in-between days of Passover and Sukkot, between the first and last days of each holiday: they are still part of the holiday, but are not full Yom Tov days, so ordinary activities like driving and cooking are allowed while non-essential work is avoided. It is half holiday, half weekday, a time for family, outings and festive meals.

## What kind of days are these?

Passover and Sukkot each begin and end with full Yom Tov days (no work, candle lighting, festive meals). The days in the middle are Chol HaMoed. The Torah calls the whole festival a time of rejoicing, so we keep the spirit of the holiday: we eat matzah on Passover, sit in the sukkah and shake the lulav and etrog on Sukkot, say special additions in the prayers, and read from the Torah. The last day of Sukkot's Chol HaMoed is [Hoshana Rabbah](/learn/hoshana-rabbah). See [Passover for beginners](/learn/passover-for-beginners) and [Sukkot for beginners](/learn/sukkot-for-beginners).

Shabbat can fall during Chol HaMoed. It is then a regular Shabbat with additions for the festival.

## What is allowed

- **Cooking, eating, drinking and preparing for the holiday.** Festive meals are a mitzvah.
- **Driving and traveling for family outings.** Visits to parks, museums and relatives are encouraged. See [Passover with kids](/learn/passover-with-kids) and [Sukkot with kids](/learn/sukkot-with-kids).
- **Work that is needed for the holiday itself,** for the needs of the poor, or to prevent a real financial loss.
- **Work that cannot be done at another time,** if you would otherwise suffer a serious loss.
- **Emergencies,** which always override.

Many Jews also go to work on Chol HaMoed because of their jobs. Jewish law has detailed rules about what kind of work is permitted. If your work is a concern, ask the Rabbi.

## What to avoid

The sages limited work on Chol HaMoed so that the days remain a holiday. Customs and rules differ in details, so these are the general guidelines:

- Non-urgent business and regular work that is not needed.
- Haircuts, shaving and cutting nails (many do these before the holiday).
- Laundry, unless needed (for children's clothes, for instance).
- Buying and selling that is not for the holiday.
- Big home projects, painting or repairs that can wait.

If you are unsure, ask the Rabbi. It is better to ask than to guess.

## Making the days special

- **Festive meals** with wine and the best foods, and guests.
- **New outings,** even a small one, every day.
- **For children,** a treat or a game, so that each day feels different from the weekday.
- **For adults,** time to learn, to visit family, to take a walk after a long winter.
- **In the sukkah** on Sukkot, shake the lulav and etrog every weekday, but not on Shabbat. See [the lulav and etrog form](/forms/lulav-etrog).

## Tefillin on Chol HaMoed

Chabad custom is not to put on tefillin on Chol HaMoed. Other communities put them on. This difference is long standing. See [tefillin](/learn/tefillin), and ask the Rabbi if you have any doubts.

## Common questions

### What is Chol HaMoed?
Chol HaMoed is the in-between days of Passover and Sukkot, which are still part of the holiday but are not full Yom Tov days, so some activities are allowed that are not on Yom Tov.

### Can you work on Chol HaMoed?
Work needed for the holiday or to prevent a serious loss is allowed, but regular, non-essential work is avoided. Ask the Rabbi about your situation.

### Can you drive on Chol HaMoed?
Yes. Driving for holiday needs and family outings is generally permitted, unlike on Yom Tov. Ask the Rabbi if you have a particular question.

### Can you cut your hair or do laundry on Chol HaMoed?
These are generally avoided, with some exceptions such as laundry for children. Ask the Rabbi.

### Do you wear tefillin on Chol HaMoed?
Chabad custom is not to wear tefillin on Chol HaMoed. Other communities do. Ask the Rabbi.
