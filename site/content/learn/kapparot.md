Kapparot (in Yiddish, "shlogn kapores") is an old custom done in the days before Yom Kippur, traditionally early in the morning of the day before Yom Kippur. A person holds a chicken, or money set aside for charity, circles it over their head while saying a short prayer, and then the chicken or the money goes to help the poor. It is a moving way to begin the holiest day of the year with thoughts of teshuvah and kindness.

## What the custom means

Kapparot is a vivid reminder. As we hold the chicken or the coins, we think: this is what I might deserve for my mistakes, but G-d in His kindness accepts my return instead. The custom is not magic and does not replace Yom Kippur itself. Its purpose is to move the heart to [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah), returning to G-d, and to open the day with [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah), which our Sages say has the power to protect and bring blessing.

## With a chicken

The custom recorded in the Shulchan Aruch and kept in Chabad is to use a chicken: a rooster for a male and a hen for a female. Customs differ for a pregnant woman; ask the Rabbi.

- The chicken is then slaughtered by a qualified shochet, in the kosher way.
- The chicken, or its value in money, is given to the poor.
- The animal must be treated gently at every step. Causing an animal needless distress is forbidden in Jewish law ([tzaar baalei chayim](/learn/tzaar-baalei-chayim)).

## With money

Many people do kapparot with money instead, and this is a common and accepted practice. You hold the money, say the same prayer with the words adjusted for money, and then give it to tzedakah. There is no fixed amount; some give the value of a chicken for each person. Parents often do kapparot for young children, holding the money or chicken over the child's head.

## How it is done

1. **Begin with verses** from Psalms and Job that start "Bnei adam, yoshvei choshech vetzalmavet," "Children of man who sit in darkness and the shadow of death" (Psalms 107:10). They are printed in the siddur and in Yom Kippur prayer books.
2. **Circle the chicken or the money** over your head while saying the main prayer. The verses and the circling are done three times in all.
3. **The words** (for money) are: "Zeh chalifati, zeh temurati, zeh kaparati. Zeh hakesef yelech litzedakah, va'ani ekanes ve'elech lechayim tovim aruchim ulshalom." This means: "This is my exchange, this is my substitute, this is my atonement. This money will go to charity, and I will enter and go on to a good, long life and to peace."
4. **With a chicken**, the words say "this rooster" or "this hen will go to its death" in place of the money going to charity.
5. **Give the tzedakah**, or give the chicken or its value to the poor.

The exact wording for a man, a woman, or someone doing it for others is in the siddur.

## Common questions

### Is it okay to do kapparot with money?
Yes. Doing kapparot with money and giving it to tzedakah is a common and accepted practice.

### When is kapparot done?
Traditionally early on the morning of the day before Yom Kippur. It may also be done earlier during the Ten Days of Repentance.

### Can I do kapparot for my children?
Yes. Parents may do it for young children. Older children can say the words themselves.

### Does kapparot replace Yom Kippur?
No. It prepares the heart for Yom Kippur. The atonement of the day comes through [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah), prayer and the fast itself. See the [Yom Kippur fast](/learn/yom-kippur-fast).
