After a Bar or Bat Mitzvah, a Jewish teen is responsible for keeping the mitzvot: a boy counts toward a minyan and wears tefillin on weekday mornings, a girl lights Shabbat candles and takes on her own mitzvot, and both are accountable for Shabbat, fasting and their daily choices. This is the start of an active Jewish life, not a graduation from it.

## What actually changed

The change happened on the Hebrew birthday, whether or not there was a party. From that day, the teen is a Jewish adult in religious terms: responsible for mitzvot and for his or her own choices. The earlier years of mitzvot were preparation. Read [Bar Mitzvah](/learn/bar-mitzvah) and [Bat Mitzvah](/learn/bat-mitzvah) for the meaning of the day.

By the letter of the law, a boy does not have to repent for things done before he became Bar Mitzvah, but it is proper for him to take on some form of teshuvah (return and improvement) for youthful mistakes. For most families this is a quiet good resolution rather than anything dramatic.

## For a boy

- **Tefillin each weekday morning.** After starting about two months before, he keeps it going. Read [Tefillin](/learn/tefillin) and [starting tefillin before the Bar Mitzvah](/learn/starting-tefillin-before-bar-mitzvah). Keep the tefillin checked; see [checking tefillin](/learn/checking-mezuzahs-and-tefillin).
- **He counts in a minyan,** the quorum of ten adult Jews for communal prayer. See [minyan](/learn/minyan). That means a congregation can wait for him, and that is a real responsibility.
- **He can be called to the Torah** for an aliyah and can lead parts of the prayers. See [aliyah](/learn/aliyah).
- **Prayer and learning.** A daily prayer, even a short one, and regular study; see [prayer](/learn/prayer).

## For a girl

- **Shabbat candles.** Lighting Shabbat candles, separating challah and prayer are among the mitzvot a girl takes on. Girls can begin lighting at about age three; one who has not may start on the Shabbat closest to her Bat Mitzvah. See [girls lighting Shabbat candles](/learn/girls-lighting-shabbat-candles) and [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).
- **A personal mitzvah** such as daily prayer, charity or acts of kindness.
- **Learning** why the mitzvot matter. See [Bat Mitzvah](/learn/bat-mitzvah).

## For both

- **Shabbat and kosher.** Keeping [Shabbat](/learn/shabbat) and [kosher](/learn/kosher-kitchen) are now the teen's own mitzvot, not only the parents'. Ask the Rabbi how to take them on in a way that fits your family.
- **Fasting.** Children are gradually trained to fast, and the stricter view is for them to complete the Yom Kippur fast in the years before. Health always comes first: a young person who cannot fast safely should not. After Bar or Bat Mitzvah, the full fasts apply, with the usual health exceptions. Ask the Rabbi about your child, and see [Yom Kippur fast](/learn/yom-kippur-fast) and [fast days](/learn/fast-days).
- **Tzedakah** that the teen gives on his or her own. See [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).

## Staying connected

Many teens drift after the party, which is natural. A few ways to keep the door open:

1. **Keep one fixed habit,** such as tefillin or a Friday-night candle.
2. **Come to Shabbat.** See [Shabbat in Lamorinda](/learn/shabbat-in-lamorinda) for the weekly table.
3. **Find friends.** Check [Jewish teens in Lamorinda](/learn/jewish-teens-in-lamorinda), the [kids and teens program](/programs/kids-and-teens) and [events](/events), and call to hear what is planned.
4. **Continue the mitzvah project** the family began.
5. **Talk with the Rabbi.** Teens often ask better questions than adults.

## Common questions

### What does a boy have to do after his Bar Mitzvah?
He puts on tefillin on weekday mornings, counts in a minyan, and keeps all the mitzvot as an adult. Ask the Rabbi to help him take on each step.

### What changes after a Bat Mitzvah?
A girl becomes responsible for the mitzvot, including Shabbat candles, challah and prayer. She also chooses a personal mitzvah to grow in.

### Does my teen have to fast on Yom Kippur now?
After Bar or Bat Mitzvah the fast applies, unless health prevents it. Ask the Rabbi about your child's health and preparation.

### Do we have to keep Shabbat after the Bar Mitzvah?
Shabbat is now the teen's own obligation. Families grow at different speeds. Ask the Rabbi for a first step that fits your home.

### What if my teen loses interest after the party?
That is common. Keep one small habit, find friends and stay in touch with the Rabbi. Call 925-384-7242.

### Can a Bar or Bat Mitzvah teen lead a prayer?
A boy can lead parts of the service. Ask the Rabbi what suits your teen and the community.
