The Talmud names seven women as prophetesses of the Jewish people: Sarah, Miriam, Deborah, Hannah, Abigail, Huldah and Esther. A prophet is someone who received a message from G-d for the people. These seven women show the courage, wisdom and faith of Jewish women throughout history.

## Who were the seven prophetesses?

Chabad.org presents the seven, drawing on the Talmud (Megillah 14a). Here is a short card on each.

### 1. Sarah

Sarah was Abraham's partner in teaching the world about one G-d, and tradition says she surpassed him in prophecy. Chabad.org tells of the miracles in her tent: a cloud of glory, Shabbat candles that burned all week, and bread that stayed fresh. **Lesson:** a Jewish home, with its light, is holy. See [Abraham and Sarah](/learn/abraham-and-sarah).

### 2. Miriam

Miriam foresaw that her parents would have a son who would redeem Israel, and she encouraged them when they lost hope. After the sea split, she led the women in song, and a well traveled with the people in the desert in her merit. **Lesson:** faith in the future. See [Miriam](/learn/miriam).

### 3. Deborah

A judge who sat under a palm tree and led Israel to victory with Barak. **Lesson:** leadership and gratitude. See [Deborah](/learn/deborah).

### 4. Hannah (Chanah)

Hannah had no children and prayed from the heart in the Tabernacle. Chabad.org notes that many laws of Jewish prayer come from her silent prayer. Her son was the prophet Samuel. **Lesson:** the power of heartfelt prayer. See [Hannah and her prayer](/learn/hannah-and-her-prayer).

### 5. Abigail

A wise woman married to a harsh man, Nabal. When Nabal refused King David's men, Abigail brought food and gently persuaded David not to take revenge. **Lesson:** wisdom and peace can stop violence.

### 6. Huldah

When a Torah scroll was found during repairs to the Temple, King Josiah sent to ask Huldah what it meant. She delivered G-d's message honestly, including a warning, and also a promise for the humble king. **Lesson:** speak the truth, with respect.

### 7. Esther

A queen who used her position to save her people from Haman's plan. Chabad.org notes that she is the only woman to whom a book of the Bible is attributed as author. **Lesson:** speak up when your people need you. See [the Purim story](/learn/purim-story) and [the Megillah](/learn/megillah).

## Why are there seven?

The Sages count seven women whose prophecies are part of the Bible. Chabad.org notes that this shows that the highest spiritual level was open to women. Jewish teaching also says that women in each generation have a special strength of faith. See [Jewish women](/learn/jewish-women) and [the Matriarchs](/learn/the-matriarchs).

## What they teach girls and women today

- **Faith:** Sarah and Miriam trusted in G-d's future.
- **Prayer:** Hannah.
- **Leadership:** Deborah, Esther.
- **Wisdom:** Abigail.
- **Courage to speak:** Huldah.

These are good stories to share with a daughter, especially before a [Bat Mitzvah](/learn/bat-mitzvah).

## Common questions

### Who are the seven prophetesses in Judaism?
They are Sarah, Miriam, Deborah, Hannah, Abigail, Huldah and Esther, according to the Talmud.

### Were there really female prophets in the Bible?
Yes. The Talmud counts seven, whose stories appear in the Torah and the Prophets.

### Which prophetess was a judge?
Deborah, the only woman among the judges of Israel.

### Which prophetess is credited with a book of the Bible?
Esther, whose Megillah is read on Purim.

### Why is Hannah important for prayer?
Many laws of prayer, such as praying quietly with the lips moving, are learned from Hannah's prayer.
