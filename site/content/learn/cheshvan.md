Cheshvan is the eighth month of the Jewish year, counting from Nissan, and the second counting from Tishrei. It comes right after Tishrei, the month packed with Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot and Simchat Torah, and it usually falls in October and November. It is the only month of the year with no holidays and no fast days at all.

## Why "Mar Cheshvan"?

The month is often called **Mar Cheshvan**. One explanation is that "mar" means bitter: after the joy of Tishrei, a month with no holidays can feel a little flat. Another explanation is that "mar" also means a drop of water, as in the verse "like a drop from a bucket," because Cheshvan is when the rainy season begins in the Land of Israel.

## Taking Tishrei home

Chassidic teaching sees Cheshvan as the real test of Tishrei. The holidays filled us with inspiration; Cheshvan is when we bring it into ordinary days: work, school, family. The true measure of a holiday is what we do the day after. A small new habit taken on now, like a daily mitzvah or a weekly class, is how Tishrei lasts all year.

## Days to know in Cheshvan

- **7 Cheshvan:** in the Land of Israel, the prayer for rain is added to the weekday Amidah. Outside Israel it starts in early December. See [prayers for rain](/learn/prayers-for-rain).
- **11 Cheshvan:** the yahrzeit of **Rachel our Mother**. Many people visit her tomb near Bethlehem, give tzedakah and pray for their families, remembering how Rachel "weeps for her children" and G-d promises that they will return.
- **17 Cheshvan:** the day the Flood began in the time of Noah. A year later, on **27 Cheshvan**, the earth was dry and Noah's family left the ark.
- **20 Cheshvan:** the birthday of the fifth Chabad Rebbe, Rabbi Sholom DovBer of Lubavitch (the Rebbe Rashab), founder of the Tomchei Temimim yeshivas.

## The month waiting for its holiday

King Solomon finished building the first Holy Temple in Cheshvan, but it was dedicated only eleven months later, in Tishrei. The Midrash says that Cheshvan was "promised" a celebration of its own: when Moshiach comes and the third Temple is built, its joy will belong to Cheshvan. So even the quietest month is waiting for the greatest celebration.

## Common questions

### When is Cheshvan this year?
Cheshvan begins about a month after Rosh Hashanah, usually in October. See our [Jewish calendar](/calendar) for this year's dates.

### Is there anything special to do in Cheshvan?
Keep the good resolutions of the holidays going. Add a mitzvah, join a class, or [do a mitzvah this week](/mitzvah).

### Why does Cheshvan sometimes have 29 days and sometimes 30?
Cheshvan and Kislev are the two months whose length changes from year to year, so that the Jewish holidays fall on the right days of the week.
