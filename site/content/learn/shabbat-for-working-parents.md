Between work, school, sports and everything else, Friday can feel like the busiest day of the week. Yet many busy families find that Shabbat is exactly what they need: one day each week when the phones go away and the family sits together. You do not need to do everything at once. Here is how to make it work.

## Start with one ritual

Pick one thing and keep it every week:

- **Light [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles)** before sunset on Friday.
- **Say [Kiddush](/learn/kiddush)** over wine or grape juice and eat challah together.
- **A family Friday-night dinner,** with phones off.
- **Bless the children** before the meal.

When one ritual feels natural, add another. See [Shabbat for Beginners](/learn/shabbat-for-beginners).

## Plan ahead

- **Check the time.** Candle lighting changes every week. Look it up on [our Shabbat times page](/shabbat) early in the week.
- **Shop by Thursday.** Keep a standing Shabbat shopping list.
- **Cook ahead.** Make a big pot of soup or a main dish on Thursday night, or cook double one week and freeze half.
- **Set the table early,** even Friday morning before work. It sets the mood for the whole day.

## Keep the menu simple

Shabbat food does not have to be elaborate. A good Shabbat meal can be challah, a soup, one main dish and a salad. Rotating three or four menus saves a lot of thinking. Store-bought challah and dessert are perfectly fine. See [Shabbat Meals](/learn/shabbat-meals).

## Getting home on time

In winter, Shabbat can begin before 5 PM. Some ideas:

- **Talk to your employer** early about leaving on time on Fridays; many are understanding when asked in advance.
- **Block your Friday afternoon calendar** so meetings do not run late.
- **Shift tasks to Thursday** whenever you can.
- **Have a "last call" time** for errands, after which everything waits until after Shabbat.

See [Shabbat in Winter](/learn/shabbat-in-winter) and [Jewish Holidays at School and Work](/learn/jewish-holidays-at-school-and-work).

## Involve the kids

Children love jobs: setting the table, putting coins in the [pushka](/learn/pushka) before candle lighting, braiding challah, choosing a song. Ask each child to share one good thing from their week. More ideas in [Shabbat With Kids](/learn/shabbat-with-kids).

## Put the devices away

Many families say the biggest gift of Shabbat is a break from screens. Choose a spot, such as a basket or a drawer, where phones go before candle lighting. Even parents who are not yet keeping every detail of Shabbat often find that this one change transforms the evening.

## Common questions

### We cannot keep all of Shabbat yet. Is it still worth starting?
Yes. Every mitzvah counts on its own. Lighting candles or having a family dinner is a real and meaningful step.

### What if I get home after candle lighting time?
Candles must be lit before sunset; they are not lit after Shabbat has begun. Plan around the time, and ask the Rabbi about your situation.

### How do we handle Friday-night sports or events?
Many families talk to coaches and organizers early. Every family's situation is different; the Rabbi is glad to think it through with you.
