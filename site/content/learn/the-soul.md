Judaism teaches that every person has a soul, a spiritual life force that gives us awareness, feelings and purpose. The Jewish soul, called the *neshamah*, is described as "a part of G-d above," eternal and pure, which came into this world on a mission.

## A part of G-d

The Torah says G-d "breathed into his nostrils the breath of life" (Genesis 2:7). The Tanya, the foundational book of Chabad Chassidus, explains that when you breathe out, the breath comes from deep inside you. In the same way, the soul comes from G-d's innermost self. It is, in the Tanya's words, "truly a part of G-d above."

That is why the soul can never be lost or destroyed. Every morning we say: "My G-d, the soul You placed within me is pure."

## Five levels of the soul

Jewish mystical teaching describes five levels of the soul, from the most connected to the body to the most connected to G-d:

1. **Nefesh:** the life force that animates the body.
2. **Ruach:** the spirit, the source of emotions.
3. **Neshamah:** the breath of intellect and understanding.
4. **Chayah:** the "living" level, beyond understanding, which senses G-d.
5. **Yechidah:** the "single" essence, completely one with G-d.

The *yechidah* is the core of the soul. It is the source of a Jew's deep, unbreakable bond with G-d, which can show itself suddenly in moments of great love, sacrifice or need.

## Two souls

The [Tanya](/learn/tanya) teaches that each Jew has two souls:

- **The G-dly soul,** which longs to connect with G-d, do good and grow.
- **The animal soul,** which drives our natural needs and desires, and focuses on the self.

Life is a gentle struggle between them. The goal is not to crush the animal soul, but to guide it and refine it, so that our whole self, body included, serves a higher purpose.

## Why the soul came down

Before birth, the soul lived in a spiritual world close to G-d. Why would it come down into a body, with all its limits and struggles? Chassidus explains that this descent is for the sake of a greater ascent. Through Torah, prayer and mitzvot, the soul brings G-dliness into the physical world, making it a home for G-d. That is something it could never do in heaven.

Every person has a unique mission that only they can fulfill.

## Caring for your soul

- **Torah study** is food for the soul.
- **Mitzvot** are its garments, the way it expresses itself in the world.
- **Prayer** is its moment of connection and closeness.
- **Kindness and joy** let the soul shine.

## Common questions

### What happens to the soul after death?
The soul lives on and returns to its Source, where it continues to grow. Read more in our guide to the [afterlife](/learn/afterlife).

### Do non-Jews have souls?
Yes. Every human being is created in the image of G-d and has a soul. Jewish mystical texts describe the Jewish soul as having a particular mission connected to the Torah and its mitzvot.

### What is a "pintele Yid"?
It is Yiddish for "the little point of a Jew." It describes the spark of Jewishness inside every Jew that never goes out, no matter how distant a person may feel.

### Can a soul be damaged by mistakes?
The core of the soul always remains pure. Mistakes can cover it, but [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah), returning to G-d, can always uncover its light.
