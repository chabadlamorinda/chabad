Here are 18 Chanukah facts that surprise even people who have lit the menorah all their lives. Chanukah is not mentioned in the Bible, potato latkes came long after cheese pancakes, and the holiday can begin on any day of the week except Tuesday.

## The story and the Sages

1. **Eight nights, one flask.** The Talmud teaches that the Greeks defiled the Temple's oil, and only one sealed flask was found, enough for one day. It burned for eight. The Sages made these days a holiday of Hallel and thanksgiving. Seven stands for the natural world, and eight for what goes beyond it, so eight is called the number of miracles.
2. **A silent holiday.** Chanukah is not mentioned in the 24 books of the Bible, because the Men of the Great Assembly sealed the canon before the miracle. No tractate of the Talmud is devoted to it. It appears briefly in Tractate Shabbat, in a discussion of Shabbat candles.
3. **Syrians, Greeks, Hellenists or Yevanim?** These names all describe the same group. After Alexander the Great's death, the Seleucid Greek Empire, based in Syria, stationed soldiers in Judea. "Yavan" is the Hebrew form of Greece. Jews who adopted Greek ways were called Mityavnim.
4. **Were the Maccabees royalty?** No. They were Kohanim, priests, not of the royal line of David from the tribe of Judah. Their successors took the kingship, and the monarchy soon fell into power struggles. Meet [the Maccabees](/learn/the-maccabees).
5. **You light a Hillel menorah.** The House of Hillel taught adding one light each night, as we do. The House of Shammai taught starting with eight and decreasing. Tradition holds that Shammai's way will be followed when Moshiach comes. See [Hillel and Shammai](/learn/hillel-and-shammai).

## Dates and lighting

6. **Any day but Tuesday.** Most holidays begin on only four days of the week. Because the month of Cheshvan can have 29 or 30 days, Chanukah can start on any day except Tuesday. See [why Chanukah's date changes](/learn/why-chanukah-changes-dates).
7. **Light after dark.** Many communities, Chabad among them, light shortly after sunset; others light after nightfall. Either way, the menorah needs enough fuel to burn at least half an hour after nightfall, because its purpose is to bring light into the darkness and publicize the miracle.
8. **Doorway or window?** Some place the menorah at a window. Others, Chabad among them, light it inside, in a doorway. In Talmudic times it stood at the entrance to the courtyard, on the left, with the mezuzah on the right, making a doorway full of mitzvahs.
9. **Straight line.** The flames must be level, each clearly distinct, not arranged in a semicircle or unevenly. See [the Chanukah menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah).
10. **Nine flames.** The menorah holds nine flames, because the shamash, the helper light, lights the others and sits apart. Read about [the shamash](/learn/what-is-the-shamash).

## The menorah

11. **"Menorah" means lamp.** The Temple had a seven-branched gold candelabrum, kindled daily, and its oil miracle inspired the Sages to establish Chanukah.
12. **One per person or one per home.** Some Sephardic communities use one menorah per household, so the number of lit candles is visible at a glance. Ashkenazim customarily have one per person.
13. **A Modern Hebrew word.** Speakers say *hanukkiah* for the Chanukah lamp, a modern term attributed to Hemda Ben Yehudah (1873 to 1951).
14. **The Israeli dreidel.** In Israel, the dreidel's shin (ש) is replaced by pey (פ), so it says "a great miracle happened here." The four letters add up to 138, the value of *tzemach*, a name for the Messiah. See [Chanukah in Israel](/learn/chanukah-in-israel).

## Foods and the modern world

15. **Cheese before potatoes.** An older custom was cheese pancakes, recalling Judith, who fed a Greek general dairy food before saving her village. Potatoes were unknown in the Old World until the late 16th century, so potato latkes came later.
16. **Chanukah in the USSR.** Avraham Genin, a Chabad chassid who lost a foot in World War II, lit the menorah privately for most of his life. In 1991 he lit a giant menorah in the Kremlin Palace of Congresses before about 6,000 people.
17. **Chanukah in space.** In December 1993, astronaut Jeffrey Hoffman, on a mission to repair the Hubble Space Telescope, brought a dreidel and a traveling menorah. Candles were not possible in space, so he showed his supplies by live satellite, spun the dreidel in the air and wished Jews everywhere a happy Chanukah.
18. **Chanukah goes public.** In 1973, yeshivah students in Manhattan mounted a menorah on a station wagon. In 1974 a menorah was lit at Independence Hall in Philadelphia, and in 1975 a 22-foot menorah in San Francisco's Union Square. By 2016, Chabad-Lubavitch had set up more than 15,000 public menorahs, with events in more than 90 countries. Read about [public menorah lightings](/learn/public-menorah-lighting).

## Common questions

### Is Chanukah in the Bible?
No. Chanukah is not mentioned in the 24 books of the Bible, which were sealed before the miracle. The Talmud tells its story briefly.

### Why do we eat latkes on Chanukah?
Fried foods recall the oil that burned in the Temple for eight days. Jewish tradition adds that cheese pancakes, recalling Judith, were an older custom, and potato latkes came later. See [latkes](/learn/latkes).

### Can Chanukah start on any day of the week?
It can start on any day except Tuesday, because Cheshvan can have 29 or 30 days.

### Who were the Maccabees?
A family of priests, the Kohanim, who led the revolt against the Seleucid Greek rulers. They were not from the royal line of David.

### Was a menorah ever lit in space?
Not with candles, which were not possible. In 1993 astronaut Jeffrey Hoffman brought a traveling menorah and a dreidel and showed them by live satellite.

### How many public menorahs are there, and is there one near Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda?
By 2016 there were more than 15,000 public menorahs worldwide. In Lafayette, join Chabad of Lamorinda's Light Up Lafayette lighting at Lafayette Plaza Park.
