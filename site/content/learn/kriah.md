Kriah (pronounced kree-AH) means "tearing." It is one of the oldest Jewish mourning customs: a mourner tears a garment he or she is wearing, as Jacob tore his clothes when he thought Joseph had died. The tear is an outward sign of an inner loss, and a way to let grief out rather than hold it in.

## Who tears kriah?

Kriah is done by the seven close relatives who mourn: a father, mother, son, daughter, brother, sister or spouse. Both men and women tear. Women tear in a modest way, for example on an inner layer or a garment that can be pinned closed afterward.

Whether young children tear, and how, depends on their age. Ask the Rabbi.

## When is it done?

The original time for kriah is at the moment of death or when the news arrives. Today it is usually done just before the funeral, when the family is together and the Rabbi can guide them.

- Kriah is done **standing**, as a sign of strength in the face of loss.
- It is **not done on Shabbat or a Jewish holiday.** If a funeral follows a holiday, the Rabbi will explain what to do.

## Which side?

- **For a parent:** on the **left** side, over the heart, on the front of the garment near the collar.
- **For any other relative:** on the **right** side.

The tear is vertical, starting from the neckline, about a handbreadth (roughly three to four inches) long. Often the Rabbi makes a small cut to start it, and the mourner tears the rest by hand. For a parent, the tear is made by hand.

Many people have seen a black ribbon pinned on and cut instead. The traditional practice, and the one we follow, is to tear an actual garment, such as a shirt, sweater or blouse.

## The blessing

At the time of kriah, the mourner says the blessing that Jews say on hearing of a death:

**Baruch atah Ado-nai Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, dayan ha'emet.**
"Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, the true Judge."

It is a hard blessing to say. It expresses that even when we cannot understand, we trust that G-d is just. The Rabbi will say it with you, word by word.

## How long is it worn?

The torn garment is worn throughout [shiva](/learn/shiva), except on Shabbat, when mourners change into Shabbat clothes. After shiva, the tear may be loosely basted for other relatives and fully repaired after the 30 days of [sheloshim](/learn/sheloshim). For a parent, the custom is never to sew it back neatly. The details depend on the situation, so ask the Rabbi.

## Common questions

### What should I wear to the funeral if I will tear kriah?
Wear something you are comfortable tearing and wearing for the week, such as a regular shirt, sweater or blouse. Avoid a good jacket or anything you will miss.

### What if I heard the news late and missed the funeral?
Kriah is still done within the first 30 days. After 30 days, it is done only for a parent. Call the Rabbi and he will guide you.

### Do I need to tear a new garment if I change clothes during shiva?
For a parent, there are customs about tearing again; for other relatives, generally not. Customs vary, so ask the Rabbi.

### Is kriah done for a relative who was not Jewish?
This is a sensitive question with different answers in different cases. Please speak with the Rabbi privately.
