Shmita (שמיטה, also spelled shemitah) is the Sabbatical year: every seventh year the Land of Israel rests from farming, and personal loans are released. Chabad.org counts the next shmita as the year 5789, from September 20, 2028 to September 9, 2029.

## What shmita means

Shmita means "release." The Torah commands: "Six years you shall sow your field... but in the seventh year the land shall have a Sabbath" (Leviticus 25). Jews began counting the cycles once they settled the Land of Israel, and each cycle begins on Rosh Hashanah in the month of Tishrei. These laws apply to the Land of Israel. For a picture of the weekly rest, read about [Shabbat](/learn/shabbat).

## The two parts of shmita

1. **Release of the land.** Farmers stop sowing, pruning and working their fields. Owners give up ownership of what grows by itself, and anyone may take it. It becomes common property, so rich and poor share it.
2. **Release of debts.** The Torah tells creditors to release the hand from loans to fellow Jews. Because this could discourage lending, the Sages established the *pruzbul*, a legal arrangement that lets loans continue. Ask the Rabbi if you want to know how it works in practice.

The year after seven cycles, the fiftieth, was the Jubilee (Yovel). It is not observed today.

## Why? Six reasons

Chabad.org explains six reasons that Torah scholars give for shmita:

- **Rest for the soil,** a break for the land itself.
- **A year-long Shabbat,** reminding us that G-d created the world and rested.
- **Making up for Shabbats** the fields did not rest in the previous six years.
- **Faith and humility.** After years of effort, we remember that success comes from G-d, who owns the land.
- **Unity and generosity.** Owners share what grows. The same idea guides us in [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).
- **Liberation.** Trusting G-d lifts the weight of worry. Shmita also hints at the peace of the Messianic age.

## The promise of trust

The Torah promises a blessing in the sixth year that will produce enough for three years. A farmer who stopped working his fields was making a statement of faith. Faith like this is called *bitachon*, as in [Bitachon: trust in G-d](/learn/bitachon). The year also let the nation focus on spiritual pursuits, and the synagogues and study halls filled.

## Shmita for those who are not farmers

Chabad.org teaches that its lessons still speak to most Jews, who are not farmers. We can use the year to focus more on our spiritual mission and on faith, and less on material pursuits and personal talents. In Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, where careers and schedules are full, a "Shabbat for the year" is a good reminder to pause, to give and to trust. Shmita also reminds us how deeply the Land is tied to our people; read [Israel and the Jewish people](/learn/israel-and-the-jewish-people).

## Common questions

### What is shmita?
Shmita is the Sabbatical year, every seventh year, when the Land of Israel is left to rest from farming and loans between Jews are released.

### When is the next shmita?
By Chabad.org's count, the next shmita is 5789, from September 20, 2028 to September 9, 2029.

### Does shmita apply outside Israel?
The land laws apply in the Land of Israel. Its lessons of rest and trust can guide Jews everywhere. Ask the Rabbi about the details.

### What is a pruzbul?
A pruzbul is a legal document from the Sages that allows creditors to collect loans after shmita. Ask the Rabbi for how it is done.

### Why do Jews keep shmita?
It is a commandment of the Torah, and its purposes include trust in G-d, humility, generosity and spiritual focus.

### What happens to the crops during shmita?
Crops that grow on their own are ownerless and free for anyone to take.
