The Mourner's Kaddish is a short prayer recited by mourners in memory of a loved one. Remarkably, it never mentions death: it is a declaration praising G-d, and saying it brings great merit and elevation to the soul of the person who passed away.

## What does Kaddish mean?

Kaddish means "holy" or "sanctification." It is written mostly in Aramaic, the everyday language of Jews in Talmudic times, so that everyone could understand it. It opens with the words:

**Yitgadal v'yitkadash shmei rabba**
"May His great name be exalted and sanctified."

The congregation responds with the central line: **"Amen. Yehei shmei rabba mevarach l'alam ul'almei almaya"** ("May His great name be blessed forever and ever").

## Why say a prayer that praises G-d?

When someone dies, it can feel like the world has a little less light. By standing up in public and praising G-d at a moment of loss, the mourner affirms faith and adds holiness to the world. Jewish tradition teaches that this brings merit to the soul of the departed and helps it ascend to higher levels in heaven.

## Who says Kaddish?

- **Children** say Kaddish for a parent. Traditionally, sons have this obligation.
- **Other mourners** (spouse, sibling, child) may say it as well.
- **If there is no one to say it,** another person can be asked or hired to say it on the family's behalf.

## How long is Kaddish said?

- **For a parent:** 11 months from the day of death. It is not said for the full 12 months, because tradition teaches that only the very wicked need a full year of elevation, and we assume our parents did not.
- **For other relatives:** 30 days (shloshim).
- **Every year after:** on the [yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit), the anniversary of the death.

## When and where is Kaddish said?

Kaddish requires a minyan, a quorum of ten Jewish men over age 13. It is said during the three daily prayer services: morning (Shacharit), afternoon (Mincha) and evening (Maariv). Mourners stand while reciting it.

If it is hard to get to a minyan, do not worry. There are other ways to honor your loved one, such as learning Torah, giving tzedakah, and doing mitzvot in their memory.

## Common questions

### Can a woman say Kaddish?

Practices vary among communities. A daughter's love and prayers bring great merit to a parent's soul in many ways, including giving tzedakah, lighting candles and studying Torah. Ask the Rabbi for guidance on your situation.

### What if I do not read Hebrew?

Kaddish is usually printed in transliteration in prayer books. You can also read along slowly. Practice helps, and no one expects perfection.

### Why do I need a minyan to say Kaddish?

Kaddish is a public sanctification of G-d's name, and Jewish law defines a public gathering as ten adult men. Without a minyan, Kaddish is not said.

### Can Kaddish be said for a non-Jewish relative?

This is a sensitive question with different answers in different cases. Please speak with the Rabbi privately. There are always meaningful ways to honor a loved one's memory.
