Judaism sees women as spiritual equals to men, with a unique and essential role in Jewish life. Jewish women have shaped the Jewish people from the very beginning, and Jewish identity itself passes through the mother.

## Equal souls, different roles

The Torah teaches that both man and woman were created "in the image of G-d." Men and women have equally holy souls. Their roles in mitzvot are sometimes different, like two instruments in an orchestra, each essential to the music.

Jewish teaching holds that women were given an extra measure of binah, intuitive understanding. This is one reason a girl becomes [bat mitzvah](/learn/bat-mitzvah) at 12, a year earlier than a boy.

## The mitzvot of Jewish women

Women are obligated in most mitzvot, including all of the Torah's "do not" commandments, Shabbat, kosher, prayer, and many more. Generally, women are exempt from certain time-bound positive mitzvot, such as tefillin or hearing the shofar at a set time, partly in recognition of their essential role in the home and family.

Three mitzvot are especially associated with Jewish women:

1. **Lighting [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles):** bringing light and peace into the home each Friday evening.
2. **Separating [challah](/learn/challah):** taking a small piece of dough when baking bread, recalling the gift given to the Kohanim.
3. **Family purity:** the laws of [mikvah](/learn/mikvah), which bring holiness to marriage.

## The Jewish home

The Torah describes the home as a mikdash me'at, a "miniature sanctuary." In Jewish thought, the home, not the synagogue, is the center of Jewish life. Kosher food, Shabbat dinners, holidays and the values children absorb are all shaped at home. Jewish tradition gives the woman of the house, the akeret habayit, the leading role in creating this atmosphere.

## Inspiring women in Jewish history

- **Sarah, Rebecca, Rachel and Leah:** the matriarchs, whose faith built the Jewish people.
- **Miriam:** sister of Moses, who led the women in song at the Red Sea.
- **The women in Egypt:** the Talmud says the Exodus happened in the merit of righteous women.
- **Devorah:** a prophetess and judge who led Israel.
- **Ruth:** a convert whose loyalty made her the ancestor of King David.
- **Esther:** the queen who saved the Jewish people, celebrated on Purim.

The sages also teach that the final redemption will come in the merit of righteous women.

## Women and Torah study

Jewish women study Torah, especially the laws they need to know and the inner meaning of Judaism. [The Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe) strongly encouraged Torah learning for women and girls, including the deep teachings of Chassidus.

## Common questions

### Why do women and men sit separately in synagogue?

A mechitzah (partition) helps everyone focus on prayer rather than on social interaction. It reflects the holiness of the space, not a difference in value.

### Do women need to pray?

Yes. Women are obligated to pray daily, though the requirements differ somewhat from men's. Ask the Rabbi or Rebbetzin for guidance on what to say.

### Why do girls light Shabbat candles?

The Rebbe encouraged girls from about age three to light their own candle every Friday before their mother, bringing more light into the world.

### Is there a special holiday for women?

[Rosh Chodesh](/learn/rosh-chodesh), the start of each Hebrew month, is traditionally considered a special day for women.
