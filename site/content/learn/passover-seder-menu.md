A simple Passover Seder menu is charoset, chicken soup, a make-ahead brisket, a couple of vegetable sides and fruit for dessert, all prepared before the holiday begins and all kosher for Passover. Most of it can be made days ahead, so the host can sit at the Seder table and enjoy it.

This plan is for first-time hosts in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda. Start with [Hosting Your First Seder](/learn/hosting-your-first-seder) and the [Passover Shopping List](/learn/passover-shopping-list), then use this menu to fill in the meal.

## First, three Passover rules for the kitchen

- **Everything needs to be kosher for Passover.** Read the labels on every packaged item, including sauces, spices, wine and vinegar. See [Kosher for Passover](/learn/kosher-for-passover). For a doubtful item, ask the Rabbi.
- **No kitniyot.** Ashkenazi Jews, including Chabad, do not eat rice, corn, beans, lentils and similar foods on Passover. See [Kitniyot](/learn/kitniyot). Follow your family custom.
- **Chabad custom: no gebrokts.** Chabad avoids matzah that has been soaked in liquid, so matzah meal and matzah balls are not used. If your family eats them, that is its own custom; ask the Rabbi what is right for you.

## Charoset: the sweet paste

Charoset (חרוסת) is a sweet mixture that recalls the mortar and bricks of Egyptian slavery. At the Seder the bitter herb is dipped into it and shaken off before eating. Chabad.org gives a simple recipe:

- 1 red apple and 1 pear, peeled and finely diced
- 1 cup chopped or ground walnuts
- ½ cup sweet red wine

Stir everything together, chill it, and add a little more wine just before serving. For a nut-free version, use 1 cup of desiccated coconut with ½ cup raisins in place of the walnuts. For a smoother paste, pulse it in a food processor. It is a good idea to make extra, because leftovers are delicious. See the [Seder Plate](/learn/seder-plate) for where it goes.

## Soup, without matzah balls

Begin with a golden [Jewish chicken soup](/learn/jewish-chicken-soup). Serve it clear with carrots, or ask guests to add the vegetables. Soup improves when made a day or two before, and it freezes well.

## The main dish: brisket

A brisket is ideal for the Seder because it is made in advance and improves by waiting. Chabad.org's Passover pulled brisket uses a 3 to 4 pound second-cut brisket with a sauce of horseradish, mustard, ketchup, water, garlic, brown sugar, vinegar, salt and pepper. The sauce is cooked on the stove for 10 minutes, poured over the meat in a sealed pan, baked for 15 minutes at 425°F, then left at 200°F overnight, at least 6 hours. The meat is shredded and rewarmed in the sauce. It serves 6 to 8. Check that every ingredient is kosher for Passover. For a full brisket guide see [Brisket](/learn/brisket).

## Sides and dessert

Roasted potatoes, roasted carrots or a big green salad are simple, kosher for Passover and need no recipe. Dessert can be fresh fruit, with the leftover charoset on the side. If you need something richer, ask a friend who has a favorite Passover dessert to bring it.

## A cooking timeline

- **Two weeks before:** Clean and prepare the kitchen (see [Passover Cleaning](/learn/passover-cleaning)) and shop with the list.
- **Several days before:** Make the soup and freeze it. Buy your matzah and horseradish.
- **Two days before:** Make the charoset and cook the brisket.
- **The day before the Seder:** Shred the brisket, prepare vegetables, set the table and arrange the Seder plate.
- **Seder day:** Reheat, and let others help. Check [this week](/this-week) for candle-lighting times and see the [holiday calendar](/jewish-holidays) for the date.

## Common questions

### What do you serve at a Passover Seder?
A traditional Seder meal has charoset, soup, a meat main such as brisket, vegetable sides and fruit or a flourless dessert, all kosher for Passover.

### Can I make the Seder meal ahead of time?
Yes. Soup, charoset and brisket all improve when made a day or more in advance, which is why they are favorites for Passover.

### What is charoset made of?
Charoset is made of apples, pears and walnuts with sweet red wine, a mixture that recalls the mortar of Egypt. A nut-free version uses coconut and raisins.

### Why doesn't Chabad eat matzah balls on Passover?
Chabad custom avoids gebrokts, matzah soaked in liquid, so matzah meal is not used during Passover. Ask the Rabbi about your family's custom.

### Can I eat rice or beans at the Seder?
Ashkenazi custom, including Chabad, does not use kitniyot such as rice, corn and beans on Passover. Many Sephardic families do. Follow your family custom or ask the Rabbi.
