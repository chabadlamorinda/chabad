Judaism teaches that every person has free will: the power to choose between good and bad. This freedom is a basic principle of the Torah. Without it, mitzvot, reward and teshuvah would have no meaning.

## "Choose life"

Before the Jewish people entered the Land of Israel, Moses told them: "I have set before you life and death, blessing and curse. Choose life" (Deuteronomy 30:19).

Maimonides writes that every person can become as righteous as Moses or as wicked as the worst king. No one forces a person to be good or bad. Each of us decides.

## What is in our hands?

The Talmud says, "Everything is in the hands of Heaven except the fear of Heaven." In other words, G-d decides many things about our lives: our health, our circumstances, our talents and challenges. But how we respond, whether we choose to serve G-d and do good, is truly up to us.

This means free will is about moral and spiritual choices. We do not choose everything that happens to us. We choose who we become.

## If G-d knows the future, are we free?

This is an ancient question. If G-d already knows what I will choose, how can my choice be free?

Maimonides answers that G-d's knowledge is not like human knowledge. G-d is beyond time, so His knowing does not cause our choices. We cannot fully understand how both are true, just as we cannot fully understand G-d. But both are true: G-d knows, and we choose.

## The daily struggle

The [Tanya](/learn/tanya) teaches that each person has a G-dly soul and an animal soul, and they pull in different directions. Feelings and desires may arise on their own, and that is not a failure. What matters is what we do with them.

The Tanya gives a powerful tool: **the mind can rule the heart.** You may not control every feeling, but you can control:

1. **Thought:** what you choose to dwell on.
2. **Speech:** what you say.
3. **Action:** what you do.

A person who always chooses well in these three areas, even while struggling inside, is called a *beinoni*. The Tanya teaches that this level is within reach of everyone.

## Free will and teshuvah

Because we are free, we can also change. [Teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah), returning to G-d, is possible at any moment. No past mistake takes away a person's power to choose good now.

## Living with free will

- **Take responsibility.** Your choices matter and shape the world.
- **Do not judge others harshly.** You do not know their challenges.
- **Make it easier to choose well.** Set up your life, friends and habits to support good choices.
- **Start small.** One good choice makes the next one easier. The Mishnah says, "One mitzvah leads to another."

## Common questions

### Does Judaism believe in fate?
Judaism teaches that G-d guides the world and many circumstances are set. But our moral choices are free, and prayer, tzedakah and teshuvah can change a person's path.

### If I struggle with temptation, does it mean I am a bad person?
No. Struggle is part of being human. The Tanya teaches that a person who struggles and still chooses good brings great joy to G-d.

### Are some people more free than others?
Everyone faces different challenges, and G-d judges each person according to their situation. But every person has the power to make the next right choice.
