A Jewish husband is taught to honor his wife even more than he honors himself. In Chabad teaching this is the first duty of a husband, and the rest of a peaceful home grows from it: respect, commitment, caring, listening and valuing the ways the two of you are different.

## "Honor your wife more than yourself"

Chabad.org's "Different But Equal" tells of a husband who claimed that his wife must listen to him because he was "the master of the house." The Lubavitcher Rebbe replied that there is an earlier obligation: a man should honor his wife more than he honors himself. Do that first, said the Rebbe, and the respect and love of his wife will follow.

Chabad.org also brings the sages' teaching that the blessings a man receives come on account of his wife: "Honor your wife so you may become wealthy." A husband who treats his wife as a blessing in his life is on the right track. See [shalom bayit](/learn/shalom-bayit) and [the Woman of Valor](/learn/eishet-chayil), the song many husbands sing to their wives on Friday night.

## Five things that build harmony

Chabad.org lists five Torah teachings for a peaceful marriage. In plain words:

1. **Respect.** It starts with self-control over selfish impulses. Value your wife and show it: let her influence you, accept her right to make decisions and affirm her worth in words and deeds.
2. **Commitment.** Your spouse comes first, above parents, friends, work and your own interests. Stand together in hard times and feel her pain as your own.
3. **Caring.** You are often the only person who can meet your spouse's needs. Be kind even when you do not feel like it: greet her warmly, comfort her, say thank you.
4. **Closeness.** It grows through listening, which means understanding, not only hearing. Do not interrupt, set aside judgment and advice, summarize what you heard and ask how she feels.
5. **Accepting differences.** You have different talents and temperaments. Divide responsibilities by strength and praise your wife's unique gifts, like the tribes of Zebulun and Issachar, who supported one another.

## Words matter

A husband's words build or break a home. Chabad.org's "Different But Equal" teaches that men and women share the same purpose, to join body and soul and make the world better, and that each uses the abilities G-d gave them. The goal is not for husband and wife to become alike, but to respect their differences. If your wife tells you she was hurt, listen to understand. ## Common questions

### How should a Jewish husband treat his wife?
He should honor her more than himself, with respectful words, caring actions and real listening. This is the teaching the Lubavitcher Rebbe gave a husband who thought he was the boss.

### Is the husband the head of the household in Judaism?
Chabad teaching does not stress authority first. The Rebbe pointed a husband to his duty to honor his wife before anything else. For questions about your own home, speak with the Rabbi.

### Does "Honor your wife so you may become wealthy" mean money?
Not only. Chabad.org brings it with the sages' teaching that a man's blessings come on account of his wife, so honoring her brings blessing into the whole home.

### What can I do for my wife this week?
Choose one of the five teachings above: say thank you, listen without advice or put her needs first once.

### What if we argue a lot?
Start with listening and honor, and speak privately with the Rabbi or Rebbetzin: [talk to the Rabbi](/help/talk-to-the-rabbi).
