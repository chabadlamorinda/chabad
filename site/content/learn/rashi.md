Rashi is Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki (1040 to 1105), a French rabbi from Troyes whose short, clear commentary on the Torah and the Talmud is printed on nearly every page of both. The name Rashi is simply the initials of his name, and for almost a thousand years he has been the first teacher of nearly every Jewish student.

## Who was Rashi?

Rashi was born in Troyes, in the Champagne region of France. He studied in the great Torah centers of Worms and Mainz in Germany, and then returned home to teach. Tradition says he supported himself as a vintner. His writing is famous for being short, plain and precisely chosen, as if every word were weighed. He was a humble man, and he often wrote in his commentary, "I do not know."

His daughters married scholars, and his grandsons became leading Talmud teachers in their own right.

## What does Rashi's commentary do?

Rashi explains the simple meaning of a verse, and also draws on the Sages to answer the question a thoughtful reader would ask: "Why does the Torah say it this way?" He also brings Midrash, stories that deepen the text. A single line of Rashi can explain a difficult word, resolve a puzzle, or add a moral lesson. Rashi's commentary on the Talmud is considered so essential that a Talmud is hard to learn without it.

Among the first Hebrew books ever printed, in 1475, was Rashi's commentary on the Torah, which shows how central it was.

## What is Rashi script?

When you open a Chumash, the Rashi text is printed in a special semi-cursive typeface called Rashi script. It is not Rashi's own handwriting. Early printers adopted this style to tell the commentary apart from the Torah text. Once you learn the standard [Hebrew alphabet](/learn/hebrew-alphabet), Rashi script comes quickly with a little practice.

## Rashi in Chabad

Chabad tradition studies the weekly [Torah portion](/learn/weekly-parsha) with Rashi every day, as part of the [daily Torah study](/learn/daily-torah-study) cycle. For decades the Rebbe gave talks in which he looked closely at a single Rashi comment and asked why Rashi chose those words. Chassidic students enjoy this way of learning because it shows how much wisdom a few lines can hold.

## How to read the parsha with Rashi

1. Pick a short section of the week's [parsha](/parsha).
2. Read the verse slowly.
3. Read Rashi's comment and ask, "What question is he answering?"
4. Ask a friend or the Rabbi about anything unclear.

## Common questions

### Who was Rashi?
Rashi was Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki, an eleventh-century French sage whose commentary on the Torah and Talmud is still studied worldwide.

### What does Rashi stand for?
Rashi is an acronym for Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki.

### What is Rashi script?
Rashi script is the typeface used in printed books for Rashi's commentary. It is named after him, though he did not write in it.

### Why does every Chumash include Rashi?
Because his explanations are clear and rooted in the Sages, and they have guided Jewish students for almost a thousand years.

### Can a beginner learn Rashi?
Yes. Start with English translations and one short passage at a time.
