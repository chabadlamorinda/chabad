Shabbat morning services follow a set order: morning blessings and psalms, the Shema and its blessings, the silent Amidah, the Torah reading with the Haftarah, a second Amidah called Musaf, closing prayers, and then Kiddush with food and conversation. Chabad.org says the whole service can last about three hours, and you are welcome to come partway through.

## The quick order at a glance

1. Morning blessings and psalms of praise
2. The Shema and its blessings
3. The Amidah, said silently while standing
4. The Torah reading and the Haftarah
5. A talk, often
6. Musaf, an additional Amidah for Shabbat
7. Closing prayers and songs
8. Kiddush

For a close look at the parts that are the same every morning of the week, see [The Morning Service (Shacharit)](/learn/shacharit-morning-service). Here we focus on what makes Shabbat different.

## 1. Warming up: blessings and psalms

The service opens with morning blessings and a long section of psalms and songs of praise, called *Pesukei d'Zimra* (פסוקי דזמרה), "verses of song." Shabbat adds extra hymns and psalms to this section, which is why it takes longer than a weekday. Do not worry if you arrive later. The synagogue is often still filling up, and arriving partway through is normal.

## 2. The Shema and the Amidah

The prayer leader calls the congregation to bless G-d (Barchu), and everyone answers. Then comes the Shema, with its blessings, and the Amidah, the silent standing prayer at the heart of every service. Please stay quiet while people are praying it, and do not walk in front of them.

## 3. The Torah reading

This is the high point of the morning. The Torah scroll is taken from the ark, carried through the congregation, and read aloud from the bimah, the raised reading platform. The weekly portion, the *parsha*, is divided among several people who are called up for an *aliyah* (עֲלִיָּה, "going up"). To see this week's portion, visit [the parsha page](/parsha); to understand the reading, see [Torah Reading](/learn/torah-reading) and [Aliyah](/learn/aliyah). The scroll is then lifted and dressed; see [Synagogue Honors](/learn/synagogue-honors).

After the Torah comes the [Haftarah](/learn/haftarah), a reading from the Prophets that is connected to the portion or the season.

## 4. A talk

Chabad.org notes that the rabbi often gives a sermon on Shabbat and holiday mornings. Where it falls in the service can differ from one synagogue to another. Ask the Rabbi what to expect.

## 5. Musaf and the closing prayers

[Musaf](/learn/musaf) is an additional Amidah for Shabbat, recalling the additional offering in the Temple. It is followed by closing prayers and songs, including Aleinu. People often sing together at the end.

## 6. Kiddush and lunch

The service usually ends with Kiddush: the blessing over wine, followed by food and conversation. This is the best time to meet people. Read more in [Kiddush](/learn/kiddush), and if you would like to sponsor one, see Sponsoring a Kiddush.

## Tips for first-timers

- Use a siddur with English and learn how to [find your place](/learn/finding-your-place-in-the-siddur).
- Leave your phone at home or turned off; see Synagogue Etiquette.
- Ask a neighbor if you are lost. Everyone was new once.

## Common questions

### What happens at Shabbat morning services?
The service moves from morning blessings and psalms to the Shema, the silent Amidah, the Torah reading and Haftarah, Musaf, closing songs and Kiddush.

### How long are Shabbat services?
According to Chabad.org, Shabbat morning prayers with Musaf last about three hours on average. Call 925-384-7242 to ask when services begin and how long they usually run.

### Can I come late to Shabbat services?
Yes. Many people arrive partway through. Come in quietly and join in.

### Why is the Torah read on Shabbat?
The Torah is read aloud in synagogue on Shabbat morning, and the weekly portion is read in turn so the whole Five Books are completed each year. See Torah Reading.

### What is Musaf?
Musaf (מוּסָף, "additional") is an extra Amidah said on Shabbat and holidays. See the Musaf guide.

### Is there food after the service?
Usually yes. Kiddush is a light meal with wine and conversation. Ask the Rabbi what is planned.
