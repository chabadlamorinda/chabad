When two people marry, two families join. In-laws can be a great blessing, extra love, wisdom and grandparents for the children, and they can also be a source of tension. Jewish tradition gives clear guidance on both sides.

## Honor your in-laws

Jewish law teaches that a person should honor their father-in-law and mother-in-law. Moses went out to greet his father-in-law Yitro and bowed to him, and King David called his father-in-law Saul "my father." Kindness to a spouse's parents is also kindness to your spouse.

## Your marriage comes first

The Torah says: "A man shall leave his father and his mother and cling to his wife." Married children honor their parents, but decisions about their home, their children and their lives belong to the couple. Parents who respect this are usually welcomed more, not less.

## For married children

- **Speak about your in-laws respectfully,** especially to your spouse.
- **Let each spouse handle their own parents** when there is a problem.
- **Plan holidays fairly** and early, so no family feels left out.
- **Include grandparents** in the children's lives: calls, visits, Shabbat meals. See [Jewish grandparents](/learn/jewish-grandparents).

## For parents of married children

- **Give advice only when asked,** and accept a "no thank you" gracefully.
- **Welcome the new spouse as your own child.**
- **Respect their Jewish choices,** whether more or less observant than yours.
- **Never criticize your child's spouse** to your child.

## When there is tension

Pick one small step toward peace: a kind word, a thank-you note, a gift for no reason. Assume good intentions. If a pattern keeps hurting your marriage, talk it through with the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin.

## Common questions

### Do I have to honor my in-laws like my own parents?
Jewish law requires honoring in-laws, though the obligation is not as extensive as for one's own parents. Kindness and respect are the heart of it.

### My parents and my spouse don't get along. What do I do?
Stand by your spouse in private, honor your parents in words and actions, and look for calm settings where everyone can connect. Ask the Rabbi for guidance.

### How do we divide the holidays?
Many couples alternate, share Seder nights, or host both families together. Decide as a couple and tell both sides early.
