The priestly garments (bigdei kehunah, בִּגְדֵי כְּהֻנָּה) were the special clothes worn by the Kohanim, the priests, while serving in the Mishkan and later the Holy Temple. Every Kohen wore four garments, and the Kohen Gadol, the High Priest, wore eight. The Torah describes them in Parshat Tetzaveh (Exodus 28).

## The four garments of every Kohen

According to Chabad.org's summary, every ordinary priest wore these four garments:

1. **Ketonet (כֻּתֹּנֶת), a tunic.** A long linen shirt reaching to the heels.
2. **Avnet (אַבְנֵט), a sash.** A long sash wound over the heart, made of linen woven with wool in blue, purple and red.
3. **Migbaat (מִגְבַּעַת), a turban.** A linen headdress. The sources describe the turban a little differently for the High Priest and the other Kohanim, and the names differ, so we keep it simple here.
4. **Michnasayim (מִכְנְסֵי), trousers.** Linen trousers, knee-length.


## The four extra garments of the High Priest

The High Priest wore the four above, plus four more:

1. **Choshen (חֹשֶׁן), the breastplate.** A square of richly woven cloth, folded and hung on the chest.
2. **Ephod (אֵפוֹד).** Something like an apron worn from behind, made of blue, purple and crimson wool, linen and gold thread. Straps came over the shoulders, and on each strap was a stone engraved with the names of six tribes.
3. **Me'il (מְעִיל), the robe.** A robe of blue wool worn under the ephod. Along its hem were bells alternating with decorative "pomegranates," so that his steps were heard in the sanctuary.
4. **Tzitz (צִיץ), the golden plate.** A plate of gold worn across the forehead, engraved with the words "Holy to G-d" (Kodesh LaHashem).

## The breastplate and its twelve stones

On the breastplate were twelve precious stones in four rows of three, each engraved with the name of one of the twelve tribes. Chabad.org notes that the identity of the stones is uncertain, and more than thirty identifications have been suggested. The Sages link each stone to the character of its tribe. For example, Zebulun's white pearl hints at its trade, and Issachar's sapphire hints at its Torah study.

Within the folds, tradition says, was the Urim VeTumim, a parchment with G-d's sacred Name, through which G-d guided the people on weighty decisions. The Chassidic teachers draw two lessons: every person is unique, with his or her own "stone" and special qualities, and even something that looks lifeless can shine when used for holiness. The breastplate was the High Priest's way of carrying all Israel on his heart.

## The golden plate: "Holy to G-d"

The tzitz was fastened with blue-dyed wool across the High Priest's forehead. The Sages say it brought forgiveness for impudence, called "hardness of the forehead." The Lubavitcher Rebbe's explanation, as Chabad.org presents it, is that the forehead is the seat of the will, which is above thought and feeling. True holiness starts when we bring our will to G-d.

## What clothes teach us

The Torah says the garments were made "for honor and for splendor." Clothes do not make a person holy, but they do shape how we act. In Jewish life, we dress for [Shabbat](/learn/shabbat-clothes) in a way that honors the day, and we think about [modesty](/learn/tzniut) as a way of expressing who we are. When a Kohen in a Lamorinda family stands up for the priestly blessing or is called first to the Torah, he is continuing a tradition that goes back to Aaron: read about [the High Priest Aaron](/learn/aaron-the-high-priest) and [Kohen, Levi, Yisrael](/learn/kohen-levi-yisrael).

Today there is no Temple, so no one wears these garments. Jewish tradition looks forward to the day when the [Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple) is rebuilt in the time of Moshiach, and the Kohanim will serve again.

## Common questions

### What did the High Priest wear?
The High Priest wore eight garments: a tunic, sash, turban and trousers (like every Kohen), plus a breastplate, ephod, robe and a golden plate on his forehead.

### What are the priestly garments called in Hebrew?
They are called bigdei kehunah (בִּגְדֵי כְּהֻנָּה), which means "garments of the priesthood."

### What was the ephod?
The ephod (אֵפוֹד) was an apron-like garment of the High Priest made of wool in blue, purple and crimson, plus linen and gold. The breastplate was attached to it.

### What stones were on the High Priest's breastplate?
There were twelve stones, one for each tribe, set in four rows of three. Scholars have suggested more than thirty identifications of the stones, so the exact stones are uncertain.

### What was written on the High Priest's golden plate?
It said "Holy to G-d" (Kodesh LaHashem). It was worn on his forehead.

### Does anyone wear the priestly garments today?
No. They were worn only during service in the Mishkan and the Holy Temple. Jewish tradition looks forward to their return when the Temple is rebuilt.
