A prophet (navi, נָבִיא) in Judaism is a person chosen by G-d to deliver a message to the Jewish people: to guide them, rebuke them when they go astray and comfort them in hard times. Prophecy is not fortune-telling. The Talmud counts 48 prophets and seven prophetesses whose messages were meant for all generations, and the greatest of all was Moses.

Here is a simple map of the prophets, their books and why they matter to readers in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda today.

## What does a prophet do?

Chabad.org describes prophets as guides. They told the people what G-d wanted of them, challenged kings and ordinary people, and offered comfort in hard times. A prophet is a messenger, not someone who predicts lottery numbers. Many of their predictions were warnings, with a hope that people would change.

Chabad.org explains that other prophets heard from G-d only at times, often in a dreamlike state, while Moses could speak with G-d whenever he wished. That is why Moses is called the greatest of the prophets. Read more in the guide to [Moses](/learn/moses).

## How many prophets were there?

The Talmud says there were 48 prophets and seven prophetesses whose words were recorded for future generations. Chabad.org notes that there were many more prophets, about 1,200,000 according to the Talmud, but only those whose message was needed in later times are written down.

The seven prophetesses are Sarah, Miriam, Deborah, Hannah, Abigail, Huldah and Esther. Read about [Miriam](/learn/miriam) and [Hannah](/learn/hannah-and-her-prayer).

Chabad.org's list of well-known prophets includes:

- **Abraham and Sarah**, the first prophets, who spread the belief in one G-d. See [Abraham and Sarah](/learn/abraham-and-sarah).
- **Moses**, who received the Torah at Sinai.
- **Samuel**, who anointed Saul and David as kings.
- **Elijah**, who confronted false worship on Mount Carmel. See [Elijah](/learn/elijah-the-prophet).
- **Isaiah and Jeremiah**, who warned of destruction and offered words of comfort.
- **Ezekiel**, who saw visions in exile, including the dry bones.
- **Jonah**, sent to the city of Nineveh.
- **Esther**, the queen whose story we read on Purim. See [Purim](/learn/purim-for-beginners).

## The books of the Prophets

The Hebrew Bible, the [Tanach](/learn/tanach), is made of three parts: Torah, Nevi'im (Prophets) and Ketuvim (Writings). Nevi'im includes:

1. **The earlier prophets:** Joshua, Judges, Samuel and Kings.
2. **The later prophets:** Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel and the Twelve Minor Prophets, which were gathered into one book.

Daniel and Esther, by tradition, are in the Writings.

## The Haftarah: the prophets in the synagogue

Every Shabbat and holiday, after the Torah is read, a passage from the Prophets is read. This is the Haftarah (הַפְטָרָה). It usually connects to the theme of the Torah portion or to the holiday. The comforting words of Isaiah, for example, are read after Tisha B'Av. See the guide to the [Haftarah](/learn/haftarah).

## Why did prophecy stop, and will it return?

The tradition is that prophecy ended in the early years of the Second Temple period, and that Maimonides and the sages teach it will return in the time of the Messiah. Why it ended is a deeper subject: please ask the Rabbi. See [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach).

## What can we learn from the prophets?

The prophets teach that how we treat each other matters to G-d as much as ritual does. They ask us to be honest, to care for the vulnerable and to return to G-d. That message is as relevant to a family in the East Bay as it was in ancient Jerusalem.

## Common questions

### What is a prophet in Judaism?
A prophet is a person chosen by G-d to deliver a message to the Jewish people. Prophets guided, rebuked and comforted. They were not fortune-tellers.

### How many prophets are there in Judaism?
The Talmud counts 48 prophets and seven prophetesses whose words were recorded for all generations. There were many more whose messages were for their own time.

### Who were the seven prophetesses?
They are Sarah, Miriam, Deborah, Hannah, Abigail, Huldah and Esther.

### Who was the greatest prophet?
Moses. Chabad.org explains he could speak to G-d at any time, while other prophets received prophecy only at certain moments.

### Are there prophets today?
Tradition teaches that prophecy ended long ago and will return in the time of the Messiah. For personal questions, ask the Rabbi.

### What is the Haftarah?
It is the reading from the Prophets that follows the Torah reading on Shabbat and holidays. It usually echoes the Torah portion.
