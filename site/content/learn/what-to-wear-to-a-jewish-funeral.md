To a Jewish funeral, wear modest, simple clothes: for men a suit, or nice slacks and a button-down shirt, and for women a modest skirt and top or a dress. Men cover their heads, and a basket of kippahs is often at the door.

Going to a funeral is almost always better than staying away. Chabad.org calls accompanying the departed "one of the greatest acts of kindness," because it is a kindness that can never be repaid.

## What to wear

Dress for respect, not for show.

- **Men:** a suit, or nice slacks and a button-down shirt. Cover your head with a hat or a kippah. If you do not have one, ask at the door.
- **Women:** a modest skirt and top, or a dress.
- **Everyone:** keep it quiet and simple, nothing that draws attention.

The family's closest relatives will often be wearing a torn garment. That is [kriah](/learn/kriah), and it is a sign of grief, not a mistake.

## What the service is like

A Jewish funeral is short and plain. The casket is usually closed, because Jewish tradition does not consider it proper to gaze at the dead. A few brief prayers are said, and the casket is then accompanied to the grave.

Chabad custom is not to give eulogies, so the service is very brief. Other families and communities do have eulogies, so follow the lead of the family and the Rabbi. For more on the whole day, see our guide to [Jewish funeral customs](/learn/jewish-funeral) and [funerals near Lafayette](/learn/jewish-funerals-near-lafayette).

## What to do as a guest

1. **Arrive on time** and turn off your phone.
2. **Say little.** You do not need to speak. Follow the lead of the grieving family, and avoid small talk.
3. **Say Psalms.** Instead of chatting, you may recite [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim). Funeral homes often provide books of Psalms.
4. **Walk with the casket.** It is a mitzvah to accompany it, at least four cubits (about six feet).
5. **Do as the people around you do.** If you are unsure, watch and follow.

## At the grave

Filling the grave is part of the Jewish funeral. In the Chabad tradition, the men present fill it with earth. Taking a turn is considered an honor, and the shovel is not passed from hand to hand. Each person sticks it in the earth for the next to pick up, showing that this is not ordinary work. If you are unsure whether to join, ask the Rabbi or the funeral director beforehand.

Flowers are not a Jewish funeral custom. Many people give [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah) in memory of the person instead.

## Comforting the mourners

After the burial, those present form two lines, and the mourners walk between them. As they pass, we say:

*Hamakom yenachem etchem besoch she'ar avelei Tzion viYerushalayim.* "May the Omnipresent comfort you among the other mourners of Zion and Jerusalem."

Say these words, and nothing more is needed. Before this moment, the sages teach, the mourners' grief is too raw for words of comfort; your quiet presence is the comfort. You can read more in our guide on [comforting mourners](/learn/comforting-mourners).

Afterward, everyone who attended washes their hands before going home: pour water from a cup three times over each hand, alternating right and left, and do not dry them.

## Common questions

### What do you wear to a Jewish funeral?
Wear modest, simple clothing: a suit or nice slacks and button-down shirt for men, and a modest skirt and top or a dress for women. Men also cover their heads.

### Do you bring flowers to a Jewish funeral?
No. Flowers are not a Jewish funeral custom. A donation to tzedakah in the person's memory is a more fitting gesture.

### What do you say to a mourner at a Jewish funeral?
Say the traditional words, "May the Omnipresent comfort you among the other mourners of Zion and Jerusalem," as the mourners pass between the two lines after the burial. Otherwise, silence and presence are enough.

### Should I go to the funeral if I am not sure I should?
Attending is almost always better than staying away, since accompanying the departed is one of the greatest acts of kindness. If you have questions about the day, call the Rabbi beforehand.

### Why is the casket closed?
It is not considered proper to gaze at the dead, so the casket stays closed.

### Should I visit the family after the funeral?
Yes. Visiting the mourners during [shiva](/learn/shiva) is part of comforting them. The mourners' first meal after the funeral is explained in [the meal of condolence](/learn/seudat-havraah).
