Abraham and Sarah were the first Jewish couple, who, around 3,800 years ago, discovered the One G-d and taught the world about Him. Every Jew is a child of Abraham and Sarah, and so are those who choose Judaism, as converts.

## Abraham, the man who found G-d

Jewish tradition teaches that Abraham (Avraham, אַבְרָהָם) looked at the world and asked, "Who made all this?" A well-known Midrash tells that he grew up in the home of Terach, who made idols, and that Abraham broke them to show that they were powerless. Abraham was not content with understanding the truth himself. He shared it with everyone he met.

## Lech Lecha: "Go for yourself"

G-d told Abraham, "Go from your land, from your birthplace, from your father's house, to the land that I will show you" (Genesis 12:1). Abraham left everything familiar, and G-d promised to make his children a great nation. That journey began Jewish history; see the [Jewish history overview](/learn/jewish-history-overview).

## Sarah, a prophet and a mother of the Jewish people

Sarah (שָׂרָה) stood beside Abraham as an equal. Rashi says that her prophecy was even stronger than his, when G-d told Abraham to "listen to her voice." The Sages teach that Sarah's tent had three blessings: her Shabbat candles stayed lit from one Friday to the next, her dough was blessed, and a cloud of Divine presence hovered above her tent. That is why Jewish women light [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) to this day.

## The open tent

Abraham and Sarah's tent was open on all sides so that any traveler could come in and be welcome. They offered food, drink and rest, and gave guests the chance to discover G-d. This is the model of [hachnasat orchim](/learn/hachnasat-orchim), welcoming guests, which Jewish homes still strive to follow.

## The ten tests

Tradition counts ten trials that Abraham passed, from leaving his home to the binding of Isaac. The Mishnah lists them in Pirkei Avot (5:3) as a sign of his love for G-d. Sources differ on exactly which trials are counted, so ask the Rabbi if you'd like to see the lists. At 99, Abraham also circumcised himself, the beginning of the mitzvah of [brit milah](/learn/brit-milah).

## Why they are our ancestors, and for converts too

Torah says Abraham and Sarah brought souls "that they made in Charan" (Genesis 12:5), which the Sages explain as people they taught to believe in G-d. Converts to Judaism are traditionally called the "son" or "daughter" of Abraham and Sarah, and say "our G-d and G-d of our fathers" like all other Jews. See [converting to Judaism](/learn/converting-to-judaism).

## Common questions

### Who were Abraham and Sarah?
They were the first patriarch and matriarch of the Jewish people, the couple who discovered G-d and taught the world about Him.

### Why is Abraham the father of Judaism?
Because he was the first to teach the oneness of G-d and began the family from which the Jewish people descend.

### What is the story of Abraham smashing the idols?
It's a Midrash saying that Abraham broke his father's idols to show they have no power. It is not written in the Torah text itself.

### What were the ten tests of Abraham?
They were ten trials of faith, culminating in the binding of Isaac. Lists differ among the commentators.

### Are converts children of Abraham and Sarah too?
Yes. Converts are traditionally called children of Abraham and Sarah.
