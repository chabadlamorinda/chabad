A busy professional can learn Tanya in about five minutes a day by using the daily Tanya lesson online, listening to a short audio class on the commute, or reading one short chapter at a time. You do not need a free hour, only a repeated small habit.

## Why Tanya suits busy people

The [Tanya](/learn/tanya), by Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi, the Alter Rebbe, is often described as a "one size fits all" life manual. Its subject is the thing every working person faces: the pull between what is easy and what is right, and how to carry calm and meaning into ordinary hours. Each chapter is short, so one idea fits in one sitting. For the book as a whole, see [what is the Tanya](/learn/tanya).

## Three easy ways to start

All of these are free online, and the daily Tanya page lists the formats described here.

1. **The daily Tanya.** There is a Tanya reading for every day, following the yearly cycle. You can read it in English, Hebrew or both. For many people, one daily portion is a few minutes.
2. **Short audio.** Each day's page lists recorded classes by different teachers, from about 3 to 23 minutes, and podcasts on common apps. The library also lists a "Five Minute Tanya" audio series.
3. **Tanya in Plain English.** This is a modern, easy-to-read English version by Tzvi Freeman, with a navigator by topic, chapter and type of class, and print options.

You can also receive "Lessons in Tanya" by email and use the Daily Study app, which the daily Tanya page lists among its options.

## Which ideas help most at work

The Tanya in Plain English page features several chapters on everyday struggles. We suggest these starting points, which have a clear workday use.

- **Chapters 26 to 28** speak about anxiety, self-blame and sadness. Chapter 26 compares the inner struggle to wrestling and says energy and an open heart win. Tanya's guidance is spiritual, and professional help is also right for lasting anxiety or low mood. See [Tanya in hard times](/learn/tanya-in-hard-times).
- **Chapter 32** teaches love for every Jew, because all Jewish souls share one source. It is a useful lens for a tense meeting or a difficult colleague.
- **Chapter 33** teaches finding joy in awareness of G-d's presence. See [Tanya on gratitude](/learn/tanya-on-gratitude).
- **Chapter 12** describes the ordinary person who feels temptation and anger but rejects them and answers with kindness. That is the heart of [the beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni). The same chapter says he does not let resentment, jealousy or anger rule him and repays wrongs with good.

For work stress and time, see [Jewish wisdom for busy professionals](/learn/jewish-wisdom-for-busy-professionals); for more workday ideas from the Tanya, see [Tanya at work](/learn/tanya-at-work).

## A realistic routine

This is a suggestion, not a rule from the Tanya.

- Pick one fixed moment: first coffee, the train, lunch.
- Read or listen to one portion.
- Keep one sentence from it in mind for the day.
- Skip a day without guilt and start again.

Whether you work in Lafayette, Moraga or Orinda or commute to the city, the habit travels with you.

## Common questions

### How can I learn Tanya if I have no time?
Use the daily Tanya lesson or a short audio class online. Many are only a few minutes long. Start with one chapter or one portion a day.

### Is there a short daily Tanya lesson?
Yes. There is a free daily Tanya reading online with audio and video classes of varying lengths, and a "Five Minute Tanya" audio series. See [daily Torah study](/learn/daily-torah-study).

### Which Tanya chapters are best for beginners?
Tanya in Plain English highlights Chapters 26 to 28 on anxiety, self-blame and sadness, Chapter 32 on love for every Jew and Chapter 33 on celebrating being Jewish. See [a Tanya learning plan](/learn/tanya-learning-plan) for a chapter-by-need guide.

### Do I need to read Hebrew?
No. The Tanya is available in English and Hebrew side by side, and Tanya in Plain English is entirely in English.

### Can Tanya help with work stress?
It offers spiritual perspective on trust, joy and relationships. For ongoing stress or anxiety, professional help is also right. Ask the Rabbi about your situation.
