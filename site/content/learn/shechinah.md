The Shechinah (שכינה) is the Divine Presence: G-d as He "dwells within" our world. The word comes from the Hebrew shochen (שכן), "to dwell," and Chabad.org describes it as the same One G-d, in the mode in which He is close and present among us.

## What the Shechinah means

Judaism teaches that G-d is beyond the universe and also within it. The Shechinah is a name for this second side: G-d dwelling in the lowest, most ordinary places, including our homes, our offices and our lives. The Torah gives the purpose in the command to build the Tabernacle, a sanctuary where G-d would dwell among the Jewish people. Chabad.org notes that the Shechinah did not settle among the Jewish people until the Tabernacle was built and prepared.

We use words like "dwelling" because we are speaking of closeness. G-d is not limited to one place, but there are places and moments where His presence is felt more.

## Levels of presence

Chabad.org teaches that the Shechinah is revealed "in a variety of forms and degrees," and that the Divine light is "filtered" so each recipient receives what is good for him. In its description, the highest levels are Mount Sinai, then the Holy of Holies in the Temple, then a prophet receiving a message, then synagogues and study halls, and then a group of ten people occupied with Torah. See [the Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple) and [the Western Wall](/learn/western-wall).

The Mishnah (Avot 3:6) teaches that when ten people sit together and are occupied with Torah, the Shechinah rests among them. The commentators add that even one person studying Torah is accompanied by it.

## The Shechinah in difficult places

A beautiful idea in Chabad teaching is that the Shechinah does not only dwell in holy places. Chabad.org tells us that it descended to Egypt with Jacob and shared the Jewish people's suffering until the Exodus. At the burning bush, G-d showed that He would dwell even in a lowly thorn bush while the Jewish people were in distress.

That is a comforting message: you are never alone, even in a dark time. If you are going through something hard, you are welcome to talk with the Rabbi or call [for help](/help).

## Our souls and the Shechinah

Chabad.org explains that the human soul is a kind of reflection of the Shechinah: the Shechinah descends into this world to find and redeem sparks of holiness, and our souls take part in the same journey. So the work of making the world a home for G-d is not only for rabbis. It belongs to everyone, each day.

## Brief note on "she"

The Hebrew word Shechinah is grammatically feminine, and Kabbalistic writings, including the Chabad.org article, use "she" when speaking of this aspect of G-d. It is not a separate god and does not mean G-d has a gender. It is a way of speaking about the aspect that relates closely to us, and ask the Rabbi to say more if you are curious.

## Inviting the Shechinah in

Here are some simple ways Jews invite G-d's presence:

1. **Shabbat.** Making the home peaceful, lighting candles, sitting together. See [Shabbat day](/learn/shabbat-day).
2. **Torah study.** Even one person with a book, or ten people at a class.
3. **Prayer with a minyan.** A synagogue is called a small sanctuary.
4. **Loving fellow Jews.** Peace between people makes room for G-d. Read [Ahavat Yisrael](/learn/ahavat-yisrael).
5. **A mezuzah and a warm home.** Make the home a dwelling.

Families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda can begin with one: a Friday-night candle.

## Common questions

### What is the Shechinah in Judaism?
The Shechinah is the Divine Presence, G-d "dwelling within" the world. The word comes from the Hebrew shochen, "to dwell."

### Where does the Shechinah dwell?
It is revealed in different degrees: highest at Mount Sinai and the Holy of Holies, and also in synagogues, study halls, and wherever ten people are occupied with Torah. Chabad.org adds that it came to Egypt with Jacob and was present even at the burning bush.

### Is the Shechinah female?
The word is grammatically feminine and Kabbalistic texts use "she" for it, but G-d is not a gender. It is a way of speaking about G-d's closeness.

### Does the Shechinah still exist now that the Temple is gone?
The Divine Presence is not limited to the Temple. Our tradition teaches that it also rests in synagogues, study halls, and homes. Ask the Rabbi for more on its connection to the Western Wall.

### How can I bring the Shechinah into my home?
Through Shabbat, Torah study, prayer, kindness and peace in the home. A mezuzah on the doorpost is a good start.

### Can one person study Torah and have the Shechinah with him?
Yes. Chabad.org says the commentators on the Mishnah teach that even one person studying Torah is accompanied by the Shechinah.
