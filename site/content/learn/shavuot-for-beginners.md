Shavuot is the holiday that celebrates G-d giving the Torah to the Jewish people at Mount Sinai. It comes seven weeks after Passover, and it is marked by hearing the Ten Commandments in synagogue, eating dairy foods, decorating with flowers and greenery, and learning Torah. It is a wonderful holiday for families, because children play a special role.

## What Shavuot is

The name means "weeks." On the second night of Passover we begin counting seven weeks, called the Omer, and Shavuot arrives at the end of the count. See [counting the Omer](/learn/counting-the-omer). Passover freed us from Egypt, and Shavuot gave that freedom its purpose: the Torah. Outside Israel it is two days; in Israel, one.

It also has other names: the Festival of the First Fruits and the Harvest Festival, because in ancient times people brought the first fruits of the season and two wheat loaves to the Holy Temple. Our sages call it "the time of the giving of our Torah."

## Hearing the Ten Commandments

On the first day of Shavuot we hear the Ten Commandments read in synagogue, and the custom is to stand for the reading. According to our sages, every Jewish soul stood at Sinai, including the souls of people born later, which is why every Jew is invited to hear them again, as if for the first time. Read about [the Ten Commandments](/learn/ten-commandments).

Chabad stresses bringing children, even the youngest babies. Before giving the Torah, G-d asked for guarantors, and the Jewish people offered their children. So the voices of children in the room are part of the day. Bring the kids!

## Dairy foods

Cheesecake, blintzes and ice cream are the foods of Shavuot. There are many explanations: the Torah is likened to milk and honey, the Torah was given on Shabbat when meat could not be prepared properly, and the Hebrew word for milk, chalav, adds up to 40, for the forty days Moses was on the mountain. Some also eat a dairy meal and then a meat meal later, with the Torah's kosher laws in mind. See [Shavuot dairy foods](/learn/shavuot-dairy-foods).

## Greenery and flowers

We decorate synagogues and homes with flowers, plants and greenery. Sinai was in a desert, but our sages teach that it bloomed when the Torah was given. Our tradition also teaches that on Shavuot the trees are judged for their fruit. Lamorinda's spring gardens make this an easy custom.

## Learning all night

Many people stay up the first night of Shavuot learning Torah. A Midrash says the Jewish people overslept on the morning of the giving of the Torah, and our staying awake repairs that. Even an hour or two of learning counts. Whether you come to a class or learn at home, see [learning on Shavuot night](/learn/shavuot-night-learning) and the [adult classes](/programs/adult-education).

## Candles and Yizkor

We light candles before the holiday begins, from an existing flame. On the second day, Yizkor is said; see [Yizkor](/learn/yizkor). Some also read the Book of Ruth, a story about a woman who chose the Torah and the Jewish people of her own free will.

## Common questions

### What is Shavuot?
Shavuot is the Jewish holiday celebrating the giving of the Torah at Mount Sinai. It ends the seven weeks of counting the Omer after Passover.

### Why do we eat dairy on Shavuot?
Several reasons are given: the Torah is compared to milk, the two-loaf offering in the Temple, and the fact that the Torah was given on Shabbat, when meat could not yet be prepared in a kosher way. Whichever reason, cheesecake is welcome.

### Why do we stay up all night on Shavuot?
Because a Midrash says the Jews overslept on the morning the Torah was given. Learning through the night shows our eagerness to receive the Torah.

### Why do we stand for the Ten Commandments?
We stand to honor the moment of Sinai, as if we are receiving the commandments ourselves. Every Jewish soul is said to have stood there.

### Why do we decorate with greenery on Shavuot?
In memory of the desert blooming at Sinai, and because trees are judged on Shavuot for their fruit.

### How many days is Shavuot?
Two days outside Israel and one day in Israel.
