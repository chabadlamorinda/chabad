## What happens at Rosh Hashanah services

Rosh Hashanah (ראש השנה, the Jewish New Year) is the day we crown G-d as King and ask for a good, sweet year. The heart of the service is the shofar (שופר, a ram's horn), sounded in a pattern of long and short blasts, which Chabad.org describes as a wake-up call for the soul. Services include special prayers, Torah readings and the shofar blasts, with the Rabbi's explanations. You do not need to read Hebrew or know the tunes: follow along in an English-Hebrew prayer book. See [Rosh Hashanah for Beginners](/learn/rosh-hashanah-for-beginners) and [the shofar](/learn/shofar).

Children are welcome. Try [Rosh Hashanah with kids](/learn/rosh-hashanah-with-kids) for ideas, and the [kids' corner](/kids).

## Tashlich at the Lafayette Reservoir

Tashlich (תשליך, "you shall cast away") is a short ceremony in which we symbolically cast our sins into the water, reciting verses from the prophets and Psalms, ending with Micah's words, "Who is a G-d like You...". Chabad.org explains how it works:

1. **When:** on the first day of Rosh Hashanah, ideally between Mincha and Maariv in the afternoon. If the first day is Shabbat, Chabad postpones it to the second day.
2. **Where:** ideally by a natural body of water with fish, such as a lake or river. Our local gathering is at the Lafayette Reservoir, a recreation area run by the East Bay Municipal Utility District, which posts its own park rules; please follow them.
3. **What to do:** stand by the water and say the Tashlich prayers from the machzor (holiday prayer book). Afterward, shake out the corners of your garment, or, in the Chabad custom, your tzitzit.
4. **Fish:** feeding them is not part of the ritual and is problematic on a holiday, so do not bring bread.
5. **Who:** everyone, including children. A minyan is not required.

Read more in [Tashlich](/learn/tashlich).

## Yom Kippur services

Yom Kippur (יום כיפור, the Day of Atonement) is the holiest day of the year, a day of fasting, prayer and returning to G-d. Chabad of Lamorinda holds Yom Kippur services at the Lafayette Park Hotel, from Kol Nidre through Yizkor and Neilah, followed by a break-fast. Yizkor (יזכור) is the memorial prayer for loved ones who have passed. Read [Yom Kippur for Beginners](/learn/yom-kippur-for-beginners), the [fast](/learn/yom-kippur-fast), and [Yom Kippur with kids](/learn/yom-kippur-with-kids). For the Yizkor list, see the [Yizkor form](/forms/yizkor).

If you have a health condition, never fast without asking your doctor and the Rabbi.

## What to bring and wear

Dress comfortably and modestly. Bring a heart ready to listen. On Yom Kippur, some people wear white, and leather shoes are traditionally not worn. Details on that are in the Yom Kippur guide, and the Rabbi is happy to answer any question.

## Common questions

### Where are the High Holiday services in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda?
Chabad of Lamorinda holds Rosh Hashanah services and a Shofar and Tashlich gathering at the Lafayette Reservoir, and Yom Kippur services at the Lafayette Park Hotel. Call 925-384-7242 for this year's times.

### Do I need a ticket or membership for the High Holidays?
No. No tickets or membership are needed, and every Jew is welcome. Reservations just help us save you a seat.

### Where can I hear the shofar in Lafayette?
At the Rosh Hashanah services and at the Shofar and Tashlich gathering at the Lafayette Reservoir. Dates are on the [Rosh Hashanah page](/holidays/rosh-hashanah).

### What is Tashlich and how is it done?
Tashlich is a ceremony of casting sins into water, done by a lake or river on the first day of Rosh Hashanah, with prayers from the machzor. See [Tashlich](/learn/tashlich).

### Can children come to High Holiday services?
Yes. Children are welcome, and there are guides for families.

### When is Yom Kippur this year?
The dates are on the [Jewish holidays](/jewish-holidays) page. They are computed for each year.
