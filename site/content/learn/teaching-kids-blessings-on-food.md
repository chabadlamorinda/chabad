Children can learn which blessing to say before eating by sorting food into six simple groups: bread, grain foods, grape juice, fruit from trees, food from the ground, and everything else. Jewish tradition teaches that around age three a child begins to say blessings over food, and a simple start is the best one.

## Why teach kids blessings?

A blessing (*bracha*, בְּרָכָה) is a short thank-you to G-d before we enjoy something. Saying it teaches a child that food does not simply appear: it is a gift. It also turns snack time into a small daily moment of gratitude. For the full background, see our guide to [Jewish blessings over food](/learn/blessings).

## The start of every blessing

Every blessing begins with the same words, in Hebrew *Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam,* "Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe." Then comes the ending for the food. For a young child, it is fine to learn just the ending at first, and add the beginning in time. Others answer "Amen."

## The simple chart: which blessing for what

1. **Bread:** הַמּוֹצִיא לֶחֶם מִן הָאָרֶץ, *hamotzi lechem min ha'aretz*. Kids' question: is it bread?
2. **Cake, cookies, crackers, pasta:** בּוֹרֵא מִינֵי מְזוֹנוֹת, *borei minei mezonot*. Is it made from flour or grain?
3. **Wine or grape juice:** בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הַגָּפֶן, *borei pri hagafen*. Is it from grapes?
4. **Fruit that grows on trees (apples, oranges, nuts):** בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָעֵץ, *borei pri ha'etz*. Does it grow on a tree?
5. **Vegetables and food that grows from the ground (carrots, strawberries, potatoes):** בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָאֲדָמָה, *borei pri ha'adamah*. Does it grow from the ground?
6. **Everything else (meat, fish, dairy, eggs, candy, drinks):** שֶׁהַכֹּל נִהְיָה בִּדְבָרוֹ, *shehakol nih'yeh bidvaro*. Anything else?

**The easy rule for a child:** if you are not sure, say *shehakol*. After the fact, it is the blessing that covers almost any food. Bread is a special case: before bread we wash our hands first, and after a meal with bread we say [Grace After Meals](/learn/birkat-hamazon). Some foods, like rice, mixtures and processed fruit, are tricky, so ask the Rabbi.

## Simple ways to teach it

1. **Start with one blessing.** Pick the one your child uses most. For many kids it is *shehakol* (juice, candy) or *ha'etz* (apples). Say it with them for a week.
2. **Use a chart on the fridge.** Pictures of foods next to each blessing help a child who cannot read yet. Draw a bread, a cake, grapes, an apple, a carrot and a star for "everything else."
3. **Make it a game.** Hold up food and ask "Which blessing?" Let the child win a point for each right answer. Wrong answers are fine; just say the right one together.
4. **Sing it.** Many children learn the blessings from a short tune faster than from speaking.
5. **Say it every time.** A child learns what a parent does. When you say the blessing before you eat, the child sees that it matters.
6. **Make a lunchbox card.** A small card in the lunchbox reminds a child at school.

## Tips for young children

- A very young child can say just *Baruch atah Ado-nai* and the ending, or only "Amen" after you.
- Do not make it a test or a power struggle. Warm and short is best.
- Wait until the child is sitting calmly, with the food in front of them, and say the blessing before the first bite.
- Praise the effort more than the accuracy.

## After eating

Children can also learn to say thank you after a meal. For bread meals, it is [Birkat Hamazon](/learn/birkat-hamazon), and for other foods there are shorter blessings; see [blessings after food](/learn/blessings-after-food). Children often learn a kids' version with songs first. Ask the Rabbi where to begin with your child.

## Common questions

### Which blessing do kids say on a snack like crackers?
Crackers are made from grain, so the blessing is *borei minei mezonot*. When in doubt, a child can say *shehakol*.

### What blessing do children say on fruit?
For fruit that grows on a tree, such as apples, the blessing is *borei pri ha'etz*. For fruit that grows from the ground, such as strawberries, it is *borei pri ha'adamah*.

### Can a child say a blessing in English?
Blessings are best said in Hebrew, but it is accepted to say them in a language you understand. For a young child, starting in the language they know is fine, while adding Hebrew over time. Ask the Rabbi for guidance.

### What if my child forgets the blessing?
If the food is still there, say the blessing now and keep eating. Gently remind the child next time.

### Do kids need to say a blessing on water?
Yes, water is *shehakol*. If someone drinks only to swallow a pill, no blessing is said.

### What age should a child start saying blessings?
Around age three. Many families start earlier with a simple "Amen."
