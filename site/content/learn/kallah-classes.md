A kallah is a bride. Before her wedding, a Jewish bride traditionally meets with a teacher for a series of private classes, called kallah classes. They are one of the most meaningful parts of getting ready for marriage, because they prepare not only for the wedding day but for the home and marriage that follow.

## What a kallah learns

Kallah classes are personal and practical. They usually cover:

- **[Family purity](/learn/family-purity)** (taharat hamishpacha): the monthly rhythm of separation and reunion between husband and wife, and how to keep it day to day.
- **The [mikvah](/learn/mikvah):** what it is, how to prepare, and what the first immersion before the wedding is like.
- **Building a Jewish home:** [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles), [separating challah](/learn/hafrashat-challah), a kosher kitchen, a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) on every door, and the warmth of a Jewish table.
- **Shalom bayit:** how Judaism sees marriage, respect, communication and making peace. See [Shalom Bayit](/learn/shalom-bayit).
- **The wedding itself:** the meaning of the customs, the fast and prayers of the wedding day, and the week of [Sheva Brachot](/learn/sheva-brachot).

No one is expected to know any of this beforehand. Many brides come with little background, and that is perfectly fine.

## When to start

It is best to begin a few months before the wedding. This leaves time to learn without rushing, to ask questions, and to review. The teacher also helps the bride plan her wedding date around her cycle and her first mikvah immersion, so it helps to start early, ideally before the date is set. See [Choosing a Wedding Date](/learn/choosing-a-wedding-date).

## How the classes work

- **Private and one-on-one.** Classes are usually held with just the bride and her teacher, at a pace that suits her.
- **Several sessions.** The number depends on the bride's background and questions.
- **Confidential.** Nothing personal is shared. Every question is welcome, however basic or personal.
- **An ongoing relationship.** After the wedding, many women keep their kallah teacher as the person they call with questions for years.

## Why it matters

The Sages call a Jewish home a "small sanctuary." The laws a bride learns before her wedding bring holiness, renewal and closeness into married life. Many women say their kallah classes shaped the way they think about marriage, and gave them a lifelong sense of meaning and pride.

## Common questions

### I am not very observant. Are kallah classes for me?
Yes. Every Jewish bride is welcome, whatever her background. The classes start wherever you are and are warm and without judgment.

### My fiancé is not observant. Can I still come?
Of course. Many brides start learning on their own, and many grooms join in later. The Rabbi is also glad to meet with your fiancé for [chosson classes](/learn/chosson-classes).

### How long before the wedding should I call?
A few months before is ideal, and before the date is set if possible. If your wedding is sooner, call anyway; classes can be arranged to fit.
