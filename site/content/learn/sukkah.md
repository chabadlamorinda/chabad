A sukkah is a temporary hut with a roof of branches or other plant material, where Jews eat their meals during the seven days of Sukkot. It recalls the clouds of glory that protected our ancestors in the desert, and how G-d still shelters us today.

## What is a sukkah?

The Torah says, "You shall dwell in sukkot for seven days... so that your generations will know that I made the Children of Israel dwell in sukkot when I brought them out of the land of Egypt" (Leviticus 23:42 to 43).

The most important part of a sukkah is the roof, called schach. It must be made of something that grew from the ground and is now detached, like branches, bamboo or palm leaves. The walls can be made of almost any sturdy material.

## Why we sit in a sukkah

- **Remembering G-d's care.** The sukkah recalls the clouds of glory that surrounded the Jewish people for forty years in the desert.
- **Trust.** Leaving the comfort of our home for a simple hut reminds us that our security comes from G-d.
- **Unity.** The sukkah is a mitzvah you enter with your whole body. Chassidic teaching says it surrounds every person equally, from head to toe.

## How to build a kosher sukkah, step by step

1. **Choose the spot.** It must be under the open sky, not under a tree, balcony or roof overhang.
2. **Build the walls.** You need at least two full walls and part of a third. Most people build three or four. The walls must be sturdy enough not to sway in a normal wind.
3. **Check the height.** The inside should be at least about 40 inches (10 handbreadths) high, and not more than about 30 feet (20 cubits).
4. **Check the size.** The minimum is about 28 by 28 inches (7 by 7 handbreadths), but build it big enough for your table and guests.
5. **Put on the schach.** Place it after the walls are up. Use branches, bamboo poles, or schach mats made for this purpose. Do not use anything that was processed into a utensil, food, or a material that can become impure, such as metal, plastic or fabric.
6. **Make it shady.** The schach must give more shade than sun inside, but ideally you can still see some stars through it at night.

Decorating the sukkah with fruit, pictures and lights is a beautiful custom.

## The blessing

Each time you eat bread or cake in the sukkah, say this blessing after the blessing on the food.

**Hebrew:** בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו, וְצִוָּנוּ לֵישֵׁב בַּסֻּכָּה

**Transliteration:** Baruch atah Ado-nai, Elo-heinu melech ha'olam, asher kid'shanu b'mitzvotav, v'tzivanu leishev basukkah.

**Translation:** Blessed are You, L-rd our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments, and commanded us to dwell in the sukkah.

## Living in the sukkah

During Sukkot, meals with bread or grain foods are eaten in the sukkah. On the first night, eating at least an olive-sized piece of bread in the sukkah is an obligation. Many also drink and snack there, and some sleep in it. Chabad custom is to eat everything, even water, only in the sukkah, but not to sleep there.

## Common questions

### What if it rains?
If rain would spoil your food, you are exempt and may eat indoors, except on the first night, when you should wait a while or make Kiddush and eat a little bread in the sukkah. Chabad custom is to eat in the sukkah even in the rain. Ask the Rabbi for guidance.

### Can I build a sukkah on my deck or balcony?
Yes, if nothing is above it but open sky. A balcony with another balcony overhead does not work.

### When do I take the sukkah down?
Keep it up through the end of Sukkot. It may be taken down after the holiday.

### Do women have to eat in the sukkah?
Women are not obligated, since it is a time-bound mitzvah, but they are welcome to eat in the sukkah. Customs differ on whether women say the blessing, so ask the Rabbi what to do in your family.
