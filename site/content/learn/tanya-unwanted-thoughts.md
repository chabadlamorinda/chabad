Tanya teaches that an unwanted thought is not a sin and not a verdict on who you are; what matters is what you do with it. Chapters 12, 27 and 28 say to push the thought away right away, not to argue with it, and, in Chapter 27, even to feel glad that you chose well.

For people who worry that their mind is "bad," this may be one of the most freeing ideas in the [Tanya](/learn/tanya).

## A thought is not a deed

According to Chapter 12 of the Tanya, the beinoni, Tanya's everyday ideal (see [the beinoni](/learn/the-beinoni)), may still find that a wrong thought rises on its own, because the heart still craves such things. What makes him who he is, is that he rejects it at once and does not dwell on it or plan to act. Tanya's image is that he thrusts it aside "as it were with both hands."

Willingly entertaining the thought is different; that is a choice. But the thought arriving is not.

## Chapter 27: rejoice, don't be crushed

Chapter 27 takes this further. It speaks of sinful thoughts that come while a person is busy with ordinary affairs. If he pushes them away, he fulfills the Torah's command not to follow after his heart and eyes, and the chapter says this should make him glad. The chapter quotes the Sages' teaching that when a person passively refrains from sin, he is rewarded as though he had actively done a mitzvah.

Tanya adds two honest points:

- **Being very upset can come from pride.** A person who is devastated that such thoughts come to him has forgotten who he is. He is not a tzaddik, and the chapter says that tzaddikim do not have such thoughts. (This is about ordinary passing thoughts; thoughts that frighten you and will not stop are a different matter, see below.)
- **Some discomfort is right, but not despair.** Being completely indifferent would end the struggle. But even if the battle lasts a lifetime, Tanya says not to be deeply depressed over it.

## Chapter 28: thoughts during prayer and study

What about thoughts that arrive when you are trying to pray or learn? Chapter 28 says these are not a cause for joy, as they disturb the service, but also not a reason to give up. Tanya's guidance:

1. **Do not argue.** Answering the thought only invites another one. The chapter compares it to someone who heckles you to throw you off; the best response is to act as if you do not hear him.
2. **Turn your attention back right away** and strengthen your focus.
3. **If it will not leave, ask G-d for compassion,** as a father has mercy on his children.
4. **Do not be discouraged or feel worthless.** Pray with more joy and resolve.

The chapter even suggests that when the Divine soul strives harder in prayer, the animal soul may push back with distraction, so an intruding thought can mean that your prayer has real effort in it. This is an important comfort for anyone whose mind wanders in the [prayer](/learn/kavanah-in-prayer).

## When thoughts are more than a distraction

Tanya's guidance here is spiritual. If unwanted thoughts are frequent, frightening or repetitive, or you cannot stop them, this can be a medical matter such as obsessive-compulsive disorder or anxiety, and professional help is also right when needed. Please talk to a doctor or therapist. Learning Tanya and getting help can go side by side. For a personal situation, ask the Rabbi. See [anxiety and worry](/learn/anxiety-and-worry).

## Common questions

### Are unwanted thoughts a sin in Judaism?
Tanya does not treat a thought that comes against your will as a sin. The beinoni rejects it at once and does not dwell on it. Willingly entertaining a wrong thought is a different matter.

### What does Tanya say to do with a bad thought?
Push it away right away, as the text says, "with both hands," and turn your mind to something holy or useful. Do not argue with it.

### What should I do about thoughts during prayer?
According to Chapter 28, ignore them as if you cannot hear them, turn back to the words, and ask G-d for help if needed. Do not conclude that your prayer is worthless.

### Should I feel guilty when a bad thought comes?
Tanya says no guilt over a thought you reject; Chapter 27 says to be glad. Feeling devastated can come from forgetting that you are not a tzaddik.

### What if I cannot stop intrusive thoughts?
Please see a doctor or therapist; this can be a medical issue and help is available. Tanya's guidance is spiritual, and professional help is also right.
