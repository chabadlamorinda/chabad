Jeremiah (יִרְמְיָהוּ, Yirmiyahu) was the prophet who warned the Jewish people for about forty years before the destruction of the First Temple in Jerusalem, who mourned it in the Book of Lamentations (Eichah), and who then gave the exiles a message of hope and return.

## Who was Jeremiah?

According to Chabad.org, Jeremiah was born into a family of priests in Anatoth, in the territory of Benjamin. His father was Hilkiah, the High Priest. He began prophesying under King Josiah, when he was young, and said "I am still a boy." G-d reassured him, and he never stopped speaking, at risk of his life. Two other voices of his time were the prophet Zephaniah and the prophetess Huldah. See [what is a prophet](/learn/what-is-a-prophet).

## His message

Jeremiah called the people back to G-d. He recalled their devotion in the wilderness, and he said they had abandoned the source of living waters. He taught that wisdom, wealth and power have no lasting value. What matters is to know G-d and to practice kindness, justice and righteousness. The person who trusts in G-d is like a tree planted by water.

After the good king Josiah fell in battle, the kings who followed were worse. Jeremiah warned that an invasion would come, and he was ridiculed and imprisoned. Even in his warnings, he said G-d would not destroy His people completely.

## The destruction and Lamentations

Jeremiah lived to see Jerusalem and the [Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple) destroyed. He wrote Eichah, Lamentations, which Jews read on [Tisha B'Av](/learn/tisha-bav-for-beginners) as we remember. See also [the Three Weeks](/learn/three-weeks). The destruction is a time of mourning, but Jeremiah's words also give courage.

## A letter to the exiles

Jeremiah sent a letter to the Jews who were exiled to Babylon. Chabad.org tells us that he told them not to believe false prophets who promised a quick return. Instead: "Build houses and dwell in them, plant gardens and eat their fruit." Marry, have children, and "seek the peace of the city" where you live and pray for it. He said the exile would last seventy years, and then G-d would bring them home, with plans "for peace and not for evil, to give you a future and a hope." The prophecy came true, and the letter has guided Jews through generations living far from home: live fully as Jews wherever you are, and be a blessing to your neighbors. This is a message for Jews in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda today.

## The promise of return

Jeremiah also spoke of Rachel weeping for her children, and G-d replying: "Restrain your voice from weeping... your children will return to their own border." We read this on Rosh Hashanah. Tradition sees Rachel as the mother who cries for every Jewish child, and G-d's answer is a promise that none are lost.

## What Jeremiah teaches us in hard times

- Tell the truth with love.
- Don't lose hope. Even after tragedy, G-d promises a future.
- Build. Plant and build even while waiting for better times.

## Common questions

### Who was the prophet Jeremiah?
Jeremiah was a Jewish prophet who warned the people before the Temple's destruction, and who comforted them with a promise of return.

### Why do we read Lamentations?
It is read on Tisha B'Av to mourn the destruction of the Temples. Jewish tradition attributes it to Jeremiah.

### What did Jeremiah say to the exiles?
He told them to build homes, plant gardens, raise families and pray for the peace of the city where they lived.

### How long did the Babylonian exile last?
Jeremiah foretold seventy years, after which the Jews returned and built the Second Temple.

### What is "Rachel weeping for her children"?
It is Jeremiah's image of the matriarch Rachel mourning her exiled children, with G-d's promise that they will return.
