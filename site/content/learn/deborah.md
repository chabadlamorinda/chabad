Deborah (דְּבוֹרָה) was a prophetess and the only woman among the judges who led the Jewish people after Joshua. She judged Israel from beneath a palm tree, led the nation to victory over the Canaanites with Barak, and composed a song of thanks to G-d that we read in synagogue to this day.

## Who was Deborah?

Chabad.org teaches that Deborah was the fourth of the judges and one of the seven women prophets of the Bible. She lived in the hills of Ephraim, between Ramah and Beth-El. The people had drifted from the Torah, and G-d let them be oppressed for twenty years by Jabin, king of Canaan, whose general Sisera had many iron chariots. When they cried out, G-d sent Deborah.

## Under the palm tree

Deborah sat to judge in the open air, under a palm tree, so that no one would be alone with her in a closed room. People came for guidance, and she taught them to return to G-d. Our Sages note that her husband Lapidoth supplied thick wicks for the lights of the Sanctuary at Shiloh, and they saw in this a picture of how she spread the light of Torah. Her palm tree, standing tall with its branches up, is a symbol of the Jewish people lifting their eyes to heaven.

## Deborah and Barak

Deborah called Barak son of Avinoam and told him to gather ten thousand men at Mount Tabor. Barak said he would go only if she came with him. She agreed, but told him that the honor of the victory would go to a woman.

G-d threw Sisera's army into confusion. Rain turned the battlefield to mud, the chariots were stuck, and the army fled. Sisera escaped on foot to the tent of Yael (Jael), who gave him milk and, when he fell asleep, ended the threat to the Jewish people. The honor went to Yael, as Deborah had said. Afterward the land had peace for forty years.

## The Song of Deborah

Deborah and Barak sang a song of thanks, recorded in the Book of Judges (chapter 5). It is the haftarah we read with the portion Beshalach, when we also read the Song at the Sea. This is why that Shabbat is called [Shabbat Shirah](/learn/shabbat-shirah), the Shabbat of Song. See [what a haftarah is](/learn/haftarah). The song praises those who stepped forward, gently rebukes those who held back, and gives thanks to G-d.

## What Deborah teaches today

Chabad teaching stresses the strength of Jewish women in every generation. Here are lessons for women, girls and families:

- **Lead with humility and faith.** Deborah led as a servant of G-d, giving the credit to G-d.
- **Light the way.** Her wicks teach that one person can bring light to many.
- **Work together.** Deborah and Barak led as a team.
- **Give thanks and sing.** Victory turns into a song.

Deborah is a wonderful person to learn about before a [Bat Mitzvah](/learn/bat-mitzvah). See also [Miriam](/learn/miriam) and [Jewish women](/learn/jewish-women).

## Common questions

### Who was Deborah in the Bible?
Deborah was a prophetess and the only woman among the judges of Israel, who led the nation after the death of Joshua.

### Why did Deborah sit under a palm tree?
She judged in the open so that she would never be alone in a closed room with men, and the palm tree became a symbol of the Jewish people looking up to heaven.

### Who was Barak?
Barak was the general who led ten thousand men from Naphtali and Zebulun into battle on Deborah's word.

### What is the Song of Deborah?
It is the song of thanks Deborah and Barak sang after the victory, in the Book of Judges chapter 5.

### When do we read about Deborah?
Her song is the haftarah of the portion Beshalach on Shabbat Shirah. See [this week's parsha](/parsha).
