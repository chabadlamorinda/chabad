Jewish law generally does not permit autopsy, because the body remains holy after death and must be treated with honor and buried whole. There are rare exceptions, for example when the law requires it or when it may save a life, and each case must be decided by a rabbi, never by the hospital alone.

If you are facing this question now, please stop and call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242 before signing anything. We are so sorry for your loss, and you do not have to decide this alone.

## Why Judaism protects the body after death

The Torah teaches that every person is created in the image of G-d. According to the Chabad.org Jewish funeral guide, this holiness does not end when the soul departs: "One may not do violence to the human form even when the breath of life has expired." The body is laid to rest tenderly and lovingly, as a final act of respect. Chabad teaching explains that the soul still feels a connection to the body in the first days after death, which is why tradition treats it with such care. For the full picture of the days after a death, see [the Jewish funeral](/learn/jewish-funeral) and [the Chevra Kadisha and tahara](/learn/chevra-kadisha-and-tahara).

## When an autopsy may be required or permitted

Chabad.org explains that there are exceptions to the general rule. Some examples it lists:

- **Required by the authorities.** When a death falls under government jurisdiction (for example a suspected crime or an accident), the medical examiner decides. This is the law of the land, and a rabbi can help the family with what follows.
- **Medical need of the living.** For instance, a hereditary disease where the findings could protect relatives, or when another known person has the same deadly illness and a qualified medical authority believes an autopsy could yield vital information.
- **A request by the person who died.** Rabbis differ on this, and it must be judged individually.

These are examples, not a checklist you can apply yourself. Whether a particular case qualifies is a question for a rabbi who understands both Jewish law and the medical facts. Chabad.org adds that the decision should not be made by the doctor alone, however close the doctor is to the family.

## What to do, step by step

1. **Call the Rabbi first.** Phone 925-384-7242, or visit [talk to the Rabbi](/help/talk-to-the-rabbi). Say that a death has occurred and an autopsy has been mentioned.
2. **Do not sign a consent form yet.** Consent is usually given by the person legally responsible for burial, often a spouse or child. Take the time to speak with the Rabbi first.
3. **Ask what is legally required.** We make no legal claims here. If the authorities say an autopsy is required, tell the Rabbi, who can guide you on how to protect the dignity of the body.
4. **If an autopsy is performed,** Chabad.org notes that any removed part of the body should be returned so it can be buried with the person. The Chevra Kadisha (the holy burial society) will explain how.
5. **Let us carry the practical load.** Burial is meant to take place quickly, and the Rabbi can help with the funeral, [aninut](/learn/aninut) (the time between death and burial) and [shiva](/learn/shiva).

## Common questions

### Does Judaism allow autopsy?
Generally no, because the body is holy and must be buried whole. Autopsy is permitted only in unusual cases, such as a legal requirement or a real medical need, and a rabbi must be asked.

### What should I do if the hospital asks for permission for an autopsy?
Do not sign yet, and call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242. He will help you understand the options and speak with those involved.

### What if the law requires an autopsy?
When the authorities require it, that is a different situation from a voluntary one. Tell the Rabbi right away so he can guide you on how to protect the body's dignity and on the burial.

### Can the family decide for itself that an autopsy is fine?
It is not a decision for the family or the doctor alone. Ask a rabbi, who will weigh the Jewish law and the medical facts.

### Why is there so much emphasis on quick burial?
Jewish tradition honors the person by laying them to rest without delay. See [the Jewish funeral](/learn/jewish-funeral) for what happens and why.

### Is organ donation the same question?
No. Saving a life is among the highest values of Jewish law, and the rules for organ donation are different. Read [organ donation, the Jewish view](/learn/organ-donation-jewish-view) and ask the Rabbi.
