The easiest Chanukah crafts for kids use things you already have at home: a potato for stamping wrapping paper, a jar for a dreidel snow globe, air-dry clay for dreidels, and paper and markers for cards. These all work well, and each one ends with something the family can use or give during the eight days.

## Potato-stamp Chanukah wrapping paper

This is a simple project for school-age children, with a grown-up handling the knife.

1. Cut a large potato in half and press a metal Chanukah cookie cutter (a menorah, dreidel or star) about half an inch into the cut side.
2. An adult carefully cuts away the potato around the cutter so the shape stands out. Pat the stamp dry with a paper towel.
3. Pour a little acrylic paint on a plate, dip the stamp and press it onto paper, fabric napkins or a tablecloth. Fabric paint works best on cloth.

Use the paper for the presents you will give, or for a card for a grandparent.

## A glittery dreidel snow globe

A [dreidel](/learn/dreidel) is a four-sided spinning top marked with the Hebrew letters nun, gimmel, hey and shin, for "Nes gadol hayah sham," "A great miracle happened there." The snow globe uses two small dreidels:

1. Glue one dreidel flat inside a jar lid and a second on top of it, with hot glue. This step is for an adult. If the dreidels are wood, a coat of clear nail polish first keeps them from molding.
2. Let the glue dry completely.
3. Fill the jar with water, add a tablespoon or two of glycerin (it slows the falling glitter) and the glitter.
4. Screw the lid on tightly, turn the jar over and watch it sparkle.

## More ideas children love

- **Clay dreidels.** Shape air-dry clay in several colors, add the four letters and let them dry. Then play with them.
- **Chanukah cards.** Cardstock, markers and stickers are enough. Younger children can color a printed page. Send the cards to a grandparent or a neighbor.
- **Canvas art.** Tape the shape of a menorah or dreidel on a small canvas with masking tape, paint the whole canvas, and peel the tape off when dry to reveal the design.
- **A giant menorah of building bricks.** Let a group of kids build one together, a good activity for a party. See our guide to [hosting a Chanukah party](/learn/hosting-a-chanukah-party).
- **Decorate a donut.** Ready-made donuts, icing and sprinkles. See [sufganiyot](/learn/sufganiyot), the jelly doughnuts of Chanukah.
- **A gelt purse or bag.** A homemade pouch for [Chanukah gelt](/learn/chanukah-gelt) makes a nice present. Many families find a video helpful for this one.

## A craft menorah and the real thing

Children love making their own menorah from clay, wood or jars. It makes a lovely decoration and a great way to learn how a menorah looks: eight lights in a straight row, plus the shamash, the helper candle, set apart. (A Chanukah menorah has straight arms, in a single line, with the eight lights at the same height.)

If you want to actually light a craft menorah, ask first: the mitzvah is done with real oil or wax flames, so the holders must be sturdy, deep enough for the oil or candle and made of something that does not burn. When in doubt, display the craft menorah and light a proper one beside it. See [how to light the menorah](/learn/chanukah-menorah) and [menorah fire safety](/learn/menorah-fire-safety). Never leave a child alone with a lit flame.

## Common questions

### What are easy Chanukah crafts for kids?
Potato-stamp wrapping paper, clay dreidels, handmade cards, a dreidel snow globe and a decorated donut are all simple and use common materials.

### What is a good Chanukah craft for toddlers?
Stamping with a potato shape, finger painting or coloring a printed menorah page, with an adult guiding every step. Avoid small pieces and glitter that can be swallowed.

### Can I light a menorah my child made?
Only if it is safe and made for real flames, with sturdy non-flammable holders and the eight lights in a straight row. Otherwise keep it as a decoration. Ask the Rabbi if you are unsure.

### What letters go on a dreidel?
Nun, gimmel, hey and shin, which stand for "A great miracle happened there." In Israel the last letter is pey, for "here."

### How can crafts teach my child about Chanukah?
Talk while you work: why we light eight nights, why we light where others can see the menorah, and what a miracle means. Children remember what they make with their hands.
