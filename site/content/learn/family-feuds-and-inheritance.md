Few things hurt like a family torn apart. Siblings stop speaking over a parent's estate, cousins over a family business, relatives over who did more for an aging parent. The Torah takes these rifts seriously, and offers a path back.

## Where family disputes come from

- **Inheritances and wills,** especially when they are unclear or seem unfair.
- **Caring for aging parents:** who visits, who pays, who decides. See [caring for aging parents](/learn/caring-for-aging-parents).
- **Family businesses** and shared property.
- **Old hurts** that resurface at weddings, funerals and holidays.

## Preventing the fight

- **Make a clear will,** and a [halachic will](/learn/halachic-will) so it is valid in Jewish law too.
- **Talk about it while parents are alive.** Explain decisions, so children don't have to guess.
- **Keep careful records** of who paid for what in a parent's care.

## When the dispute has started

1. **Pause before you speak.** Grief and money are a hard mix; give it time after a loss.
2. **Separate the money from the relationship.** "I want to work this out fairly, and I want us to stay family."
3. **Avoid going through lawyers first.** Legal battles between relatives often cost more than the inheritance and the relationship together.
4. **Mediation and compromise (pesharah):** Jewish law praises a fair compromise that both sides accept.
5. **A Beth Din,** if needed: a rabbinic court can rule on the estate according to Jewish law and civil law agreements. See [disputes](/help/disputes).

## Bringing a family back together

- **Remember what the parent would have wanted:** almost always, children at peace with each other.
- **Use the calendar:** Yom Kippur, a yahrzeit, a simcha, are natural moments to reach out.
- **Take one small step:** a call, a message, an invitation.
- **Forgive, even when you are not asked.** See [forgiveness and making peace](/learn/forgiveness-and-making-peace).

## Common questions

### My sibling took more than their share. What can I do?
Start with a calm conversation, then mediation. If needed, a Beth Din or the courts can decide. Ask the Rabbi first about the right path.

### Does Jewish law require following my parent's will?
Generally, children should honor a parent's last wishes. There are details in Jewish law about how an estate passes; the Rabbi can explain them for your case.

### We haven't spoken in years. Is it too late?
It is never too late while you are both alive. Many families reconcile after decades.
