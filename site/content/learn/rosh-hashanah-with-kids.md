Rosh Hashanah is the birthday of the world and the start of the Jewish new year. For children it can be sweet in every sense: apples and honey, the sound of the shofar, and a fresh start.

## The new year, simply explained

"Rosh Hashanah means 'head of the year.' On this day G-d made the first person, Adam, and every year on this day we ask G-d to be our King again and to give us a good and sweet year. It is like a new beginning: we think about how we can be even better, and G-d gives everyone a fresh start."

## Hearing the shofar

The main mitzvah of Rosh Hashanah is to hear the shofar, a ram's horn, blown on both days (except on Shabbat). Its call is like a cry from the heart that wakes us up. Children, girls and boys, should hear it too, even if they come only for the shofar blowing and not the whole service. Teach them the three sounds before the holiday: tekiah (one long blast), shevarim (three short ones) and teruah (many quick ones). See our [shofar](/learn/shofar) guide.

## Apples in honey and other sweet foods

On the first night, dip a slice of apple in honey, say the blessing on the fruit, and then: "May it be Your will to renew for us a good and sweet year." Children love choosing their own apple and dipping it. Many families also eat pomegranate (may our good deeds be as many as its seeds), the head of a fish (to be a "head and not a tail"), and other foods with good wishes, called simanim. See [Rosh Hashanah foods](/learn/rosh-hashanah-simanim).

## Round challah

For Rosh Hashanah the challah is round, like a crown for G-d our King and like the year that goes round and round. We dip it in honey instead of salt. Kids can help braid and coil the dough into a circle; see our [challah](/learn/challah) guide.

## Tashlich as a family walk

On the afternoon of the first day (the second day, if the first is Shabbat), we walk to water with fish in it, like a pond, lake or creek, and say the Tashlich prayer, asking G-d to throw away our mistakes. Make it a family walk: children can look for the fish and say a few words along. See [Tashlich](/learn/tashlich).

## Making New Year cards

Before the holiday, children can make "Shanah Tovah" cards for grandparents, teachers and friends: draw a shofar, an apple, a honey jar or a pomegranate, and write "Ketivah vachatimah tovah" (may you be written and sealed for a good year).

## One good deed for the new year

Big resolutions are hard for anyone. Help each child choose one good thing to add: saying Modeh Ani in the morning, putting a coin in the [pushka](/learn/pushka) before school, or helping set the Shabbat table. Write it on a card and keep it on the fridge.

## What to bring to synagogue

A small bag makes a long morning easy: a snack and water, a children's siddur or Rosh Hashanah picture book, a quiet toy, and a sweater. Leave electronic devices at home, and plan a short walk outside when needed. More ideas are in [holidays with toddlers](/learn/holidays-with-toddlers).

## Common questions

### Do children need to stay for the whole service?
No. The most important part for children is hearing the shofar. Bring them for that, and stay as long as it is pleasant for them.

### Can a baby or toddler come to the shofar blowing?
Yes, everyone is welcome. If a little one cries, step out and come back in time to hear the blasts.

### What do we wish each other on Rosh Hashanah?
"Shanah Tovah" (a good year), or the full blessing "Ketivah vachatimah tovah," may you be written and sealed for a good and sweet year.
