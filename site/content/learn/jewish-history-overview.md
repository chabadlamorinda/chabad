Jewish history begins nearly four thousand years ago with Abraham, who taught the world that there is one G-d. It runs through the giving of the Torah at Sinai, two Holy Temples, long centuries of exile, and the remarkable survival of a people held together by Torah.

## The Patriarchs and Matriarchs

Abraham and Sarah left Mesopotamia for the land of Canaan, which G-d promised to their descendants. Their son Isaac and grandson Jacob, together with Rebecca, Rachel and Leah, are the founders of the Jewish people. Jacob, also called Israel, had twelve sons who became the twelve tribes.

## Egypt, Exodus and Sinai

Jacob's family went down to Egypt, where their descendants were later enslaved. Moses led them out in the [Exodus](/learn/exodus-story), about 1313 BCE according to the traditional count. Seven weeks later, at Mount Sinai, G-d gave the Torah. After forty years in the desert, Joshua led the people into the Land of Israel.

## Judges, Kings and the First Temple

For several centuries the people were led by judges and prophets. Then came the kings: Saul, David and Solomon. King Solomon built the First Temple in Jerusalem. After his time the kingdom split in two. The northern tribes were exiled by Assyria, and later the Babylonians destroyed [the Holy Temple](/learn/the-holy-temple) and exiled the people of Judea.

## The Second Temple

Jews returned under Ezra and Nehemiah and built the Second Temple. The Men of the Great Assembly set the basic form of Jewish prayer. In this period came the story of Chanukah, when the Maccabees rededicated the Temple. The Romans destroyed the Second Temple in 70 CE.

## The Sages

To preserve Torah in exile, Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi compiled the [Mishnah](/learn/mishnah) around 200 CE. The [Talmud](/learn/talmud) was completed a few centuries later in Babylonia. These became the foundation of Jewish law and learning.

## The long exile

Jewish communities spread across the Middle East, North Africa and Europe. Great teachers arose, among them Rashi in France and [Maimonides](/learn/maimonides) in Spain and Egypt. Jews often faced persecution, including the Crusades and the expulsion from Spain in 1492, yet Torah learning continued everywhere they went.

## Chassidus

In the 1700s, the [Baal Shem Tov](/learn/baal-shem-tov) began the Chassidic movement, teaching love of every Jew and joy in serving G-d. His student's student, Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, founded Chabad and wrote the [Tanya](/learn/tanya). The [Chabad Rebbes](/learn/chabad-rebbes) led the movement through the generations.

## Modern times

The twentieth century brought both tragedy and rebirth. Six million Jews were murdered in the Holocaust. Jewish life was rebuilt in Israel, America and around the world. After the war, the Lubavitcher Rebbe, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson, sent emissaries to communities everywhere to bring Jewish life to every Jew.

## The thread that holds it together

Empires rose and fell, yet the Jewish people survived. The Sages explain that the secret is the Torah and its mitzvot, passed down faithfully from parent to child for more than three thousand years.

## Common questions

### How old is the Jewish people?
Counting from Abraham, about 3,800 years. Counting from the giving of the Torah at Sinai, more than 3,300 years. The Hebrew calendar counts years from creation; see our [Jewish calendar](/learn/jewish-calendar) guide.

### What are the most important events in Jewish history?
Many would name the Exodus, the giving of the Torah at Sinai, the building and destruction of the two Temples, and the long exile. Each is remembered through a holiday or a fast day.

### How did Judaism survive without a Temple?
The Sages made prayer, Torah study and acts of kindness the center of Jewish life. The synagogue, the study hall and the Jewish home became the places where G-d's presence is felt.
