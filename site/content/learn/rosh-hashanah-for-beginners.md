Rosh Hashanah is the Jewish New Year, but it looks nothing like December 31st: a ram's horn, a round challah dipped in honey, a day in the synagogue, and a quiet sense of standing before G-d and starting again. If this is your first time, here is what to expect.

## What the day is

Rosh Hashanah falls on the first two days of the Hebrew month of Tishrei, usually in September or early October. Our sages teach that it is the anniversary of the creation of Adam and Eve.

That is the heart of the day: on Rosh Hashanah we crown G-d as King. Chassidic teaching explains that a king needs a people who want him, so each year we renew the relationship: we accept G-d as our King, and He renews His care for the world. It is also the Day of Judgment, when the year ahead is written: a day of hope, not fear, when the slate is wiped clean. See [the month of Elul](/learn/elul), the month of preparation before it.

## The shofar: the one mitzvah of the day

The central mitzvah is to hear the [shofar](/learn/shofar), a ram's horn blown during the morning service (except when the first day falls on Shabbat), a hundred blasts in all.

Why a horn? The shofar is a cry without words. Maimonides calls it a wake-up call: "Sleepers, awake from your slumber." Chassidic teaching adds that it is the cry of the soul itself, calling to its Father like a child who has no words, only a voice. You need no Hebrew for this mitzvah; you only need to be in the room and listen. If you can come for one thing, come for the shofar. Many communities arrange a later shofar blowing for those who could not come; call the Rabbi if you need to hear it.

## The festive meals

Both evenings begin with candle lighting and a holiday meal much like a Shabbat dinner, with Kiddush and challah. The challah is round, for a year that comes full circle, and dipped in honey. Then come the *simanim*, symbolic foods with a short wish each: an apple in honey for a sweet year, a pomegranate for a year full of merits, the head of a fish so that we may be "a head and not a tail." See [the Rosh Hashanah simanim](/learn/rosh-hashanah-simanim).

## Tashlich

On the afternoon of the first day (or the second, if the first is Shabbat), we walk to water with fish in it, a creek or a pond, and say a short prayer about G-d casting our sins "into the depths of the sea." It is short, outdoors and wonderful for children. See [tashlich](/learn/tashlich).

## Candles and greetings

Candles are lit before the holiday begins on both evenings, with the festival blessing and [Shehecheyanu](/learn/shehecheyanu). See [Shabbat and holiday times](/shabbat-times) for Lafayette.

The greeting is "Shanah tovah" (a good year) or "Shanah tovah u'metukah" (a good and sweet year); on the first night many add "May you be written and sealed for a good year." See [holiday greetings](/learn/holiday-greetings).

## If you can only come for part

Come anyway. If you can be there for the shofar, that is the mitzvah itself. If you can only come for the evening meal, you will hear Kiddush and taste the apple and honey. Chassidic teaching stresses that on Rosh Hashanah G-d wants above all our heart: one sincere moment of "You are my King, and I want to be Yours" is the whole day in miniature.

Bring the kids; see [Rosh Hashanah with kids](/learn/rosh-hashanah-with-kids). Ten days later comes [Yom Kippur](/learn/yom-kippur-for-beginners); see [the holiday page](/holidays/rosh-hashanah) for this year's dates and events.

## Common questions

### I don't read Hebrew. Can I still take part?
Yes, fully. Chabad prayer books generally have English on every page, the rabbi explains what is happening, and hearing the shofar needs no reading at all. See [your first time at synagogue](/learn/first-time-at-synagogue).

### What should I wear?
Neat and modest, as for a nice occasion. Men wear a kippah (most synagogues keep extras at the door), and many wear something white or new in honor of the day.

### Is Rosh Hashanah one day or two?
Two days, everywhere in the world, including Israel, each a full holiday with the shofar, meals and services.
