Yes: in California, a landlord or an HOA generally cannot ban a mezuzah on the entry door or door frame of your home. Since 2020 two laws, Civil Code sections 1940.45 (rentals) and 4706 (HOAs and condo associations), protect religious items there, with a few narrow limits. This is general information, not legal advice.

For Jewish families renting or owning in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, this means you can fulfill the mitzvah of mezuzah on the front door with confidence.

## What the law says

Both sections came from SB 652 (2019) and took effect January 1, 2020 (bill text). According to summaries of the statutes on Davis-Stirling and by Epsten, they work like this:

- **Landlords (section 1940.45):** a landlord may not enforce a rule that bans displaying a religious item on the entry door or door frame of your rental, as long as you display it because of your sincere religious beliefs.
- **HOAs and condo associations (section 4706):** the association's governing documents cannot prohibit a member from displaying a religious item on the entry door or door frame of their home.
- **The limits.** The rules can still apply if the item threatens public health or safety, interferes with opening or closing the door, violates a law, or has obscene content, or if it is larger than 36 by 12 inches in total, together with any other religious items on the door. If the association is repairing or replacing the door or frame, it may ask you to take the item down temporarily, with notice to you, and you may put it back afterward.

A mezuzah case is only a few inches tall, so the size limit is almost never an issue. Please read the current text on the state's official site, or ask a lawyer, if a dispute arises. The protection covers the entry door and frame of your own home, not hallways, lobbies or common areas.

## If your landlord or HOA objects

1. **Stay friendly.** Many objections come from a misunderstanding. A short, polite note works.
2. **Cite the law.** Mention Civil Code 1940.45 (rentals) or 4706 (HOAs), and say that a mezuzah is a small religious item that does not affect the door or safety.
3. **Keep it simple:** use screws or strong double-sided tape, and do not damage the frame.
4. **Ask for help.** If the issue continues, speak with an attorney or a tenants' or homeowners' organization. We cannot give legal advice.

## The Jewish side: renters and mezuzah

A renter keeps the mitzvah too. According to Chabad.org's guidance, outside Israel the obligation for a rented home begins after thirty days, but the Chabad custom, as advised by the Rebbe, is to put up the mezuzahs right away when moving in, without a blessing at first, and to ask the Rabbi about the details. Chabad.org also says a mezuzah should not be removed from a doorpost and left bare unless it will be put back or replaced with another.

**When you move out:** if the next tenant is Jewish, ask the Rabbi about leaving the mezuzahs in place; Chabad.org says to leave them unless there is concern they will be thrown away or disrespected. If the new tenant is unknown, take them with you and have them checked. A mezuzah is holy and should never be thrown away. ## Sukkahs, menorahs and common areas

The statutes above are about the entry door and frame. A sukkah on a balcony or a menorah in the window is a different matter: your lease, building rules and fire safety may all apply. The best approach is to speak with the board or manager early, offer to follow safety rules, and ask the Rabbi for a solution that fits. ## Common questions

### Can my HOA tell me to take down my mezuzah in California?

Generally no. Civil Code 4706 stops an association from prohibiting religious items on the entry door or frame, with limits for safety, door operation, legality and size. This is not legal advice.

### Can a landlord in California ban a mezuzah?

Generally no. Civil Code 1940.45 protects displaying a religious item on a rental's entry door or door frame, within the same limits.

### How big can a mezuzah be under California law?

The statutes allow limits only for religious items larger than 36 by 12 inches in total on the door or frame. A mezuzah is far smaller.

### Do I need a mezuzah if I rent?

Yes. Chabad.org says the obligation begins after thirty days outside Israel, but the Chabad custom is to put them up on moving in. Ask the Rabbi about the details.

### Should I take my mezuzah when I move out of a rental?

If the next tenant is Jewish, leave it in place, unless there is concern it will be discarded. Otherwise take it and have it checked. Ask the Rabbi.
