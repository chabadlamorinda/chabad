In Jewish law, you do not formally sit shiva for a grandparent. The laws of mourning apply to a father, mother, husband or wife, son, daughter, brother and sister; grandparents are not among them.

That does not mean the loss is small. Grandchildren grieve deeply, and Jewish tradition gives you many meaningful ways to honor a grandparent and to support your family.

## Who formally mourns

The seven close relatives are mourners in Jewish law: father, mother, husband or wife, son, daughter, brother and sister, including half-siblings. The list comes from the Torah (Leviticus 21:1 to 3). See [who sits shiva](/learn/who-sits-shiva).

So when your grandparent passes away, it is your parent, the grandparent's child, who sits [shiva](/learn/shiva) and who is a mourner for the year. A grandchild is not obligated in these laws.

If you are unsure about a particular custom, such as whether to say Kaddish for a grandparent or to observe any mourning practices, please ask the Rabbi. Practice can depend on the family and the situation.

## Supporting your parent

You may be the one who helps the mourners most. In the mitzvah of comforting mourners (nichum aveilim), visitors may talk about the person's life, join in prayer, give charity or simply be present, without expecting the mourners to appear cheerful.

- Be with your parent in the shiva house.
- Listen and share memories.
- At the funeral, say the traditional words to the mourners. See [comforting mourners](/learn/comforting-mourners).
- Help with practical things, and see [what to bring to a shiva house](/learn/what-to-bring-to-a-shiva-house).

## Ways to honor a grandparent

There is nothing greater for a soul than to have more good deeds done in its merit. Children, relatives and friends are all encouraged to take this on.

1. **Learn Torah in their memory**, especially Mishnah. See [learning in memory of a loved one](/learn/learning-in-memory-of-a-loved-one).
2. **Give tzedakah** in their name.
3. **Take on a mitzvah**: for example, an improvement in a mitzvah you already keep.
4. **Light a [yahrzeit candle](/learn/yahrzeit-candle)** on their yahrzeit, and learn the date by the Hebrew calendar.
5. **Pass on their story.** Write down what they taught you, or what they lived through. The [ethical will](/learn/ethical-will) is a Jewish way to pass values on.

You can also read about [Jewish grandparents](/learn/jewish-grandparents) and the special place of grandparents in the Jewish family.

## Common questions

### Do you sit shiva for a grandparent?
No, not formally. The seven relatives who sit shiva are a father, mother, husband or wife, son, daughter, brother and sister. A grandchild can still visit, comfort and honor.

### Do grandchildren say Kaddish for a grandparent?
Kaddish rests first on the children of the deceased. Whether a grandchild says it is a question to ask the Rabbi.

### How can I honor my grandparent?
Learn Torah, give tzedakah, take on a mitzvah, light a yahrzeit candle and keep their memory alive with stories. Relatives and friends are all encouraged to do good deeds in the person's merit.

### How do I comfort my parent after my grandparent died?
Be present, listen, share memories and help with practical needs. Visitors do not need to cheer the mourner up.

### Should I go to my grandparent's funeral?
Yes. Accompanying the departed is one of the greatest acts of kindness, and your family will need you. See [what to wear to a Jewish funeral](/learn/what-to-wear-to-a-jewish-funeral).
