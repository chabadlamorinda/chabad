Women in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda generally use the Chabad mikvah in Berkeley, and the visit is arranged privately through Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski of Chabad of Lamorinda at 925-384-7241. You do not need to be a member of anything, and you do not need to have been to a mikvah before.

## Where is the mikvah near me?

A mikvah (מִקְוֶה) is a pool of natural water used for ritual immersion, and a kosher one must be built according to detailed Jewish law. For families in Lamorinda and the East Bay, the Chabad mikvah in Berkeley is the one women here generally use. Rather than searching for an address and opening hours, call or text the Rebbetzin. She will tell you how to arrange your visit and answer any question. This page does not list hours or fees, because they are best confirmed with her directly.

## How to arrange a visit, step by step

1. **Call or text the Rebbetzin** at 925-384-7241. You can also use the Call, Text and WhatsApp buttons on our [talk to the Rabbi](/help/talk-to-the-rabbi) page.
2. **Say what the visit is for**: a first visit, before a wedding, monthly immersion, or conversion. A few words are enough.
3. **Ask how to prepare.** Preparation is simple and she will walk you through it.
4. **Go with an easy heart.** Privacy and modesty are central, and the attendant is there only to help.

If you prefer to keep everything private, say so. It is the normal way.

## What a first visit is like

Many women are nervous before the first time, and many later call it deeply moving. In general, you bathe and prepare carefully at home or at the mikvah, the attendant makes sure nothing is in the way of the water, you immerse fully and say a blessing, and you are free to add a private prayer. The attendant is discreet and warm. Details are in our guide to [what a mikvah is](/learn/mikvah).

## When women go to the mikvah

- **Monthly**, for married women, as part of [family purity](/learn/family-purity) (taharat hamishpachah). This is personal and best learned privately.
- **Before a wedding.** A bride immerses shortly before the chuppah. See [Jewish weddings in Lamorinda](/learn/jewish-wedding-in-lamorinda) and [kallah classes](/learn/kallah-classes).
- **Conversion** includes immersion before a beit din. Read about [converting to Judaism](/learn/converting-to-judaism).

## What about men?

Some men immerse before Yom Kippur, and many Chassidic men immerse before prayer or Shabbat. If you would like to know where and how, ask Rabbi Labkowski at 925-384-7242.

## Common questions

### Is there a mikvah near Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda?

Women here generally use the Chabad mikvah in Berkeley. Contact Rebbetzin Tzipora Labkowski at 925-384-7241 to arrange a visit.

### How do I book a mikvah visit?

Call or text the Rebbetzin at 925-384-7241 and tell her briefly what the visit is for. She will help with the details and preparation.

### Is the mikvah private?

Yes. Privacy and modesty are central to mikvah, and every conversation with the Rebbetzin is confidential.

### I have never been to a mikvah. Is it too late?

It is never too late. A woman married for many years can begin at any time, and the Rebbetzin will guide you gently.

### Do I need to be a member of Chabad to use the mikvah?

No. Every Jew is welcome, and no membership is required to ask or to arrange a visit.

### Can I go to the mikvah before my wedding?

Yes. A bride traditionally immerses shortly before the wedding, and the Rebbetzin helps brides prepare. Call as early as you can.
