Judaism encourages remarriage. A widow, widower or divorced person is free to marry again, as long as Jewish law's requirements are met, and a get (a Jewish divorce document) must precede a second marriage if the first ended in divorce. The ceremony is joyful and simpler than a first wedding.

This guide follows Chabad.org's overview of remarriage. Since each case differs, please ask the Rabbi.

## Judaism wants you to have a full life

Chabad.org notes that Judaism "seeks to encourage those who are alone to seek meaningful and richer lives." A new marriage does not betray a late spouse, and the Torah says "it is not good for man to be alone" (Genesis 2:18). For many seniors in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, a second chapter brings companionship and joy.

## If your first marriage ended in divorce

A Jewish divorce, a [get](/learn/jewish-divorce), is required before a new Jewish marriage. A civil divorce is not enough under Jewish law. If you have not received a get, speak with the Rabbi first, before making any plans. The consequences of skipping it are serious, and the Rabbi will help gently and privately.

## If you are widowed

A widow or widower may marry again after mourning. Ask the Rabbi about the waiting period and about how it relates to [the year of mourning](/learn/year-of-mourning) and [sheloshim](/learn/sheloshim). In the rare case of a childless widow with a husband's brother, there is a special ceremony called chalitzah, and the Rabbi can explain. Honoring a late spouse, with a yahrzeit candle and Kaddish, goes on ([yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit)).

## How the ceremony differs

According to Chabad.org:

- The ketubah wording changes; the word for "maiden" is replaced by the word for a widow or a divorcee, and the amounts are adjusted ([ketubah](/learn/ketubah)).
- The seven days of festive meals with the sheva brachot are held for only one day, unless one of the two has never been married ([sheva brachot](/learn/sheva-brachot)).
- Some customs of a first wedding, such as the veil, are not required.
- Other details, including who walks and who escorts, are left to the couple.

Ask the Rabbi for precise guidance on your situation. See [Jewish wedding customs](/learn/jewish-wedding-customs).

## Children, family and feelings

Adult children may feel grief, loyalty or worry. Chabad.org suggests that the couple decide with "intelligence and sensitivity" whether children from earlier marriages take part. Go slowly, include them, speak plainly about the plans, and see [blended families](/learn/blended-families). Everyone is allowed to feel mixed emotions.

## Common questions

### Does Judaism allow a second marriage?
Yes. Judaism encourages remarriage for widows, widowers and divorced people.

### Do I need a get to remarry?
If your first marriage ended in divorce, yes. A Jewish divorce must precede remarriage; a civil divorce alone is not enough. Ask the Rabbi first.

### Can a widow or widower remarry?
Yes, after the mourning period. Ask the Rabbi for the timing.

### Is a second Jewish wedding different?
Somewhat. The ketubah wording differs, the sheva brachot are shorter, and some customs are optional.

### Should children come to the wedding?
That is for the couple to decide with sensitivity. Include them as warmly as possible.
