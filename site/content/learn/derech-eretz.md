Derech eretz (דֶּרֶךְ אֶרֶץ) literally means "the way of the land," and in everyday speech it means decency, courtesy and respect toward other people. The Sages teach that derech eretz comes before Torah: a person who is not decent cannot truly live a Torah life.

## What does derech eretz mean?

Chabad.org explains that the phrase refers to what people commonly do, or what they ought to do. It has several meanings in Jewish sources: ethical, responsible living, ordinary social behavior, being a respectful person, and earning a living. Today most people use it to mean being respectful, especially to parents, elders and teachers, although the article notes this is not the oldest meaning of the words.

The Talmud uses derech eretz for many small, practical behaviors: not eating too quickly or too much, not staring at others while they eat, not speaking too loudly, greeting people pleasantly, and spending only what you can afford. In short, it is living with dignity and being considerate of others.

## "Derech eretz comes before Torah"

In Ethics of Our Fathers (*Pirkei Avot*) 3:17, the Sages teach that if there is no Torah there is no derech eretz, and if there is no derech eretz there is no Torah. Chabad.org points out that good habits and manners are the groundwork on which Torah can stand. A person might know a lot of Torah, but if they treat people badly, something is missing. The reverse is true too: Torah refines our behavior. Read more in [Pirkei Avot](/learn/pirkei-avot) and [Being a Mensch](/learn/being-a-mensch).

Chabad teaching adds that good character is the base for everything. Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, the first Chabad Rebbe, taught that there can be no Torah without fine character. See [Chinuch](/learn/chinuch).

## What derech eretz looks like every day

**At home.** Say "please" and "thank you." Greet people when you come in. Honor your parents and listen to them. See [Honoring Parents](/learn/honoring-parents).

**At the table.** Wait until everyone is served, do not talk with a full mouth, and thank the cook. Do not criticize food. Offer to help clear the table.

**With elders and teachers.** Stand when an older person enters, offer a seat, and speak with respect. See [Honoring the Elderly](/learn/honoring-the-elderly).

**With waiters, drivers and cashiers.** Everyone is made in G-d's image. A smile and a thank you cost nothing. Be generous with kindness.

**With neighbors.** In Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, neighbors share streets and school pickup lines. A wave and a hello make a neighborhood.

**On the phone and in the car.** Do not interrupt, do not text while someone is talking to you, and drive with patience.

**Online.** Speak online as you would speak face to face. Avoid gossip and mockery. See [Lashon Hara](/learn/lashon-hara) on harmful speech.

## The second meaning: earning a living

In some Jewish sources, derech eretz means working for a living. In *Pirkei Avot* 2:2, Rabban Gamliel praises Torah study combined with derech eretz, because the effort of both keeps a person from sin. The idea is that a good life joins learning with honest work. The word itself, "the way of the land," is the ordinary way of the world, and Judaism says to bring holiness into it.

## Teaching it to children

Children learn manners by watching. Say the words yourself. Praise your child when you see kindness. Practice at the Shabbat table, where everyone takes a turn to speak. Read [Raising Kind Children](/learn/raising-kind-children).

## Common questions

### What does derech eretz mean?
Derech eretz means "the way of the land," and today it refers to decency, courtesy and respect toward others. In older Jewish sources it can also mean ethical living or earning a living.

### What does "derech eretz kadmah laTorah" mean?
It means "derech eretz comes before Torah." The Sages teach that decent behavior is the foundation for Torah, and that Torah without good character is incomplete.

### Is derech eretz a mitzvah?
It is a core Jewish value that runs through many mitzvot, such as honoring parents and elders and being kind to others. For specific questions, ask the Rabbi.

### How does Judaism teach respect for elders and teachers?
Jewish tradition asks us to honor elders and Torah teachers, for example by standing in their presence and speaking respectfully. Read the guide on honoring the elderly.

### How can I teach derech eretz to my children?
Model it daily, praise kind behavior, and practice at the table with simple rules. Make Shabbat dinner a place for listening to each other.
