Hebrew school is worth it when it gives a child what no other part of the week can: a Jewish identity they feel proud of, the ability to read Hebrew, friends who share their heritage, and real experience of Shabbat and the holidays. Chabad teaches that Jewish education (*chinuch*) is the greatest gift parents can give, because it lets a child grow up knowing who they are.

## What does Hebrew school actually give a child?

Parents often ask whether one more commitment is worth it. Here is what children usually gain:

- **Identity.** A child who knows the stories, songs and customs does not have to guess what being Jewish means. They carry it with confidence into middle school, high school and college.
- **Hebrew reading.** Being able to open a prayer book and read along changes how a child feels in synagogue. It takes time, so starting early helps.
- **Holidays that make sense.** Children who bake matzah, shake a lulav or hear the Megillah in context remember why we do these things.
- **Friends.** In a place like Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, many Jewish children are the only Jewish child in their class. Hebrew school gives them peers who understand their holidays.
- **Readiness for Bar and Bat Mitzvah.** A child who has learned steadily for years comes to [Bar or Bat Mitzvah](/learn/bar-mitzvah) prepared and relaxed, not cramming.

## What Chabad teaches about educating children

In Jewish tradition, parents have a duty called *chinuch* (חִנּוּךְ), "education": to train children in mitzvot as soon as they are able, so that the habits of a Jewish life are already theirs when they become adults. This begins very early, with simple things like blessings and the Shema around age three, and grows step by step. You can read more in our guide to [chinuch](/learn/chinuch).

The goal is not to force. It is to let a child taste Jewish life while they are young, so that later they choose it because it feels like their own. Hebrew school is one partner in that, along with the home.

## Hebrew school works best together with home

No class once a week can do it alone. Children pick up the most when what they learn on Sunday appears at home: a Shabbat candle, a blessing before dinner, a question about the parsha. Even ten minutes a day makes a difference (see [Jewish learning at home with kids](/learn/jewish-learning-at-home-with-kids)). When parents are curious too, children notice.

If you did not grow up with Hebrew school yourself, you are not behind. Many parents learn alongside their children, and adults are welcome to learn too.

## What to look for in a Hebrew school

- Teachers who are warm and who make children want to come back.
- Hands-on learning, with songs, crafts, stories and celebrations, not only desks and worksheets.
- A clear path for Hebrew reading.
- A welcoming spirit for every family, whatever their background or membership.
- Honest communication with parents.

## Common questions

### Is Hebrew school worth it if my child only goes once a week?
Yes, as long as the learning continues at home. A Sunday class gives structure, friends and a teacher; a few minutes at home through the week makes it stick.

### What age should a child start Hebrew school?
Chabad teaches that Jewish education starts as soon as a child can speak, and many children join a program around age three through songs and stories. Formal Hebrew reading usually begins a little later. Ask the Rabbi if you are unsure about your child.

### Will my child resist going to Hebrew school?
Some do. Children usually enjoy it more when classes are hands-on and friendly, when a friend comes along, and when parents show that it matters. If your child is unhappy, talk to the teachers.

### Does my family need to be religious to send a child?
No. Families from every background and level of observance are welcome at Chabad of Lamorinda, and no membership is required.

### Can a child still prepare for Bar or Bat Mitzvah if they start late?
Yes. Many children begin in later years and succeed with personal preparation. See [Bar and Bat Mitzvah in Lamorinda](/learn/bar-bat-mitzvah-in-lamorinda) or call us to talk about your child.

### Is Hebrew school only for children?
Hebrew school is for children, but adults can learn too. See [Hebrew reading for adults](/learn/hebrew-reading-for-adults).
