Erev Yom Kippur, the day before Yom Kippur, is a day of preparation with its own customs: ask forgiveness from anyone you have wronged, give tzedakah, immerse in a mikvah, eat two festive meals, ask for honey cake (lekach), bless your children, and light candles before the fast begins. Eating well is itself a mitzvah on this day.

## Why this day matters

Yom Kippur atones for sins between a person and G-d, but not for what we did to other people until we have made peace with them. So the day before Yom Kippur is the last chance to set things right. See [Yom Kippur for beginners](/learn/yom-kippur-for-beginners) for the whole holiday.

## A timeline for the day

1. **Morning: kapparot.** Early in the morning, many pass a chicken, or money, over their head while saying a traditional text; the chicken or its value is given to charity. Read [kapparot](/learn/kapparot) for the custom and the choice of money.
2. **Ask forgiveness.** Approach anyone you wronged, even by words, and name the wrong (unless that would embarrass them). If they refuse, try again, up to three times. The person asked should forgive quickly and not be stubborn.
3. **Ask for lekach.** It is customary to ask for, and to receive, a piece of honey cake during the day. It is an old custom; ask the Rabbi for its meaning.
4. **Mikvah.** Many immerse on this day. See [mikvah for men](/learn/mikvah-for-men).
5. **Give tzedakah.** Give generously. It is a great source of merit. Read [tzedakah](/learn/tzedakah).
6. **Festive meals.** The main mitzvah of the day is to eat and drink well. There are two festive meals, one earlier in the day and one just before the fast. Many families eat kreplach, filled dumplings.
7. **Minchah.** The afternoon service is prayed relatively early to leave time for the final meal. Before Minchah, men traditionally receive symbolic "lashes," a reminder to repent.
8. **Bless your children.** Before going to the synagogue, parents bless their children. The customary words invoke Ephraim and Menashe for sons, and Sarah, Rebecca, Rachel and Leah for daughters. Read [blessing your children](/learn/blessing-your-children).
9. **Light candles.** Women and girls light the holiday candles 18 minutes before sunset; check the time on [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times). Two blessings are said, one for the candles of Yom Kippur and Shehecheyanu. Memorial candles also burn: a 25-hour candle in the synagogue, and for those whose parents have passed away, one at home.
10. **Go to Kol Nidre.** The fast begins at candle lighting. Read [Kol Nidre](/learn/kol-nidre).

## When someone will not forgive you, or has passed away

If you try and are refused, the Alter Rebbe's Shulchan Aruch says you may ask again, and, if still refused, tell ten people that you tried. If the person has passed away, ten people gather at the grave and the wrong is named there. These situations are delicate. Ask the Rabbi, or read [forgiveness and making peace](/learn/forgiveness-and-making-peace).

## Common questions

### What do you do the day before Yom Kippur?
Ask forgiveness from anyone you wronged, give tzedakah, eat two festive meals, immerse in a mikvah, bless your children and light candles before the fast.

### Is it a mitzvah to eat on Erev Yom Kippur?
Yes. The main mitzvah of the day is to eat and drink well, with two festive meals.

### What is lekach on Erev Yom Kippur?
Lekach is honey cake. It is customary to ask for and receive a piece on this day.

### Who should I ask forgiveness from before Yom Kippur?
Anyone you have wronged, even by words. Name the wrong unless it would embarrass them.

### When do you light candles for Yom Kippur?
Women and girls light 18 minutes before sunset, and the fast begins. Check local times on [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times).

### Do you bless your children before Yom Kippur?
Yes. Parents bless their children before going to the synagogue, using the customary words or their own.
