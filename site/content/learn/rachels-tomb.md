Rachel's Tomb (Kever Rachel, קֶבֶר רָחֵל) is the grave of our matriarch Rachel, wife of Jacob, on the road to Bethlehem. Rachel is the one matriarch not buried in the Cave of Machpelah, and Jewish tradition teaches that she was buried by the road so that she could weep and plead for her children when they passed by in exile.

## Why Rachel was buried on the road

Rachel passed away giving birth to Benjamin while Jacob's family was traveling to Hebron. Jacob buried her near Ephrath, which is Bethlehem, and did not bring her to the family's burial cave. Years later, when Jacob was old and asking Joseph to bury him in Machpelah, he explained that he had buried her there by G-d's command (Genesis 48:7).

Rashi, the great commentator, explains why. Centuries later, when the Jewish people would be taken into exile after the destruction of the First Temple, they would pass along that road. Rachel would come out, weep and beg G-d for mercy. Our Sages read the Torah's story as a gift of love from Rachel, who waited alone for her children. Her story is told more fully in [Rachel, Our Mother](/learn/rachel-our-mother).

## Rachel's tears and G-d's promise

The prophet Jeremiah describes the scene: "A voice is heard on high, lamentation and bitter weeping; Rachel is weeping for her children" (Jeremiah 31:14). G-d answers that her deeds will be rewarded and that her children will return to their land.

A Midrash on Lamentations tells how the Patriarchs and Moses pleaded for mercy, and G-d was silent until Rachel spoke. She reminded G-d that she had given Leah the secret signs, so that her sister would not be shamed when Leah married Jacob. She asked G-d to show the same restraint toward Israel. G-d promised to bring the Jewish people back to their place for Rachel's sake.

The lesson is this: Rachel is the model of a Jewish mother. She sees the good in her children even when they are far from where they should be, and she never gives up on them. We can learn from her to look kindly at one another (see [Ahavat Yisrael](/learn/ahavat-yisrael)).

## The tomb through history

- **The first monument.** Jacob set up a monument on the grave. This is linked to the custom of leaving a stone when visiting a grave (see [Visiting a Grave](/learn/visiting-a-grave)).
- **Joseph.** According to the Midrash, young Joseph, being taken to Egypt, ran to his mother's grave and prayed there.
- **Sir Moses Montefiore.** In 1841, he and his wife, Judith, enlarged the structure with walls and a long room to shelter visitors. This is the building seen in most older pictures.
- **Closed and reopened.** From 1948 the site was closed to Jewish visitors. After 1967 it was opened to them again.

## Praying at Rachel's Tomb

Jews come to pour out their hearts to Rachel. Women who hope for a child often pray there, because Rachel herself was childless for many years. Inside, men and women pray on separate sides of the room. If you are a Kohen, ask the Rabbi about the halachic question of entering the compound. Before any trip, check current travel guidance for how to reach the site.

If you cannot go, you can pray where you are. Name a person who needs healing or help, and say a chapter of [Tehillim](/learn/tehillim). See also [Praying for Others](/learn/praying-for-others).

## Common questions

### Where is Rachel's Tomb?
It is on the road to Bethlehem, just south of Jerusalem. Rachel is buried there, not in the Cave of Machpelah in Hebron.

### Why was Rachel not buried with Jacob in Machpelah?
Jacob buried her on the road by G-d's command, so that she could comfort her children when they passed by in exile, as Rashi explains.

### What does "Rachel weeping for her children" mean?
It is Jeremiah's image of Rachel crying for the Jewish people in exile. G-d comforts her and promises that her children will come home.

### Why do women pray at Rachel's Tomb?
Rachel is the model of a devoted mother, and she was childless for many years before giving birth. Many women pray there, especially those hoping for children (see [Jewish Support for Infertility](/learn/infertility-jewish-support)).

### Can I pray to Rachel from California?
Jewish tradition is to pray to G-d, and to ask the Patriarchs and Matriarchs to plead for us. You can pray from anywhere, and the Rabbi can guide you.
