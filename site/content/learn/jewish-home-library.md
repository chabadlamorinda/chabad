A Jewish home library is a collection of holy books, called sefarim, kept in the home for prayer and study. Even a small shelf with a Chumash, a siddur and a book of Tehillim turns a house into a place of Torah.

## Why a Jewish library matters

The Torah is the heart of Jewish life, and books are how it lives in the home. A shelf of sefarim:

- Makes learning easy, any time.
- Shows children that Torah is part of everyday life.
- Brings holiness into the home.

The Lubavitcher Rebbe launched a campaign encouraging every Jewish home to have basic holy books, and every Jewish child to have their own small library of Jewish books. Like a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) on the door, holy books inside mark a home as a Jewish home.

## The essential books

Start with these:

1. **Chumash.** The Five Books of Moses, ideally with a translation and commentary such as Rashi. This is the foundation, used to follow the weekly [Torah portion](/parsha).
2. **Siddur.** The daily prayer book, for weekday, Shabbat and holiday prayers. A bilingual siddur helps beginners.
3. **[Tehillim](/learn/tehillim).** The Book of Psalms, said in times of need, joy and every day.
4. **Tanya.** The foundational book of Chabad Chassidus, a guide to the soul and serving G-d. See our guide to the [Tanya](/learn/tanya).

## Books to add next

- **Machzor:** prayer books for Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur.
- **Haggadah:** for the Passover Seder.
- **Tanach:** the full Hebrew Bible, including Prophets and Writings.
- **A guide to Jewish law:** such as the Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, a concise code of practical halacha.
- **Pirkei Avot:** "Ethics of the Fathers," wisdom about character and living well.
- **Mishnah and Talmud:** for those ready to go deeper.
- **Rambam's Mishneh Torah:** a complete code of Jewish law. The Rebbe instituted a daily study cycle of the Rambam.
- **Children's books:** Jewish stories, holiday books and picture books.

## A library for every child

The Rebbe encouraged that each child have their own Jewish books, including a siddur, Chumash and Tehillim, with their name inscribed, kept in their room. It helps children feel personal ownership of Torah.

## Caring for holy books

Holy books deserve respect. Common customs:

1. **Keep them off the floor**, and do not place them face down.
2. **Do not put other objects on top of a sefer.**
3. **Arrange by holiness:** a Chumash or Tanach is placed above other books.
4. **Close them when not in use.**
5. **Kiss a sefer** if it falls, and pick it up right away.
6. **When worn out**, do not throw them away. Worn holy books are placed in a genizah and buried respectfully. Contact us and we can help.

## Common questions

### Do the books have to be in Hebrew?
No. Translations are a wonderful way to learn. Many people use bilingual editions with Hebrew on one side and English on the other. What matters is that you learn.

### Can I use a phone app instead?
Apps are useful, especially when traveling. But physical books in the home have a presence that screens do not. Note also that apps cannot be used on Shabbat.

### Where should I keep the books?
Anywhere respectful, ideally in a bookcase in a main room where family members see them and use them. Avoid bathrooms and similar places.

### How do I start learning from these books?
Pick one, such as the weekly Torah portion or a short daily section of Tehillim, and set a regular time. A class can also help.
