A Jewish overnight camp is a sleepaway summer camp where children live together for a session of Jewish learning, Shabbat, kosher food and outdoor fun. Chabad.org describes camp as one of the strongest ways to build Jewish identity, and families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda can start with our own day camp for young children and ask the Rabbi about overnight options.

## Why Jewish camp matters

Chabad.org lists many reasons to send a child to Jewish summer camp. It says that informal, hands-on education helps build lasting Jewish bonds and identity, and that camp teaches Torah, mitzvahs and Jewish history, especially important for children who do not attend a Jewish school during the year. It also points to the joy: sing-alongs, hiking, plays, color war and friendships, and children who come home eager for next summer.

Chabad.org adds that the Chabad Gan Israel camp network operates in about seventy countries, and a Gan Israel directory lists hundreds of camps. A Chabad.org news report on one Gan Israel overnight camp described swimming, kayaking, hiking, drama, bonfires and time for prayer, with separate sessions for girls and boys. Details vary camp by camp, so always ask.

## What to look for

When you compare camps, ask these questions:

- **Kosher food.** Is all the food kosher, including snacks and trips? Chabad.org notes that good Jewish camp provides kosher meals that nourish body and soul.
- **Shabbat.** How is Shabbat kept, and what does a Shabbat at camp feel like? Is it welcoming to children from every background?
- **Jewish learning.** What do children learn, and is it joyful rather than a lecture?
- **Staff.** Who are the counselors, how are they chosen and trained, and what is the ratio of staff to campers?
- **Safety and health.** What are the medical care, water safety, supervision and communication policies?
- **Homesickness plan.** How does the camp help a child who misses home? Can parents write or call, and when?
- **Fit.** Does the camp suit your family's level of observance, and your child's personality?

## Is my child ready?

Readiness is about the child, not only the age. Ask yourself:

1. Has your child enjoyed sleepovers and time away from home?
2. Can your child manage basic routines: dressing, a shower, medicine if needed?
3. Does your child want to go? A child who chooses camp usually does best.
4. Would a shorter first session help?

Many children start with day camp, then move to overnight when they are ready. For toddlers and preschoolers in Lafayette, [Lamorinda Jewish Summer Camp](/programs/summer-camp) is for ages 2 to 4; see [Jewish summer camp in Lamorinda](/learn/jewish-summer-camp-in-lamorinda). For older children, the Rabbi can tell you what he knows about overnight camps; call 925-384-7242.

## Cost and timing

Camp is a real investment. Ask each camp what the fee covers (food, activities, trips, travel), whether there is financial aid, and what the deadlines are. Registration often opens in winter, and popular sessions can fill, so start looking early. Chabad.org's Gan Israel directory is one place to begin, and the Rabbi can help you ask the right questions.

## Preparing your child

- Visit or talk with the camp director before enrolling, and meet other families if possible.
- Practice a few nights away, such as with grandparents.
- Learn a few camp songs and the blessings together; see [Shabbat in Lamorinda](/learn/shabbat-in-lamorinda).
- Pack a note from home, labeled clothing and any medication as the camp instructs.

## Common questions

### What is a Jewish overnight camp?
A Jewish overnight camp is a sleepaway summer camp that includes Jewish learning, Shabbat and kosher food along with sports, nature and friendship.

### What should I look for in a Jewish sleepaway camp?
Look for kosher food, a meaningful Shabbat, caring and trained staff, clear safety rules and a plan for homesickness. Visit or speak with the director first.

### What age should my child start overnight camp?
It depends on the child. Look for comfort with sleepovers, self-care and a wish to go. Many start with day camp first.

### Does Chabad of Lamorinda run an overnight camp?
Our summer camp in Lafayette is a day camp for ages 2 to 4. For overnight camps, call the Rabbi at 925-384-7242 to ask.

### Is Gan Israel a Chabad camp?
Yes. Chabad.org describes the Gan Israel camp network as operating in about seventy countries.

### How do I handle homesickness?
Prepare together, ask how the camp helps children who miss home, and send a note from home. Talk with the Rabbi if you have a specific concern.
