Shevat (שבט) is the eleventh month of the Hebrew calendar, counting from Nissan, and it is best known for Tu BiShvat, the New Year for Trees on the 15th of the month. It falls in the heart of the winter rains, the season in which trees begin to wake up.

## What is Shevat?

The Torah does not name the months. It calls Nissan the first month, which makes Shevat the eleventh. The name first appears in the Book of Zechariah, and Chabad.org notes that it is likely related to the heavy rains of late winter, and rain is linked in Jewish tradition with blessing.

Its zodiac sign is Aquarius, the water-bearer, which fits the season. Shevat usually arrives in winter, after [Tevet](/learn/tevet), and leads towards [Adar](/learn/adar). For how months work, see [the Jewish calendar](/learn/jewish-calendar). ## Tu BiShvat: the New Year for Trees

On the 15th of Shevat (Tu BiShvat), trees begin a new year for purposes of tithes and fruit. The day is marked by eating fruits, especially those of the land of Israel, and by planting and appreciating nature.

Many families set a Tu BiShvat table. See [the Tu BiShvat seder](/learn/tu-bishvat-seder) and [the seven species](/learn/seven-species) for what to put on it. The holiday page is [Tu BiShvat](/holidays/tu-bishvat).

## Moses' last words, and the Torah in every language

Chabad.org tells that Moses spent Shevat preparing for his passing, which came the next month. In these weeks he spoke the words of the Book of Deuteronomy, including his rebuke of the people, out of love and respect for them.

A Midrash adds that Moses spent the month translating the Torah into the 70 major languages of the time. The lesson is that every Jew has a connection to the Torah, even without reading it in the original language. That is the spirit of our Torah classes in Lafayette: come as you are.

## Shabbat Shirah

The Shabbat on which the Torah portion Beshalach is read is Shabbat Shirah, the Shabbat of the Song of the Sea, and it usually falls in Shevat. Families put out food for the birds before Shabbat begins. See [Shabbat Shirah](/learn/shabbat-shirah).

## A few important days for Chabad

Chabad communities also mark 10 Shevat, the day on which Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn passed away in 1950 and, a year later, the Rebbe took on leadership of the movement, and 22 Shevat, the day on which the Rebbe's wife, Rebbetzin Chaya Mushka Schneerson, passed away in 1988. Chabad.org lists both among the facts of the month.
