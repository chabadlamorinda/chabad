Every marriage has disagreements. Judaism does not expect a couple never to argue; it teaches how to disagree with love, and how to come back to peace. The sages say that to make peace between husband and wife, G-d Himself was willing to let His holy name be erased. That is how precious [shalom bayit](/learn/shalom-bayit), peace in the home, is.

**If anyone in the home is afraid for their safety, call 911, or the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. Shalom bayit never means accepting abuse.**

## Honor first

The Talmud teaches that a husband should love his wife as himself and honor her more than himself, and a wife is taught to honor her husband. When both partners focus on giving honor rather than getting it, most fights lose their fuel.

## Arguing the right way

- **Pick the moment.** Not when someone is hungry, exhausted or rushing out the door, and never in front of the children or guests.
- **Speak about yourself.** "I felt hurt when..." works better than "You always...".
- **One issue at a time.** Leave old arguments in the past.
- **Listen to understand,** not to answer. Repeat back what you heard.
- **No insults, ever.** Words can be apologized for, but they are hard to forget. The Torah forbids hurting another with words (ona'at devarim).

## Coming back to peace

- **Apologize first,** even if you were only partly wrong. Aaron the High Priest was beloved because he made peace between people.
- **Give the benefit of the doubt.** Assume your spouse meant well.
- **Do something kind** after a fight: a coffee, a note, a favorite dinner.
- **Bring holiness into the home:** Shabbat candles, a Shabbat meal together, a [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) checked, the laws of family purity. Many couples find these bring a new warmth.

## When to get help

If the same fights repeat, if there is silence for days, or if one of you feels unheard, it is time to talk with someone. The Rabbi or the Rebbetzin can listen privately, give a Torah perspective, and help you find a good couples counselor if needed. Asking for help is a sign of how much you care.

## Common questions

### Is it a failure to see a marriage counselor?
Not at all. Investing in your marriage is a mitzvah. A good counselor who respects your values can make a big difference.

### My spouse doesn't want to talk to anyone. What can I do?
Start by yourself. Changing how one person responds often changes the whole dynamic. The Rabbi or Rebbetzin can talk with you alone.

### Should we stay together for the children?
Every situation is different and deserves careful guidance. Talk privately with the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin, and with a professional. See also [Jewish divorce](/learn/jewish-divorce).
