Shabbat begins at sunset on Friday, but its peace begins earlier, in the preparing. The sages taught, "Whoever toils on the eve of Shabbat will eat on Shabbat." Getting ready is not a chore before the mitzvah; it is part of it.

## The great sages cooked too

The Talmud tells that the greatest rabbis of their day prepared for Shabbat with their own hands: one would salt the fish, another would light the stove, another would chop vegetables. Shammai, whenever he found a fine food during the week, set it aside "for Shabbat." Honoring Shabbat means treating it as an honored guest, and you prepare for a guest yourself.

## A simple Friday checklist

**In the morning or earlier in the week**
- Plan the menu and shop: challah (or bake it, see [challah](/learn/challah)), wine or grape juice, food for Friday night and Shabbat lunch.
- Cook what you can ahead. Food for Shabbat day must be cooked before Shabbat and kept warm on a hot plate or blech. See [cholent](/learn/cholent).

**In the afternoon**
- **Set the table:** a white tablecloth if you have one, your nicest dishes, two challahs under a cover, the Kiddush cup.
- **Candles:** set them up and check the time. Candles are lit 18 minutes before sunset; see [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times) for Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda.
- **Lights and appliances:** decide which lights stay on, set timers, switch the fridge light off, leave the hot plate on.
- **Prepare clothes** and take a shower, so everyone greets Shabbat fresh and dressed for a special day.
- **Phones and screens:** finish your messages and put them away.

**Just before candle lighting**
- Put a few coins in the [pushka](/learn/pushka) for tzedakah.
- Gather the family, light the candles and say the blessing. See [lighting Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles).

## Preparing the heart

The busiest Friday can end in peace. Many people take a quiet moment before candle lighting to let go of the week: forgive, breathe, and welcome Shabbat with a smile. Chassidic teaching compares the week to the work of the field and Shabbat to the harvest: what we did all week comes into its own on Shabbat.

## When Friday is too short

In winter, Shabbat can begin before 5 PM. Do what you can ahead, keep the menu simple, and remember that one candle lit on time and a family meal are more important than a perfect table. See [Shabbat in winter](/learn/shabbat-in-winter) and [Shabbat for working parents](/learn/shabbat-for-working-parents).

## Common questions

### What time do I light Shabbat candles?
18 minutes before sunset on Friday. Our [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times) page shows the exact time each week for Lafayette, Moraga, Orinda and nearby towns.

### Can I cook on Shabbat itself?
No, cooking is one of the kinds of work we refrain from on Shabbat, so food is prepared beforehand and kept warm. See [the 39 categories of work](/learn/thirty-nine-melachot).

### I am running late and the time has passed. What should I do?
If the 18 minutes have passed but the sun has not yet set, light right away. Once the sun has set, do not light: Shabbat has begun; welcome it anyway with Kiddush and a meal, and next week start a little earlier. Ask the Rabbi if you have questions.
