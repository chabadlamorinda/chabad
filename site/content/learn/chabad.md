Chabad, also called Chabad-Lubavitch, is a worldwide Jewish movement rooted in Chassidic teachings, known for its warmth, its focus on Jewish learning and mitzvot, and its Chabad centers that welcome every Jew. Today Chabad serves Jews in more than 100 countries, from big cities to small towns.

## What does Chabad mean?

Chabad is an acronym for three Hebrew words for intellectual faculties:

- **Chochmah:** wisdom, the first spark of an idea.
- **Binah:** understanding, developing the idea in depth.
- **Daat:** knowledge, internalizing it so that it shapes feelings and actions.

This reflects Chabad's approach: understanding G-d and Judaism with the mind, so it can truly move the heart and guide daily life.

**Lubavitch** is the name of the town in Russia that was the movement's headquarters for over a century. It means "town of brotherly love."

## A short history

- **1775 or so:** Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, known as the Alter Rebbe, founded Chabad as a branch of the [Chassidic](/learn/chassidus) movement. He wrote the [Tanya](/learn/tanya), Chabad's foundational text.
- **1800s to 1900s:** a line of seven Rebbes led the movement through persecution under the Czars and the Soviets.
- **1940:** the sixth Rebbe, Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn, escaped Europe and arrived in New York, establishing headquarters at 770 Eastern Parkway in Brooklyn.
- **1951 to 1994:** the seventh Rebbe, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson, known simply as [the Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe), transformed Chabad into a global movement.

## What Chabad believes and values

- **Love for every Jew.** Every Jew is precious, without labels or judgment.
- **Every mitzvah counts.** Doing one more mitzvah, even a small one, makes a real difference.
- **Joy.** Judaism should be lived with happiness and enthusiasm.
- **Learning.** Understanding Torah and Chassidus deepens one's connection.
- **Preparing for [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach).** Every good deed brings the world closer to redemption.

## Shluchim: emissaries

Chabad centers are run by shluchim, couples who move to a community to serve it for life. They lead prayer services, holiday celebrations, Hebrew schools, classes and much more, and they are there in times of joy and sorrow.

## What to expect at a Chabad House

- **Everyone is welcome.** You do not need to be observant, affiliated or knowledgeable.
- **No membership required.** Most Chabad centers do not require membership dues to attend.
- **A home-like feel.** Many programs happen around a Shabbat table with good food and conversation.
- **Traditional practice.** Services follow traditional Jewish law, with prayer books that include English.

## Common questions

### Do I have to be Orthodox to go to Chabad?

No. Chabad welcomes every Jew, whatever their background or level of practice. Many people who come to Chabad events would not describe themselves as Orthodox.

### Is Chabad a separate denomination?

Chabad does not see itself as a denomination. It follows traditional Jewish law and serves all Jews, without labels.

### How is Chabad funded?

Each Chabad center is generally funded locally, through the generosity of its community.

### Are non-Jews welcome at Chabad?

Chabad's programs are designed for the Jewish community, and friends and family members are often welcome at events. Chabad also teaches the [Noahide laws](/learn/noahide-laws) for all people.
