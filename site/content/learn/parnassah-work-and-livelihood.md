"Parnassah" is the Jewish word for a livelihood, making a living. Judaism does not see work as something separate from spiritual life. Earning an honest living, supporting a family and building something good are themselves part of serving G-d.

## Work is a blessing

"Six days you shall work and do all your labor," says the Torah, before it says "and the seventh day is Shabbat." The Psalms add: "When you eat from the labor of your hands, you are happy, and it is good for you." The Sages taught that a parent must teach a child a trade, and that a person who lives from their own work is greater than one who only fears Heaven. Work brings dignity.

## Effort and faith together

Judaism asks us to do two things at once. We make a real effort: study, work hard, plan wisely. And we know that success comes from G-d, not only from our effort. Chassidic teaching compares our work to a "vessel": we build the vessel through our natural effort, and G-d fills it with blessing. That is why worry is not needed: we do our part and trust Him for the rest. See [bitachon, trust in G-d](/learn/bitachon).

## Honest business

The Talmud says that one of the first questions a person is asked after life is "Did you deal honestly in business?" Fair prices, honest scales, keeping your word and paying workers on time are all mitzvot. See [Jewish business ethics](/learn/business-ethics).

## Giving from what you earn

The Torah's custom is to give a tenth of one's income to tzedakah, called [maaser](/learn/maaser). Many people find that giving makes them feel richer, not poorer. The Sages read the verse "aser te'aser," "you shall surely tithe," as a promise: "give a tenth so that you will become wealthy."

## A day off every week

Shabbat is a gift for everyone who works. One day a week we stop: no emails, no deals, no work, and we remember that the world runs without us. Many people say Shabbat makes them better at their jobs, not worse.

## Blessing your work

- Put up a kosher [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) on your office or store door.
- Keep a [pushka](/learn/pushka), a charity box, at work.
- Pray for success in your own words, or say Psalm 145, which includes "You open Your hand and satisfy every living thing."
- Learn a few minutes of Torah before the workday, even on your phone.

## Common questions

### Is it wrong to want to be successful?
No. Wanting to provide for your family and build something good is healthy. The question is how: honestly, with balance, and remembering who gives success.

### What if I lost my job or my business is struggling?
It is hard, and you are not alone. Keep making efforts, keep up your prayers and tzedakah even if small, and talk to someone. The Rabbi and the Rebbetzin are here to listen and help; call 925-384-7242.

### Can I work on Shabbat if my job requires it?
Shabbat is very important, and there are often creative solutions. Every situation is different, so ask the Rabbi.
