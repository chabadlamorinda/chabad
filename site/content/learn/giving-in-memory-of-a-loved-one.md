Giving tzedakah in memory of a loved one is a way of doing for them a good deed they can no longer do themselves, and Chassidic teaching teaches that this brings merit to the soul and elevates it. It is done at the yahrzeit (the anniversary of the death), at Yizkor, and at any time you wish to remember someone.

## Why giving helps the soul

Chassidic philosophy explains that after death a person can no longer perform mitzvot, but the living can do so in their merit. Children, relatives and friends take on extra Torah learning (especially Mishnah), give to charity and increase good deeds *l'ilui nishmat* (לְעִלּוּי נִשְׁמַת, "for the elevation of the soul") and *l'zechut* (in the merit of) the person who passed away.

Chassidic teaching explains that on each yahrzeit the soul rises higher, and that every mitzvah done in its merit helps it ascend ever higher. Giving is a physical good deed done in this world, the kind of act the departed cannot do any longer.

## At the yahrzeit

Jewish tradition recommends giving extra charity on the yahrzeit, the anniversary of the passing, for the soul's elevation. The day is also a time to honor the person's memory.

Other customs on the yahrzeit include lighting a candle that burns for 24 hours, studying Mishnah, attending a minyan and saying Kaddish, and serving refreshments after services with a *l'chaim* in the person's memory. Some people sponsor a Kiddush on a nearby Shabbat. See [Yahrzeit](/learn/yahrzeit) and [Sponsor a Kiddush](/sponsor-a-kiddush).

## At Yizkor

The central part of Yizkor (יִזְכּוֹר), the memorial prayer, is a private pledge to give charity after the holiday in honor of the departed, made "without obligating myself with a vow." Chassidic philosophy explains that the soul gains merit when the memory of its good deeds inspires loved ones to do good. Yizkor is said in the synagogue four times a year: on Yom Kippur, Shemini Atzeret, the last day of Passover and the second day of Shavuot. See [Yizkor](/learn/yizkor).

## A fund, a plaque, a Kaddish

The tradition says that establishing a charitable fund or an institution with a positive goal in the merit of the deceased is "of particular merit." Giving can take many forms, and you can choose what fits your family.

## What to choose

There is no single right way. Some families give a set amount each year on the yahrzeit. Some give at Yizkor. Others dedicate part of their yearly giving to a cause that the person loved. ## Common questions

### How can I give tzedakah in memory of a loved one?
You can give charity on the yahrzeit, pledge at Yizkor, or set up a fund or program in their merit. Jewish tradition describes all of these.

### Does tzedakah really help the soul of someone who has passed away?
Chassidic teaching teaches that the living can bring merit to the departed through mitzvot, including charity, and that this elevates the soul. For personal questions, ask the Rabbi.

### What is the Yizkor charity pledge?
At Yizkor each person privately pledges to give charity after the holiday in memory of the departed, without making it a vow. It is the main part of the prayer.

### Can I arrange Kaddish if I cannot say it myself?
Chabad of Lamorinda can arrange Kaddish for a loved one. Use the form on the [Kaddish page](/kaddish) or call 925-384-7242.

### Can a memorial plaque be added in the synagogue?
Yes. A memorial plaque can be added in the synagogue at Chabad of Lamorinda. Please consult the Rabbi about it.

### Is giving in memory the same as maaser?
Giving to a worthy cause can be maaser. Ask the Rabbi how it applies to your situation. A donor-advised fund grant to Chabad of Lamorinda counts as maaser.
