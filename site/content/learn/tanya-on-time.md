The Tanya teaches that a person is free at any moment to shake off the "spirit of folly" that dulls him to G-d and to choose a good deed. Because the Divine light within the soul is not bound by time, an action that takes only a moment can be more momentous than one that takes much longer.

For busy families in Lafayette, Moraga and Orinda, who often feel there is never enough time, this is not a lesson in squeezing in more. It is a reminder that a moment's choice is real.

## "At any time and moment"

Chapter 25 of the Tanya says: "For at any time and moment, a person is capable and free to rid himself of the spirit of folly." The chapter says that every Jew, even someone who has sinned, has a hidden love of G-d, and that a person can choose to act on it at any moment. When a mitzvah is called for, that love moves him to do it. When temptation comes, it helps him resist.

The point is that you are never stuck in what you did yesterday. You may begin again now.

## Time does not rule the soul

Chapter 25 also says that the Divine light within the soul is not bound by time. It rules over time. That is why the chapter teaches that an action which took only a moment can be more momentous than one that takes much longer.

It also describes the connection a mitzvah creates. Above, the union is eternal, because G-d and His will are above time. Below, in this world, the bond lasts while we are engaged in Torah or a mitzvah. The chapter adds that idle, purposeless activity breaks the connection, and a sincere return and request for forgiveness restore it.

This does not mean every minute must be spent in study or prayer. The Tanya's point is that the way back is always open. See [teshuvah](/learn/teshuvah).

## One hour that counts

Chapter 4 quotes the Sages: "One hour of repentance and good deeds in this world is better than the whole life of the World to Come." In the World to Come, the chapter says, the soul receives a glimmer of the Divine radiance. In this world, through Torah and mitzvot, it is united with G-d Himself. So the hours of this life are precious.

## Starting the day, and starting again

Chapter 41 describes a way to begin the day: remember that G-d stands over you, thank Him for your soul and bring that readiness to prayer. See [what the Tanya says about prayer](/learn/tanya-on-prayer). The same chapter says that before a long period of study you should renew this preparation at least every hour, since each hour brings a different flow of spiritual energy. The idea is to begin again as often as you need to.

Chapter 11 of Igeret HaTeshuvah says: "Throughout one's days, one should experience teshuvah." Teshuvah, return, is not only for one season. It can be part of every day. For a guided look at the end of each day, see [cheshbon hanefesh, the nightly accounting](/learn/cheshbon-hanefesh).

## How to make a moment count

These are ideas that follow from the chapters above; how you use them is your choice:

- When you notice you have drifted, remember that you are free to choose again right now.
- Do one short mitzvah with your full attention, for example [a blessing before you eat](/learn/blessings). Chapter 25 teaches that an act of a moment can carry great weight.
- Begin the day with thanks. See [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani).
- Set aside time for Torah. See [Why Study Torah?](/learn/tanya-on-torah-study).

For the general Jewish view of time, see [making every day count](/learn/making-every-day-count) and [the purpose of life](/learn/purpose-of-life).

## Common questions

### What does the Tanya say about time?
The Tanya says that at any moment a person is free to choose to do good, and that the Divine light of the soul is not bound by time, so an act of a moment can carry great weight. Chapter 25 explains this.

### Can one moment really change a person?
Chapter 25 says a person can, at any time and moment, rid himself of the spirit of folly, and Chapter 4 quotes the Sages that one hour of repentance and good deeds in this world is better than the whole life of the World to Come.

### How do I stop wasting time, according to Judaism?
The Tanya does not give a time-management system. It says idle, purposeless activity breaks the connection to G-d and that returning sincerely restores it. Ask the Rabbi for personal guidance.

### Is it too late to start?
No. Teshuvah is available at every moment. Igeret HaTeshuvah says one should experience teshuvah throughout one's days.

### How should I start the day, according to Tanya?
Chapter 41 describes remembering that G-d stands over you and thanking Him for your soul before you pray. Many people say [Modeh Ani](/learn/modeh-ani) as soon as they wake.
