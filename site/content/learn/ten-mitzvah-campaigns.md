The ten mitzvah campaigns, or *mivtzoim*, are ten mitzvot that the Lubavitcher Rebbe, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson, asked every Jew to take on and to share with others. Each one is simple enough to start today, and each one opens a door to a deeper Jewish life.

## Why the Rebbe chose these ten

Beginning with the tefillin campaign in 1967, and through the 1970s, [the Rebbe](/learn/the-rebbe) called on his followers to reach out to every Jew with one practical mitzvah at a time. He taught that a single mitzvah, done even once, creates an eternal bond between a Jew and G-d, and that each good deed can tip the balance of the whole world toward good. The ten he chose touch every part of life: the heart, the mind, the body, the home and the family. This is the spirit behind Chabad's [Mitzvah Tanks](/learn/mitzvah-tanks).

## The ten campaigns, with a first step for each

**1. Ahavat Yisrael: love for every Jew.**
"Love your fellow as yourself," Rabbi Akiva taught, is a great principle of the Torah. *First step:* do one kind thing today for someone who does not expect it.

**2. Chinuch: Jewish education.**
Every Jewish child deserves a Jewish education, the key to the Jewish future. *First step:* teach a child a blessing, or look into [Hebrew school](/programs/hebrew-school).

**3. Torah study.**
Torah is G-d's wisdom, and learning it connects our minds to Him. *First step:* set aside a few minutes a day, even one verse. See [daily Torah study](/learn/daily-torah-study).

**4. Tefillin.**
Jewish men from bar mitzvah age put on [tefillin](/learn/tefillin) every weekday, binding head, heart and hand to G-d. *First step:* put on tefillin once, with a friend or a rabbi to guide you.

**5. Mezuzah.**
A kosher [mezuzah](/learn/mezuzah) on every doorway marks a Jewish home and brings G-d's protection. *First step:* put one on your front door, or have yours checked.

**6. Tzedakah.**
Giving charity daily makes kindness a habit. *First step:* place a [pushka](/learn/pushka) (charity box) in your kitchen and drop in a coin each weekday.

**7. A home filled with Jewish books.**
Every home should have at least a Chumash (Five Books of Moses), a siddur and a book of Tehillim. *First step:* see our guide to the [Jewish home library](/learn/jewish-home-library).

**8. Shabbat and holiday candles.**
Jewish women and girls light [Shabbat candles](/learn/shabbat-candles) before sunset every Friday and before holidays, bringing light and peace into the home. *First step:* light this Friday. Find the time on our [Shabbat times](/shabbat-times) page.

**9. Kosher.**
Eating kosher food nourishes the soul as well as the body. *First step:* take on one step, such as not mixing meat and milk. See our guide to [kosher](/learn/kosher).

**10. Family purity.**
The laws of *taharat hamishpacha*, centered on the [mikvah](/learn/mikvah), bring holiness and renewal to a marriage. *First step:* learn about them privately with the Rebbetzin.

## Other campaigns

The Rebbe later added more, such as uniting every Jewish child through a letter in a children's Torah scroll, sharing the [Noahide laws](/learn/noahide-laws) with all humanity, and learning about [Moshiach](/learn/moshiach).

## Common questions

### Do I have to do all ten?
No. The Rebbe taught that every mitzvah stands on its own and is precious. Start with one that speaks to you, and add another when you are ready.

### Can I do a mitzvah if I am not observant?
Yes. A mitzvah is never "all or nothing." Every Jew can do every mitzvah, whatever their background or level of observance.

### Why share mitzvahs with others?
The Rebbe taught that we are all responsible for one another. Helping another Jew do a mitzvah is a mitzvah of its own and an act of love.
