Cleaning for Passover means removing chametz (leavened wheat, barley, spelt, oats and rye foods) from the places where it might be, not scrubbing the whole house. Dirt and dust are not chametz, so Passover cleaning is not spring cleaning: a thorough search of the kitchen, car, bags and pockets matters far more than the windows and the ceiling fan.

## Chametz, not dirt

Chametz (חָמֵץ) is food made from wheat, barley, spelt, oats or rye that has risen or come in contact with water and fermented. During Passover we may not eat it, own it or benefit from it. See [what chametz is](/learn/chametz). The mitzvah of cleaning is to make sure there is none where we live.

That is a relief. You do not need to wash the walls, clean the closets where food never goes, or take apart the house. If it is a place chametz might have been brought, check it. If not, leave it.

## A room-by-room plan

- **Bedrooms and play rooms.** Check where food or snacks were eaten: under beds and cushions, toy boxes, school bags. Vacuum and shake out.
- **Living and dining rooms.** Vacuum couches and chairs, especially where children eat. Wipe tables and dining chairs. Check books and papers if food was eaten over them.
- **Bathrooms and the rest of the house.** Only where food was brought (children's vitamins and snacks, for instance). Ask the Rabbi about medicines and cosmetics that contain chametz.
- **The car.** Vacuum seats, cup holders and the trunk; children's snack crumbs hide everywhere.
- **Office, handbags, backpacks and pockets.** Chametz gets everywhere. Empty each one.
- **The kitchen last.** Cabinets, drawers, the refrigerator, the freezer, counters, the sink, the stovetop and the oven. This is where the work is. Many families set aside a corner of the kitchen with their chametz and seal it, then sell it.

## Special help for the kitchen

Surfaces used for chametz, such as counters, sinks, stovetop and oven, are cleaned thoroughly, and some also need to be made kosher for Passover (kashered) or covered. The methods depend on the material and the item: ask the Rabbi. For more on dishes, see [kosher for Passover](/learn/kosher-for-passover).

## A 10-day schedule

Count back 10 days from the night of the search for chametz (the evening before Passover eve; ask the Rabbi when it falls this year) and give each day a task:

1. Days 10 to 8: closets, drawers, bookshelves, toys.
2. Days 7 to 6: bedrooms, living rooms, the car.
3. Days 5 to 4: bathrooms, bags, the office.
4. Days 3 to 2: the kitchen, cabinets and refrigerator.
5. Day 1: the oven, the final wipe and covering the counters.

Then comes the night search for chametz with a candle and a feather (Bedikat Chametz), and the burning of the chametz in the morning. Ask the Rabbi how to do it and when the time to stop eating chametz is this year; see also [selling your chametz](/learn/selling-your-chametz).

## Cleaning with young children

Let the children take part: a small bucket, a feather, a flashlight. Hide ten pieces of bread for the search, a tradition many families enjoy. And remember that the rule "dirt is not chametz" means we are allowed to stop. See [Passover with kids](/learn/passover-with-kids).

## The meaning behind the cleaning

Chassidic teaching sees chametz as ego and pride, the puffed-up "I" that rises like dough. Matzah is humble. Cleaning the home is a chance to clean the heart: what do I carry that I can let go of? That is why even a modest, careful cleaning is a spiritual preparation.

## Common questions

### How do I clean my house for Passover?
Remove all chametz from the places it might have been brought, starting with bedrooms and cars, then the kitchen, which is the most thorough. You do not have to clean the whole house.

### Is Passover cleaning the same as spring cleaning?
No. Dirt and dust are not chametz. The mitzvah is to remove chametz, so a clean closet with no food in it is already fine.

### Do I have to clean my car for Passover?
If food was eaten in the car, yes: vacuum it for crumbs and check the places children eat.

### How do I get my kitchen ready for Passover?
Clean cabinets, drawers, the refrigerator, counters, sink, stove and oven, then cover or kasher the surfaces as needed. Ask the Rabbi about each appliance.
