These are the same details as on our [legacy page](/legacy). "Chabad of Lamorinda" is the name to use in wills, trusts and beneficiary forms; your attorney should confirm the exact legal name and form when drafting.

## Three things to know before you begin

- **You may leave as much as you wish, even your entire estate.** The limit of a fifth on giving applies during life, not at death, so an entire estate can be left to Chabad of Lamorinda. See [How Much Should I Leave to Tzedakah in My Will?](/learn/how-much-to-leave-to-tzedakah-in-a-will).
- **A legacy gift counts as maaser,** the tenth of income given to tzedakah. See [Maaser](/learn/maaser).
- **A gift can carry a name.** Chabad of Lamorinda gladly offers dedication opportunities, such as dedicating a room or a [memorial plaque in the synagogue](/learn/memorial-plaque-in-the-synagogue). Consult the Rabbi before you write a dedication into your will, so it can be agreed in advance.

## Sample clauses for a will or living trust

Fill in the blanks, and let your attorney fit the wording to your plan and to the law of your state. In a living trust, your attorney will usually write "the trustee shall distribute" in place of "I give and bequeath."

**(a) A specific sum**

> I give and bequeath to Chabad of Lamorinda, a tax-exempt organization described in Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code (EIN 47-4642750), 3477 Golden Gate Way, Lafayette, California 94549, the sum of ________ dollars ($________), to be used for its general purposes.

**(b) A percentage of the estate**

> I give and bequeath to Chabad of Lamorinda, a tax-exempt organization described in Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code (EIN 47-4642750), 3477 Golden Gate Way, Lafayette, California 94549, ________ percent (____%) of my estate, to be used for its general purposes.

**(c) The entire residuary estate, or the entire estate**

> I give all the rest, residue and remainder of my estate, of whatever kind and wherever situated, to Chabad of Lamorinda, a tax-exempt organization described in Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code (EIN 47-4642750), 3477 Golden Gate Way, Lafayette, California 94549, to be used for its general purposes.

To leave everything, your attorney can make this the gift of your entire estate.

**(d) A gift in memory or in honor of someone**

> I give and bequeath to Chabad of Lamorinda, a tax-exempt organization described in Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code (EIN 47-4642750), 3477 Golden Gate Way, Lafayette, California 94549, the sum of ________ dollars ($________) [or ________ percent of my estate], in loving memory of [in honor of] ________, to be used for Jewish education for future generations [or: for its general purposes].

**(e) A gift for a named dedication, such as a room**

> I give and bequeath to Chabad of Lamorinda, a tax-exempt organization described in Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code (EIN 47-4642750), 3477 Golden Gate Way, Lafayette, California 94549, the sum of ________ dollars ($________), to dedicate ________ (such as a room) in the name of ________, as agreed with Chabad of Lamorinda. If the dedication cannot be carried out, the gift shall be used for its general purposes.

**(f) Chabad of Lamorinda as a successor (contingent) beneficiary**

> If ________ does not survive me, I give the share that would have passed to that beneficiary to Chabad of Lamorinda, a tax-exempt organization described in Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code (EIN 47-4642750), 3477 Golden Gate Way, Lafayette, California 94549, to be used for its general purposes.

## Beneficiary forms, stock and donor-advised funds

These gifts usually do not need a new will. The account's own form, or a short instruction, does the work.

**(g) A retirement account (IRA, 401(k), 403(b)) or life insurance**

> Beneficiary: Chabad of Lamorinda, 3477 Golden Gate Way, Lafayette, CA 94549, a 501(c)(3) organization, EIN 47-4642750. Share: ____%.

Write this on the beneficiary designation form from the account custodian or insurer. See [Leaving Your IRA or 401(k) to a Jewish Cause](/learn/leaving-your-ira-to-a-jewish-cause) and [Giving Through Life Insurance](/learn/life-insurance-gift-to-charity).

**(h) A gift of stock or securities**

> I give and bequeath to Chabad of Lamorinda, a tax-exempt organization described in Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code (EIN 47-4642750), 3477 Golden Gate Way, Lafayette, California 94549, ________ shares of ________ [or all of my shares of ________], to be used for its general purposes.

Chabad of Lamorinda has a Fidelity brokerage account for gifts of stock. When the time comes, your executor, trustee or advisor calls the Rabbi or the Rebbetzin, and we will give them the transfer details. See [Giving Stock to Chabad](/learn/giving-stock-to-chabad).

**(i) A donor-advised fund recommendation**

> Upon my death, I recommend that ________ percent (____%) of the balance of my donor-advised fund, ________ [fund name and account], be granted to Chabad of Lamorinda, 3477 Golden Gate Way, Lafayette, CA 94549, a 501(c)(3) organization, EIN 47-4642750.

Use your fund's own successor or beneficiary form for this. A donor-advised fund grant counts as maaser. See [Your Donor-Advised Fund After You](/learn/donor-advised-fund-successor-chabad).

## Common questions

### Can I leave my entire estate to Chabad?
Yes. The limit of a fifth on giving applies during life, not at death, so an entire estate can be left to Chabad of Lamorinda. Use clause (c) as a starting point with your attorney.

### Does the limit of a fifth apply to a bequest?
No. The fifth (chomesh) is a limit on giving during life; it does not apply at death.

### Does a legacy gift count as maaser?
Yes. A legacy gift counts as maaser, and so does a donor-advised fund grant. Ask your tax advisor about the tax side.

### Can I name a room or make a dedication?
Yes. Chabad of Lamorinda gladly offers dedication opportunities, such as dedicating a room or a memorial plaque in the synagogue. Contact the Rabbi to discuss it before you write it into your will.

### Do I have to tell the Rabbi about my bequest?
No, it is your choice. But he would be honored to thank you and to make sure your gift is used as you wish.
